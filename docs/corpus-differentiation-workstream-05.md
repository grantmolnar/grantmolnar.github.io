# Corpus Differentiation Workstream 5: Adversary and Institutional Voice Audit

## Judgment

Workstream 5 is complete. The six audited cases already differed in plot function and degree of culpability, but current GM-facing prose repeatedly stepped outside the fiction to deliver a similar verdict: an official or antagonist had committed serious harm, retained practical value, could help under constraint, and would answer later. That cadence made unlike figures sound as though they occupied one moral category.

The repair removes the shared adjudicative summary and assigns each case a distinct behavior under evidence, bargaining, public exposure, and loss of control. It preserves mixed institutions where they are structurally necessary, but it does not turn technical usefulness into moral credit or make every adversary capable of the same negotiated rehabilitation.

## Comparative diagnosis

The comparison asked five questions of each case:

1. What fact will the adversary concede, and in what form?
2. What does the adversary do when the conceded fact threatens authority?
3. What kind of bargain remains possible?
4. What changes under public exposure?
5. What practical usefulness, if any, survives loss of command?

The prior sources contained strong answers, but repeated phrases such as “can help and still answer,” “reasons are real,” “may remain under bounded authority,” and “the good was real” often replaced those answers with a common editorial verdict. The repair moves judgment back into observable conduct.

## Distinct conduct

### Corven Dast

Dast treats verified danger as a proposed service cut and every signature as a transfer of blame. He writes a reading on the station slate, demands the ward, cistern, or crew to be deprived beside it, and treats an unsigned order as proof that only he is willing to govern. He follows executable reductions exactly while recording their costs against the person who signed. His remaining utility is narrow and physical: bypasses, keys, and the service conduit. If he works the Bell, he must speak every affected corridor by its inhabited name while another officer repeats the order and removes the key.

### Kest Mourne

Kest Mourne does not admit institutional guilt through Edda Marr's remorse. A fact becomes admitted only when it enters all six sector ledgers, travels down the command tubes, changes the wall map, and alters a standing order. The finale makes the admission physical: duplicate minutes are released, the false southern map is removed, a corrected plate is mounted, and southern keys pass to mixed Kest Mourne and Lorn crews.

### Captain Dorion Vey

Dorion never enlarges Kallias's warrant in private or pretends it says less than it does. He writes each concession twice, names witnesses and effective hours, protects Ianthe from assassins because his first order requires a living princess, and resumes the custody demand at the exact clause where shared danger interrupted it. Once Myrine records its third judgment, he stops physical recovery inside sanctuary. His literal obedience makes him reliable within a bad warrant and dangerous at its edge.

### Orlo Vane

Orlo concedes one object at a time and revises the sentence around it. He never volunteers the next fact. When the ghost hearths can no longer be denied, he asks that beneficiary names be read aloud even though sealed warrant numbers prove the scheme, then asks recipient families to stand so judgment appears to take food from them. The relief was real. His murder preserved authorship, sole discretion, and the right to decide which rule became mercy.

### Lord Mael Taran

Mael does not hide the Great Muster or seek absolution. He annexes safeguards. Every danger becomes an argument that one more key, keeper, witness, wagon, or dead voice must pass beneath his hand. When challenged, he demands an alternative command structure with named offices, keys, expiry, and an executable defense order. If one is supplied, he tests every clause for a seat, countersignature, override, or custody share that keeps him inside it. Any negotiated role exists only where the final covenant explicitly places him.

### Judith Crowl

Judith's useful care is not an independent public good she later corrupted. She made beds, medicines, meals, and receiving forms effective because they place frightened people inside rooms, records, and obligations she can inventory. Public exposure does not move her toward confession or reform; it identifies the first relationship she will punish. She never offers restitution, shared custody, or surrender of a claim. Every concession substitutes a new victim or preserves her title to the old one.
