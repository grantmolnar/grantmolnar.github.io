# Beasts, Grave, and Wells Operating Aid

Use this sheet when the party enters the Menagerie, King's Narrow Grave, or Counterweight Wells. Record persistent consequences in `DUNGEON-STATE.md`.

## Quiet-beast handling

Every release needs:

1. **Appetite:** the exact excess to consume.
2. **Boundary:** where the work ends.
3. **Return:** the trail or object leading to a prepared resting place.

A failed operation widens the appetite, breaks the boundary, or severs the return. Do not default to generic hostility.

## Wells operating principle

Every operation needs three separate answers:

- **Mass:** enough equivalent physical or magical load.
- **Path:** the chain, frame, water, memory, institution, or population receiving it.
- **Timing:** the safe sequence of brakes, pulses, and releases.

### Three wells

| Well | Current load problem | Principal controls | Typical gains | Principal permanent loss if dropped |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Royal | Too light after Queen's chain cut | Crown Brake, royal sequence, Crown Drum | Crown repair or unloading, upper frame control | Royal lift and return routes, clean restoration, upper-frame stability |
| Civic | Overloaded by shifted surface and Low Choir burdens | Six-arm distributor, Chapel frame, Ropeworks links | Common Measure, distributed settlement, surface stabilization | Distributor, buried civic infrastructure, long-term redistribution |
| Funerary | Water and memory out of phase | Mourning Brake, saints, Iron Choir timing | Memory return, water balance, Low Choir toll relief | Stored memory, memorial vessels, lower pressure margin |

### Operator capacity

- One operator: hold one brake.
- Two trained operators: conduct one local transfer.
- Three crews: conduct a coordinated three-well movement.
- Substitutes: appeal wing, Warden detail, quiet beast, Ropeworks drum, Reliquary saint, or destructive one-way release.

## Standard Wells operations

| Operation | Necessary preparation | Immediate gain | Cost or displaced burden |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Brace one well | Shaft dogs, chain wraps, water, or beast-assisted brake | Stable platform and time | Route or transfer disabled; frame takes slow strain |
| Exchange load | Receiving brake set, path open, timed release | Rebalances selected systems | Named receiver takes the difference |
| Install accepted burden | Chapel-recognized frame, fittings, mass, operators | Opens replacement path | Missing declaration arm becomes a concrete future defect |
| Remove Crown load | Receiving mass or deliberate drop | Safer Crown extraction | New path must carry royal load |
| Emergency human hold | Pilgrim's Yoke, consent, relief, medical support | Short brake reset window | Bodily strain and coercion risk |
| Drop a well | Final dogs accessible, destination path open | Absorbs a major strain event | Permanent route, asset, memory, or settlement loss |

## Drop decision

Before releasing final dogs, state aloud:

1. what immediate strain the drop absorbs;
2. which routes and assets will be destroyed;
3. which people, memories, or institutions are exposed;
4. which finale architectures remain; and
5. who accepts the decision and who merely suffers it.
