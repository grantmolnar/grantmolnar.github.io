# Extraction, Salvage, and Safe Camp Aid

The dungeon's valuable objects are also weights, evidence, tools, memories, people, and claims. Extraction should therefore be adjudicated through hands, routes, custody, and displaced load rather than a generic treasure total.

## The extraction questions

For every substantial removal, answer:

1. **What is being moved?** Person, body, memory, tool, structural mass, evidence, food, or luxury.
2. **Who carries or operates it?** Name the people, beast, ghost crew, hoist, saint, or counterweight.
3. **Which route is used in its current state?** A route that existed earlier may now be flooded, inverted, claimed, or carrying another load.
4. **What was the object doing before removal?** Replace structural mass, custodial work, light, pressure, food, or testimony where necessary.
5. **Who claims it and who witnesses the claim?** Custody is not ownership, and evidence is not ordinary salvage.
6. **What is delayed while extraction occurs?** Advancing pressure is often the true price.

## Broad load classes

These classes are descriptive and can be mapped to any rules system.

| Class | Examples | Ordinary requirement | Typical complication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hand load | Tablet, flask, small tool, single memory cup | One free hand or secure container | Prevents climbing, operating, or helping simultaneously. |
| Person load | Injured survivor, Warden shell, trapped bearer | One dedicated carrier or two supporting helpers | The carried person has needs, testimony, and route preferences. |
| Team load | Common Measure frame, copper saint part, crown-bar | Several people, a litter, hoist, or strong mechanism | Requires coordination and occupies a route. |
| Structural load | Multiple crown-bars, major chain, counterweight mass | Prepared haul system and replacement weight | Removal changes the room immediately. |
| Distributed load | Water pressure, stored memory, accepted duty | A route, recipient, or maintained system | Cannot be placed in a sack; follows the nearest open path. |

A rescued person should change later play when plausible. A surgeon can stabilize mnemonic injury. A lamplighter changes the oil economy. A rigger can operate the Chapel. A porter may expose a contract or carry heavy salvage. Rescue is not only a count of survivors.

## Salvage categories

### Operating assets

Examples include ward-oil, emergency seals, splice links, brake sequences, silver-root fiber, the Pilgrim's Yoke, and quiet-beast handling equipment.

- Their value is highest when used.
- Selling or hoarding them should remove a concrete capability.
- If copied, distinguish knowledge from the physical fitting needed to act.

### Structural and replacement mass

Examples include crown-bars, bone loam, water weight, chain sections, and trial crown segments.

- Mass alone does not establish a load path or correct timing.
- Removing mass requires replacement or an immediate state change.
- A valuable bar installed in a Well is not lost economically; it has become public infrastructure and a custody problem.

### Evidence

Examples include Orren's rider, debt records, genealogy chains, Warden testimony, and identified memory.

- Record chain of custody and any copies.
- Evidence can be true but incomplete, physically load-bearing, or dangerous to its owner.
- Public disclosure, protected custody, and operational use are separate decisions.

### Personal and ceremonial treasure

Examples include royal grave goods, jeweled fittings, reliquary silver, Company pay, and House Vey objects.

- This is the safest category for conventional reward.
- It may still belong to a household, trust, grave, or unpaid worker.
- Taking it should not silently remove a necessary counterweight; check the room description first.

### Memory and names

- A memory has an owner, subject, carrier, witnesses, and possible public significance.
- Finding a memory does not grant the right to expose or install it.
- Copying may split testimony from personal recollection.
- Returning a memory can create gratitude, contradiction, grief, or a new claimant.

### Living tools and continuing persons

Warden shells, tablets, quiet beasts, ghosts, and inherited names are not ordinary equipment.

- Ask about consent, appetite, boundary, return, and continuity.
- A useful being may refuse the use for which it was created.
- A dead or partial person may still be property under someone's law; the adventure does not require the party to accept that law as moral fact.

## Caches

A cache is safe only if its room state, custodian, and replacement role are named.

Record:

- contents;
- exact location and concealment;
- local state required for access;
- custodian or witness;
- whether the cached objects were serving as weight or tools;
- what event threatens the cache; and
- the intended retrieval route.

A cache should create future play. Rivals may find it, water may move it, a faction may claim it, or the room may require it before the party returns.

## Safe camps

A camp is an operating achievement, not a pause button. It must secure the local system as well as the doors.

### Chapterhouse camp

**Requirements:** stable roof section, surface watch, current vent and evacuation information.

**Advantages:** resupply, public communication, ordinary medical care, secure evidence copies.

**Risk:** surface authorities issue new instructions, rivals enter another route, and the tremor clock advances normally.

### Ropeworks camp

**Requirements:** at least one drum braced, ghost rhythm understood, two routes controlled.

**Advantages:** central access and heavy extraction capacity.

**Risk:** sleeping crews may become part of a repeated work sequence; every lower state change reaches the drums.

### Lantern or Reliquary camp

**Requirements:** stable water, one lit or sealed route, smoke outlet, one oil measure or durable seal, watch assigned to a control.

**Advantages:** delayed tremor advance once, clean water or breath support, access to rescued specialists.

**Risk:** wick-eaters gather at the boundary; memory vapor spreads through sleepers.

### Barracks camp

**Requirements:** recognized truce or command, tablet custody agreement, salt protection.

**Advantages:** disciplined watch, repair work, Warden crews.

**Risk:** rest becomes factional endorsement; deep orders may arrive during sleep.

### Feast camp

**Requirements:** household invitation, food and water accounting, protection of cups and names.

**Advantages:** best social recovery, memory stabilization, compact work, safe care for rescued people.

**Risk:** the party consumes household resources and imports pursuit or toll pressure.

### Chapel camp

**Requirements:** stable orientation, set brakes, no person bound into an unattended station.

**Advantages:** access to several regions and review of burdens or agreements.

**Risk:** a remote load change rotates the room; the Chapel assigns sleeping people to roles.

### Wells camp

**Requirements:** all three gauges readable, brakes assigned, no unresolved slipping path.

**Advantages:** ideal for final preparation and heavy equipment.

**Risk:** every major lower change demands an operator; there is no passive watch.

### Choir or Black Rain camp

These are emergency stations, not ordinary camps. Rest is possible only after commands and pressure routes are bounded. A sleeper may become an address, recipient, or nearest open path.

## Rest consequences

When a full rest occurs, choose the consequence that follows the current state.

- advance the tremor sequence;
- consume one oil measure, food allotment, or brace interval;
- move a rival expedition;
- worsen one trapped person's condition;
- change one water or light state;
- let one faction issue an order or claim a route;
- expose a cache;
- release a memory fragment;
- migrate a creature; or
- require a camp operator to forgo full recovery because the system needs continuous attention.

A secured camp may prevent or soften one consequence. It should not freeze the whole dungeon.

## Reward without system-specific prices

Use several forms of reward:

- immediately useful operating assets;
- conventional portable wealth;
- public or factional credit;
- custody or access rights;
- rescued specialists;
- evidence that changes future negotiations;
- restored names or memories;
- routes and safe camps;
- maintenance funds; and
- the right to define what happens to a dangerous artifact.

The final reward should follow the chosen ending. A party that spends the treasury on civic infrastructure has not “lost the treasure”; it has converted portable wealth into political leverage, public safety, and future obligations.
