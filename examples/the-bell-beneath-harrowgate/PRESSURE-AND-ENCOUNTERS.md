# Pressure and Encounter Procedures

Use this aid to keep the dungeon active between authored set pieces. These procedures are system-agnostic. They determine what changes, who arrives, and what is at risk; the table's chosen rules system determines exact checks, damage, initiative, and recovery.

## When pressure advances

Advance the pressure state when one of the following occurs:

- the party takes a full rest without a secured operating camp;
- a major operation fails in a way that leaves force unassigned;
- a loud conflict continues after its objective has already been decided;
- the party moves substantial salvage without preparing a route;
- an open water, memory, light, or load path is left unattended;
- a faction receives evidence that changes its interests; or
- the GM needs to show that another active group has used the time.

Do not advance pressure merely because the players discuss a hard decision. Advance it when the fiction says systems, people, or rivals had time to act.

## The four pressure questions

Whenever pressure advances, answer these in order:

1. **Which system moves?** Water, ward-light, memory, counterweight, or fault pressure.
2. **Which route receives it?** Name the region or population. Never say only that pressure rises.
3. **Who notices or exploits the change?** Choose a faction, survivor, creature, or mechanism.
4. **What new choice appears?** A pressure event should create a decision, not only subtract resources.

## Tremor sequence

The dungeon begins after the third uncontrolled toll. Track broad steps rather than minutes.

| Step | Physical sign | Human consequence | GM use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0: Aftershock | Dust falls upward; chains tick; water reverses in cups. | Survivors can still be rescued from starting positions. | Establish routes and immediate urgency. |
| 1: Drawing strain | Lamps gutter toward dark branches; upper water surges. | Air pockets, injured delvers, and unprotected memories become unstable. | Force the first rescue-versus-preparation choice. |
| 2: Cross-load | One system begins carrying another's work. | Factions move to secure crews, records, or exits. | Introduce changed-room consequences and rival movement. |
| 3: Crown descent begins | Royal chains tighten; black rain rises; the Bell's approaches wake. | Surface cracks widen and Low Choir memory loss accelerates. | Final preparations must name a route and a population. |
| 4: Sounding | The Bell attempts a complete pressure release. | Every unbounded path receives force. | Resolve a prepared finale or cascading survival. |

Prepared operations can delay a step, repeat a step under controlled conditions, or redirect its consequence. They should not erase the clock without replacing it with a maintained arrangement.

## General pressure event table

| 3 | A load ghost repeats a fatal operation with one step corrected. | Learn from it, interrupt it, or recruit it as temporary labor. |
| 4 | A faction crew arrives with a valid but limited order. | Cooperate, revise the order, conceal the current state, or refuse passage. |
| 5 | A rescue signal comes from a route the party planned to sacrifice. | Delay, delegate, alter the plan, or record the abandonment. |
| 6 | A portable asset becomes a necessary local counterweight. | Leave it, replace it, accept route failure, or move the burden to a person. |
| 7 | A remembered name no longer matches the person who answers. | Restore, witness uncertainty, protect both claimants, or choose continuity. |
| 8 | Rivals move treasure through an unsafe route. | Intercept them, help them, let the route fail, or bargain for the useful mass. |

| 10 | A safe route becomes physically easier and politically controlled. | Accept terms, open an alternate route, force passage, or create witnesses. |

## Reaction posture

Most dungeon actors begin with a purpose rather than a fixed attitude. Determine posture from what they see the party doing.

| Posture | Signs | What changes it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cooperative | Offers a route, warning, or bounded labor. | Breaking terms, concealing consequences, or treating a person as equipment. |
| Bargaining | Names a price, witness, duty, or custody condition. | Concrete reciprocal terms or proof that delay is more dangerous. |
| Guarded | Observes, blocks one route, or asks for authority. | Shared evidence, rescue, recognized standing, or a visible common threat. |
| Adversarial | Seizes controls, closes routes, or attacks an objective. | Loss of command, proof of manipulation, a credible exit, or a superior claim. |
| Desperate | Risks self, room, or bystanders for one immediate aim. | Rescue capacity, a substitute route, or proof that the aim survives delay. |
| Compelled | Repeats a mechanism, oath, appetite, or inherited role. | Correct address, fulfilled procedure, interruption of the compulsion, or release. |

A successful social approach should usually change posture or terms. It need not produce friendship.

## Encounter objectives

Combat-capable scenes should have an objective beyond defeating opponents. Use one or more:

- cross before the route changes;
- hold a mechanism through one phase;
- rescue or extract a named person;
- preserve evidence or structural mass;
- prevent a broad command from reaching its address;
- move a creature toward a safe appetite target;
- keep a brake, saint, or shutter in a chosen state;
- survive long enough for another team to complete an operation;
- force a faction to abandon exclusive control; or
- retreat without losing the route, witness, or carried burden.

Opponents may remain active after the objective is won. That is often preferable to turning every conflict into extermination.

## Faction movement

When a faction gains time, give it one concrete action.

### Ember Company

- rescues a stranded crew;
- moves salvage toward Orren or Maelin;
- braces a route for later extraction;
- destroys or preserves contract evidence;
- abandons an unsafe command; or
- claims that labor already performed creates ownership.

### Salt Wardens

- closes or opens one gate;
- secures a tablet or shell;
- issues one bounded or overbroad command;
- assigns a crew to Wells, Garden, Chapel, or pursuit;
- challenges a recognition; or
- offers release, repair, or literal obedience as the only safe policy.

### Low Choir households

- move food, children, cups, or records;
- withdraw one compact weight;
- offer a witness or carrier;
- close a route to protect a household;
- demand return of a specific memory; or
- expose a hidden cost the surface has ignored.

### House Vey and surface authorities

- send a custody instruction;
- fund a practical need with attached terms;
- order evacuation or refuse it;
- recognize or reject a temporary authority;
- seek the Crown, debt evidence, or royal voice; or
- narrow a public promise once the immediate danger appears reduced.

## Using skipped optional regions

An unvisited optional region should remain active in the fiction.

- The Menagerie may release a beast, suppress one hazard at a distance, or become the destination of a displaced creature.
- The King's Narrow Grave may be stripped by rivals, sealed by the retained king's voice, or become a post-adventure custody crisis.
- The Mouth Below may answer through Black Rain, accept a falling object, return a memory, or become newly accessible after the finale.

Skipping a region removes direct control and local knowledge. It should not make the region cease to exist or secretly invalidate a required ending.
