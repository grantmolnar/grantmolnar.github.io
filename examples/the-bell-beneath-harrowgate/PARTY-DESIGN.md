# Party Design: The Open Chain

The Open Chain is a temporary descent commission assembled because no Harrowgate office can be trusted with the whole emergency. Torren Pike names it as a porter’s joke: a chain whose links can open is a poor chain and an excellent rescue tool. Below Harrowgate, the joke becomes policy. Every burden needs a release, every command a limit, and every link a person who can answer for what passes through it.

The five characters are written for adaptation to any rules system. Their useful distinctions are practical competencies, witnessed authority, personal debts, and incompatible priorities. No member is the party's moral center. Each is correct about one danger and tempted to make that danger the measure of every decision.

## Dema Harl

**Function:** Municipal surveyor, quarry engineer, and keeper of Harrowgate's current foundation maps.

**What she brings below:** Dema can read load paths, water stains, settlement cracks, old quarry marks, and the difference between a brace that holds and one that only postpones failure. She carries wax tablets, chalk, a plumb line, a folding rule, three bridge nails, and the authority to order a surface evacuation within the old quarry district.

**Prior connection to the dungeon:** Six years ago, Dema copied and signed the map that allowed three quarry vent mouths to be built over. The closure was legal, profitable, and based on a report that described the vents as obsolete ceremonial shafts. The present cracks prove the report was false or dangerously incomplete. Dema did not write it, but her clean copy made it executable.

**Opening position:** Harrowgate needs a controlled continuation long enough to evacuate exposed streets, understand the lower machinery, and replace the Bell with distributed civic works. Dema opposes immediate destruction because an uncontrolled fracture will follow the same buried stone lines now running beneath crowded districts.

**Private fear:** She fears that every humane objection will arrive as a delay and every delay will become another toll. She also fears that engineering language lets decision-makers hide people inside words such as capacity, redundancy, and acceptable loss.

**Red line:** No one may break the Bell or open a major vent without naming the force's route to the surface and clearing the population above it.

**Temptation:** Dema is willing to accept temporary coercion if it buys time. The Inverted Chapel can therefore recognize her as exactly the sort of emergency authority that becomes permanent through competence and exhaustion.

**How she argues:** She draws the route of a proposed consequence. When someone says, “The Wells can take it,” she asks which Well, through which brake, during which phase, with which crew standing beside it. Her pressure is not rhetoric but the refusal to let an abstraction remain unlocated.

**Table voice:** Short declarative clauses while working; long, exact sentences when forced to explain a moral choice. She names materials and districts before principles: “Royal Well, west brake, two crews. Now tell me who may stop us.”

**Growth demanded by the adventure:** Dema must learn that a load path is incomplete until it includes consent, maintenance, and remedy. Distributed stabilization becomes her preferred ending only after other people can stop, revise, and review the system she designs.

## Sister Calve Norn

**Function:** Funerary sister, advocate for uncompleted dead, and ritual witness to voluntary release.

**What she brings below:** Calve recognizes burial law, mourning machinery, oath residues, inhabited remains, and the difference between a continuing person and a useful imitation. She carries clean salt, burial thread, a handbell without a clapper, and letters from three Harrowgate families asking that recovered memories not be treated as municipal property.

**Prior connection to the dungeon:** Calve's order once certified the Salt Wardens as “faithful dead continuing in office.” She has spent years arguing that the certification was too broad. Her former teacher still cites it to justify involuntary Warden repair and the reuse of recovered tablets.

**Opening position:** Queen Avarra's Crown should be freed, the Funerary memory draw should end, and no person—living, dead, reconstructed, inherited, or civic—should be fitted into the Bell without continuing consent.

**Private fear:** Calve worries that she loves release in the abstract. Ending an old obligation can abandon people who survived by building homes, food systems, and identities inside it. She can imagine freeing the Low Choir so completely that their community ceases to exist on terms they did not choose.

**Red line:** The party will not knowingly establish a coerced replacement burden or join a Warden shell and tablet against the expressed wish of either continuing claimant.

**Temptation:** Calve may prefer a clean ending to a livable one. The Mouth Below fascinates her because it appears to offer relation without machinery, even though its bargains are less human and less reviewable than the Bell.

**How she argues:** She asks who can withdraw and what remains of them after withdrawal. She often grants the practical premise of another character's plan, then identifies the person whom the plan has converted into material.

**Table voice:** Quiet questions, exact repetitions, and ritual formulas stopped before their comforting conclusion. She rarely raises her voice. Anger makes her literal: “You said temporary. Name the hour it ends.”

**Growth demanded by the adventure:** Calve must distinguish release from abandonment. She comes to support a compact that preserves chosen Low Choir households, Warden continuities, and Avarra's testimony while ending involuntary extraction.

## Ilyra Vey

**Function:** Licensed factor of House Vey, cadet claimant, contract reader, and negotiator for royal salvage.

**What she brings below:** Ilyra can read mortuary seals, trace title through contradictory records, price salvage, identify concealed riders, and turn a disputed object into divided custody rather than immediate violence. She carries a Vey signet that opens no ancient mechanism by itself, a copy of Lady Vey's warrant, a purse for emergency purchases, and blank receipts already stamped with her own name rather than the House's.

**Prior connection to the dungeon:** Lady Vey sent Ilyra only after the third toll, when secrecy had failed and public witnesses were unavoidable. Ilyra did not negotiate the Ember Company contract or issue Halren Coss's second payment schedule. Her instructions are legal, narrow, and deliberately incomplete: preserve the Crown, establish accountable custody, and negotiate in public. They say nothing about what Ilyra should do if the Crown is a person, a public machine, or evidence against the Crown's own dynasty.

**Opening position:** The Crown must enter accountable custody. Leaving it in the Bell perpetuates concealed violence; destroying or abandoning it erases evidence and invites future claimants to invent their own history. House Vey has the strongest available continuity of custody, though not necessarily the right to exclusive use.

**Private fear:** Ilyra has spent her career proving that a cadet relation can serve a great house without pretending to belong at its center. The King's Narrow Grave can recognize her as more royal than anyone above has ever allowed. She fears wanting that recognition enough to confuse personal vindication with lawful settlement.

**Red line:** The Crown, royal splice links, trust funds, and debt records must not vanish into unrecorded private possession. Every removal requires a carrier, claim, intended use, and witness.

**Temptation:** The Grave can grant Ilyra a bounded but real command voice. She can use it to bypass several hard negotiations and tell herself she is merely preserving options.

**How she argues:** Ilyra separates powers that others bundle together. Custody is not use; use is not ownership; ownership is not immunity; evidence is not treasure; a claim is not a command. She offers structures before she offers trust.

**Table voice:** Polished, dry, and concrete. She gives every concession a noun and every noun a custodian: “Custody to Vey, use by public vote, Witness outside both.” Under stress, her sentences become more aristocratic and less honest about desire.

**Growth demanded by the adventure:** Ilyra must turn dynastic custody into a public trust whose divided keys include Low Choir and civic witnesses. She does not renounce House Vey; she makes its best claim smaller, clearer, and answerable.

## Torren Pike

**Function:** Former Ember Company porter, rope runner, and survivor of Orren Saye's divided expedition.

**What he brings below:** Torren knows expedition marks, haul discipline, climbing systems, salvage habits, and the lies a company tells itself when a contract becomes indefensible. He carries a patched Company harness, Corven Braid's old knot knife, a list of missing names, and no legal authority beyond being able to identify who was ordered where.

**Prior connection to the dungeon:** Torren entered the upper works during Orren's first reconnaissance and brought two injured porters back before the Crown party reached the Ropeworks. He had seen a preparatory order to open the Queen's chain, but not the cut itself. Bound by Company secrecy and afraid of being charged with desertion, he reported the order only to Ember's surface clerk. When the dying porter emerged after the third toll and confirmed that Orren had executed it over Maelin Rook's refusal, Torren gave Marr his copy and joined the public commission. He has since called his earlier withdrawal a rescue, a mutiny, and a desertion depending on who asks. Several missing delvers may believe he abandoned them.

**Opening position:** Rescue comes before treasure, mechanisms, and claims. Captain Maelin Rook and every reachable Company survivor must be found or accounted for. Orren's concealed rider and abridged sequence should be exposed, but Torren is wary of civic officials using the exposure to make ordinary laborers carry the blame.

**Private fear:** Torren knows rescue can become another form of refusal. He can always identify one more trapped person, body, memory, or name whose recovery justifies postponing a final operation. He also fears meeting survivors who chose Orren after Torren left.

**Red line:** No route may be collapsed, flooded, or sealed until named people on that route are evacuated or deliberately left by a recorded decision.

**Temptation:** Torren wants Orren publicly defeated. The Narrow Grave gives him opportunities to humiliate or abandon the factor even when taking Orren alive would preserve testimony and reduce later scapegoating.

**How he argues:** Torren names people. When Dema speaks of clearing a route, he asks whether that includes the porter behind the third brake. When Ilyra speaks of a trust, he asks who carries the bar up the ladder. He distrusts elegant summaries because workers disappear inside them.

**Table voice:** Informal, sensory, and impatient with titles. He uses rope and hauling metaphors without noticing. When frightened, he jokes once and then becomes brutally direct.

**Growth demanded by the adventure:** Torren must accept that rescue includes future people. Stabilization, records, maintenance funds, and public duty can be rescue even when no trapped person is visible.

## Miren Glass

**Function:** Harrowgate public notary, tenants' advocate, and elected witness for the quarry wards.

**What she brings below:** Miren can take testimony under pressure, distinguish consent from compliance, draft bounded emergency authority, recognize when a promise lacks a remedy, and communicate with the surface through civic runners. She carries the town's emergency seal, a packet of household petitions, twelve blank memory-return receipts, and a list of streets that cannot survive another unexplained evacuation order.

**Prior connection to the dungeon:** Miren's younger brother Olan lost the memory of their mother's face during the second uncontrolled toll. The loss makes the crisis personal but does not make her the owner of every recovered memory. She has already refused the Council's request to classify all mnemonic salvage as public evidence.

**Opening position:** No final operation is legitimate unless Harrowgate and the Low Choir can identify the duty, its bearers, its term, its limits, and a remedy. Miren supports emergency action before perfect agreement, but only if the action creates its own path to review and withdrawal.

**Private fear:** She fears that procedure is the luxury of the safe. Every additional witness, declaration, and receipt consumes time while people are drowning below. She also fears that without those acts, whoever survives will write necessity into permanent title.

**Red line:** The party will not call silence consent, inherited office ownership, or temporary necessity a self-renewing mandate.

**Temptation:** The Chapel can validate Miren's formulas and make her believe that a well-drafted compact is equivalent to a living coalition. The Low Choir households can still refuse, reinterpret, or outgrow what she writes.

**How she argues:** Miren asks four questions: Who speaks? Who bears? For how long? What happens when they fail? She converts moral language into obligations but remains suspicious of obligations no ordinary household can understand.

**Table voice:** Plain public sentences with deliberate repetition. She avoids legal ornament. When another character hides behind expertise, she asks them to state the decision as it would be announced in the street above.

**Growth demanded by the adventure:** Miren must learn that remedy is not only a clause. It requires money, access, replacement crews, records, and people willing to reopen a decision after the emergency has passed.

## Productive fault lines

The party begins with four durable disputes.

### Rescue versus system preparation

Torren treats every reachable survivor as an immediate obligation. Dema argues that operating a room before searching it may save more people and prevent a rescue from creating another failure. Neither position should collapse into selfishness versus compassion. The dungeon repeatedly makes preparation part of rescue and makes delay genuinely costly.

### Liberation versus continuity

Calve wants the Crown, memories, and continuing dead released from involuntary use. Dema and Miren insist that abrupt release can kill living communities. The Feast, Barracks, and Deep Bell force them to distinguish ending coercion from ending institutions.

### Custody versus common ownership

Ilyra believes dangerous and evidentiary objects require defined custody. Miren fears that custody will harden into private title. The Grave and treasury let Ilyra prove that unowned salvage is often stolen salvage, while the Chapel and Low Choir require her to divide the keys she would prefer to hold.

### Emergency authority versus remedy

Dema can act faster than Miren can establish public legitimacy. Miren can make Dema's work answerable but cannot make stone wait. Their strongest settlement combines a short technical command, independent witnesses, visible accounts, funded maintenance, and automatic review.

## What keeps the party together

- Each member has authority the others lack and cannot safely counterfeit.
- Torren's missing-person list gives every abstract system a human test.
- Dema's maps keep moral urgency from becoming blind physical action.
- Calve can recognize persons and releases that contracts and gauges cannot.
- Ilyra can prevent evidence, treasure, and custody from collapsing into seizure.
- Miren can turn temporary cooperation into obligations that survive the descent.
- All five agree that Orren's concealed contract and the old Funerary draw cannot simply continue unchanged.
- All five agree that no one member should hold the Crown alone.

## Likely division of labor

This is a narrative allocation, not a mechanical party composition.

| Situation | First lead | Necessary second voice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Load path, brace, vent, water transfer | Dema | Torren names exposed people; Miren records authority |
| Rescue, climb, extraction, expedition evidence | Torren | Dema checks route; Ilyra secures chain of custody |
| Warden continuity, memory return, release | Calve | Miren records consent; Dema checks operating consequence |
| Treasure, genealogy, contracts, royal mechanisms | Ilyra | Calve tests personhood; Miren divides powers |
| Compact, emergency mandate, public communication | Miren | Dema states technical limits; Ilyra funds and houses custody |

## Demonstrated arc

The canonical playthrough does not produce unanimous philosophy. It produces a workable hybrid none of the five proposed at the entrance.

- Dema abandons the hope that a competent engineer can safely own emergency continuation.
- Calve accepts continuing institutions when their members choose them and possess release.
- Ilyra relinquishes exclusive Vey custody while preserving the Crown and debt evidence in a divided public trust.
- Torren accepts that maintenance funds, vent crews, and review dates are rescue obligations.
- Miren accepts that the first operation must proceed under incomplete consent, provided its term, witnesses, and remedy are active before the Bell sounds.

Their final arrangement frees the Crown from the Bell, ends the automatic Funerary memory draw, and distributes pressure among the Royal Well's replacement mass, prepared quarry vents, Garden roots, and visible civic maintenance. The Common Measure is used as a transfer frame and emergency governor rather than installed as a new permanent sovereign burden. The result is deliberately noncanonical: it saves Harrowgate, preserves most of the deep community, and creates an expensive public institution whose future failure remains possible.
