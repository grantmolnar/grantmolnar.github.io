# The Mouth Below

**Role:** optional, end  
**Tags:** optional, super-depth, unknown, end

A listening fault learns repeated exchanges and turns every bargain into a future pattern.

## Opening View

At the basalt lips, everyone becomes conscious of repetition. A heartbeat. A drip. The scrape of a boot, corrected too late. The Mouth waits for one of them to happen again. Beyond the threshold, the chamber will take the shape of the first pattern offered. Repeat it, and the shape begins to become law. It asks nothing. It listens for an answer you may already have given.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

The Mouth Below is the old records’ name for a place where fault, resonant cavity, and intelligence cannot be separated. It consumes patterned vibration and returns altered pressure, matter, and memory. It learns recurrence more readily than intention. A promise spoken once means little here; an exchange repeated twice has already begun to become law.

The Bell can be resolved without entering. Descent trades operating certainty for a chance to change the relationship beneath the entire machine: release from the old bargain, restored memories, a safer pressure path, the Crown's freedom, or power over the fault. The Mouth promises nothing in human terms; it answers patterns enacted clearly enough to recur.

Avarra mistook response for obedience. Later engineers mistook recurrence for machinery. The deepest choice is whether to repeat either error.

## Reaching the Mouth

There are three practical descents:

- the Deep Bell clapper line lowers directly through the sounding shaft during Crown Descent or after the Bell is secured;
- the Black Rain sluice opens a wet route through pressure ribs and mineral glass;
- the Garden's Last Teeth roots reach a dry fissure if they were not burned, flooded, or spent in collapse.

A party may force another route through broken Bell masonry or a dropped Well. Such entries are unstable and may close behind them.

Useful preparations include an anchored return line, controlled sound, a named memory vessel, black-water pearls, a Quiet Beast, Avarra's Witness or Refusal, and someone above able to interrupt the Bell. Each defines one part of the exchange before the Mouth chooses it.

## The threshold

The shaft narrows into two ridges of warm basalt like closed lips. Beyond them is no visible chamber until a sound is offered. A spoken name produces a corridor lined with moments from that person's memory. A hammer stroke produces an angular cavern of repeating impacts. Silence produces depth without distance, where the party can feel other heartbeats but not locate them.

Crossing the threshold requires making a pattern. The first pattern becomes the Mouth's provisional understanding of why the party has come.

Examples:

- a Warden cadence suggests command and return to station;
- a Low Choir memory cup suggests exchange of remembered lives;
- Crown metal suggests royal recurrence and Avarra's old bargain;
- black-water rhythm suggests pressure relief;
- a quiet-beast handling call suggests appetite, boundary, and return;
- a personal song suggests relationship with the singer rather than Harrowgate as a whole.

The party can correct an unfortunate first pattern, but every repetition teaches it more deeply.

## What lives here

### The Unsaid Brood

Small pale forms move at the edge of any created chamber. They have no mouths and wear impressions of faces that were almost remembered. They feed on abandoned clauses, interrupted names, and promises whose limits were never spoken.

When threatened, the Brood removes the final term from an action: a rope is tied but not to anything; a warning names danger but not whom it threatens; a command opens a gate but loses its stopping condition. Protect language and intent as carefully as bodies. Complete unfinished statements, feed the Brood harmless discarded patterns, contain it in memory cups, or destroy it and release its stolen omissions into the chamber.

### The Long Listener

Far below any temporary floor lies a shape assembled from black water and fault glass. It mirrors the posture of whoever speaks most regularly. The shape is only the form the phenomenon uses to maintain a conversation.

The Long Listener learns through recurrence. If attacked repeatedly, it learns combat as the exchange. If offered names repeatedly, it learns names as food. If given bounded pressure pulses and a consistent return, it can learn a civic venting pattern. Its apparent emotions reflect the pattern offered to it and should not be trusted as proof of human motives.

### Returned things

The Mouth sometimes gives back objects or memories altered toward stability:

- a cracked memory returns without pain but missing the face that caused it;
- a black-water pearl returns as clear glass holding one future tremor;
- a broken chain returns flexible and alive, seeking a recurring load;
- a dead person's voice returns able to answer only repeated questions;
- a lost name returns attached to the wrong duty; or
- a Crown command returns stripped of genealogy and usable by anyone who repeats its cost.

Each return shows what the Mouth understood the offering to mean.

## The grammar of settlement

A stable settlement requires three enacted terms.

### Offering

What will recur from Harrowgate to the Mouth? Possible offerings include:

- bounded vibration from civic machinery;
- pressure pulses routed through an opened quarry throat;
- chosen memories copied rather than removed;
- royal cadence without Crown ownership;
- black-water minerals or heat;
- seasonal music, labor, or public witness; or
- the destruction of a structure whose breaking teaches a new path.

An offering must be physically possible and politically maintainable. A promise to send what no one controls is only noise.

### Boundary

What may the Mouth take, affect, or follow? Boundaries can be marked through Refusal, Chapel acceptance, root growth, salt tablets, repeated silence, mapped geography, or a person willing to become the first living limit.

A vague boundary such as “harm no one” is not legible. A workable boundary names places, kinds of memory, durations, thresholds, and stopping conditions. The Mouth obeys patterns better than abstractions.

### Return

What does Harrowgate receive, and what happens when either side fails? Returns may include:

- reduced fault pressure;
- restoration or release of stored memory;
- closure of the royal command path;
- predictable tremor warnings;
- safe black-water minerals;
- a new vent landscape; or
- the Crown freed from acting as clapper.

The return must recur. A single gift may solve the crisis; it establishes no settlement.

Witness is not a fourth mechanical term, but without witnesses no human institution can know whether the exchange remains what was accepted.

## Possible settlements

### The open throat

The party teaches the Mouth to release pressure through a permanently opened quarry and uninhabited ravine. Harrowgate loses land and gains a strange steaming valley. The Bell can be silenced or reduced to warning duty. This requires geography, evacuation, and continuing maintenance of the boundary.

### The civic cadence

Harrowgate sends regular bounded vibration from mills, pumps, bells, or public works. The Mouth returns predictable pressure relief and warning patterns. This makes everyday civic labor part of the settlement and prevents one crown from owning it. Failure to maintain the cadence causes tremors rather than moral judgment.

### The memory exchange

Volunteers copy chosen memories into cups or Iron Tongues and offer those copies. The Mouth returns stolen Low Choir fragments or releases them from the fault. This can repair generations of loss, but copied memory remains a real intimacy and can become exploitation under another name.

### The royal unbinding

Avarra's Witness repeats the original limited surrender while Refusal denies all later additions. The Crown's cadence is offered once, then broken into a nonhereditary public pattern. The Mouth releases its learned expectation of royal command. The Crown becomes materially powerful but loses exclusive control over the fault.

### The deep lease

A person, faction, Quiet Beast, or constructed burden remains as a recurring interpreter. The Mouth gains a stable partner; Harrowgate gains adaptable relief. This is the closest settlement to the old Bell and the most likely to become another sentence. Consent, duration, relief, and return must be especially explicit.

### The severance

The party uses collapse, Refusal, and a massive nonrecurring offering to break every learned pattern between Harrowgate and the Mouth. Pressure returns to natural fault behavior. The region experiences earthquakes and long geological change without a directing intelligence. This may be liberation, catastrophe, or both.

## Negotiating through action

Negotiate through repeated demonstrations, never propositions.

1. The party enacts an offering in miniature.
2. The Mouth returns an interpretation.
3. The party accepts, rejects, or corrects it through a changed pattern.
4. The Mouth tests the proposed boundary.
5. The party demonstrates the return and failure condition.

The Iron Choir can translate complex sequences. The Deep Bell can amplify them. A Quiet Beast can consume one unwanted component. Avarra's faculties can compare, refuse, and witness. Low Choir memory keepers can identify when an offered memory has been altered beyond recognition.

Three clear exchanges may establish a provisional settlement. More exchanges improve precision but deepen the Mouth's expectation that the participants themselves will continue.

## Conflict below

Violence is possible and sometimes rational. The Unsaid Brood may strip limits from the party's terms. A rival claimant may try to teach the Mouth exclusive royal obedience. Deep Rank Wardens may attempt to seal the threshold. The Long Listener may reproduce a prior uncontrolled attack until someone changes the pattern.

Combat changes the negotiation:

- repeated weapon rhythms become an offering of conflict;
- collapsing terrain can mark a boundary;
- a protected retreat line demonstrates return;
- sacrificing a black-water pearl can interrupt learned cadence;
- Refusal can deny one repeated pattern; and
- Sable can consume contagious panic before the Mouth learns it as Harrowgate's voice.

Manifestations can be wounded and local structures destroyed. The fault survives; violence only makes future contact difficult, dangerous, or differently shaped.

## Queen Avarra below

The Long Listener recognizes royal cadence before it recognizes Avarra as a person. If the retained will is reconstructed, Witness can show the distinction between her original error, her later surrender, and the machine built afterward. Refusal can end the assumption that every Crown bearer speaks for her. Measure can compare proposed returns without deciding that efficiency equals justice.

Avarra may offer her retained royal cadence for unbinding. Doing so ends her ability to command the Mouth and weakens the Crown's old authority. She regards this as freedom if the decision is hers and as execution if imposed by a claimant suppressing Refusal.

If only part of her will is present, the settlement reflects that mutilation. Measure alone creates an efficient but indifferent exchange. Refusal alone can sever the old path without building another. Witness alone may recover truth and memory while leaving pressure unresolved.

## Failure states

- **Unbounded appetite:** the Mouth learns to take a class of memory, sound, or pressure without a stopping condition.
- **Walking cadence:** the learned pattern attaches to a person or carried Crown and follows them away from Harrowgate.
- **False return:** the Mouth gives an apparent benefit whose cost appears elsewhere or later.
- **Borrowed face:** the Long Listener stabilizes around a speaker and begins being treated as that person.
- **Deep closure:** the threshold collapses, trapping the party or sealing the Mouth after an incomplete exchange.
- **Royal recurrence:** a claimant establishes exclusive Crown cadence and revives the possibility of sovereign fault command.

Failure creates a campaign problem with a known origin and pattern; annihilation is unnecessary.

## Treasure and salvage

The Mouth can yield fault glass, living chain, returned memories, future-tremor pearls, and altered command metal. Each treasure embodies a term of exchange. Removing it without understanding that term may continue the settlement through the carrier.

The greatest treasure is a changed relation between Harrowgate and the fault. It cannot be sold without also transferring duty.

## Returning upward

A settlement is not complete until it is enacted through the Deep Bell, Black Rain gates, Iron Choir, civic infrastructure, or landscape. The party must return, communicate the terms, and survive the final transfer. If the route closes, allies above may need to interpret the last demonstrated pattern without explanation.

The Mouth may provide a return path shaped by the agreement: a clear-water column, a living chain, a corridor of restored memories, or a fault opening in the quarry. A failed or predatory exchange may return the party somewhere the Mouth considers equivalent rather than where they intended.

## Carry-forward states

Record:

- entry route and whether it remains open;
- first pattern offered and how it was corrected;
- Unsaid Brood and Long Listener behavior;
- offering, boundary, return, witnesses, and failure condition;
- memories, pressure, Crown cadence, or people committed;
- Avarra faculty involvement and any royal unbinding;
- provisional tests and the Mouth's interpretations;
- treasure removed and recurring duty attached to it;
- who remains below, if anyone; and
- how the settlement is enacted above.

Keep the Mouth stranger than a person and more responsive than a natural hazard. It offers no escape from responsibility; it makes the bargain visible.
