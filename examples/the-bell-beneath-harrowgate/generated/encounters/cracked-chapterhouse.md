# The Cracked Chapterhouse

**Role:** necessary, start  
**Tags:** entrance, surface, command, rescue

Harrowgate argues beneath rising dust while two sealed descents wait for the word rescue.

## Opening View

The dust rises before the Bell sounds. It lifts from the altar crack in one pale sheet, and every argument in the chapterhouse stops with it. Public seals bar the old processional stair; across the floor, Tarrow’s survey stakes lean toward the quarry wound. Only when the dust begins to settle does Warden Marr finish the word he had been trying to say: “Rescue.”

## Encounter Material

## Situation

Harrowgate has used the old chapterhouse as storehouse, plague ward, election hall, and now command post. Each use left its own lock, chalk mark, and grievance. A crack runs from the sealed processional stair to the outer wall. Warm air rises through it with lamp soot, wet stone, and the iron taste of blood. Dust comes first when the Bell draws breath; the council has learned to stop speaking.

The bell beneath Harrowgate has sounded three times without rope or ringer, each earlier in the damaged cycle than the one before. The first toll cracked chimneys and was blamed on quarry settling. After the second emptied the north well in a column of black rain, the council closed the affected streets and sent signals down both old entrances. No answer came. The third toll, before dawn today, dropped a tenement wall and left nine townspeople unable to remember the names of their oldest children. Tarrow's gauges are already returning to their pre-toll positions. If nothing below changes, he expects the fourth before midnight.

The Ember Company entered the buried complex five days ago and spent its first two days surveying in silence. One Ember porter brought two injured workers out before the Crown party reached the Ropeworks. That porter had seen a preparatory order to open the Queen's chain, but not the cut itself, and reported it only to Ember's surface clerk. A wounded porter returned through the quarry at dawn after the third toll. He reported that the company reached the old Ropeworks, that Factor Orren Saye executed the order, and that Captain Maelin Rook tried to stop him. He died before naming who survived. The earlier survivor then gave Warden Marr the copied order. A player character may be that survivor; otherwise the porter briefs the commission, offers to guide the first descent, and remains available for surface rescue. Until the dying porter's testimony, Harrowgate knew the Bell had failed; it did not know a living expedition had broken it.

## The commission

**Warden Elian Marr**, Harrowgate's elected civil officer, asks the party to descend, find any living members of the Ember Company, and prevent another uncontrolled toll. He can promise town funds, salvage rights to ordinary treasure, and public authority to requisition labor or close streets. He cannot grant ownership of the royal crown said to hang inside the bell.

**Lady Merrow Vey**, the last acknowledged descendant of Harrowgate's buried kings, has arrived with six retainers and a sealed genealogy. The third toll has made secrecy useless, so she admits that a Vey recovery trust funded the Ember Company's survey and authorized recovery only after the buried house was made safe. She denies authorizing a chain cut or any race against Harrowgate's custody. Her copy of the contract contains no second payment schedule. She offers a much larger reward for the Crown of Measure and insists that her ancestor's crown was stolen by rebels and made into the bell's clapper. She will accept a repaired bell only if the crown is returned to her after Harrowgate is safe.

**Master Iven Tarrow**, the town's quarry engineer, cares less about dynastic law. His survey stakes show that each toll transfers strain from the buried complex into the inhabited ridge. He believes the dungeon contains a deliberate pressure-release system, but he cannot tell whether repairing, silencing, or destroying it is safest without seeing its lower works.

The three patrons agree on rescue and immediate stabilization. They disagree about everything that follows.

## What the party knows at the outset

Give the players these facts without requiring investigation:

- the bell is physically beneath Harrowgate;
- the abandoned cloister stair and the quarry cleft are both open routes below;
- the Ember Company entered two days before the first toll, and a dying porter says Orren ordered one royal chain cut over Captain Rook's refusal;
- the old complex used water, lamps, and immense counterweights as one machine;
- the Crown of Measure is believed to hang within the Deep Bell; and
- another uncontrolled toll is likely before midnight.

Do not hide these premises. Exploration reveals how the systems work, who remains alive, and which endings the party can still afford.

## Two entrances

### The processional stair

The blocked stair descends from the chapterhouse crypt to the Hall of Bent Knees. Clearing it is slow but controlled. It allows a stretcher team, ordinary lanterns, and a retreat line. The first obstruction is a stone door whose petition slot still moves.

### The quarry cleft

A fresh collapse has exposed a maintenance shaft in Harrowgate's working quarry. It is faster and reaches the Lantern Cistern side of the upper dungeon, but the descent crosses broken hoists, unstable shelves, and the hunting path of something that has learned to follow lamplight.

Divide the expedition, choose one descent, or establish both. The stair favors negotiation and orderly retreat; the cleft favors speed, surprise, and early access to the water system.

## The fourth-toll clock

Begin at **Tremor 1: unsettled**. Advance the clock when the party takes a full rest in unsafe ground, makes a major failed attempt against the bell machinery, abandons a large noisy conflict, or deliberately spends hours on excavation or salvage.

1. **Unsettled:** dust falls; small mechanisms slip; creatures move toward warmer chambers.
2. **Shifting:** one previously easy vertical route becomes difficult until braced or counterweighted.
3. **Black rain:** water reverses through one open conduit; change the state of a cistern or flooded room.
4. **The fourth toll:** Harrowgate suffers a major collapse, every unprotected creature in the dungeon experiences memory bleed, and the Deep Bell's hanging structure loses another degree of stability.

Treat midnight as Tarrow's best projection, not a stopwatch. Failed operations and major delay close the cycle; bracing, venting, crews, shortcuts, and safe camps buy time. Telegraph every advance before it lands.

## Useful preparations

The council can provide rope, quarry spikes, timber shores, oil, stretchers, chalk, canvas, and six disciplined volunteers. Specialized requests create choices:

- Tarrow can spare a compact brake winch or a set of four wall shores, not both.
- Lady Vey can lend the genealogy seals or two heavily armed retainers, not both.
- Marr can send the town surgeon below or keep her ready for the next surface collapse.

None is mandatory. Each solves a class of problem while leaving another exposed.

## Immediate complications

Use one before descent if the opening needs pressure:

- a child recognizes a returned Ember Company pack and claims its owner is still alive;
- Chief Factor Halren Coss's deputy attempts to insert a private instruction into the party's commission;
- the blocked stair exhales a human voice reciting names no one in the chapterhouse remembers;
- a quarry horn reports movement beneath the new cleft; or
- the floor crack widens enough to reveal a brass chain plate carrying a partial dungeon plan.

## Results and return

The chapterhouse remains the safest surface base until a fourth toll breaks it. Bring rescued delvers, recovered records, faction envoys, and disputed treasure here. Everything carried across this floor becomes public: Crown custody, protected prisoners, evacuation orders, and promises made below in Harrowgate's name.
