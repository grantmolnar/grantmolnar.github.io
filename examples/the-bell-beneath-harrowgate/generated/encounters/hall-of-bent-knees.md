# The Hall of Bent Knees

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** gate, social, puzzle, wardens

Two hundred stone petitioners make every entrant declare the burden they will carry.

## Opening View

The ceiling makes you bow before the petitioners do. Two hundred stone figures kneel in ordered rows, their faces worn away, their burdens still exact: bread, blade, broken wheel, dead child, sealed mouth. The far door offers no latch. Beside it, a bronze scale holds one empty pan and room enough for a name, a burden, and whatever makes the two belong together.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

The processional stair ends in a hall designed to make pride physically inconvenient. Two hundred statues kneel in ordered rows, each carrying a different petition: loaf, sword, dead child, broken wheel, water jar, sealed mouth. Mineral dust has erased their faces but not their burdens. The far door has no handle. Beside it, a bronze scale waits for an entrant to make weight and meaning agree.

The hall was once the lawful public entrance to the buried house. Its machinery still distinguishes petitioners, officers, prisoners, and thieves. It enforces declared purpose and recorded burden, whether the purpose is merciful or wicked.

## The petition scale

To open the inner door, someone must place one of four things on the scale:

- a written name accepted as the entrant's own;
- an object the entrant is honestly willing to leave behind;
- a burden taken from another person in the hall; or
- blood sufficient to mark the petition plate.

Nothing placed on the scale must be lost forever. Entrants can retrieve an object from the inner return channel, share a burden, present a true alias, use an Ember Company tally, or dismantle the mechanism. The scale establishes the house's grammar: declared purpose moves machinery, and every movement transfers weight.

False declarations do not trigger a trap. They open the wrong door. A petitioner claiming to be a prisoner reaches holding cells; a thief reaches the tally cage; an officer reaches the inspection gallery. Each route is usable and each shows different clues.

## The first Salt Wardens

**Sister Vesra Quill** and three salt-preserved wardens occupy the inspection gallery. Their skin is gray-white and cracked around old stitching. They are neither mindless dead nor living people in ordinary need of food. Their memories are held in salt tablets fitted behind the sternum.

Vesra opens with three questions:

1. Who bears responsibility for the broken Queen's chain?
2. Does the party intend to restore the bell, remove the crown, or merely survive?
3. What burden will Harrowgate accept if its safety depends on people buried below it?

She knows the party cannot answer all three yet. Honest uncertainty earns a temporary passage mark. A claim of absolute right—royal, civic, religious, or martial—causes her to demand a formal petition and collateral.

Vesra belongs to the **Continuance** faction among the Wardens. She believes the bell must be stabilized and rung under control until a lawful replacement for the crown exists. She admits that the old settlement harms the Low Choir, but she considers an uncontrolled collapse worse.

## Opposition

The hall's active threats depend on how the party enters:

- forcing the far door animates burden-bearer statues that grapple, pin, and redistribute carried equipment rather than simply dealing damage;
- presenting stolen royal seals opens the officer route but wakes a bronze examiner that attempts to confiscate them;
- spilling blood in excess wakes the tally cage and marks the donor for recognition by deeper ward mechanisms; and
- attacking Vesra causes the inspection gallery to seal while a warning travels to the Salt Barracks.

Use the kneeling rows as narrow lanes, low sightlines, and stone barriers waiting to be toppled.

## Routes and discoveries

The main petition door reaches the Ropeworks. The officer gallery reaches the Chain Scriptorium. The prisoner route descends toward the Salt Barracks. A maintenance crawl behind the statue carrying a broken wheel returns to the Quarry Cleft.

The hall also contains the first complete statement of the three burdens:

> Water carries the weight. Light remembers the way. The chain decides who pays.

## Negotiated passage

Vesra can grant:

- a salt mark recognized by lesser wardens;
- safe directions to the Ropeworks and Barracks;
- the name of Prior Dalca, leader of the Warden faction that favors ending the bell's sentence; and
- a promise to delay one hostile response if the party rescues trapped wardens or recovers missing salt tablets.

She cannot grant ownership of the crown and will not permit the Deep Bell to be destroyed without hearing a credible evacuation plan for Harrowgate.

## Outcomes

- **Petition accepted:** the party gains orderly access and understands burden transfer.
- **Alternative door used:** the party gains a different route and leaves a recorded declaration that deeper mechanisms may invoke.
- **Wardens allied:** the Salt Barracks begins watchful rather than hostile.
- **Wardens opposed:** later barriers close, but the party can still proceed through machinery, hidden routes, or the Low Choir.
- **Scale broken:** immediate access is easy; later return channels and lawful shortcuts fail until repaired.
