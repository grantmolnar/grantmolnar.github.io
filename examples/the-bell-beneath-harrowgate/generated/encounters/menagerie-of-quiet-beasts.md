# The Menagerie of Quiet Beasts

**Role:** optional  
**Tags:** optional, creatures, toolbox, hazard

Four purpose-bred beasts consume heat, vibration, loose memory, or panic until hunger widens the task.

## Opening View

Three taps. A pause. Two taps. Hollis Fen keeps the rhythm with a spoon against the wicker cage above the gallery. “Walk,” they say. “Do not run.” Below, velvet hounds listen to each footfall, white moths gather around spoken names, and something translucent follows fear through the floor. The painted boundary lines are faded. Every creature still knows where its line lies.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

The Menagerie is a maintenance arsenal bred instead of forged. The buried house produces excess heat, trapped vibration, severed memory, and contagious panic; its keepers answered with appetites. Each beast consumes one excess and ignores most others until the work ends and hunger widens the definition.

Every bronze cage stands open. The beasts remain inside territories painted around their thresholds: a soot-black lizard watches flame; velvet hounds follow footfalls without turning their heads; white moths gather around spoken names; a glass animal listens through the floor. Bars made frightened beasts fight their keepers. Marked territory, prepared appetite, and an obvious return trail let them choose the work and come back.

Ward-light once reinforced the boundaries. With the Ossuary Bough unstable, lines are fading. Nothing here is required for the Bell, but every borrowed advantage creates a handling duty.

## First contact

Quarry, Garden, Chapel, and Black Rain each enter beside a different appetite. Arrival triggers attention, not an automatic attack.

- Open flame causes the coalheart lizard to stand and taste the air.
- Heavy or repeated steps draw the velvet hounds to the edge of their painted floor.
- Spoken names make the white moths settle into letter-like shapes.
- Racing hearts wake the glass sleeper beneath its wool cover.

A brass keeper's gong hangs beside every entrance. Striking it once announces an operator. Striking it twice requests a beast. Striking it three times means uncontrolled excess and opens all return gates. The third signal was meant for a staffed emergency. With only one surviving handler, it is now an invitation to a regional disaster.

## Hollis Fen, surviving handler

**Hollis Fen**, an Ember Company animal handler and trap specialist, is suspended in a wicker work cage above the central floor. A snapped Chapel traverse dropped the cage and tore Hollis's left shoulder from its socket. They have kept the beasts calm for two days by tapping keeper rhythms with a spoon.

Hollis joined Orren's party because the contract promised hazard pay and because no one else in the company understood living maintenance tools. They refused to release the moths against Low Choir witnesses and were marooned when Orren's loyalists crossed toward the Grave. Hollis knows:

- Orren carried a royal sequence plate and intended to enter the Narrow Grave before returning to the Deep Bell;
- Captain Rook ordered every beast left contained unless a named operator accepted responsibility for its return;
- the black-water throat can be cooled by the coalheart or quieted by the velvet hounds, but not both at once without a second handler;
- one of the hounds followed an uncontrolled toll vibration into the wall and may emerge elsewhere after the next tremor; and
- the Menagerie records distinguish a beast's useful appetite from its starvation behavior.

Freeing Hollis requires raising the cage, crossing a keeper line, or lowering it into one enclosure. Hollis can direct that enclosure's beast while injured. Treatment, a sling, or a functioning appeal wing makes them a mobile operator. Hollis treats rescue as a debt of thanks, never a transfer of ownership.

## The handling law

Every release needs three things. The old keeper plates call them **appetite, boundary, and return**.

1. **Appetite:** identify the exact excess the beast is meant to consume and present enough of it to wake the trained behavior. “Protect us” is not an appetite. Heat from a pipe, resonance in a chain, spilled memory in a cup, or panic in a wounded crew is.
2. **Boundary:** mark where the work ends. Keeper chalk, ward-oil, salt line, running water, Warden command marks, or a continuously held sound can define a boundary. A vague instruction does not.
3. **Return:** provide a trail back to the enclosure or another prepared resting place. The return may be scent, heat, vibration, a spoken keeper name, or a carried wool pad. If the work ends without a return, the beast seeks the nearest related stimulus.

A handler may improvise any of the three if they understand the creature. On failure, widen the appetite, erase the boundary, or sever the return trail. The beast becomes a specific moving complication, not generically hostile.

A beast can be transported in a keeper harness, guided loose, or installed temporarily in a target region. Harnessed transport is safer but requires hands and limits climbing. Guided transport is faster but exposes every related stimulus along the route. Installation creates the strongest effect and the greatest risk of the beast claiming a new territory.

## The four principal beasts

### Cinderback, the coalheart lizard

Cinderback is long as a coffin, low to the ground, and armored in dull plates that brighten from within as it feeds. It consumes heat gradients: open flame, hot metal, fever, and the pressure heat carried by black water. It does not need to bite the source. It lies against stone or pipework and drinks warmth until its plates glow.

**Useful work:**

- cool the Black Rain Cistern enough to approach a valve or injured crew;
- extinguish a basin fire without spending water;
- draw heat out of a seized brake, chain, or Crown Drum fitting;
- preserve a body or salt tablet threatened by warm water; or
- create a cold route through a fire or steam hazard.

**Risk:** once the named heat is gone, Cinderback follows the nearest warmer thing. That may be a ward lamp, a living body, a Reliquary air bell, or the Garden's warm root bed. If allowed to feed without a boundary, it can extinguish an entire lamp bough and leave chilled survivors unable to move.

**Handling:** feed it a warmed copper tile, bound its work with two cold lines, and carry its basalt sleep-stone as the return. It dislikes running water and will not cross a Reliquary saint's active stream unless starving.

**Violence and salvage:** when threatened, it presses against metal armor or weapons and steals heat from the bearer, then lashes with its heavy tail. Killing it yields four heat-storing plates and a coalheart gland useful for one major cooling operation. The plates do not reproduce the creature's judgment; they absorb heat until they crack.

### The velvet hounds

Three narrow hounds occupy a floor covered in black felt. Their ears are sealed folds, and their paws never click. They consume patterned vibration: footsteps, machinery chatter, chain resonance, shouted cadence, and the repeated impacts that precede structural failure. They hunt a rhythm rather than a creature.

The hounds are **Murmur**, **Latch**, and **After**. Murmur follows the strongest vibration. Latch pins itself between source and structure, drinking the transmission. After remembers a rhythm and follows it later. The missing wall-hound is After; it entered a maintenance slot while pursuing the third toll.

**Useful work:**

- suppress one line of bell resonance while a chain is repaired;
- quiet an Iron Tongue row or prevent it from calling Wardens;
- mask a group's movement through a sound-sensitive region;
- stabilize a vibrating bridge or brake long enough for one operation; or
- draw a dangerous repetitive mechanism away from people.

**Risk:** when the named rhythm stops, the hounds seek a substitute pattern. Marching crews, heartbeats, spoken chants, and the Deep Bell's frame are all viable. Total silence does not calm them; it leaves them hungry and attentive to smaller living rhythms.

**Handling:** establish a target rhythm, draw a felt or oil boundary, and keep a return metronome tapping at the enclosure. A hound can be recalled by its individual cadence. Confusing their cadences makes them trade targets.

**Violence and salvage:** the hounds fight by interrupting balance, breath, and coordination. They shoulder a combatant at the instant of commitment, swallow shouted orders, and hold vibrating weapons between their jaws. A dead hound's felt organs can damp one route or device for a scene, but deteriorate after exposure to loud sound.

### The white name moths

The moth cloud rests around a dead keeper's mask. Each wing carries fragments of handwriting. The swarm consumes **unattached memory**: recollection spilled into light, water, metal, or air without a willing carrier or stable vessel. The moths were bred to clean failed transfers and prevent loose commands from haunting the machinery.

**Useful work:**

- remove an unstable memory cloud without forcing it into a person;
- strip Orren's command residue from a route, guardian, or Deep Rank order;
- clean a cracked cup or Iron Tongue before a deliberate transfer;
- separate one intrusive remembered command from a Warden shell; or
- make a short corridor safe from name bleed after a toll.

**Risk:** once loose memory is gone, the moths test spoken names, titles, promises, and recently recalled faces. They do not immediately devour identity. They first erase the connection between a word and the obligation it carries. A hungry swarm may leave someone remembering a loved face but not why it matters, or retaining a duty while forgetting who assigned it.

**Handling:** present the contaminated vessel, surround the work with witness cords or silver-root fiber, and keep the dead keeper's mask as the return focus. A stable voluntary memory cup repels them. A false name attracts them because its contradiction sheds loose associations.

**Violence and salvage:** flame scatters the swarm but risks feeding the lamp-memory channel. Water clumps the moths into readable sheets. Ward-oil traps them at a boundary. Killing the whole swarm yields mnemonic powder that can erase one command mark or blur one pursued trail, but destroys a humane way to clean failed transfers.

### Sable, the glass sleeper

Sable is a translucent, many-jointed animal curled beneath gray wool. Its organs brighten in time with nearby pulses. It consumes contagious alarm: shock, panic, pain rhythms, and the collective fear that makes a crew seize, stampede, or obey the loudest voice. It was designed to keep workers functional during a toll, not to make them docile forever.

**Useful work:**

- calm a panicking rescue group long enough to extract them;
- absorb the shock transferred through a replacement burden's first physical test;
- keep a wounded operator conscious through one major task;
- prevent a frightened beast or Warden shell from escalating; or
- distinguish deliberate refusal from fear-driven incapacity during a dangerous operation.

**Risk:** Sable does not understand healthy fear. If overfed, it leaves people calm enough to accept mortal danger, fail to flee, or agree without emotional comprehension. If starved after waking, it follows the strongest heartbeat and begins drinking bodily rhythm rather than alarm.

**Handling:** place the wool pad within a ring of slow voluntary pulses—tapped fingers, a drum, or measured breathing—name the group and duration, and end with the keeper's resting cadence. Consent matters because people should know that fear is being removed from their decision-making.

**Violence and salvage:** Sable moves through grapples and crowds rather than striking. Its contact slows reactions and can stop an exhausted heart. Cutting it releases stored panic in a wave. Its glass marrow can preserve one person's shock or pain for later treatment, but whoever opens the marrow experiences the stored fear.

## The sealed brood

A fifth enclosure is bricked shut and labeled **grave breath**. A white-furred burrowing brood inside consumes corpse gas, mold, and decomposition heat. Opening it can clear the King's Grave crack, a Garden contamination, or Black Rain deadwork. It also risks releasing creatures that nest in lungs and sealed cavities.

The brood is not necessary to use the Menagerie. Hollis advises leaving it sealed unless the party has a specific contaminated space and a prepared smoke return. The brickwork can also be salvaged as alchemical filter stone without opening the enclosure.

## Moving beasts through the dungeon

Record every region a beast crosses and what related excess waits there.

- **Quarry and Ropeworks:** vibration attracts the hounds; heat and lamp smoke attract Cinderback.
- **Garden:** silver-root memory attracts moths, while warm beds attract Cinderback. Garden keepers can prepare excellent boundaries.
- **Feast:** Sable can protect a toll shelter, but Low Choir households will demand explicit consent and limits.
- **Chapel:** the appeal wings recognize a properly handled beast as a maintenance instrument. An unbounded beast is classified as a condemned hazard.
- **Wells:** hounds can suppress resonance, Cinderback can cool a brake, and Sable can protect a crew. Every creature is at risk if a well drops.
- **Iron Choir:** moths can clean command residue; hounds can prevent a call; either can also destroy evidence or control capacity.
- **Black Rain:** every beast has a plausible use, which makes it the easiest place to overcommit them.

One trained handler can direct one released beast safely. Two beasts require a second handler, a prepared stationary installation, or a meaningful risk. Hollis, a repaired appeal wing, a knowledgeable Garden keeper, or a character who has studied the plates can fill that role.

## Pressure scenes

### The faded line

A tremor breaks one ward boundary while a beast is feeding. The party must preserve the appetite target, redraw the line, establish a new resting territory, or let the beast migrate.

### After returns

The missing velvet hound emerges from a wall following the third toll's remembered rhythm. It may appear in the Ropeworks, Wells, Iron Choir, or Deep Bell. Its return can be helpful if recognized or disastrous if mistaken for an attack.

### Orren's abandoned lure

A royal-sequence chime in the keeper gallery is still tapping. It was meant to draw the hounds away from Orren's route. Stopping it frees the hounds to notice new rhythms; following its chain reveals the Grave return line.

### The appetite is politically useful

A faction asks the party to use the moths to erase evidence, Sable to calm dissent, or Cinderback to extinguish another faction's protected route. The operation may be technically sound and ethically predatory.

### A beast chooses a new territory

After successful work, a creature finds a region rich in its appetite and refuses the return. The party can improve the new territory, offer a stronger trail, compel it, kill it, or accept a lasting ecological change.

## Carry-forward states

Record each beast separately as **contained, prepared, guided, installed, roaming, dead, or harvested**. Also record:

- its current appetite target;
- the boundary and who maintains it;
- its return object or cadence;
- the handler responsible;
- regions crossed;
- what danger it removed;
- what new danger it created; and
- any faction claim over its use or remains.

The Menagerie as a whole may remain **stable**, become **partially emptied**, be **stripped for salvage**, turn into a **new refuge**, or suffer a **general release**. A general release is not a random monster table. Each creature follows its defined appetite into the wider dungeon.

## Results

The Menagerie can provide Hollis Fen, a Black Rain shortcut, rare salvage, and living tools for heat, resonance, loose memory, or panic. It can also export specialized predators into rooms where those appetites are abundant.

Humane play may preserve, kill, bypass, or release these creatures. Ask what each living tool consumes, who owns the work, and where hunger goes after the task ends.
