# The Ropeworks of Three Burdens

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** hub, machinery, vertical, rescue

Three burden drums turn the upper house; each repaired route strains another system.

## Opening View

A drum moves. A hand’s breadth only. It is enough. Black water climbs a service channel; silver memory lines draw taut and begin to sing; the Crown chain jerks above a bridge crowded with survivors. Three instructions shine on the drum housing where Orren’s copied schedule left blank metal. Then the bridge drops another inch.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

Here the buried house admits that it is a machine. Three immense drums govern lifts, water, bridges, memory lines, and the Deep Bell's frame. No burden moves invisibly; every transfer appears as motion, slack, or strain on the other two drums.

The Ember Company reached this chamber intact. Factor Orren Saye used a royal seal and an abridged extraction schedule to access the Crown Drum. His copy called the outer chain a release restraint. The complete sequence, still cut into the drum housing, requires operators to unload the Crown Drum, set the Water Drum forward, and arrest the Memory Drum before that chain is opened. Orren's copy omits those three lines.

Captain Maelin Rook saw live strain on all three drums and refused the cut. Orren ordered it anyway, arguing that delay would let Harrowgate seize the Crown. The struggle left the outer chain severed, the Water Drum in reverse, and the Memory Drum half-braked. The first uncontrolled toll followed within the hour.

## The three drums

### The Crown Drum

Black rope and bronze chain descend toward the Counterweight Wells and Deep Bell. One of three royal chains is severed. The drum now slips whenever the mountain flexes. Bracing it reduces immediate movement but prevents the main royal lift from descending.

### The Water Drum

Ceramic-jacketed ropes operate sluices between the Lantern Cistern, Reliquary Under Water, and Black Rain Cistern. It currently runs in reverse, sending warm lower water upward in pulses. Correcting it can drain one region only by flooding another unless secondary gates are opened locally.

### The Memory Drum

Silver-gray cords connect ward lamps, speaking tubes, and the Choir of Iron Tongues. Its brake stores the impressions taken during each toll. Releasing it restores fragments of memory to nearby sufferers but extinguishes ward-light and alerts the Low Choir.

For every operation, name what moves, what stops, and where the displaced burden lands.

## Survivors on the hanging bridge

Engineer **Pell Varo**, shield-bearer **Dain Cor**, and two wounded porters are trapped on a bridge between the Water and Crown drums. A snapped lift cage blocks their retreat. Pell can explain the immediate machinery and repair one major component if given tools and protection. He is furious with Orren but does not know whether Captain Rook survived.

Rescuing them can be handled through climbing, rotating a drum, counterweighting the bridge, using the quarry winch, crossing from the Scriptorium gallery, or reaching the bridge's lower anchor from the Salt Barracks.

## Active threats

**Load ghosts** appear as translucent workers whenever a drum moves without a declared operator. They repeat the last fatal labor performed at that station: hauling, cutting, catching, or being dragged into a brake. They can be fought, but naming a responsible operator and accepting the drum's visible mark makes them stand down.

**The slipping Crown Drum** advances toward failure during prolonged conflict. Telegraph each slip with a hard metallic report and a corresponding lurch in the shaft.

**Ember Company deserters** may occupy the upper control booth. They want the surviving porters rescued but fear Captain Rook will execute them for siding with Orren. They can offer crossbows, route knowledge, and false testimony.

## Courses of action

### Stabilize first

Shore the Crown Drum, lock the Water Drum, and leave Memory untouched. This creates the safest upper hub but closes the royal lift and leaves lower flooding unresolved.

### Restore transit

Repair the lift controls and accept continued strain on the Crown Drum. Travel becomes fast, wounded can be evacuated, and the Counterweight Wells are easier to reach. A later tremor may drop the lift if the crown chain remains unrepaired.

### Reverse the water

Send the Water Drum forward. The Reliquary begins draining while the Black Rain Cistern rises. This opens treasure and a route to the Inverted Chapel but worsens the deepest pressure hazard.

### Release stored memory

Ease the Memory brake. Nearby victims recover names, maps, and moments. Ward lamps dim across the dungeon, and creatures kept quiet by light begin to move. The Low Choir immediately knows someone has touched its stolen memories.

### Cut another burden free

The party can deliberately sever a drum and localize the disaster: drop a lift to lock the shaft, break a sluice line to isolate water, or cut memory cords to end the bell's reach in one region. Each creates a permanent route change and a named cost.

## Routes

- upper galleries reach the Hall of Bent Knees and Chain Scriptorium;
- a service bridge reaches the Salt Barracks;
- the water lift descends toward the Reliquary Under Water;
- the royal lift descends toward the Counterweight Wells; and
- rope ladders behind the Water Drum reach the Lantern Cistern.

A party that controls the Ropeworks can traverse much of the dungeon quickly. A party that destroys it can still proceed through regional loops but loses easy extraction.

## What Pell Varo can do

Given time, tools, and one secure work site, Pell can complete one major repair before the fourth toll:

- restore the royal lift;
- fit a temporary crown-chain splice;
- synchronize the upper water gates;
- create a controlled Memory Drum release; or
- prepare demolition that will drop the Ropeworks without propagating upward.

A second task requires a recovered maintenance plate from the Chain Scriptorium or assistance from the Salt Wardens.

## Outcomes

Record all three drums, the survivors, and whether the hub remains traversable. Control here shortens rescue and extraction; destruction leaves regional loops but makes every later operation slower and harder.
