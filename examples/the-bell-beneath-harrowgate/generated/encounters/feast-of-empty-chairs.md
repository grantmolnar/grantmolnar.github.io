# The Feast of Empty Chairs

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** faction, memory, social, refuge

The Low Choir feeds its living and absent at one table, then asks what Harrowgate will owe.

## Opening View

The bread is hot, and no one seems surprised that you have come. Fewer than half the chairs are occupied, but every empty place has been served: a clay cup, a small duty weight, a name carved by another hand. A child slides an unclaimed bowl toward you. “Who above would notice,” they ask, “if your name did not come home?”

## Encounter Material

## Situation

The Feast fills an old ration hall and the dwellings cut around it. Brick ovens warm the chamber; laundry crosses the upper galleries; cooking tile repairs the royal mosaics. Every king has been covered. The laborers remain visible. At the tables, absence is given a chair, a cup, and enough ordinary hospitality to keep it from becoming an abstraction.

The hall itself holds one hundred ninety-two chairs. Fewer than half are used by living diners. At every empty place rests a clay cup, a small duty weight, and a name incised in a different hand from the one before it. The cups do not contain ghosts. They hold witnessed memories drawn out of the ward-light or caught by the Garden's silver roots: a mother's way of testing bread with one knuckle, the order of a quarry song, the smell of rain on a street that no longer exists, the reason one person forgave another. The Low Choir sets food before these places because remembrance without ordinary hospitality becomes an archive, and an archive can be closed.

The **Low Choir** lives in the hall and its cut-stone dwellings. Shared recollection becomes harmony in the Bell's channels: one voice can be dismissed as error; several independent voices speaking the same name become a record the machine must recognize.

The third toll took seventeen names from the Feast district. Three have been recovered in the Garden. Four more survive in cups whose living owners no longer know why they weep when they touch them. If the fourth toll sounds while the Mourning Bough remains fully lit, every unshielded household here will lose another anchor memory.

## First reception

The Low Choir neither attacks strangers on a known route nor mistakes them for rescuers. A child or elder asks each visitor three practical questions:

1. **Whom did you leave above who would notice if your name vanished?**
2. **What have you carried into this hall that belongs to someone else?**
3. **Which promise made in Harrowgate are you actually empowered to keep?**

Answers may be plain. Refusal closes the household passages but leaves the public hall open. An obvious lie earns no magical punishment and no later trust. Showing a public commission, a Warden recognition mark, rescued Company testimony, food, clean water, a recovered cup, or a person the Choir feared dead changes the opening disposition immediately.

Respect household boundaries and the Feast can support a safe camp, surgery, and ordinary conversation. A full rest costs one household-day of food and clean water per two guests unless the party supplies its own or repairs a local system. A toll during the rest may still strike memory unless the party prepares cups, dims the Mourning Bough, or accepts another protection cost.

## Nara-of-the-Seventh-Name

**Nara Vale** introduces herself as **Nara-of-the-Seventh-Name**, then speaks the six names she carries: Mere, Hama, Voss, Rill, Tett, and Orra. They keep unfinished contracts, kinship, debts, and testimony addressable. The seventh name is her own, spoken last whenever someone mistakes inherited duty for ownership of its bearer.

Nara wants an enforceable compact with Harrowgate before the old arrangement continues for another season. She will accept temporary stabilization if it buys time for rescue, surface preparation, and a replacement burden. She will not accept an indefinite promise to discuss reform after the town is safe. Her preferred outcome is a Common Measure whose obligations are public, bounded, and carried by several communities rather than by one crown or one buried people.

Nara speaks in household measures: sacks of grain, gate hours, signatures, inspection dates, beds for the displaced, names of people empowered to stop the work. “Destiny does not take the night watch,” she says. Bring Nara a generous principle without an operator, route, or remedy; she answers with broth, a chair, and no endorsement.

## The four household positions

The Low Choir is not unanimous. Its households share routes, food, and memory craft, but they disagree about which danger is tolerable.

### The Warm Table

**Bera Loam**, an oven keeper with two burned hands, speaks for households dependent on Garden food, gray water, and ward-light. The Warm Table fears an immediate shutdown that leaves children hungry, invalids in darkness, and the lower dwellings flooded before Harrowgate opens surface housing.

Bera will support controlled continuance for a defined term if the party secures food, water, passage, and a stop condition. She offers cooks, stretcher bearers, Garden labor, and a safe camp. She opposes burning the Garden, extinguishing all four lamp boughs, or releasing the crown before the Wells are ready.

### The Open Cup

**Tern-of-One-Name** refuses inherited names and argues that no survival service justifies another involuntary toll. His households have prepared packs, ash shrouds, and a route toward the Quarry Cleft. They want the Mourning Bough severed and the Deep Bell silenced even if the Feast must evacuate.

Tern can provide black-water masks, guides through dark routes, and volunteers for controlled collapse. He will not consent to a replacement burden that can quietly become permanent. He can be persuaded by a short, testable emergency term with an automatic release clause; he cannot be bought with treasure or royal recognition.

### The Kept Chain

Nara's households maintain records, cups, duty weights, and surface correspondences. They seek legal recognition, regular passage, shared maintenance, and remedies that do not depend on future goodwill. They are the strongest supporters of the Common Measure, but only if the affected bearers speak for themselves at the Chapel.

The Kept Chain can supply three voluntary household weights, trained witnesses, an acceptance liturgy, and the labor needed to carry the frame. Its members will expose any attempt to use a royal seal as a substitute for consent.

### The Narrow Line

**Illa Crownless**, a genealogy keeper, descends from Avarra's household through a line excluded from House Vey's surface register. She does not claim that blood alone makes her queen. She argues that Lady Vey cannot call the crown private inheritance while ignoring the buried line that maintained its sentence.

The Narrow Line wants access to the King's Narrow Grave, public comparison of the three genealogies, and a voice in the crown's disposition. It may support return to Lady Vey, shared custody, destruction, or a civic trust depending on what the records show. Illa offers a servants' passage, mortuary signs, and one living line of the chained genealogy. She withholds them from anyone promising the crown before hearing the buried claim.

These positions overlap. A household may feed with the Warm Table, entrust records to the Kept Chain, and still plan evacuation with the Open Cup. The party does not need unanimous support. It needs to know whose labor, consent, testimony, and routes each settlement actually uses.

## Memory cups

A memory cup is a vessel for witnessed recall, not a cure-all and not a portable soul. Each cup bears a name, an anchor mark, and at least two witness strokes. A cup without a living witness becomes increasingly ambiguous. A living witness without a vessel can preserve testimony, but the next toll may shear away the sensory and emotional detail that makes it usable by the machine.

### Listening

A person may warm a cup between both hands and receive a brief sensory impression. This reveals what kind of memory it holds without transferring it. Listening is safe unless the cup is cracked, contaminated with black water, or opened during a toll.

### Witnessing

Two people who independently knew the memory may speak its name, place, and consequence over the cup. This stabilizes the vessel and lets it serve as proof at the Scriptorium, Chapel, Iron Choir, or a Warden continuity hearing. One true witness plus corroborating material evidence can suffice, but the machine marks the record as repaired rather than complete.

### Carrying

A willing creature may drink from a prepared cup and carry the memory temporarily. The carrier can recall the stored event as if present, use a forgotten route rhythm, recognize a person, or speak an acceptance line the original witness knew. Until the memory is returned, the carrier gives up one comparably vivid personal recollection. It is not destroyed, but it becomes inaccessible and may bleed into the borrowed memory under stress.

No one can be made a stable carrier merely by forcing liquid down their throat. Coercive transfer produces confusion, false familiarity, and an unreliable record. The Chapel and Iron Tongues can distinguish voluntary carrying from imposed occupancy.

### Returning and redirecting

A carried memory can be returned to its cup, poured into a compatible Iron Tongue, rooted in the Garden, or transferred to another willing carrier in the presence of witnesses. Returning the memory restores the carrier's displaced recollection. Breaking the cup releases the memory into nearby ward-light or water, where it may reach an unintended recipient.

### Shielding a toll

Four prepared cups and four willing donors can shield one household cluster, safe camp, or operating crew from the next mnemonic discharge. Each donor places a chosen memory in a cup before the toll. The memories can later be returned if the vessels survive. This is a practical defense, not an invitation to demand sacrifice from the same people already bearing the bell.

The Low Choir has twenty-three sound empty cups, eleven cracked vessels suitable only for immediate listening, and seven cups currently holding memories needed by living people. Nara will not release the last category as general-purpose tools without the owners' consent.

## Inherited names

An inherited name preserves a social address when the original bearer has been erased from living recognition. It can carry:

- a debt or obligation that someone freely accepts;
- standing to testify about a household record;
- access to a route, cup, or maintenance office keyed to that name;
- kinship continuity for children whose parent has been forgotten; and
- a claim to be heard where the old machine would otherwise treat the person as never having existed.

It does **not** transfer the original person's body, marriage, property, guilt, or authority wholesale. The bearer must state which duties they accept and which they refuse. A household must witness the inheritance. The name can later be laid down when records and relationships have another secure home.

This distinction matters at the King's Grave and Inverted Chapel. A chained genealogy can establish standing without proving exclusive ownership. A duty name can authorize maintenance without surrendering the bearer's life to the mechanism. Nara will demonstrate by speaking Mere's unfinished grain promise, Hama's route witness, and then her own refusal to inherit Voss's punishment.

## The six compact weights

The Low Choir has translated generations of grievance into six obligations that can be fitted to the Common Measure. Each is represented by one or more small duty weights. None works as symbolic apology alone; each names a continuing practice and a remedy.

1. **Witness:** Harrowgate keeps a public register of the Low Choir, recovered names, toll losses, and maintenance decisions. Records remain accessible above and below.
2. **Passage:** the people below may travel, settle, trade, and seek aid on the surface without royal or Warden leave.
3. **Provision:** Harrowgate supplies grain, clean water, medicine, and temporary housing while the Garden and cistern systems are repaired or replaced.
4. **Maintenance:** surface crews, Warden operators, and Low Choir keepers share scheduled work. No community can be assigned the whole machine by default.
5. **Limit:** every emergency burden has a stated term, inspection interval, and maximum tolerated toll cost.
6. **Remedy:** breach triggers a real response—automatic shutdown, evacuation, release of the crown, transfer of authority, or another action the affected bearers can invoke.

The weights do not need to be accepted exactly as Nara presents them. The Chapel can recognize another compact if it names equivalent authority, consent, duration, and remedy. Removing an obligation lowers the frame's legal stability and usually shifts material load onto another component. For example, refusing Provision requires faster evacuation or more Garden capacity. Refusing Remedy makes the frame resemble Avarra's coerced sentence and gives the Chapel's execution machinery leverage over it.

### What Harrowgate can presently promise

The party's public commission covers rescue, requisition, ordinary salvage, and immediate stabilization. It does not by itself authorize permanent settlement, crown disposition, taxation, or hereditary recognition.

Useful surface commitments may come from several sources:

- Warden Marr can promise emergency passage, temporary housing, public records, and a council hearing.
- Master Tarrow can commit quarry crews and maintenance schedules if the town survives.
- Lady Vey can bind House Vey's grain stores, genealogy archive, retainers, and claim to the crown, but not the elected town.
- a recognized Warden commander can bind buried-house operators and access routes;
- Low Choir households can bind their own labor, weights, and consent; and
- rescued witnesses can expose later repudiation even when they cannot legislate.

A coalition of bounded promises is therefore more credible than one grand signature. The Common Measure is designed to hold several such obligations side by side.

## Social and operating scenes

### The place that should not be empty

One chair is newly set for a living person the party has met: Tavia Hest, Rhea Colm, Jessa Noll, Pell Varo, or another endangered delver. The cup at the place contains a memory caught during the third toll. Returning the person, carrying the memory to them, or admitting that the party left them behind changes the Feast's disposition more than abstract persuasion.

### The disputed child

Two households remember raising **Soli**, a ten-year-old cup keeper. Soli remembers both homes and refuses to choose which memory is false. A recovered Garden name shows that the households were once one extended family divided by an earlier toll. The scene demonstrates that restoration can complicate identity rather than simply repair it.

### The last bowl

The Warm Table has enough toothcap and salt greens for three household-days after the current meal. Feeding rescued outsiders, preparing an evacuation, and supporting a deep operating crew all draw from the same stock. The party can bring supplies, improve the Garden, shorten its stay, or knowingly spend someone else's margin.

### The surface voice

If the party can reach the Chapterhouse by runner, chain message, or released memory, the Low Choir asks for one surface authority to hear and answer the six obligations before the fourth toll. Marr, Tarrow, Lady Vey, or a delegated party member each creates a different compact. Delay is permissible, but temporary continuance then requires explicit limits.

### A cup that implicates an ally

One recovered memory shows a respected Warden, royal officer, or Ember Company member choosing to redirect a past toll into the Low Choir. The memory is partial but genuine. The party can preserve it, confront the implicated faction, use it as leverage, or suppress it. Nara cares less about punishment than whether the new settlement prevents the same private decision.

## Routes

- **Garden food stair:** the broadest route, normally controlled by keepers. It supports stretchers and supplies but closes if the Quiet Loam floods or the Feast is attacked.
- **Barracks service hall:** a salt door admits recognized Warden crews, household envoys, or anyone carrying a valid duty tablet. Forcing it alarms the Barracks.
- **Scriptorium ration chains:** narrow hanging baskets and maintenance ledges reach the archive. They can move cups, documents, or one careful climber at a time.
- **Reliquary mourning ledge:** available at low or middle water. It enters through a wash room where cups can be cooled and decontaminated.
- **Inverted Chapel mourning stair:** a steep stair ends at the Chapel's Mourning Brake. It is the safest route for witnesses and the Common Measure, provided the Chapel has not dropped.
- **King's Grave servants' passage:** hidden behind the Narrow Line's genealogy wall. It opens only with buried-line signs, a Scriptorium concordance, or physical excavation.
- **Black-water crawl:** Tern's evacuation route reaches lower conduits toward Black Rain. It is fast, dark, and unsuitable for heavy loads or unprotected memory vessels.

## Conflict and force

The Feast has no standing army. It has doors, boiling water, smoke flues, knowledge of every crawlspace, and many people capable of making an invader's route expensive. Violence here rapidly becomes a hostage and fire problem rather than a clean battle.

If attacked, households scatter cups and children into different routes, cut ration chains, flood the wash room, and call Garden keepers or sympathetic Wardens. A victorious party can seize weights, cups, and guides, but coercive consent will fail at the Chapel and every humane ending becomes harder. Register that cost without declaring the crawl unwinnable.

## Carry-forward states

Record the Feast broadly as one of these states, then note household exceptions.

- **Sheltered:** the hall remains a refuge; households are cautious but cooperative.
- **Compacted:** enough households and surface authorities have accepted specific obligations to support a replacement burden.
- **Divided:** households support incompatible outcomes and will contribute only separately.
- **Evacuating:** routes, food, and guides are committed to moving people upward or toward a prepared deep refuge.
- **Tollbroken:** memory loss, violence, fire, or betrayal has shattered common witness; individual households may still aid the party.

Also record food remaining, cups allocated, memories carried, inherited names invoked, compact weights promised, surface authorities contacted, and which households have consented to which finale architecture.

## Results

Leave the Feast with people, duties, and terms—not just information. It can provide a safe camp, guides, witnesses, household weights, black-water equipment, genealogy standing, rescue labor, and routes to the Chapel or Grave. It can also impose deadlines and expose the human cost of apparently functional machinery.

Low Choir endorsement never gates the Deep Bell. Bypass or alienate the Feast and lose witnesses, humane operators, evacuation capacity, and some chance that a replacement will differ from the old coercion.
