# The Counterweight Wells

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** machinery, vertical, engineering, deep

Three Wells separate mass, path, and timing; every shortcut makes one loss permanent.

## Opening View

Pera Quill laughs once when the Royal weight rises. It is not amusement. Chains answer through the chamber: one line bows toward Harrowgate, another shivers with voices belonging to the dead. Pera lies pinned beneath a fallen signal frame while Captain Rook and two delvers brace the platform between the Wells. Nothing has fallen. That is the trouble.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

Three shafts fall beyond ordinary light, each crossed by a weight large enough to crush a house. The Royal Well carries Crown and cut-chain load; the Civic Well carries accepted public burdens and Harrowgate's ridge; the Funerary Well carries water, memory, and the dead built into the house.

Each mountain groan lifts the Royal weight a finger's width. Orren's cut left it light, forcing strain through the Chapel counterchain into the Civic and Funerary paths: the ridge bows while toll-memory drives toward the Low Choir.

Every plan must answer three questions:

1. **Mass:** what physical or magical load balances the system?
2. **Path:** which chains, drums, frames, and communities actually receive that load?
3. **Timing:** in what order are brakes, water, and weights moved so the bell does not take the difference all at once?

Solving only one question creates a short-term brace or a new casualty.

## Approaches

The Wells have five principal approaches.

- The **Ropeworks royal lift** reaches the Crown Brake above the Royal Well. It is fast if the Crown Drum still functions and treacherous if the royal line is loose.
- The **Barracks muster stair** reaches the Civic gallery and gives a Warden crew room to operate.
- The **Chapel transfer door** reaches the distribution frame where an accepted burden can be attached.
- The **Black Rain lower gallery** reaches piston feet and emergency drain valves.
- The **Iron Choir control walk** reaches timing chimes and remote brake commands.

Two approaches permit safe local work; three make full rebalancing much easier. Chain descent, a cut service wall, or a moving weight can substitute at greater risk.

## The surviving crews

**Engineer Pell Varo** may arrive from the Ropeworks if rescued or contacted. Without Pell, plates, experiments, Warden crews, Elra Mott, Hollis Fen, or player expertise still operate the Wells. Pell's advantage is coordination: he can conduct one major movement while others handle complications.

Three Deep Rank Wardens are already here under Orren's sealed order:

- **Istren Vale** guards the Royal Brake and believes restoring a crowned line is the fastest lawful solution.
- **Malk Salt-Under-Tongue** operates the timing chimes but has discovered that Orren's order omitted the Funerary load.
- **Pera Quill** is pinned beneath a fallen signal frame near the Civic Well and has heard the Low Choir's memory through the chains.

The three are not a unified enemy. Istren may enforce the sealed order. Malk will suspend it if shown contradictory command or physical evidence. Pera wants rescue before law. A recognized Warden commander can recall them, but old command pressure and immediate danger may still require conversation or force.

**Captain Maelin Rook** is also here if she survived the expedition's split. By default, she occupies a braced platform between the Civic and Funerary Wells with two exhausted company members, **Tomas Reed** and **Veyla Sorn**. Rook severed Orren's second demolition line and has held the platform through two tremors. She will cooperate with anyone prioritizing rescue and stabilization, but refuses to surrender her people to Lady Vey's retainers or Warden punishment before the crisis is contained.

If Rook is elsewhere, leave her field signs, cut lines, and written orders here; move the survivors accordingly. The Wells remain operable without her.

## Reading the Wells

Observable evidence lets the party learn the system without guessing.

- Plumb chains show the Royal weight rising, Civic weight descending, and Funerary weight oscillating.
- Chalk arcs left by Pell record how far each moved after the first three tolls.
- Water gauges show that the Funerary pistons take load from the Reliquary and Black Rain systems.
- Chapel response lamps identify whether a candidate replacement burden is recognized, partial, coercive, or absent.
- Iron timing chimes sound one beat before each safe brake window.
- Hairline cracks in the Civic gallery align with Harrowgate streets above.
- Names appearing in condensation on the Funerary chains identify memory and ancestral load being transferred.

A party can test a small movement with hand jacks, portable weights, water changes, or a beast. Safe experiments give clear results. Failed experiments move a component, injure a route, or advance the clock; they never hide the operating principle.

## The three wells

### The Royal Well

The Royal weight is a single red granite block bound in iron bands. A crown-shaped void runs through its center. Its chain bundle once connected directly to the Crown of Measure through the Crown Drum, Chapel royal return, and Deep Bell clapper cage.

Orren cut one royal chain and removed a sequence plate. The weight now rises, loading the bell frame and pulling the Crown toward an uncontrolled strike. Repairing the cut line requires a splice, royal sequence, or substitute coupling. The Grave contains the cleanest sequence and compatible royal metal. The Scriptorium, Ropeworks, and Wardens provide slower alternatives.

Possible Royal Well operations include:

- **splice the Queen's chain:** restore the old load path temporarily, preserving the crown's sentence unless a later extraction is prepared;
- **attach an accepted replacement:** connect the Chapel-recognized frame through the Civic distributor and gradually remove royal load;
- **install equivalent metal:** add crown-bars, reliquary segments, salvage, or other measured mass while preserving the current path;
- **brace the weight:** lock it against the shaft wall, buying time but disabling the royal lift and increasing frame strain;
- **cross-load another well:** move Royal load into Civic, Funerary, water, or surface systems with a named consequence; or
- **drop the Royal weight:** permanently sever the crown path, jerking the bell upward and making crown extraction easier but damaging the upper frame and royal routes.

The Royal Well reacts to recognized authority because its brakes were designed to prevent seizure. Authority can open the mechanism; it cannot make an unsafe load safe.

### The Civic Well

The Civic weight is not one block. It is a cage holding thousands of replaceable stones, each stamped with a district, guild, household, bridge, well, or public obligation. The cage allows distributed maintenance. It also makes hidden political choices visible: later custodians removed affluent surface weights and replaced them with unmarked Low Choir burdens.

The Civic Well is where the Common Measure attaches. Its distribution frame has six receiving arms corresponding to witness, passage, provision, maintenance, limit, and remedy. A complete compact need not use identical names, but every physical arm needs a declared source, maintainer, and failure response.

Possible Civic operations include:

- add or remove district stones to change who bears strain;
- install the Common Measure or another accepted frame;
- reconnect surface foundations through the Ropeworks and Chapterhouse;
- substitute treasury crown-bars, quarry stone, water mass, or suspended salvage;
- expose and remove the unmarked Low Choir weights;
- deliberately spread a short emergency load among consenting crews and institutions; or
- lock the cage as a temporary brace, preserving Harrowgate while preventing further redistribution.

Every new Civic load needs an accessible maintenance route. A brilliant burden sealed behind a collapsed Chapel is not a stable settlement.

### The Funerary Well

The Funerary weight is a lattice of copper coffins, memory vessels, water jackets, and bone-filled counterboxes. It receives pressure from the Reliquary, Garden, Iron Choir, and Black Rain. The original purpose was to let maintenance dead and voluntary memorial burdens continue supporting the system. Later custodians expanded it until involuntary Low Choir memory became ordinary operating mass.

The well oscillates because water and memory are arriving out of phase. A person standing near the chains hears remembered work songs, last instructions, and names the living have forgotten.

Possible Funerary operations include:

- synchronize Reliquary water and Iron Choir memory release;
- return stored memory, reducing mass and requiring replacement weight elsewhere;
- root selected memories in the Garden or transfer them to willing carriers;
- add voluntary Warden tablets or memorial vessels after explicit release;
- isolate the Funerary path, protecting memory while moving load into water or stone;
- brace the coffin lattice for one toll; or
- drop the Funerary weight, sealing many memories below and sending a pressure wave into Black Rain and the Mouth Below.

Destroying the vessels leaves the people represented in them bound. Returning memory, preserving evidence, accepting loss, and preventing immediate catastrophe may pull in different directions.

## The brake houses

Each well has a local brake and all three answer a central **Measure House**.

- The **Crown Brake** releases in seven unequal steps keyed to the royal sequence. A guessed sequence may work until the fourth or fifth step, where accumulated error becomes visible.
- The **Civic Brake** is operated by six levers. Any four can move the cage, but missing obligations determine where excess load goes.
- The **Mourning Brake** uses water pulses and voice chimes. It can be run manually by a crew, through the Iron Choir, or with a Reliquary saint connection.
- The **Measure House** compares plumb, water, and timing. It reveals which component takes the difference; it never chooses who ought to pay.

A single operator can hold one brake. Two trained operators can conduct a local transfer. A full three-well movement normally needs three crews or compensating tools: an appeal wing, Warden detail, quiet beast, prepared Ropeworks drum, hydraulic saint, or destructive one-way release.

## Replacement mass

The Wells measure equivalent load rather than noble blood. Compatible material matters because it must fit, endure, and transmit recognized authority, but the system will accept distributed stone, metal, water, memory, or living labor if properly coupled.

Useful sources include:

- the Common Measure handweights and acceptance collar;
- crown-bars and chain metal from the Narrow Grave;
- quarry stone committed by Harrowgate;
- water held in Reliquary saints or Black Rain pistons;
- Garden bone loam and root tension;
- Warden tablets voluntarily dedicated or dissolved;
- treasure, tools, armor, or structural metal carried by the party;
- temporary human crews using the Pilgrim's Yoke; and
- a quiet beast installed to consume heat, resonance, or panic during the transfer.

A source can supply mass without supplying legitimacy, or legitimacy without enough mass. The Measure House displays both separately.

## Standard operations

### Brace one well

Lock a weight to its shaft using wall dogs, chain wraps, water pressure, or a beast-assisted brake. Bracing buys time and creates a stable work platform. It disables at least one route or transfer and pushes slow strain into the frame.

### Exchange load between wells

Set the receiving brake, open the path, release the sending brake in timed steps, and confirm where the difference appears. A rushed exchange may save one region while damaging another. Name the receiving cost before the final release whenever the party has adequate gauges or expertise.

### Install an accepted burden

Bring the recognized frame through the Chapel door or an improvised route. Attach its material fittings to the Civic distributor. Load it gradually while the Royal path is reduced. Test every declaration arm. If one is partial, the corresponding excess appears as a route, labor, maintenance, consent, or remedy problem rather than generic instability.

### Remove the Crown load

This operation may free the crown, prepare a replacement, or make a controlled collapse possible. It requires a receiving mass or a deliberate drop. The party can reduce the Royal load before reaching the Deep Bell, making final extraction safer, but doing so changes routes and may alert Avarra's retained will.

### Conduct an emergency human hold

The Pilgrim's Yoke and maintenance harnesses let a crew bear a fraction of one well while a brake is reset. This is survivable for a short, witnessed interval with consent, relief rotation, and Sable or medical support. Without those protections, it becomes another coerced burden.

### Drop a well

A drop is a permanent one-way operation. Cut or release the final dogs, open a destination path, and let the weight fall into its sump. The falling mass absorbs a major strain event and buys immediate time. It also destroys routes, assets, and future options according to the well.

## Dropped-well outcomes

### Royal drop

- The crown path goes slack and the Crown becomes easier to remove.
- The Deep Bell jumps upward and damages its upper frame.
- The royal lift, Grave return, and some Scriptorium chains fail.
- Lady Vey loses the cleanest restoration option and may treat the act as destruction of inheritance.
- Harrowgate needs Civic, water, or collapse stabilization afterward.

### Civic drop

- The inhabited ridge is released from immediate load.
- The Common Measure distributor and many surface-foundation links are destroyed.
- The Wells maintenance bridge becomes a broken descent.
- A long-term distributed settlement becomes much harder.
- The town may survive the next toll while losing its buried infrastructure.

### Funerary drop

- Toll-memory stops reaching the Low Choir for a time.
- Stored memories, Warden releases, and memorial vessels are sealed or shattered below.
- Black Rain and Mouth pressure rise sharply.
- The lower bridge becomes flooded or buried.
- Iron Choir evidence may be preserved above, lost below, or violently released depending on timing.

A dropped well is not failure. It converts uncertain future risk into a named permanent loss. More than one well can be dropped, but each additional drop narrows the finale toward evacuation, collapse, or a Mouth Below settlement.

## Counterweight conflicts

### Orren's sealed order

Istren attempts to restore Royal priority while another operation is underway. The party can countermand, persuade, isolate the command line, prove omitted load, or fight over the Crown Brake.

### Rook's rescue line

Rook's platform begins to descend. Saving the crew requires Civic load, a quiet hound, Chapel chain, or abandoning another moving operation. Rook will cut her own platform loose if it prevents a mass casualty above.

### The chain chooses a memory

The Funerary weight catches a loose name or personal memory from someone nearby. Releasing it changes balance. Leaving it in place gives a small mechanical advantage at a human cost.

### A perfect mass in the wrong path

The party finds enough metal or stone, but the only open route would assign it to an unintended population or destroy a retreat. The challenge is not obtaining more weight but changing the coupling.

### The bell moves early

An Iron Choir command, tremor, or Deep Bell action advances the timing window. The party must complete a partial transfer, arrest all brakes, or deliberately spend one component.

## Failure without dead ends

On failed operations, choose a concrete state change:

- one weight moves farther than intended;
- a brake becomes hot, jammed, or one-way;
- a route opens while another closes;
- a crew is pinned or separated;
- load shifts to a named district, household, memory store, or water path;
- the tremor clock advances;
- a beast claims a new territory;
- a Warden order propagates; or
- a well becomes droppable but no longer finely controllable.

After a mistake, use new plumb marks, cracks, water levels, and voices to show exactly what moved.

## Carry-forward states

Record each well as **balanced, braced, redistributed, slipping, severed, or dropped**. Also record:

- actual or estimated mass;
- current load path;
- local brake condition;
- operators and crews;
- linked water and memory state;
- available routes;
- replacement fittings;
- named populations or structures bearing excess;
- timing windows or failed chimes; and
- whether another major operation remains possible.

The whole system may be **stable for one toll**, **prepared for crown extraction**, **prepared for replacement**, **prepared for distributed stabilization**, **prepared for controlled collapse**, or **in cascading failure**.

## Results

The Wells can rescue Rook's surviving company, recall or divide Deep Rank Wardens, attach a replacement burden, reduce the Crown load, establish a safe Deep Bell approach, or convert an imminent catastrophe into a permanent sacrifice.

The Wells decide neither justice nor Crown custody. They make every proposed answer pay its physical bill.
