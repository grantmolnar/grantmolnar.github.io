# The Garden of Teeth

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** ossuary, ecology, routes, hazard

An ossuary garden feeds the living, filters black water, catches names, and hides Harrowgate's pressure vents.

## Opening View

The Garden says your names one heartbeat too early. White caps rise from ordered jawbones in three black beds, speaking in voices that nearly belong to people you know. Warm water enters carrying oil, ash, and scraps of memory. Beyond the silver roots, someone whispers, “Do not answer.” Along the outer wall, numbered shutters wait beneath costs cut too deeply to weather away.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

The Garden grew where ossuary, filter house, and burial kitchen ceased to be separate institutions. Jawbones stand upright in black loam. White caps rise from the teeth, and silver roots weave beneath the beds, catching oil, ash, and memory from passing water. The Low Choir eats here, drinks here, remembers here, and can save the town here by destroying some part of all four.

Cool water makes the caps murmur scraps without ownership: a work song, the shape of a door, the smell of a mother's sleeve. Warm black water drives every tooth toward a complete name. Answering gives the Garden a pattern to grow toward. It may take recognition rather than fact: companions know the person is present but forget how they are related, which promises belong to them, or why the absence would matter.

The Garden is working infrastructure with four necessary jobs.

1. It filters black water before that water reaches the Lantern Cistern and Low Choir dwellings.
2. It grows toothcaps, root meal, and salt greens that feed the people below.
3. It catches mnemonic discharge that would otherwise enter surface wells or the Iron Choir in uncontrolled bursts.
4. Its dry root ways and pressure sluices cross the buried house laterally, including a route to the quarry vents.

Destroying the Garden buys immediate relief and creates water, food, memory, and pressure crises. Preserving it unchanged leaves the Low Choir dependent on ancestral remains and exposed to every toll. Show both truths in play.

## Arrival and visible choices

Five routes expose different parts of the ecology: Quarry, Lantern, Barracks, Feast, and Menagerie.

- The **quarry route** reaches the dry upper roots and the vent shutters. It is easiest for bypassing the beds and best for preparing controlled collapse.
- The **Lantern conduit** enters beside the first filter bed. Water, oil, or wick-eaters displaced above arrive here.
- The **Barracks door** opens directly into the Bitter Bed where missing salt tablets are hidden.
- The **Feast stair** reaches the tended beds, food stores, and Garden keepers.
- The **Menagerie tunnel** crosses refuse pens and can release maintenance beasts if opened carelessly.

No route requires a living name as payment. The Garden keepers know work names, salt mouthpieces, water rhythms, and covered walkways that permit safe crossing. A desperate traveler can feed the Garden a name to grow an instant bridge or reveal a path, but this is an exploit of the system, not the intended solution.

## The keepers

**Edda-of-the-Warm-Stone** is the senior keeper present. She is a broad, elderly Low Choir woman with toothcap flour on both arms and a copper cup tied over her mouth. Edda speaks through gesture, written slate, and short work names while in the beds. Outside the warm mist she is direct and impatient with anyone who calls the Garden merely a graveyard or merely a farm.

Edda’s priorities are practical:

- preserve enough food for the next month;
- stop contaminated water from reaching the Feast hall;
- recover the Warden tablets without surrendering them to the faction that caused their removal;
- keep the quarry vents available for Harrowgate’s evacuation plan; and
- prevent the Wardens or surface claimants from taking the silver root mesh as treasure.

Her apprentice, **Kell Nine-Spoons**, is a teenager who knows every dry route and has recently begun losing the reason behind several household names. Kell can guide the party quickly, but warm mist causes him to answer voices he recognizes. Protecting Kell is not required for passage. It makes later Low Choir negotiations personal and preserves an expert route guide.

Three other keepers work deeper beds and can be introduced as a group unless the scene needs individuals. They do not fight to the death for the Garden. They shut sluices, release spores, hide in root hollows, and bargain with access to food and clean water.

## The three filter beds

### The Jaw Catch

The first bed is built from ordinary burial bone and coarse salt loam. It catches grit, metal filings, blood, and the heavy black flakes that rise from the fault water. The white toothcaps here are edible if cut before they begin speaking. A careful harvest supplies a week of trail rations for a party or one day of food for the Low Choir.

The Jaw Catch is currently overloaded by Reliquary pulses. Clearing it requires shoveling hot black sediment, redirecting water, using the pallbearer or a maintenance beast, or sacrificing one section of root mat. Failure sends oily contamination toward Lantern and wakes the caps into a grasping carpet.

### The Silver Root

The middle bed is a woven mesh of pale roots threaded with old memory wire. It catches names, duties, and relational impressions. The material resembles valuable silver but cannot be removed in quantity without collapsing the filter. Small cuttings serve as mnemonic sutures, Warden-tablet repair fiber, or components for the Common Measure’s witness frame.

Memories caught here can be recovered by cooling a root, naming the intended recipient, and drawing the impression into a cup, salt veil, or living witness. Pulling without a recipient gives the nearest creature an intrusive relationship: it may remember itself as someone’s parent, debtor, murderer, or promised guide until the memory is placed elsewhere.

At the center of the mesh grows a tight black knot called **the Widow Root**. It contains decades of unreturned names from toll victims. Cutting it would release a powerful wave of memory, perhaps restoring many Low Choir records or overwhelming everyone nearby. Leaving it preserves filtration but keeps those memories inaccessible.

### The Quiet Loam

The final bed is deep dark soil mixed with dissolved salt tablets, cremation ash, and the remains of exhausted toothcaps. It removes the last mnemonic heat from the water and grows salt greens along its edges. Properly filtered water leaves here gray, cool, and safe enough for cooking.

The Quiet Loam also remembers commands. Old Warden phrases spoken nearby cause roots to form temporary straight lines, opening or closing service passages. This is how Orren’s party crossed toward the Feast route without dealing with the keepers. The command pattern remains and can be followed, erased, or turned into an ambush.

## The name hazard

Escalate the name hazard through signs; do not reduce it to one roll that deletes a character.

### Exposure signs

1. Teeth whisper work names or titles the party has used.
2. A character hears a true name spoken in the voice of someone trusted.
3. The Garden repeats a relationship: “my captain,” “her brother,” “their debtor.”
4. Another character briefly forgets why the exposed person matters.
5. The Garden grows a pale likeness or route shaped by the offered identity.

Interruption, cooling water, salt over the mouth, strong ward-light, or another person deliberately restating the relationship can stop escalation. If the Garden takes a name, record one lost recognition. Recover it through the grown cap, a returned memory, a salt veil, or reenactment of the relationship beyond the Garden's hearing.

### Safe methods

- Wear copper or waxed-cloth mouth covers and communicate by sign.
- Use temporary work names that describe the present task without claiming identity.
- Keep the nearest water cool and moving.
- Carry a lit Ossuary lamp; this suppresses speaking caps but directs caught memory toward the Iron Choir.
- Follow a keeper who taps route rhythm on jawbone rails.
- Spread dry salt across a narrow bed, accepting damage to that harvest.
- Move through the upper roots without entering the mist, if the route has not shifted.

### Dangerous leverage

A living name can be used intentionally to make the Garden act. Offering one may:

- grow a bridge across a broken filter;
- identify a route associated with that person’s duty or ancestry;
- lure a name-eating swarm away from someone else;
- awaken memory in a Warden tablet; or
- open a root gate that no current keeper knows.

The cost should match the leverage. The Garden takes a relationship, legal recognition, or remembered obligation connected to the offered name. The party can later recover it, but not by pretending no cost occurred.

## The Bitter Bed and the missing tablets

The Barracks ossuary door opens beside a bed whose caps taste of mineral bitterness and speak in clipped military cadence. Three salt tablets are rooted here.

### Prefect Ardel Quoin

Quoin’s command tablet is split diagonally and wrapped in silver root. It remembers ordering an inquiry, learning that the original Warden compact permitted release when an accepted replacement burden existed, and asking a Garden keeper to remove it before the literal-order faction could suppress the finding. It does not remember the keeper’s face, the final vote, or the years immediately before removal.

The tablet can be:

- read in place, where the Garden fills gaps with nearby memories and may create false confidence;
- cut free carefully, preserving the root as repair fiber;
- torn free quickly, losing one surviving memory but avoiding a long exposure;
- repaired in the Garden using caught Low Choir memories, which risks confusing command with the people harmed by it; or
- left as an independent witness rather than returned to Quoin’s shell.

Quoin’s recovery changes Barracks command but does not produce a perfect ruling. The damaged record proves that a release clause exists and that Quoin consented to removal. It cannot prove which current faction deserves command.

### Ora Pell

Ora’s tablet lies under a cap shaped like a closed hand. It records her refusal to seize Low Choir children during a food riot and her voluntary separation from her shell so that the literal command could not operate through her body. Ora will speak from cool brine. She asks not to be rejoined without a new freely chosen duty.

### Maston Grey

Maston’s tablet has been nailed beneath a jaw plate and nearly overgrown. It records that a former commander removed it after Maston witnessed falsification of the Low Choir ration count. Maston wants rejoining, formal testimony, and command accountability. He is furious that his blank shell has been used as evidence that tablet removal was always voluntary.

Recovering all three is valuable but not mandatory. Each tablet asks a different question about continuity, consent, and useful service.

## Food, water, and harvest

The Garden can provide concrete resources.

- **Toothcap cakes:** compact rations that remain edible for weeks if kept dry. Eating one near active ward-light may produce a harmless borrowed memory.
- **Salt greens:** bitter leaves that reduce mnemonic intrusion and help a person distinguish personal memory from transferred impressions.
- **Gray water:** filtered water safe for drinking and for Warden-tablet release.
- **Silver-root cuttings:** limited repair and memory-transfer material.
- **Name brine:** concentrated fluid that can awaken a tablet, power an Iron Tongue, or dangerously imitate authority.
- **Bone loam:** heavy, stable mass suitable for temporary counterweight fill.

The party may harvest a modest expedition supply without harming the Low Choir. Large extraction, sale, or use as replacement mass creates a claim that must be negotiated. Edda can state exactly how many household-days each proposed load represents.

## The pressure sluices

Three root-lined shutters connect the Garden to quarry cracks and sacrificial bone beds.

- **Upper Jaw** sends a modest pulse into abandoned quarry galleries. It can relieve local water pressure or buy time on the tremor clock if the surface vent is open.
- **Widow’s Run** spreads a larger surge through the Silver Root and releases caught memory into Harrowgate’s lower streets. It protects stonework but risks a surface memory event.
- **Last Teeth** opens the entire Garden as a crush and heat sink. It is a prerequisite for the safest controlled-collapse sequence, but it destroys most food beds, ancestral remains, and dry routes.

The shutters require force at two separated handles or a substitute crew, beast, harness, or Warden mechanism. They can be prepared now and opened later through Ropeworks or Iron Choir controls if the linkage remains intact.

Preparation need not sacrifice the Garden. Brace routes, evacuate stores, move tablets, warn surface crews, and decide which beds are expendable. Controlled collapse then carries visible human and material costs.

## Crossing and operating the Garden

### Guided passage

Earn Edda’s cooperation, protect a keeper, repair the Jaw Catch, return a memory, or acknowledge the Garden’s food claim. A guided group can reach any active exit without name loss unless the state changes violently.

### Silent upper crossing

Climb the silver-root ribs above the mist. This avoids the name hazard and most keepers but risks collapse, wick-eaters at lit-dark boundaries, and a long fall into speaking caps. It is the fastest route between Quarry and Barracks.

### Water crossing

Raise the local channel and float across on saint pallets, coffins, or improvised boards. This suppresses spores and exposes submerged valves. It also moves contamination and may carry loose tablets toward Lantern or Feast.

### Burn a lane

Ward-oil or ordinary fire clears a direct route. A narrow controlled burn costs one bed and releases recoverable memories in silver smoke. An uncontrolled fire can destroy the filter, spread through dried root ways, and produce a cloud in which everyone remembers incompatible versions of the dead.

### Feed the Garden

Offer bone, memory, command brine, or a living name to grow a temporary route. Bone is slow, memory is unpredictable, command brine creates obedient but brittle geometry, and a living name is fast with personal cost.

### Force the root gates

A Gate Warden crew, Ropeworks tension line, Menagerie burrower, or determined party can break through. This preserves forward movement but damages routes and may leave the Garden unable to isolate water later.

## Encounters and moving pressure

Let the Garden’s threats arise from its current state.

- **Speaking carpet:** caps knit across a path and repeat increasingly intimate names. Cutting it opens a route; cooling, feeding, or redirecting it may preserve the bed.
- **Name-eating bloom:** a cloud of pale spores pursues the strongest unspoken relationship in the group. It can be trapped in a memory cup or led into ward-light.
- **Wick-eater migration:** creatures driven from Lantern nest along the edge of the lit Ossuary bough and carry stolen reasons into the caps.
- **Blank sentinel patrol:** Ora and Maston’s shells enter from the Barracks, asking Name, Burden, Authority while the Garden speaks answers around them.
- **Black-water reversal:** one bed floods from below, exposing a safe high route and threatening food stores or tablets.
- **Keeper resistance:** Edda’s crew closes sluices and uses hooked harvest poles, sleep spores, and route knowledge to stop destructive extraction.
- **Lamp hunter:** if not dealt with in the Quarry, it stalks the upper roots, extinguishing guide lamps and forcing a choice between darkness and pursuit.

Violence changes the Garden's state; the room never resets for a later visit.

## Garden states

Track the Garden as one of several broad states, with local exceptions if useful.

### Tended

Food, filtration, and guidance function. The name hazard is controlled. Keepers can move memory and operate vents. This requires cooperation or at least restraint.

### Overgrown

Warm water or unattended memory causes rapid growth. Routes change, food becomes hazardous, and the Garden can produce powerful but unreliable bridges and visions. Cutting back takes time or a displaced cost.

### Flooded

Speaking caps submerge and most spores settle. Water routes improve, dry routes close, and tablets or silver root may tear loose. The Feast hall and Lantern Cistern receive whatever the party does not isolate.

### Harvested

The party or Low Choir removes significant food, root, loam, or memory. This supplies resources now. Record the household-days, filtration loss, and political claim rather than treating it as free treasure.

### Burned

Name hazards and many creatures die. Stored memories release in smoke, filtration fails, and Low Choir food security collapses. Some routes become clear while others cave as roots shrink.

### Vented

One or more pressure sluices are prepared or opened. The Garden becomes part of the surface survival plan. Each opened shutter has a separate cost; only Last Teeth destroys the whole system.

## Routes and carry-forward effects

- **Quarry Cleft:** upper roots and pressure shutters; best controlled-collapse preparation.
- **Lantern Cistern:** filter inlet, oil contamination, and wick-eater migration.
- **Salt Barracks:** Bitter Bed, missing tablets, and a bypass around the inner gate.
- **Feast of Empty Chairs:** food stair, gray-water outlet, and Low Choir households.
- **Menagerie of Quiet Beasts:** refuse tunnel and access to creatures that can dig, filter, or carry dangerous memory.

Record:

- state of each filter bed;
- food and clean-water security;
- Quoin, Ora, and Maston tablet locations and wishes;
- names or relationships taken, returned, or stored;
- silver-root and name-brine extraction;
- keeper disposition and Kell’s condition;
- each pressure shutter as closed, prepared, opened, broken, or inaccessible;
- route states; and
- where displaced water, memory, spores, and wick-eaters went.

The Garden pays in household-days. Before the party leaves, name what each taken resource was already doing and who must now do without it.
