# The King's Narrow Grave

**Role:** optional  
**Tags:** optional, treasure, royal, claim

A narrowing royal tomb forces blood, maintenance, and surrender claims to divide its gold.

## Opening View

“By which succession?” asks the coffin. Orren Saye has answered three times. Three sealed genealogies lie across the bier, each lawful, each granting the gold to someone else. The treasury walls press inward one knuckle at a time. Behind Orren, the wounded porter puts both hands against the gold as though shame might hold it back. The coffin asks again.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

The Grave is Aldren Harrow's treasury, command crypt, and second coffin. Gold covers every surface; each claimant must call the metal inheritance, evidence, or weight keeping Harrowgate alive.

Aldren preserved three genealogies because each answers a different legal question—and because his successors could invoke whichever one favored them. The optional Grave offers the cleanest royal sequence, compatible counterweight metal, evidence against simple versions of Lady Vey's claim, and substantial wealth. Entering buys easier repairs, extractions, bargains, and betrayals at the cost of time and a decision about what counts as treasure.

## Entrances

- The **chained genealogy** from the Scriptorium opens a shoulder-width door behind the coffin wall.
- The **Reliquary royal niche** enters through a pressure-sealed kneeling passage beneath the Closed-Eyes Saint.
- The **Feast servants' way** reaches a floor hatch used by the people who maintained the tomb without being named in court records.
- The **Chapel royal return** opens during Execution Turn or through a valid genealogy response.
- The **Choir mortuary walk** reaches the command-voice chamber behind the king's head.
- A **grave-breath crack** leads toward Black Rain. It is clogged with mold, corpse gas, and gold leaf.

Each route enters a different part of the defense. A party can leave through another exit even if it does not open the coffin.

## Factor Orren Saye

By default, Orren has reached the Grave with two surviving loyalists, **Kael Dorn** and **Mira Sedge**, plus one wounded porter who no longer supports him. Orren has not opened the coffin. He has used Lady Vey's surface seal, the stolen royal sequence plate, and Elra's Chapel turn, but the Grave keeps asking which genealogy he invokes. He hoped the question would have one correct answer.

Orren is controlled, sleepless, and furious that the chain cut has become one man's madness in every public account. He indicts rather than shouts: which concealed injustice would still be hidden if he had obeyed? His reasoning is coherent and dangerous:

- Harrowgate's authorities concealed the Crown's existence and the Low Choir's burden.
- The Salt Wardens can delay any settlement by disputing authority.
- Lady Vey has money, public standing, and a real blood claim.
- Whoever physically holds the Crown can force negotiations that records alone cannot.
- The bell was already unjust; cutting the chain merely exposed a crisis others preferred to inherit quietly.

He accepted consequences he did not understand and abandoned people when the operation failed. He does not deny either fact. “I cut the chain,” he says. “They built the room in which cutting it was the first honest act anyone could imagine.” He still believes returning empty-handed would restore the old silence.

Orren wants the royal sequence, enough crown-metal to complete an extraction frame, and a route to the Deep Bell. He may bargain for shared custody, offer contract evidence, help operate the Grave, threaten the porter, race the party, or fight over the coffin. If shown the three claims and a credible replacement plan, he may shift from seizure to enforceable leverage. If humiliated or cornered, he uses the retained king's voice to command the crypt and escape toward the Choir.

Lady Vey's recovery trust is the expedition's lawful sponsor, and Lady Vey did not authorize the chain cut. Her written contract conditions recovery on stabilization and witnessed transfer. The contract case and complete sequence show Coss and Rhun adding a possession bonus, debt rider, and abridged sequence; Orren knew the accelerated terms and proceeded without the missing operations. Vey may repudiate their method, but her secrecy, money, and chosen agents created the opportunity. The documents divide culpability rather than absolving or condemning her whole claim.

## The succession vestibule

The grave's first ten feet contain three standing plates and one empty witness niche. Each plate accepts a different form of lineage.

- The **Golden Branch** recognizes descent from Aldren Harrow and opens custody mechanisms.
- The **Burden Chain** recognizes offices and communities that have actually maintained the bell and opens treasury and counterweight mechanisms.
- The **Ash Witness** recognizes Avarra's retained surrender conditions and opens Crown-release testimony.

Any one plate opens a route deeper. Two suppress most defenses. Three allow the king's retained voice to be cross-examined instead of merely obeyed. The empty niche requires a living or preserved witness willing to state what authority they claim and what they do not.

A false claim can activate a plate if supported by seal, memory, or command. The contradiction appears later as a defense assigned to the unsupported part of the claim.

## The three genealogies

### The Golden Branch: custody by blood

Gold wire runs from Aldren's coffin through generations of acknowledged children, collateral heirs, marriages, and adoptions. Lady Merrow Vey descends from Aldren's younger daughter through a line recognized in every surviving surface register. Her descent is genuine.

The Golden Branch grants a strong claim to **custody of Aldren's royal goods**, representation of his extinct house, and participation in any transfer of the Crown. It does not prove that Aldren owned Avarra's retained authority absolutely, that custody overrides the bell's maintenance obligations, or that one descendant may endanger Harrowgate to recover property.

The genealogy also records two disinherited branches. One entered the Low Choir through a marriage the court refused to acknowledge. Another became quarry freeholders and contributed land to the bell's vents. Lady Vey's line is the cleanest, not the only blood line.

### The Burden Chain: succession by maintained obligation

Lead links hidden behind the gold name every office, household, guild, Warden rank, and buried work crew that supplied labor, money, bodies, memory, or repair to keep the bell operating. The chain treats succession as an obligation attached to performance rather than blood.

Under this genealogy, Harrowgate's civic officers, the Salt Wardens, Low Choir households, quarry workers, and maintainers all inherit portions of authority. House Vey appears repeatedly when it paid, witnessed, or repaired; it disappears during generations when it claimed title without contributing.

The Burden Chain grants standing over **maintenance funds, operating decisions, and replacement duties**. It does not grant personal ownership of the Crown or allow inherited obligation to compel unwilling descendants forever. Its later links were manipulated to hide Low Choir contributions under generic “subterranean service.”

### The Ash Witness: Avarra's retained terms

The coffin lid's underside is blackened rather than gilded. Heat reveals a genealogy of witnesses to Avarra's surrender: prisoners, envoys, foster children, oath-bearers, and descendants entrusted with particular remembered terms. It grants no throne. It names the people authorized to testify about conditions the victorious court omitted from public law.

The retained terms include:

- the Crown remains Avarra's instrument until the mountain danger can be borne without her compelled will;
- no descendant of either crown may treat the surrender as personal chattel;
- the people maintaining the burden must receive safe passage, provision, and a hearing when conditions change;
- a lawful release requires witnesses representing victor, surrendered authority, maintainers, and those exposed to the danger; and
- if those witnesses cannot agree, the Crown may be secured but not privately alienated.

Several Ash Witness lines survive among Low Choir inherited names, one Salt Warden tablet family, and House Vey's own maternal ancestry. Nara's Narrow Line has standing, but not exclusive standing. Lady Vey can participate in the Ash claim if she accepts that it limits her custody claim.

### What the genealogies do not do

The three records divide authority; they do not converge on a secret rightful heir:

- blood supports custody and representation;
- maintained obligation supports operation and funds;
- witnessed surrender terms support release and limits.

A coherent Crown settlement may involve all three, a negotiated substitute, or a deliberate rejection with known consequences. Any faction presenting one genealogy as the whole law is omitting something real.

## The retained king's voice

Aldren's body is dead. The crypt preserves a command voice made from his final judgments, court formulas, and selected memories. It speaks through the gold walls in Aldren's measured cadence. It is not a complete ghost and cannot learn freely. It can compare claims, invoke defenses, open treasury compartments, and issue commands recognized by royal mechanisms.

The voice's central directive is: **keep the victory indivisible**. It interprets separation of Crown, bell, treasury, or royal claim as defeat unless shown that all three genealogies authorize the change. This is the old regime's bias, not an objective law of the dungeon.

The party can manage the voice by:

- answering through one genealogy and accepting limited access;
- presenting two claims to force it into consultation mode;
- filling the witness niche and cross-examining contradictions;
- using a white moth to remove one retained command without erasing the records;
- transferring the voice into an Iron Tongue for later testimony;
- drowning the mortuary chamber through the Reliquary niche;
- overriding it with Warden command or Chapel authority;
- severing gold speaking strips; or
- destroying the coffin and fighting the succession defenses at full strength.

The voice can reveal what Aldren knew, but it cannot prove that his account is true. It openly admits that he kept the genealogies separate to prevent any single successor from controlling both Crown and bell.

## Succession defenses

The crypt was designed to punish seizure, not ordinary visitation. Defenses escalate according to unsupported claims.

### The narrowing walls

Gold-sheet walls bow inward around anyone carrying royal metal or claiming sole inheritance. They stop when the claimant names another standing interest or deposits equivalent weight. Bracing, removing the metal, distributing it among several bearers, or advancing through the servants' way also works.

### The gilt mourners

Six flat royal figures peel from the walls. Each carries a ceremonial tool: key, scale, chain, cup, shovel, or sword. They attempt to assign intruders to genealogy plates and confiscate objects inconsistent with the declared claim. In combat they fold around limbs, pin people against gold, and pass seized items through the walls.

The mourners can be redirected by giving them a real custodial, maintenance, or witness task. Destroyed figures become compatible gold plates but remove one defense that could guard the treasury later.

### The indivisible command

If someone tries to take Crown sequence, treasury, and claim records under a single unsupported authority, the king's voice seals exits and orders every royal mechanism below to recognize that person as temporary sovereign. This sounds like success. It also assigns the sovereign every unresolved maintenance debt and makes later machinery seek their body when a burden fails.

A claimant can renounce part of the authority, divide custody, add witnesses, or carry the dangerous recognition deliberately.

### The coffin knight

Aldren's stone effigy rises only if the coffin is forced or the retained voice is destroyed while defenses remain active. It cannot leave the narrow crypt. It uses the corridor itself as armor, driving intruders toward plates, closing exits, and making every blow risk the gold records.

The fight's objective may be to hold a plate, open the coffin, extract a person, preserve a genealogy, or reach the voice chamber. Reducing the effigy to rubble is possible but not the only resolution.

## The royal treasury

The treasury was endowed to maintain the bell in perpetuity. Later kings treated it as a dynastic reserve; later councils borrowed against it without entering the grave. Its contents therefore have several simultaneous claims.

### Six crown-bars

Six rectangular bars of royal gold and meteoric iron fit the Civic distributor or can be forged into a Crown coupling. Each bar supplies one substantial unit of compatible mass. Together they can replace much of the missing Royal load, complete a Common Measure frame, or finance an emergency evacuation and rebuilding effort.

Removing bars triggers the narrowing walls unless equivalent mass or recognized obligation is entered. The Wells do not care whether the replacement is financially valuable; the treasury defenses do.

### The chain sequence coffer

A lead coffer inside the coffin contains the complete seven-step Royal Brake sequence, measurements for the clapper cage, and three compatible splice links. These make repair or controlled Crown extraction much safer. A Scriptorium reconstruction or experimental Wells sequence can substitute.

### The maintenance trust

Sealed coin, civic bonds, quarry rights, and repair contracts could fund:

- rapid surface evacuation;
- long-term Common Measure maintenance;
- compensation and resettlement for Low Choir households;
- rebuilding quarry vents and wells;
- a professional Warden and civic operator corps; or
- Lady Vey's restoration of the royal estate.

The value is large enough to make political choices concrete but not infinite. Spending it below as counterweight or losing it in collapse removes those future possibilities.

### Personal and ceremonial goods

Aldren's sword, seal crown, victory cups, mourning cloth, and jeweled armor are valuable and symbolically charged but not essential to the bell. Marr's public commission treats them as disputed royal goods, not ordinary salvage. Lady Vey claims them. Low Choir households may claim the cups as stolen witness vessels. Orren may trade them to retainers.

### Records of debt

A hidden drawer contains Councilor Rhun's rider, generations of loans against the maintenance trust, and evidence that surface elites reduced their Civic weights while preserving profit from the treasury. Publishing the records may fracture Harrowgate during evacuation. Suppressing them preserves coordination at the cost of future accountability.

## Treasure extraction

Getting treasure out is a dungeon operation.

- The Scriptorium route favors records and small bars.
- The Reliquary niche can float heavy loads but exposes them to water and pallbearer claims.
- The Feast passage lets Low Choir porters move goods if a division is negotiated.
- The Chapel royal return is rapid in Execution Turn and marks the carrier as a claimant.
- The Choir walk allows command transfer and pursuit toward the Deep Bell.
- The Black Rain crack can move unrecorded salvage at severe contamination risk.

Every crown-bar or chest occupies carrying capacity, rigging, routes, and time. A Warden or civic seal can authorize transport. Quiet removal is possible but changes later reactions.

Leave treasure secured, move it to the Chapterhouse, install it in the Wells, divide it, or abandon it. Call it recovered only after it has been physically moved and politically assigned.

## Orren outcomes

### Bargained leverage

Orren shares the sequence and contract evidence in exchange for a witnessed Crown settlement, a share of ordinary salvage, and protection from immediate execution. He may become a difficult ally in the Wells or Deep Bell.

### Divided expedition

His wounded porter or loyalists defect after the genealogies expose the concealed debt rider. Orren retreats through the Choir with sequence or bars, creating a pursuit rather than a final fight.

### Royal claimant

Orren accepts temporary sovereign recognition to command the defenses. He gains dangerous access and becomes personally liable to the machinery. The party can exploit, rescue, negotiate with, or oppose him.

### Defeat and custody

Orren is captured, killed, or driven off. His contract, sequence plate, and testimony remain useful. His removal does not settle Lady Vey's claim or repair the damage.

### Escape with treasure

Orren takes bars, records, or ceremonial goods rather than the Crown. This may weaken a replacement or future settlement while leaving the immediate finale open.

## Pressure scenes

### Lady Vey arrives by proxy

A speaking seal or retainer reaches the crypt with new surface orders. Vey may demand custody, authorize a coalition, repudiate Orren, or prioritize evacuation after another tremor.

### The treasury must become weight

The cleanest available repair consumes wealth promised for Low Choir resettlement or surface rebuilding. The party can seek other mass, accept the trade, or use a temporary brace.

### A genealogy is damaged

Combat, water, moths, or a narrowing wall threatens one record. Saving it costs position, treasure, or escape time. A damaged genealogy remains evidence but becomes easier to contest.

### The king grants too much

The retained voice recognizes a character as sovereign, custodian, or witness beyond what they intended. The authority opens routes and assigns obligations. Renunciation is possible but must be witnessed and may close an exit.

### The fourth toll reaches the gold

The walls ring like thin bells. Each genealogy speaks through living descendants and bearers elsewhere in the dungeon. The party can protect records, use the resonance to contact claimants, or flee before the sheets peel loose.

## Carry-forward states

Record the Grave as **sealed, opened under one claim, opened under divided claims, stripped, defended, flooded, or collapsed**. Also record:

- which genealogies were read, copied, damaged, suppressed, or carried;
- who filled the witness niche and the limits of their claim;
- the retained king's voice: active, constrained, transferred, altered, or destroyed;
- Orren, his loyalists, and the wounded porter;
- crown-bars removed, installed, promised, or lost;
- chain sequence and splice links;
- maintenance funds and records;
- ceremonial treasure and custody;
- exits opened or sealed; and
- any temporary sovereign recognition or inherited debt.

## Results

The Narrow Grave can provide a clean Royal Brake sequence, compatible replacement metal, major funding, contract evidence, Orren's confrontation, a route to the Choir or Black Rain, and a principled division of royal claims.

It reveals no secretly correct sovereign. Its sharpest treasure is proof that custody, maintenance, and release were always separate—and that every generation profited by pretending otherwise.
