# The Inverted Chapel

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** vertical, puzzle, chapel, hazard

An inverted sanctuary turns judgment into position, weight, and the right to withdraw.

## Opening View

The chapel hangs roof-first beneath you. Pews, altar, witness rails, and iron candles wait fifty feet down on four strained chains. Merrit Dole hangs below the altar in penitent irons, conscious and very still. From a barricaded brake platform across the shaft, Elra Venn calls, “One more step.” Her hand settles on the release lever.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

The Chapel hangs in a shaft wider than the chapterhouse above it. From the arrival balconies, the sanctuary lies fifty feet below and upside down: pews, altar, witness rails, and iron candles fixed to the present ceiling. Four pivot chains pass through the nave into separate brake houses; a fifth descends toward the Wells. Every claimant must occupy the physical position their argument assigns to someone else.

The old court rotated the chapel when Queen Avarra was sentenced. It needed one structure to serve as courtroom, execution platform, maintenance bridge, and burden-transfer frame while denying the condemned sovereign equal ground with her judges. Later operators kept it because no other station can recognize a new legal burden and pass it into the Wells.

The mechanism reads declared roles, witnessed consent, bounded terms, remedies, and physical balance. Force the forms and it will accept an unjust arrangement with visible instability. Offer a benevolent promise without authority, duration, or remedy and it refuses the load.

## What is happening now

The sanctuary rests in **Inverted Rest**, its usual sealed state. The Mourning Brake near the Feast is intact. The Breath Brake near the Reliquary is wet but operable. The Judgment Brake toward the Scriptorium is held by an Ember Company rigger. The Service Brake near the Menagerie crawl is fouled with feathers, chain grease, and one broken guardian wing.

**Merrit Dole**, a Low Choir mourning guide, hangs beneath the altar in the chapel's penitent chains. He entered after the second toll to recover an acceptance response and declared, in frustration, that he would carry Harrowgate's whole debt if no one above would name it. The old execution compulsion treated the statement as consent. He has survived on water lowered from the Feast and cannot free himself without a valid release, a careful rotation, or destruction of the bearer station.

**Rigger Elra Mott** controls the Judgment Brake from a barricaded platform. She followed Orren Saye's search party because he promised triple hazard pay and a share of the crown metal. Orren used her to turn the chapel, took the royal route when it opened, and left her with two dead companions when a chain-wing guardian struck. Elra has wired her brake to run free if anyone rushes the platform. She wants a safe route out, written protection from both the Ember Company and Harrowgate, and proof that Orren cannot later claim she sabotaged the expedition.

Neither Merrit nor Elra is required to operate the chapel. Each can make it safer, faster, and more human.

## The five chapel states

Moving between adjacent states requires control of two pivot brakes, or one pivot brake plus the Wells counterchain, a heavy external brace, or a dangerous manual traverse. A complete half-turn normally takes several minutes and produces a clear sequence of groans, moving shadows, and shifting debris. During a tremor or fight, resolve it in stages so characters can react.

### Inverted Rest

The sanctuary hangs upside down beneath the balconies. The Mourning and Breath approaches are easiest. The Scriptorium judgment chain reaches a side rail. The Wells door is sealed behind the altar floor. The chain-wing guardians are slow because their perches are inverted.

Crossing is possible by descending onto the undersides of pews, traversing chain handholds, or rigging a line across the shaft. Loose objects fall into the lower dark. Merrit hangs twenty feet below the nearest stable surface.

### Mourning Turn

The chapel rotates one quarter-turn toward the Feast. Pews become ladders and the mourning aisle becomes a vertical wall. The Feast stair, Reliquary ledge, and Scriptorium chain all connect through the nave. Cups placed in the mourning font can be cooled, witnessed, or passed safely toward the Iron Choir.

This is the best state for rescuing Merrit without accepting his declaration. It is also the state in which old grief records become audible through the iron candles. Prolonged exposure can impose borrowed mourning on anyone carrying an open memory cup.

### Upright Hearing

The sanctuary stands as a conventional chapel, though the shaft remains open around it. The Wells door, civic acceptance collar, four declaration stations, and lower route to the Iron Choir become accessible. This is the only stable state for consecrating the Common Measure or another replacement burden.

Upright Hearing shifts physical load onto the Counterweight Wells. If the Wells are unbalanced, one floor quadrant rises while another sinks. Operators can still work, but every declaration must be made while managing a visible material failure.

### Execution Turn

The chapel rotates one quarter-turn away from the Feast. The accused rail becomes a floor, the execution chute opens toward the Choir of Iron Tongues, and the Menagerie ceiling crawl connects to the Service Brake. The old royal return toward the King's Narrow Grave can be exposed from this state if the chained genealogy is present.

Execution compulsions are strongest here. Unassigned people are sorted into accuser, bearer, witness, and remedy roles by moving chains. The state is useful for forcing a royal burden through without full consent, breaking a hostile guardian formation, or making a rapid one-way descent. It is the most dangerous state in which to improvise.

### Dropped Nave

If two brakes run free, a pivot chain is severed, or the Wells counterchain fails during rotation, the chapel drops until one or more safety chains catch it. It becomes a steep broken bridge across the shaft. Some routes open permanently and others are crushed or lost.

A dropped chapel can still be crossed and may even create the shortest path to the Deep Bell approaches. It cannot perform a clean acceptance unless raised or extensively rebuilt. Cutting it loose entirely sends enormous mass toward the lower works, changing the Wells and Iron Choir state and risking a premature toll.

## Operating the pivots

Each brake house contains a handwheel, ratchet, speaking tube, and role plate. The plates name the operators as **Mourner**, **Breather**, **Judge**, and **Servant**. These are maintenance roles, not moral ranks.

To rotate safely:

1. inspect the intended stopping dogs and clear debris;
2. establish communication among at least two brakes;
3. take or provide counterweight through the Wells chain, rigging, water, or distributed personnel;
4. release adjacent brakes together;
5. arrest at the next state; and
6. set both dogs before moving people or heavy objects.

A failed operation should change position, route, injury, or machine state rather than merely consume time. Useful failures include:

- one side moves first, turning the nave into a steep slope;
- a brake heats and must be cooled with Reliquary water or ward-oil sacrificed as lubricant;
- a safety dog catches early, leaving a route open but the acceptance station unusable;
- a guardian seizes a loose chain and redirects the motion;
- Merrit's penitent chain takes load and begins to injure him; or
- the Wells counterchain lifts unexpected mass elsewhere in the dungeon.

The Ropeworks can provide synchronized line, Pell Varo can repair one brake, Warden crews can hold a station, Garden root fiber can replace a frayed signal cord, and burden-bearer statues or quiet beasts may supply controlled mass. None is mandatory.

## Traversal without rotation

A party may cross or descend without operating the whole chapel.

- **Underside traverse:** move across pew backs and candle chains in Inverted Rest. Fast climbers need only one anchor line; wounded people and heavy loads require a second.
- **Judgment chain:** the Scriptorium route reaches a narrow rail and can be tensioned into a bridge. Doing so temporarily disturbs one archive chain family.
- **Mourning net:** the Low Choir can rig cup lines and burial cloth into a broad catching net, permitting a slower group crossing.
- **Breath lift:** controlled Reliquary water fills copper bladders beneath one balcony, raising a temporary platform. A reversal can strand it.
- **Condemned chute:** Execution Turn opens a fast one-way descent to the Iron Choir approaches. It is difficult to reverse and marks users as condemned in the lesser mechanisms.
- **Wells counterchain:** a skilled climber can descend beside the fifth chain regardless of chapel state, but movement in the Wells may carry them unexpectedly.
- **Severed bridge:** cutting selected pew and aisle chains creates a rough bridge at the cost of future acceptance stability.

The chapel is therefore a route set piece, not a mandatory rotation puzzle. Operating it well preserves options and creates leverage; bypassing it trades those benefits for speed or damage.

## The execution compulsions

Four old legal roles are built into rails, chains, and speaking stations.

- **Authority:** who has standing to assign or recognize the burden?
- **Bearer:** who actually consents to carry it?
- **Witness:** who can independently attest to the consent and terms?
- **Remedy:** who or what acts when the burden is exceeded, breached, or revoked?

The chapel reacts when people make declarations near the stations, especially in Execution Turn. A person saying “I will bear it” may be chained as Bearer. Someone accusing another may be drawn to Authority. A silent observer may be fixed as Witness. A weapon or demolition charge can be classified as Remedy.

The compulsions can be handled in several ways:

- assign the roles deliberately before entering;
- answer the stations with bounded statements;
- carry a Warden duty tablet or Low Choir witness weight that clarifies standing;
- darken the Mourning Bough and move without speaking;
- jam or cut individual role chains;
- rotate out of Execution Turn; or
- accept a temporary role and complete a formal release.

The machinery does not detect lies in the abstract. It detects contradiction between declared role, recognized authority, witnessed consent, and later action. A clever false declaration can work briefly, then return as a chain seizure or failed transfer when the contradiction becomes material.

## Merrit Dole, the penitent

Merrit is exhausted, furious, and embarrassed by the sentence that caught him. He refuses to let anyone describe his outburst as noble consent. He will cooperate with a rescue that preserves the bearer station, but he accepts destruction of the station rather than indefinite captivity.

Ways to free him include:

- rotate to Mourning Turn and speak a witnessed release distinguishing compassion from consent;
- present Nara's testimony that Merrit lacked authority to bind the whole Low Choir;
- transfer his temporary bearer role to a willing, bounded carrier and release it immediately at Upright Hearing;
- counterweight the altar, slacken the chains, and pick or cut the cuffs;
- sever the bearer rail, damaging future acceptance; or
- drop the nave onto a prepared net and extract him during the impact.

Merrit knows the mourning responses, Feast route, and names of two Iron Tongues that can store transferred memory. He can serve as a witness but will not become the Common Measure's permanent bearer. Rescuing him gives Nara evidence that the Chapel can recognize revocation. Abandoning him leaves an unresolved coercive declaration in the machinery.

## Elra Mott and the Judgment Brake

Elra has food for one day, a cracked rib, and a spool of excellent rigging wire. She believes Captain Rook may protect her but does not know whether Rook survived. She has Orren's spoken order recorded in a memory sliver: turn the Chapel, open the royal return, and leave any Low Choir witness behind.

Elra's brake barricade is defensible but not impregnable. She bargains more readily than she fights.

She will release or coordinate the brake for any two of the following:

- a protected route to the Feast, Reliquary, or surface;
- written recognition that she acted under Orren's order and later prevented a drop;
- treatment for her injury or confirmation from Rhea Colm;
- credible news of Rook, Pell, Tavia, or other Company survivors;
- a share of ordinary salvage not including the crown; or
- help recovering her companions' bodies from a guardian perch.

Threats may gain immediate compliance, but Elra will run the brake at the first sign the party intends to abandon her. Killing or disabling her leaves the wired brake live until someone understands her rig.

If befriended, Elra can operate any pivot, repair the Wells signal clutch, and testify against Orren. She admits she joined a predatory contract and asks no stranger to absolve her.

## Chain-wing guardians

Three brass guardians called **appeal wings** patrol the shaft. Each resembles a narrow human torso folded inside two latticed chain wings. One is damaged at the Service Brake. They were built to prevent unsignaled rotation and to carry last appeals between stations.

They respond to imbalance, contradictory declarations, severed chains, and unassigned heavy movement. In conflict they do not simply claw intruders. They hook equipment, exchange people's positions, pin declared roles to stations, and arrest a moving pivot at the worst possible angle.

Ways to manage them include:

- signal the intended turn through two speaking tubes;
- present a valid appeal, Warden command, or Low Choir witness response;
- assign one guardian to carry Merrit's release or another bounded declaration;
- lure them with moving counterweight rather than living prey;
- darken the Deep or Mourning Bough to reduce their perception;
- repair the damaged wing and gain a temporary operator; or
- destroy them, leaving future rotations without automatic arrest.

A repaired appeal wing can accompany the party to the Wells or Iron Choir and carry one declaration or light load. It obeys procedure, not friendship.

## Accepting a replacement burden

The Chapel can recognize the Common Measure, a repaired Crown arrangement, a temporary emergency burden, or another designed frame. Recognition does not physically balance the Deep Bell. It creates a legible legal burden that the Wells and control chains can receive.

### Material preparation

A candidate burden needs:

- a frame capable of taking the mechanical interface, such as the Common Measure's copper acceptance collar;
- enough physical mass or a clear connection to distributed mass in the Wells;
- named maintainers or operators;
- marks or witnesses connecting each component to a declared obligation; and
- a route from the Chapel to the Wells and Deep Bell.

The Reliquary provides the Common Measure handweights, collar, trial crown segments, and Pilgrim's Yoke. Other materials can substitute if engineered and documented.

### The four declarations

At Upright Hearing, speakers occupy four stations.

1. **Authority:** name the person or coalition empowered to establish this burden and the limits of that power.
2. **Consent:** name every community or bearer contributing labor, memory, property, office, or risk, and show how each assented.
3. **Duration:** state the term, inspection interval, transfer rule, and conditions under which the burden must be reconsidered.
4. **Remedy:** state what happens when consent is withdrawn, limits are exceeded, maintenance fails, or authority is abused.

The same person may speak more than one declaration, but no declaration can supply its own independent witness. Unanimity across Harrowgate is not required. Specific consent from actual bearers is.

### Testing the frame

The Chapel loads the candidate in stages. Duty weights lift, the Pilgrim's Yoke transfers a fraction of the strain, and the Wells counterchain answers. Participants can revise a term, remove a weight, add a remedy, or stop the procedure before full recognition.

A successful acceptance produces a stable response in the four stations and opens the Wells transfer door. The frame becomes **consecrated to a named burden**, not sacred in a general sense.

A partial acceptance may still be useful. It can support emergency stabilization for one toll, recognize a route or operator, or prove that a coerced component is the source of instability.

### Forced acceptance

A royal seal, Warden command tablet, or sufficient physical force can drive a burden through in Execution Turn without complete consent. This opens routes and may stabilize the bell temporarily. It also marks the frame with an execution clause: when strain exceeds its declared limit, the machinery seeks a living bearer or condemned substitute.

Forced acceptance is a viable dark choice, not a secret failure screen. Telegraph the clause through tightening chains, missing witness responses, and Merrit's sentence.

## Chapel pressure scenes

### Rotation during a tremor

The shaft moves while one brake is released. The party must choose whether to arrest early, complete the turn, protect Merrit, secure Elra's platform, or preserve a route carrying rescued people.

### The appeal that cannot be proved

An appeal wing presents a memory fragment from Queen Avarra or a forgotten laborer. The statement is relevant but lacks an independent witness. The party can delay to find corroboration, accept it as repaired testimony, or reject it and preserve procedural certainty.

### A bearer withdraws

During acceptance, one household, Warden crew, surface authority, or player character revokes consent. The Chapel pauses rather than immediately failing. The party can reduce the burden, replace the contribution, renegotiate the term, force the declaration, or abandon the attempt.

### Orren's return line

Elra can open the same royal route Orren used. Doing so improves pursuit toward the King's Grave but moves the Chapel toward Execution Turn and gives royal seals priority in the next operation.

### The shortest route is the cruelest

Dropping the chapel creates a rapid bridge to the lower works while destroying the acceptance station and threatening Merrit, Elra, or household witnesses. The party may still choose it under clock pressure.

## Carry-forward states

Record the Chapel as one of these states, plus brake and route details.

- **Inverted:** sealed but stable; acceptance unavailable.
- **Turned:** held at Mourning or Execution quarter-turn; several lateral routes improve while others worsen.
- **Upright:** Wells and acceptance stations available; physical load transferred downward.
- **Consecrated:** a named burden has been accepted, with declarations and witnesses recorded.
- **Dropped:** the nave is a damaged bridge; acceptance requires repair.
- **Severed:** the chapel has fallen or been dismantled, permanently changing lower load and routes.

Also record Merrit's condition, Elra's disposition, each brake, guardian status, declarations attempted, coercive clauses, accepted weights, and whether the Wells transfer door is open.

## Results

The Chapel can provide a safe deep route, a rescued witness, an Ember Company operator, an appeal-wing assistant, access to the Wells and Iron Choir, a royal pursuit route, and legal recognition for a replacement burden. It can also become a damaged shortcut or a weapon dropped into the lower machine.

The Chapel can be bypassed, damaged, or abandoned. Its unique gift is no moral permission; it makes consent, limits, and remedy legible to machinery built around coercion.
