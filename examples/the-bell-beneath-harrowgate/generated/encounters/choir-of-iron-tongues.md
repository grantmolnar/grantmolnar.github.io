# The Choir of Iron Tongues

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** memory, control, bells, deep

An iron choir stores severed voices and obeys only commands with source, address, and limit.

## Opening View

“Name the source.” Iron answers through seven galleries. “Name the address.” Chains draw tight in distant rooms. “Name the limit.” No limit comes. A copyist hangs in the upper tuning cage, lending the Choir his breath, while three royal tongues speak in one woman’s voice and dispute what she surrendered. The unfinished command waits for its next word.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

The Iron Choir descends in seven galleries around the Deep Bell’s upper throat. Hundreds of tongues hang above a conductor’s dais: shields, knives, keys, tools, and memorial plates remade as instruments. They swing without bells. Their voices travel through chain, water, and bone, carrying names and commands farther than any speaker can see the consequence.

Every lesser mechanism in the buried house can be addressed here. The lamp boughs, Reliquary saints, Garden shutters, Warden musters, Chapel pivots, Wells brakes, Black Rain gates, and the Deep Bell's preparatory catches all have phrases in the Choir. The system was meant to let several custodians coordinate during a toll. Later operators discovered that a command carries farther when spoken through a remembered human voice. They fed the Choir copied recollections. When volunteers became scarce, they routed involuntary memory from the Low Choir through the Lantern Cistern and into the iron.

The Choir is both control room and stolen civic archive. Silence ends immediate commands and traps the memories. A general recital restores nothing; it calls active mechanisms into contradictory motion.

## Cast

- **The Iron Cantor** is an articulated frame of tuning forks, chain fingers, and a faceless bronze hood. It sorts voices, completes familiar commands, and treats missing limits as damage to be repaired. When it cannot identify a lawful conductor, it preserves continuity by making every mechanism finish the sentence it began.
- **Hara-of-Three-Refrains** is a Low Choir memory keeper who entered through the condemned Chapel chute. One refrain is her childhood, one her dead aunt’s, and one a maintenance sequence she accepted for a season. She marks each change of voice by touching a different finger. The Choir has begun answering all three as one person.
- **Lieutenant Olt Venn** leads three Deep Rank Wardens sent before the Barracks command crisis was settled. He has an intact shell, a damaged tablet, and an order to preserve royal operation until relieved by recognized authority. He will obey a valid muster, but he interprets uncertainty as sabotage.
- **Jori Pell**, an Ember Company copyist, hangs inside the upper tuning cage with iron wire through his coat and pack. He tried to record the command lexicon. The Cantor now uses his breath to test phrases.
- **Queen Avarra** is present only in retained faculties distributed among three royal tongues. The chamber can speak with her measures, her refusal, or her witness. It cannot initially assemble a complete ghost.

## Immediate pressures

The next toll is entering its preparatory phase. The Iron Cantor is issuing a **general continuity recital**: lamp boughs brighten, Wardens receive muster calls, water gates seek their old positions, and the Funerary Well begins taking memory load. Each subsystem answers according to its current damage. The result is not restoration but synchronized strain.

Hara is trying to isolate the Low Choir register before another generation of memories is drawn upward. Olt Venn is trying to reach the conductor's dais and complete the recital. Jori Pell will lose his voice if the upper cage makes three more test strokes. Three royal tongues have begun speaking over one another in Avarra's voice.

Ignoring the recital leaves the upper control walk open, but the party reaches the Bell amid a command cascade, with fewer controls, more defenders, and another packet of Low Choir memory loaded for sacrifice.

## The chamber

### The name galleries

The upper three galleries carry personal and household memory. Each tongue is marked with a salt name, a Low Choir cup sign, a Warden tablet notch, or a blank acquisition number. Touching one produces a sensory fragment rather than an explanation: a kitchen at dawn, a quarry blast, a child's fever, a face no living bearer can presently recall.

The galleries are crossed by narrow chain bridges. A voice released without an identified recipient passes into adjacent tongues, combining memories that were never lived together. White name moths, if released from the Menagerie, can consume such unattached fragments before they spread, but they cannot determine rightful ownership.

### The command galleries

The middle three galleries carry operating phrases. Their tongues are grouped by mechanism rather than location:

1. **Light and passage** — lamp boughs, route latches, warning shutters, and Warden doors.
2. **Water and breath** — Reliquary saints, separator gates, Garden filters, and Black Rain spillways.
3. **Burden and timing** — Ropeworks drums, Chapel brakes, Wells catches, and Deep Bell preparatory locks.

A gallery can be isolated by setting two chain dampers and one memory brake. Isolation prevents remote operation of its mechanisms until the dampers are released, but it also stops contradictory commands and lets operators work locally.

### The royal register

The lowest gallery contains nine tongues cast from conquered royal instruments. Six hold old legal forms. Three retain Queen Avarra's will:

- **Measure** can compare loads, promises, and physical consequences. It recognizes disproportion and can help fit or test a burden. Choosing the bearer remains a living judgment.
- **Refusal** preserves Avarra's capacity to reject commands made through her Crown. It can break a coerced acceptance or deny a claimant, but it cannot operate the machine by itself.
- **Witness** remembers the terms of surrender, the first contact with the Mouth Below, and the distinction between what Avarra yielded and what later custodians invented. Its memories are incomplete wherever records were deliberately removed.

The three faculties answer separately until the party brings them into one cadence through evidence, living recognition, or the Crown itself. Even then, the result is a retained will under constraint—not an omniscient queen returned from death.

### The conductor's dais

The dais has three sockets and a circular speaking rail. A command is reliably accepted only when it supplies:

- a **source** the system can recognize: a Warden muster, Chapel acceptance, royal faculty, recorded custodian, or present operator taking responsibility;
- an **address** identifying the mechanism, person, or register acted upon; and
- a **limit** stating duration, stopping condition, or permitted cost.

Commands lacking a source are repeated as questions. Commands lacking an address spread to every similar mechanism. Commands lacking a limit continue until interrupted by damage, a stronger command, or the next toll. The old custodians often omitted limits when directing involuntary memory transfers.

## Entering and moving through the Choir

The chamber can be reached from five directions:

- the Chapel's lower stair opens onto the name galleries;
- the King's Narrow Grave mortuary walk enters beside the royal register;
- a service climb from the Counterweight Wells reaches the burden gallery;
- the Black Rain speaking shaft rises behind the water tongues; and
- the Deep Bell upper control walk begins beyond the conductor's dais.

No route requires the party to master the whole Choir. They can traverse hanging tongues, crawl along damping chains, use a Quiet Beast to suppress one kind of excess, accept Olt Venn's guarded escort, or break through the rear lattice. Every bypass leaves more of the chamber uncontrolled during the finale.

## Common operations

### Identify a voice

Identify a fragment through a living witness, marked cup, Warden tablet, Scriptorium record, or distinctive repeated fact. Some have no surviving claimant. Preserve, release, archive, or spend them as anonymous operating force.

### Restore a memory

Restoration requires three things: an identified memory, a receiving person or vessel, and recognition by someone who knows the memory belongs there. A memory cup can hold the fragment during transfer. The Lantern network can carry it. The Returning Breath saint can stabilize a damaged recipient. The Iron Cantor can perform the joining, but it cannot supply consent or recognition.

Restoration is imperfect when the recipient has built a life around the absence. Give the player or GM a choice of how the memory returns: vivid and disruptive, partial but integrated, or stored nearby until the person chooses to receive it. A restored recollection does not erase years of adaptation.

### Issue a lesser command

Once the party understands source, address, and limit, it can remotely prepare or operate one linked mechanism. Major operations still require local capacity. The Choir can set a Garden shutter to ready; it cannot evacuate the Garden. It can command a Reliquary saint to kneel; it cannot create air in a flooded chamber. It can call Wardens; it cannot make disputed authority legitimate.

Useful commands include:

- isolate or relight a lamp bough;
- hold a Reliquary saint in a chosen position;
- prepare a root shutter or Black Rain spillway;
- set a Wells catch for a known brake window;
- open a route latch;
- call or stand down Wardens under a recognized muster; and
- delay one phase of the next toll.

### Separate the royal faculties

The party may remove or damp one of Avarra's three tongues. This protects that faculty from destruction and prevents others from using it, but weakens any later reconstruction. A claimant may especially want Measure without Refusal, or Witness without the limits preserved by the other two.

### Reconstruct Avarra's retained will

Bringing Measure, Refusal, and Witness into one cadence requires at least two independent anchors among the Ash Witness genealogy, Avarra's Crown, an original Scriptorium surrender plate, a living witness carrying inherited terms, or a Chapel declaration that recognizes her as a wronged bearer rather than a reigning sovereign.

The reconstructed will remembers making contact with the Mouth Below during a siege. She tried to force the phenomenon to consume an enemy army's engines and instead taught it to answer royal cadence. After defeat, she surrendered the Crown's command over the Mouth for the limited purpose of stabilizing the fault. She did not consent to indefinite imprisonment, hereditary ownership of her retained voice, or the diversion of memory cost onto the Low Choir.

Avarra wants the sentence ended. She also knows that an abrupt release can kill Harrowgate. She will aid a replacement, distributed settlement, controlled collapse, or bounded emergency continuance if no one is coerced into taking her place. She refuses to certify that justice consists of handing her Crown to a descendant and repeating her authority.

## Conflict in the Choir

The Iron Cantor treats unresolved voices as dangerous material. If opposed, it uses the room rather than fighting as a single creature:

- chain fingers seize a speaker and carry them toward the register they most resemble;
- command tongues strike mechanisms elsewhere, changing light, water, or route state;
- name tongues speak intimate memories aloud, creating confusion and leverage;
- the upper cage steals breath to complete an unfinished command; and
- a royal tongue can force anyone carrying recognized royal metal to repeat a phrase.

Olt Venn and the Deep Rank Wardens fight to control stations, close dampers, and finish a valid command. They can be defeated, persuaded, mustered, relieved, or shown that their order lacks a lawful limit. Hara fights only to prevent another involuntary draw. Jori will help whoever first frees him, then demand that the copied memories not be treated as treasure.

Fight for the dais, Jori, dangerous registers, and the fate of the general recital. Every destroyed tongue may erase an irreplaceable memory, command, or witness.

## Failure states

Use named failures so the consequences remain legible.

- **General Muster:** every active Warden receives a royal continuity order and moves toward the deep chambers.
- **Name Rain:** unattached memories fall through the dungeon, causing temporary identity confusion and feeding the Mouth.
- **False Silence:** command transmission stops, but stored pressure and memory continue accumulating without warning.
- **Royal Recital:** one Avarra faculty dominates the other two. Measure produces cold optimization, Refusal breaks needed operations, or Witness traps the chamber in accusation without action.
- **Cantor fracture:** the control frame breaks into autonomous forks that continue familiar commands in nearby regions.

None blocks the finale. Each changes who or what must be handled there.

## Treasure and salvage

The Choir contains six removable command tongues, coils of silver memory wire, Warden muster forks, and private civic records. Removing a command tongue grants portable authority over one narrow mechanism if the user can still supply source and limit. Removing memory wire makes later restoration and distributed stabilization harder. The royal tongues are not ordinary salvage: carrying one means carrying a faculty of Avarra's retained will and exposing it to anyone who can make it speak.

## Carry-forward states

Record:

- which command galleries remain active, isolated, or broken;
- who controls the conductor's dais;
- whether Jori Pell and Hara are alive, free, and allied;
- Olt Venn's order and disposition;
- memories restored, preserved, released, or lost;
- whether the general recital completed;
- which lesser mechanisms are prepared remotely;
- the condition and location of Measure, Refusal, and Witness; and
- whether Avarra's retained will has been reconstructed and what it has agreed to do.

A controlled Choir grants coordination and truth. Silence grants privacy and local autonomy. Break it, and every final operation depends on runners, timing, and whatever memories survived elsewhere.
