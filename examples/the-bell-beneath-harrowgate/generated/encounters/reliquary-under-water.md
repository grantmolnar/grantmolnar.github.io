# The Reliquary Under Water

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** water, relics, rescue, hazard

Six copper saints trade breath and water around a trapped surgeon and a rejected civic burden.

## Opening View

On every third breath, the copper saint speaks with a woman’s voice. “Do not drain it.” Black water stands at the saints’ ribs. A many-armed pallbearer is drawing a drowned burden toward the figure with closed eyes, while the Open Palm keeps oil, seals, and pressure cloth lifted above the flood. The saint breathes twice more. On the third, Rhea Colm says, “Replace what you take.”

## Encounter Material

## Situation

The Reliquary is a shrine built as a breathing machine. Six copper saints stand in two rows on a gridded floor, their mouths, hands, and hollow bodies joined into one air-and-water manifold. At balance, black water reaches their ribs. At every other level, some rescue, route, or truth rises while another disappears.

The Reliquary keeps practical sacred things: tools, seals, trial burdens, and emergency fittings the old house promised never to use casually. Nearly every one is claimed.

The reversed Water Drum has made the level rise and fall in pulses. During the last rise, an Ember Company salvage team opened the central saint and triggered the reliquary's pallbearer construct. One survivor remains sealed inside the statue. Several dead workers have returned as load ghosts, endlessly moving weights between niches whose labels they can no longer read.

## The six copper saints

Each statue is a station in the shared manifold. A party can understand them from inscriptions, experimentation, Senn's guidance, Ropeworks records, or the rhythm of air moving through their mouths.

### The Saint of Returning Breath

Its open mouth draws air from the Lantern Cistern bell line. **Rhea Colm**, the Ember Company's field surgeon, is sealed inside its chest with a crushed lower leg. She has enough air for roughly one tremor interval if the Mourning Bough remains dark, longer if the bell line is primed with ward-oil or manually pumped.

### The Saint of the Open Palm

Its hands contain waxed pressure cloth, silvered oil flasks, ceramic valve wedges, and two emergency seals. Removing the right-hand cache lowers a local floodgate unless its weight is replaced.

### The Saint of Many Hands

Six hinged arms surround a rack of seventy-two bronze handweights. Each weight bears a different civic office, trade, household, or public duty. Together they form the surviving core of the **Common Measure**, a rejected design for replacing the Crown of Measure with many voluntary obligations held in one accepted frame.

### The Saint Beneath the Yoke

Its shoulders support a portable transfer frame called the **Pilgrim's Yoke**. The yoke can carry one declared burden between mechanisms for a short time. A willing bearer can use it to hold a gate, quiet load ghosts, or substitute for a missing counterweight. Each use leaves a visible gray line across the bearer's shoulders and transfers a memory or obligation with the load.

### The Saint of Closed Eyes

Its head faces the sealed royal niche. Flooding the statue to eye level opens the niche; draining below its knees closes it. The niche leads toward the King's Narrow Grave and holds one of the three genealogy seals. The mechanism reads water pressure, not blood.

### The Saint Who Kneels

Its body controls the lower culvert to the Black Rain Cistern. Forcing it upright drains the room rapidly downward. Leaving it kneeling holds the lower pressure. A half-raised position permits controlled drainage but requires continuous bracing or a counterweight from another saint.

## Water levels

Track one of four local states. The room may differ from the global water state if the Lantern separator or local saint manifold has been secured.

### High water

Water reaches the saints' shoulders and most floor salvage is submerged. The water lift, upper bell line, and Black Rain culvert are usable. The royal niche is open. Swimming is difficult but load ghosts are weak and the pallbearer moves slowly along the walls. Air pockets in the saints are generous.

### Middle water

Water reaches the saints' waists. This is the intended service level and the best state for rescuing Rhea or moving large objects. All six stations can be operated. The dry ledge toward the Inverted Chapel is reachable by a short climb. The pallbearer can move freely.

### Low water

The gridded floor, trial burdens, and bodies are exposed. Salvage is easiest and the Chapel ledge becomes a broad route. The Black Rain culvert becomes a dangerous open drop. Load ghosts regain full strength because their drowned work is visible. Rhea's air pocket collapses unless her statue is opened or externally pumped.

### Ruptured

The lower gate or floor has failed. Water, loose relics, bodies, and perhaps Rhea are being dragged toward Black Rain. The royal niche closes. The room becomes a rescue-and-extraction sequence, and the deeper cistern worsens permanently. A ruptured Reliquary can still be crossed on saint shoulders and hanging bell lines, but it cannot be restored without a major repair.

## Rhea Colm's rescue

Rhea speaks through the Saint of Returning Breath in breath-length reports: Orren's loyalists took the royal-niche route; Captain Maelin Rook followed rather than abandon the wounded; one dead salvager carried papers under a second Vey seal that did not match the crew's contract.

Credible rescue methods include:

- open the chest plate while maintaining middle or high water;
- raise the water so Rhea floats clear when the lower hatch opens;
- prime the bell line with ward-oil and cut her free from inside the statue;
- command the pallbearer to brace and open the saint;
- use the Pilgrim's Yoke to carry the statue's pressure burden; or
- drain the room and continuously pump air from the Lantern Cistern.

Simply smashing the statue works, but collapses the shared air manifold, removes the safest trapped-air route, and may kill Rhea if no one supports her leg and the internal valve cage.

Once free, Rhea works in triage order: breathing, movement, testimony that will worsen if delayed. She can stabilize survivors and identify the drowned. She asks that the Common Measure be preserved and Orren returned alive if possible. About the Crown she asks only, “What takes the weight when you take the gold?”

## The pallbearer

The reliquary's construct resembles a broad copper crab assembled from bier handles, censers, and jointed devotional arms. Its formal designation is **Bearer of Things Not Yet Relinquished**. Ember survivors call it the pallbearer.

It enforces declared custody:

- a rescuer who names a trapped person may handle tools needed for rescue;
- an operator who names a repair may move fittings and trial burdens;
- a claimant who names an inheritance may inspect, but not remove, disputed relics;
- a thief or vague scavenger is treated as an unbalanced load and pinned beneath a copper limb.

The pallbearer can be fought, trapped by water level, blinded by darkness, commanded with Senn's key, persuaded through exact declarations, or given a new custodial order by a recognized Salt Warden. Once cooperative, it can tow heavy burdens, brace the Kneeling Saint, carry Rhea, or transport one major salvage object through the water lift.

Destroying it yields valuable devotional copper and six articulated load joints. Those joints are excellent counterweight fittings, but the Reliquary loses its strongest local operator.

## Drowned load ghosts

The load ghosts are the workers killed during earlier replacement trials. They do not defend holiness. They insist that each weight have a named destination and a bearer who accepts it.

They can be:

- dispersed by ward-light;
- directed by reading the labels they can no longer see;
- quieted by moving one burden to its intended niche;
- enlisted by declaring a new trial of the Common Measure; or
- angered by stripping copper, erasing names, or dumping weights into Black Rain.

If enlisted, they can complete one coordinated lift or hold one failing gate through the next tremor. If attacked and destroyed, their burdens fall physically onto the room: gates shift, statues settle, and loose relics slide toward the culvert.

## The Common Measure

Nothing in the apparatus failed mechanically. The rejection marks treat distributed consent as insufficient royal authority. The surviving field kit is:

- seventy-two inscribed handweights in the Saint of Many Hands;
- the Pilgrim's Yoke, which proves a burden can be transferred voluntarily;
- a copper acceptance collar folded beneath the central floor grate; and
- design plates sealed inside the Saint of Returning Breath beside Rhea.

The surviving parts still require five operations before they can replace the Crown:

1. preserve or reconstruct the acceptance collar;
2. obtain enough voluntary weights or sworn substitutes to represent Harrowgate and the people below;
3. have the Inverted Chapel accept the assembled burden;
4. balance it in the Counterweight Wells; and
5. establish who maintains the obligations after the crisis.

The handweights need not all be carried. A careful party can copy their measures and manufacture substitutes, recruit actual officeholders, or combine them with royal and Warden metal. Taking the original rack provides the strongest proof and fastest finale route but strips the Reliquary of its public record.

## Other salvage

### Emergency seals

Two ceramic-and-wax seals can close a ruptured pipe, saint, or minor black-water gate. Each is single-use unless recovered before pressure sets.

### Silvered oil flasks

Three flasks carry ward-oil through black water without contamination. Ordinary bottles work only briefly and may ignite or leak memory vapor.

### The drowned contract satchel

A dead Company clerk carries three documents in one waxed case: Lady Vey's conditional survey-and-recovery contract, a concealed bonus schedule issued by Chief Factor Halren Coss for physical possession before public notice, and Councilor Samet Rhun's debt rider pledging town obligations against the Crown. The base contract requires safe detachment and a witnessed transfer. The later papers omit those conditions and pay for speed. They prove that Vey funded the expedition and that Coss exceeded the written authority in her copy; they do not by themselves prove whether she knew of the rider. Removing them without preserving the case destroys the order of seals and some signatures.

### Trial crown segments

Several copper-and-iron arcs were built to test replacement geometry. They are valuable, can repair the acceptance collar, and serve as excellent crown-chain splice material. Taking all of them makes later reconstruction easier and deprives Harrowgate of evidence that alternatives were deliberately rejected.

## Operating the manifold

### Stabilize at middle water

Balance the Open Palm cache, set the Kneeling Saint halfway, and pulse the Returning Breath bell. This is the safest general state. It requires either two operators, the pallbearer, or a temporary counterweight.

### Flood deliberately

Close the lower culvert and accept water from the Ropeworks. High water weakens load ghosts, opens the royal niche, and creates generous trapped-air routes. It also carries loose oil, bodies, and relics upward toward the Lantern Cistern if the separator is not isolated.

### Drain deliberately

Raise the Kneeling Saint or reverse the Water Drum. Low water exposes salvage and the Chapel ledge but strengthens load ghosts, threatens Rhea's air, and increases Black Rain pressure. A controlled drain is useful. A full drain without a lower plan sends the danger downhill.

### Seal the room

Use emergency fittings and the saint manifold to isolate the Reliquary from both upper and lower water. This creates a possible safe camp and preserves the room as an operating base. It consumes one seal or requires a durable repair.

### Rupture the lower gate

The party may choose speed, deny pursuit, or deliberately send water toward a controlled-collapse plan. This permanently worsens Black Rain, closes the royal niche, and may sweep useful burdens out of reach. It also opens a fast but perilous descent.

## Routes by water state

- **Water lift to the Ropeworks:** usable at high or middle water; at low water the cage hangs above the floor and requires climbing or counterweighting.
- **Mourning conduit to the Lantern Cistern:** usable as an air line at any state; physically passable only at high water or after removing grates.
- **Dry ledge to the Inverted Chapel:** narrow at middle water, broad at low water, submerged at high water.
- **Royal niche toward the King's Narrow Grave:** open only at high water or when the Closed-Eyes Saint is manually pressurized.
- **Lower culvert to Black Rain:** swimmable at high water, controlled at middle water, a hot vertical drop at low water, and a torrent when ruptured.

Keep at least one route open in every water state. The level changes what is easy, what is exposed, and who is endangered.

## Hard choices

- Rescue Rhea immediately, or spend her remaining air stabilizing a more favorable water state.
- Take the Common Measure rack for the strongest replacement route, or leave its public record intact and copy it under pressure.
- Preserve the pallbearer as an operator, or break it for fittings needed elsewhere.
- Drain for salvage and accept greater danger below, or flood for rescue and risk contaminating the Lantern Cistern.
- Open the royal niche for a fast route after Orren, or hold the room at the level best suited to the wounded and the Common Measure.
- Return named relics to Low Choir households, place them under Warden custody, honor Lady Vey's legal claim, or treat them as Harrowgate's emergency repair stock.

## Clues and useful discoveries

Searches, declarations, and successful operations can reveal:

- the copper saints can preserve air and control local water without dumping the whole room into Black Rain;
- the Common Measure failed politically rather than mechanically;
- a replacement burden requires voluntary weights, an accepted frame, Chapel recognition, and Wells balancing;
- Orren's loyalists used the royal niche route, followed by Captain Rook;
- Chief Factor Halren Coss issued a concealed accelerated-possession schedule beyond the base contract;
- the lower culvert touches the mountain fault; and
- the Chapel pivot station can be reached across the dry ledge.

## Outcomes

Record:

- the water level and whether it is stable, locally isolated, or ruptured;
- Rhea Colm's fate, testimony, and willingness to aid the party;
- whether the pallbearer is cooperative, hostile, destroyed, or reassigned;
- the disposition of the Common Measure, Pilgrim's Yoke, acceptance collar, seals, flasks, and trial crown segments;
- which claims were acknowledged and which evidence was preserved;
- whether the royal niche, Chapel ledge, and Black Rain culvert are open;
- whether the load ghosts were enlisted, quieted, or destroyed; and
- what additional pressure was sent upward or downward.

A visit can succeed without stripping the room. Leave with one rescued person or strong operator, one replacement-burden asset, and one water decision that follows the party deeper.
