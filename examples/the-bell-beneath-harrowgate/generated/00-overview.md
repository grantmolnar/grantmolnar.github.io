## Synopsis

Three nights running, the Deep Bell has sounded beneath Harrowgate with no rope and no ringer. Dust rises before each toll. Wells climb their walls. People above wake holding memories lost by people below. The Ember Company descended under a secret, conditional recovery contract; Factor Orren Saye then trusted an abridged royal sequence and cut the chain carrying the Crown's live load. The buried house is now passing strain among water, ward-light, memory, command, and counterweight systems. The adventurers must rescue the scattered expedition, learn whom each system has been spending, and choose an ending: repair the old burden, replace it, divide it, collapse it, free the Crown, or bargain with the listener beneath the fault. Every answer needs a bearer and a route.

## Premise

The Deep Bell is drawing breath for a fourth toll, and each broken cycle closes faster. Two descents remain open. Survivors are scattered below. Every repair shifts danger into another system. Enter the buried house, learn what its safety has cost, and decide which burdens Harrowgate will carry in public.

## Explanation

The Deep Bell is a pressure engine built around Queen Avarra's coerced surrender. Her Crown of Measure became clapper, counterweight, and legal authority: one person made the junction between mountain strain and public command. Later custodians diverted part of every toll's mnemonic cost into the labor community now called the Low Choir.

Five days ago, Lady Merrow Vey's trust hired the Ember Company to verify the Crown and recover it only after the buried house was made safe. Her chief factor, Halren Coss, secretly accepted Councilor Samet Rhun's possession rider: take the Crown before Harrowgate could assert custody, and Rhun would supplement the fee from town debts pledged against it. Rhun also supplied an abridged extraction schedule. It called the Crown Drum's outer chain a release restraint and omitted the three operations that would have exposed its live load. Orren Saye knew he was racing public notice. Captain Maelin Rook saw all three drums under strain and refused the cut. Their struggle severed the chain, reversed the Water Drum, and left the Memory Drum half-braked.

The first toll passed for quarry settlement. After the second, Harrowgate closed wells and signaled both entrances. The third collapsed a tenement. At dawn, a dying porter named Orren's order and Rook's refusal. Only then did the council know the cause and possess a public mandate for another descent. Tarrow expects the shortening cycle to close again before midnight.

The broken house now shifts strain among water, light, memory, command, and weight. Lady Vey can repudiate the rider and chain cut, but not the secret expedition that enabled them. No ending restores safety and justice together. Rescue, route preparation, preserved evidence, and voluntary obligations determine what can be saved—and who may still refuse the settlement.
