## Summary

The Low Choir's inhabited refuge and negotiating hall, where empty places, memory cups, inherited names, and six compact weights make obligation visible.

## Reference Material

## Refuge and table

The Feast of Empty Chairs is a living Low Choir hall rather than a memorial ruin. Occupied tables feed households and workers; empty chairs hold places for people absent, forgotten, endangered, or still claimed by duty. The hall tests visitors through whom they return, whom they name, and which absence they are willing to acknowledge.

## Cups, names, and compact

Memory cups allow listening, witnessed stabilization, voluntary carrying, return, redirection, and toll shielding. Inherited names are chosen standings and duties, not ownership of the bearer. Negotiation uses six compact weights: witness, passage, provision, maintenance, limit, and remedy. Different households may accept different combinations.

## What the hall can promise

The Feast can provide refuge, food, guides, witnesses, memory practice, household commitments, and maintainers for a future settlement. No table or representative binds every household, and no dramatic promise substitutes for a named limit and remedy. Current chairs, cups, positions, stores, guests, promises, and hostility remain changing state.
