# The Chain Scriptorium

**Reference ID:** `8e4c88cb-067e-4701-9364-7c61e104a710`  
**Kind:** Place  
**Aliases:** Chain Scriptorium, the Scriptorium  
**Tags:** archive, burden records, genealogy, evidence custody

## Summary

A burden-indexed brass archive whose moving chains preserve maintenance law, dynastic claims, condemned promises, and the context needed to distinguish evidence from extraction.

## Reference Material

## Archive by burden

The Chain Scriptorium stores plans, judgments, repair orders, successions, and condemned promises on hanging brass plates. Records are indexed by the burdens and offices they governed rather than by ordinary geography. At its reading post, a declared purpose brings forward the records historically used by people claiming the same duty.

## Chains, routes, and proof

Water records hang low, royal plates high, and memory records warm on silvered links. Chain families connect the Ropeworks, Barracks, Reliquary, Feast, Chapel, King's Narrow Grave, Iron Choir, and controlled-collapse routes. The three genealogies distinguish blood custody, maintained obligation, and Avarra's retained surrender terms.

## What removal costs

The Scriptorium can expose omitted operations, open record-defined routes, supply maintenance plates, and preserve evidence of alternatives and abuses. It does not answer questions without the duty implied by them. Removing plates changes what can later be proved, and stripping a chain family destroys the physical index that gives a record meaning. Its current damage, plates removed, routes opened, and keeper remain changing state.
