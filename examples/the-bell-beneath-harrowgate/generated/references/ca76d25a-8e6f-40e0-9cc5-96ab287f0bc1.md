# The Common Measure

**Reference ID:** `ca76d25a-8e6f-40e0-9cc5-96ab287f0bc1`  
**Kind:** Object  
**Aliases:** Common Measure, the Measure frame  
**Tags:** replacement burden, distributed consent, acceptance collar, maintenance

## Summary

A rejected but workable distributed-burden design that replaces singular royal authority with voluntary weights, witnessed acceptance, bounded duration, and continuing maintenance.

## Reference Material

## Design and surviving parts

The Common Measure was rejected politically rather than mechanically. Its surviving design uses seventy-two inscribed civic handweights, voluntary or sworn substitutes, a copper acceptance collar, and the Pilgrim's Yoke as proof that a burden can be transferred without making one bearer permanent. Design plates and trial segments preserve the geometry.

## Recognition and operation

A usable Measure requires an intact or reconstructed collar, sufficient voluntary weight, Inverted Chapel recognition, Counterweight Wells balancing, and identified maintainers after the crisis. Acceptance must name authority, consent, duration, and remedy. Refusal must remain able to halt a transfer whose stated limit disappears.

## What the frame still needs

The Common Measure can distribute obligation and make later transfer accountable. It is not a perfect answer, a sovereign person, or proof that every represented community consented. It cannot supply its own mass, operators, maintenance, or political legitimacy. Components recovered, copies made, weights pledged, bearers chosen, Chapel status, Wells fit, duration, and post-crisis use remain changing state.
