# Court Ledger: The Concord of Aurelune

This ledger preserves the adventure’s maintained political design. It is a GM-facing reference rather than encounter prose.

## Legal threshold

A valid Concord of Open Hands requires:

- seven of ten Great Banner seals;
- at least one Spring, Summer, Autumn, and Winter seal;
- one exact petition text;
- a named undertaking and maximum custody term;
- a preserved royal interest in the Sunseed; and
- certification by the Lord Chamberlain.

House Eiral's banner seal and the Chamberlain's certification are separate acts. The vacant Ashen Bough can vote only if the court seats a claimant before the Crown Conclave.

## The clock outside the court

- A defeated Shadowfell assault left the Pall operating without its makers.
- The Pall completed its outer ring the night before the opening audience.
- A noon-market stone from Orison remembers its old shadow beneath the Sunseed; the remedy is demonstrated, not speculative.
- Orison's Lantern Road compact guarantees an emergency audience and a Crown Passage but does not grant treaty equality.
- The root-casket must enter that passage before moonrise after Court Day Seven.
- The six-, eight-, or thirteen-day custody term begins when the Sunseed is uprooted and is distinct from the passage deadline.
- Caelir opens evacuation immediately and commissions a six-day royal expedition on Court Day One; the court's public work makes that fallback executable.

## Recognition grammar

This is a banner ledger, not an office-allocation table. Record what each house can truthfully recognize about Orison, the Crown, the Sunseed, and the other banners; which visible court object carries that recognition; and which later bargain would make the banner turn away. A seal is the final expression of a relation already made public, not compensation for completing an errand.

## Coalition ledger

### House Ilyrion, the Unfurling Branch

**Season:** Spring  
**Court voice:** Lady Saelira Ilyrion  
**Opening posture:** Open and publicly sympathetic  
**Public ground:** Orison is a sovereign ally facing extinction, not a dependent asking Eladrin charity.  
**Preferred settlement:** Name Orison as a treaty partner; give one mortal envoy a place in joint custody and operational deliberation.  
**Alternative settlement:** A separate recognition compact guaranteed by two banners; or a petition preamble that disclaims wardship and conquest.  
**Red line:** Language placing Orison, its envoys, or the recovered city under unilateral Eladrin protection.  
**Useful leverage:** Ilyrion can reassure Aven Tal, supply Spring balance, and provide credible public idealism.  
**Spillover:** Maelith sees this as an attack on fosterage. Orelle dislikes sovereign immunity from collateral. Thalan fears broad precedent.  
**Truthful deception style:** Generous universals with narrow exceptions. Saelira says, "No free people should be governed without consent," while treating emergency military command as something other than government.  
**Binding question:** "Will your seal remain if the petition gives no Eladrin person unilateral authority over Orison's civil government during or after custody?"

### House Maelith, the White Hart

**Season:** Spring  
**Court voice:** Prince-Regent Othalan Maelith  
**Opening posture:** Open but condescending  
**Public ground:** Great powers may entrust relics only through persons whose obligations survive the crisis.  
**Preferred settlement:** An Orison heir or senior envoy enters voluntary fosterage in Aurelune until the Sunseed returns.  
**Alternative settlement:** Reciprocal high-status hostages; a dynastic marriage compact; or a return guarantee co-sealed by Orelle and Namaris.  
**Red line:** A text declaring all forms of Eladrin wardship illegitimate or recognizing Aven Tal without a limiting doctrine.  
**Useful leverage:** Maelith can normalize the loan as a dynastic trust rather than constitutional defeat.  
**Spillover:** Ilyrion opposes coerced fosterage. Vaudren demands satisfaction before accepting Maelith leadership.  
**Truthful deception style:** Technical kinship. Othalan calls a hostage "a guest of the White Hart" and can truthfully describe every protection the guest receives while omitting the inability to leave.  
**Binding question:** "May the named guest depart Aurelune at any time without forfeiting rights, property, or the Sunseed's continued custody?"

### House Serathiel, the Noon Spear

**Season:** Summer  
**Court voice:** Marshal Ysandre Serathiel  
**Opening posture:** Opposed to an unguarded loan; open to a military compact  
**Public ground:** The Crown cannot save Orison by exposing Aurelune to invasion.  
**Preferred settlement:** Place the expedition under an Eladrin supreme commander and assign allied troops to the eastern border.  
**Alternative settlement:** A thirteen-day maximum term with no extension; a credible substitute Dawnward; or direct allied reinforcement under Serevin command.  
**Red line:** Civilian joint custody with no military authority and no replacement border force.  
**Useful leverage:** Serathiel supplies Summer balance, troops, transport, and public proof that the plan is operationally serious.  
**Spillover:** Ilyrion fears occupation. Serevin welcomes border troops but resists Serathiel control. Thalan dislikes open-ended command powers.  
**Truthful deception style:** Statements of capability heard as predictions. Ysandre says, "The Gloam Host can cross the eastern passes in two days," inviting the inference that it will.  
**Binding question:** "Do you assert that the Gloam Host presently intends to invade during the proposed term, or only that it possesses the capacity?"

### House Vaudren, the Red Rose

**Season:** Summer  
**Court voice:** Lord Avar Vaudren  
**Opening posture:** Open, but refuses a Maelith-led coalition  
**Public ground:** A public undertaking requires a public order of honor that no participant can later deny.  
**Preferred settlement:** Maelith formally acknowledges Vaudren's precedence at the coming solstice and apologizes for the previous equinox slight.  
**Alternative settlement:** Neutral sponsorship; a witnessed adjudication; a formal challenge resolved by champions; or petition language denying any house sole authorship.  
**Red line:** A petition presented as Maelith's initiative or a private settlement that leaves the public record unchanged.  
**Useful leverage:** Vaudren can turn the Masque, supply Summer balance without military command, and enforce public unity once satisfied.  
**Spillover:** Maelith resists humiliation. Eiral dislikes a petition built around personal satisfaction.  
**Truthful deception style:** Courtesy that preserves accusation. Avar says, "I have never called Prince Othalan dishonorable," after spending a season describing acts only a dishonorable prince would perform.  
**Binding question:** "Will you seal a petition chaired by neither Vaudren nor Maelith if the public record states that precedence remains unresolved?"

### House Orelle, the Golden Sheaf

**Season:** Autumn  
**Court voice:** Comptroller Mirelle Orelle  
**Opening posture:** Open at a price  
**Public ground:** The risk imposed on Aurelune must be allocated before generosity makes it invisible.  
**Preferred settlement:** Trade privileges in Orison and a revenue-backed indemnity for verified Dawnward losses.  
**Alternative settlement:** Collateral held in trust; guarantees from wealthy allied states; a capped indemnity; or a broad coalition that shares the cost among banners.  
**Red line:** Unlimited Aurelune liability paired with Orison's complete immunity from repayment.  
**Useful leverage:** Orelle can finance replacement wards, transport the expedition, and make material promises credible.  
**Spillover:** Ilyrion opposes exploitative privileges. Namaris demands auditable definitions. Orison may be saved under terms that compromise its future.  
**Truthful deception style:** Correct totals with chosen categories. Mirelle can truthfully say the loan "costs three million crowns" while counting investments that remain Orelle property.  
**Binding question:** "Which listed costs become unrecoverable losses if the Sunseed returns on time and the Dawnward suffers no breach?"

### House Namaris, the Amber Quill

**Season:** Autumn  
**Court voice:** Archivist-Lord Pelion Namaris  
**Opening posture:** Open to a narrow and reviewable text  
**Public ground:** A court that compels the Crown must know exactly what rule it creates.  
**Preferred settlement:** Audit rights, defined custody, precise return triggers, and an express statement that the petition creates no general right to royal relics.  
**Alternative settlement:** A sunset clause; a formal constitutional review; or parallel side covenants that keep house bargains out of the central instrument.  
**Red line:** Rhetorical language whose legal effect depends on goodwill after the vote.  
**Useful leverage:** Namaris can expose drafting defects, stabilize seals, and give Thalan a path to support.  
**Spillover:** Narrow language may frustrate Ilyrion's recognition goals and Serevin's desired recall authority.  
**Truthful deception style:** Precedent selection. Pelion quotes a controlling sentence accurately while omitting later cases that distinguish it.  
**Binding question:** "Are there later precedents that limit the rule you have just quoted, and will you identify them before asking us to rely on it?"

### House Thalan, the First Frost

**Season:** Winter  
**Court voice:** Duchess Eirath Thalan  
**Opening posture:** Opposed on constitutional grounds  
**Public ground:** Emergency compassion does not erase the Crown's duty or the limits of compelled action.  
**Preferred settlement:** A narrow finding of necessity, a short term, preserved royal co-custody, and a sunset preventing general citation.  
**Alternative settlement:** Caelir joins the petition voluntarily; or the court adopts a broader reform after full deliberation rather than by crisis precedent.  
**Red line:** Language celebrating the king's defeat, denying the Crown a continuing interest, or treating the Sunseed as ordinary public property.  
**Useful leverage:** Thalan supplies Winter balance, constitutional legitimacy, and resistance to later procedural challenge.  
**Spillover:** Reformers dislike the narrow precedent. Eiral values the protection of royal dignity.  
**Truthful deception style:** Historical analogy with unstated judgment. Eirath recites exactly what followed the last compelled loan and lets listeners assume every disaster was caused by the loan.  
**Binding question:** "Which of those consequences do you claim were caused by the compelled custody rather than by the war already underway?"

### House Serevin, the Silent Fir

**Season:** Winter  
**Court voice:** Warden Caldus Serevin  
**Opening posture:** Opposed until the eastern border is protected  
**Public ground:** The Dawnward's weakness will be observable, and the marches must not pay Orison's price alone.  
**Preferred settlement:** Allied troops under Serevin command and authority to recall the Sunseed if a defined border breach occurs.  
**Alternative settlement:** Substitute wards funded by Orelle; Serathiel reserves; a shorter term; or a recall recommendation requiring multiple custodians rather than unilateral power.  
**Red line:** No border mitigation and no lawful response to an actual invasion.  
**Useful leverage:** Serevin supplies Winter balance and the strongest practical argument against the king's claim that the loan is impossible.  
**Spillover:** Unilateral recall may doom Orison. Serathiel contests command. Orelle charges for substitute wards.  
**Truthful deception style:** Selective intelligence. Caldus says, "Every eastern report describes movement," without adding that some movements are ordinary seasonal musters.  
**Binding question:** "Which movements exceed the established seasonal baseline, and which do you assess as preparations for an attack?"

### House Eiral, the Twilight Laurel

**Season:** Equinox  
**Court voice:** Lord Chamberlain Theron Eiral  
**Opening posture:** Neutral in office; privately open to a broad settlement  
**Public ground:** A lawful majority must remain capable of governing the consequences of its act.  
**Preferred settlement:** Eight or more seals, a shared public purpose, preserved royal dignity, and a draft that does not depend on mutually contradictory side promises.  
**Alternative settlement:** Seven seals plus Caelir's voluntary joinder; or a neutral chair and explicit conflict-resolution mechanism among custodians.  
**Red line:** A bare majority assembled to humiliate the Crown or a text Eiral must certify through an aggressive interpretation.  
**Useful leverage:** Banner vote, schedule control, certification, and the ability to present the outcome as concord rather than seizure.  
**Spillover:** Winning Eiral often requires giving up partisan credit and softening terms attractive to one bloc.  
**Truthful deception style:** Institutional distinction. Theron says, "The Chamberlain has taken no position," while House Eiral's agents bargain actively.  
**Binding question:** "Apart from your official duty to certify, what conditions govern House Eiral's banner seal?"

### The Ashen Bough

**Season:** None for balance purposes  
**Court voices:** Lady Mirath Avarre and Aven Tal  
**Opening posture:** Vacant  
**Public ground:** The court must decide who lawfully bears House Avarre's obligations before accepting its vote.  
**Lady Mirath's settlement:** Seat her as banner-holder or military regent with full voting authority. She seals the petition if it includes credible military command.  
**Aven Tal's settlement:** Recognize the sealed adoption as inheritance. He seals a petition that recognizes Orison's political equality.  
**Alternative settlement:** A temporary regency with the banner abstaining; a divided office that cannot vote; or a later constitutional review after the Conclave.  
**Red line:** Neither claimant accepts a settlement that brands their claim fraudulent.  
**Useful leverage:** A tenth available vote and a way to reshape alignments among Spring, Summer, and Winter houses.  
**Spillover:** Mirath strengthens Serathiel and alarms Ilyrion. Aven strengthens Ilyrion and alarms Maelith and Thalan. Leaving the seat vacant narrows coalition options without creating a new enemy.  
**Truthful deception style:** Competing legal nouns. Mirath truthfully denies that Aven is the lord's "natural heir"; Aven truthfully denies that Mirath was ever named "successor." Each phrase avoids the other's actual claim.  
**Binding question:** "Do you deny the legal validity of the adoption instrument itself, or only its power to transfer the banner?"

## Principal incompatibilities

These tensions should generate politics across encounters.

### Equality versus wardship

Ilyrion wants Orison treated as a sovereign partner. Maelith's easiest guarantee resembles noble wardship. Both can support if the delegation separates political recognition from personal security, but neither accepts the other's preferred framing.

### Command versus independence

Serathiel wants operational authority. Ilyrion and Orison's envoys resist foreign command. Serevin wants border command rather than expedition command. A divided command structure can satisfy everyone on paper and fail in practice.

### Public honor versus broad legitimacy

Vaudren can be won through visible satisfaction. Eiral does not want the rescue identified with a personal feud. A neutral authorship formula may solve both at the cost of denying credit to houses that need it.

### Compensation versus rescue without domination

Orelle can make the loan materially safe. Its preferred trade terms burden the city being rescued. Ilyrion opposes exploitation, while Namaris can make even harsh terms cleanly enforceable.

### Useful recall versus usable artifact

Serevin needs a response to genuine invasion. A unilateral recall right can remove the Sunseed before Orison is saved. A multi-party trigger is slower and safer but may be unacceptable at the border.

### Narrow precedent versus transformative settlement

Thalan and Namaris prefer a narrow emergency rule. Ilyrion and Aven Tal want the crisis to establish wider recognition. The delegation may separate the rescue petition from a later constitutional compact, but reformers must trust that "later" is not refusal.

### Seven seals versus a governable concord

A bare majority can compel Caelir. Eiral prefers breadth, and the king can use the coalition's internal contradictions at the final debate. The eighth seal may be more valuable than the easiest seventh.

## Royal counteroffers

Caelir's responses should be public or readily anticipated.

### Evacuation without rescue

Royal gates and fey roads can evacuate perhaps one-third of Orison before the Pall closes. Houses can support this honestly while claiming to support "saving Orison's people."

### Royal expedition without transfer

Caelir offers to send the Sunseed only if it remains under exclusive royal command. This may satisfy Serathiel, Maelith, and Serevin while defeating the delegation's custody terms.

### Reduced term

The Crown offers six days rather than thirteen. The loan becomes safer for Aurelune and operationally dangerous for Orison.

### Substitute relics

The Crown offers lesser artifacts that can preserve districts or evacuation corridors but cannot destroy the Pall.

### Voluntary joinder

If the delegation preserves royal dignity and reaches a coalition already capable of compelling him, Caelir may join after negotiating real changes to the instrument. This can occur with seven seals or a broader concord. It strengthens cooperation without making royal assent the source of validity.

## Petition clauses to track

The final draft should make the delegation choose among concrete language.

- **Undertaking:** destroy the Pall, preserve Orison, evacuate Orison, or restore natural day.
- **Custody:** delegation custody, royal custody, joint custody, or rotating custody.
- **Command:** delegation command, Eladrin command, divided authority, or purpose-limited vetoes.
- **Term:** six days, thirteen days, completion of undertaking, or an absolute date.
- **Recall:** none, royal, Serevin, multi-party, or objective breach trigger.
- **Liability:** Orison, allied guarantors, shared banners, or Crown risk.
- **Return:** physical delivery, replanting beneath the throne, or restoration of the Dawnward.
- **Credit:** royal gift, court concord, joint act, or unnamed public service.
- **Precedent:** general, emergency-only, relic-specific, or expressly nonprecedential.
- **Orison's status:** ward, protected ally, treaty partner, or unspecified.

A petition can be legally clear and politically incoherent. Side covenants can solve some house-specific demands, but the central instrument must not promise incompatible command, recall, or custody.

## Current court procedures

The court-wide procedures are now authoritative.

- The Masque records public grounds, sufficient conditions, associated instruments, and royal alternatives without converting declarations into seals.
- The Petition Chamber keeps separate Crown-instrument, associated-instrument, and declaration dockets.
- Material amendments release dependent seals; corrected exact text may be reaffirmed in the same sitting.
- The Crown Conclave calls seasonal balance before seal count, permits narrow corrective recesses, and distinguishes lawful compulsion from voluntary royal joinder.
- Caelir's royal instrument offers a six-day exclusive-Crown expedition, evacuation corridors, and lesser relics as a complete alternative.

## Political route coverage

The maintained corpus tests cover six representative coalition shapes:

1. a bare-majority reformist concord;
2. a martial security coalition;
3. a mercantile-constitutional coalition;
4. a seven-seasonal-banner royal concord using neither Eiral nor the Ashen Bough;
5. a nine-banner broad concord that leaves the Ashen seat vacant deliberately; and
6. a late recall correction that releases four seals and recovers them through exact reaffirmation.

Each test records the banners pursued, the public ground holding each seal, the exact clause or covenant satisfying it, the king's most effective lawful answer, amendment effects, and the resulting operational and constitutional costs. No tested route required a new encounter, revelation, clue, or edge.

## Demonstration coalition

The Grey Lantern Delegation's demonstration journal uses the seven seasonal banners other than Ilyrion:

- Maelith for reciprocal personal surety and preserved royal custody;
- Serathiel for expedition command and the enacted security package;
- Vaudren for witnessed satisfaction and neutral public credit;
- Orelle for bounded liability, ward finance, and ordinary reconstruction contracts;
- Namaris for exact custody, audit, return, and associated-instrument review;
- Thalan for narrow necessity, sunset, review, and voluntary royal joinder; and
- Serevin for a funded eastern reserve and objective recall.

The coalition makes three deliberate exclusions.

- Ilyrion supports departure but does not seal a settlement that stops short of equal Orison co-custody.
- House Eiral does not join the majority; Theron's certification remains an official duty rather than a banner concession.
- The Ashen Bough remains vacant because the delegation refuses to settle inheritance doctrine for an emergency vote.

The final instrument is certified before Caelir joins it. Royal joinder adds cooperation and shared responsibility; it does not supply the legal force of the petition.

## Voice and coherence reconciliation

The final source preserves every house's public ground, preferred settlement, alternatives, red line, leverage, spillover, and binding question. The prose pass sharpened how those positions sound at the table.

- Saelira Ilyrion speaks in moral universals, then protects exceptions inside institutional categories.
- Othalan Maelith makes confinement sound like kinship and obligation sound like honor.
- Ysandre Serathiel states military capacity with such precision that listeners supply the prediction.
- Avar Vaudren never utters the accusation whose ceremonial consequences he demands.
- Mirelle Orelle lets impeccable accounts decide which costs count.
- Pelion Namaris answers with the precedent that governs until someone asks for the precedent that limits it.
- Eirath Thalan places facts in historical sequence and leaves causation to the listener.
- Caldus Serevin speaks least, but conditions every promise on observable border facts.
- Theron Eiral separates office, household, seal, and certification so rigorously that an unasked distinction can govern the court.
- The Ashen claimants tell incompatible truths about blood, designation, regency, and recognition without reducing the succession to a hidden answer.

These distinctions alter cadence and presentation, not coalition logic. The binding questions remain the GM's reliable means of closing each courtier's chosen gap.
