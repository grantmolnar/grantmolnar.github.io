# Design Notes: The Concord of Aurelune

## Design objective

This is a large political adventure set in an Eladrin high court. The delegation is not investigating what happened. It is trying to make something happen: assemble a lawful coalition that obliges an unwilling king to share a powerful royal artifact.

The governing question is:

> What coalition will the delegation build, and what will it allow that coalition to make of the undertaking?

The court's universal oath against lying changes the texture of political speech without reducing politics to a puzzle about verbal loopholes. Every important actor technically honors the oath and spiritually rejects its purpose. The court's deceptions are visible instruments of bargaining, delay, blame, and plausible commitment.

Every political encounter must therefore provide:

1. a vote, certification, resource, or public condition that matters without an informational reward;
2. at least two credible settlements, not one correct concession;
3. consequences for other houses and for the final petition;
4. language that can be made binding if the delegation insists on precision; and
5. a reason to revisit the encounter after the coalition changes.

The adventure must not collapse into seven isolated persuasion checks. Support is relational. A term that secures one banner can repel another, create a public obligation, alter the petition, or give the king a better counteroffer.

## Premise

Nine days before the opening audience, a Shadowfell host kindled the Pall of Night over Orison as a siege-work. Orison's defenders drove the host away on the third night. The work did not end with its makers. It continued replacing daylight, memory, and seasonal change with permanent Shadowfell dusk.

Orison's first couriers crossed the forming boundary twice. They brought out the first measurements, then returned with witnesses and the surviving council's sealed emergency commission after neighboring governments dismissed those measurements as panic. On the night before the audience, the Pall completed its outer ring. At moonrise after Court Day Seven it will close the Lantern Road, erase the remaining planar approaches, and begin sustaining itself. Ordinary armies can hold the perimeter and evacuate part of the population. They cannot save the city intact.

The Sunseed of Aurelune can. It is a living ember from the first sunrise over the Feywild. A paving stone carried from Orison's noon market warms, sheds black frost, and remembers its old shadow beneath the Sunseed. Aurelune's Dawnward keepers reproduce the effect and agree with Orison's measurements: properly borne to Orison and planted at the Pall's center, the relic can force the blighted region to remember its rightful sky.

The Lantern Road compact gives Orison an emergency audience and access to a Crown Passage, not recognition as Aurelune's treaty equal. The passage can place the root-casket at Orison's western gate before the final moonrise; one road from the western gate still reaches the Pall's center. Once the Pall feeds itself, the passage cannot cross the ring. The Sunseed may still break the darkness if already inside. The deadline therefore governs entry, while the six-, eight-, or thirteen-day custody term begins when the relic is uprooted.

The Sunseed is not an unused treasure. It anchors the Dawnward around Aurelune, moderates violent seasonal transitions, and strengthens the kingdom's eastern border against the Gloam Host. Removing it for even thirteen days creates a real strategic risk.

King Caelir III therefore refuses the delegation's request at the opening audience. His refusal is prudent, self-interested, and politically useful. He opens evacuation gates immediately and orders a six-day Crown expedition studied as a fallback, but he will not uproot the relic until passage, command, and replacement defense are executable. The coalition's public work can strengthen its own petition and the king's rival plan at the same time.

Aurelune's constitutional order provides another path. The Concord of Open Hands allows seven of the ten Great Banners to compel the Crown to place a royal relic in joint custody for one named undertaking. The petition must:

- bear seven valid banner seals;
- include at least one Spring, Summer, Autumn, and Winter banner;
- name the undertaking and maximum term;
- identify custody, command, liability, and return conditions;
- preserve a royal interest in the relic; and
- receive certification from the Lord Chamberlain that the text is internally consistent and lawfully adopted.

Once certified, the king must comply. He can bargain before certification, challenge language, offer alternate aid, pressure houses, and make public arguments against the petition. He cannot veto a valid Concord.

## No mystery

Nothing important depends on discovering a hidden culprit, secret conspiracy, concealed identity, or suppressed explanation.

At the opening audience, the delegation can learn:

- the exact voting threshold;
- the seasonal-balance requirement;
- the Lord Chamberlain's role;
- why the king opposes the loan;
- what each house publicly wants;
- which rivalries make particular coalitions difficult;
- why the Sunseed matters to Aurelune as well as Orison; and
- that the Crown will try to split any emerging majority with narrower offers.

Some courtiers withhold details, delay commitments, or describe their interests selectively. Those acts create leverage and uncertainty. They do not conceal the premise of the adventure. The GM should tell the players when a speaker is using a recognized court technique. The question is usually not, "Is this misleading?" but, "What exactly will this person bind themselves to, and what does that cost elsewhere?"

## The Oath of the Unbroken Word

Every sovereign, peer, officer, advocate, and recognized petitioner who speaks beneath the Argent Canopy swears:

> Within the King's peace, I will not assert what I believe false, deny what I believe true, or set my seal to words I do not intend to bind me.

The oath is magically enforced. A knowing violation takes the speaker's court voice for a year and causes every mirror beneath the Canopy to repeat the broken sentence in the speaker's own voice. No reigning sovereign has attempted to survive that humiliation. No Great House treats the sanction as metaphorical.

The oath governs false assertion and bad-faith sealing. It does not require relevance, completeness, cooperation, stable future intention, or ordinary-language meanings where court precedent supplies a narrower one.

Aurelune's political culture has built an admired discipline around that distinction.

### Ordinary techniques of truthful deception

#### Omission

A courtier states every fact favorable to the desired inference and none of the facts that defeat it.

#### Narrow scope

A statement is true only of the named hour, office, room, person, title, account, or legal category.

#### Belief management

A noble arranges not to receive information and then truthfully says, "I know of no such order."

#### Reported speech

A courtier accurately reports that another person made a claim and relies on the audience to hear endorsement.

#### True intention

A noble says, "I intend to support the petition," honestly describing the present moment while retaining every means of changing that intention before sealing.

#### Controlled conditions

A promise depends on a condition whose satisfaction the speaker can prevent without breaking the promise.

#### Court terms

Words such as *custody*, *war*, *heir*, *protection*, *consent*, and *return* have technical meanings established by precedent. A sentence may be literally binding and practically useless.

#### Comparative truth

"No house has offered more" can mean every house offered nothing.

#### The unanswered question

A speaker gives a true answer to an adjacent question. Refusing to answer directly is lawful; doing so publicly carries a political cost.

### Running oath-bound dialogue

Truthful deception should produce decisions, not grammar traps.

For every important evasive statement, the GM should know:

1. its literal truth;
2. the false or incomplete inference it invites;
3. the political reason for inviting that inference;
4. a direct formulation that would close the gap; and
5. what the speaker will do if pressed to answer or refuse.

Do not make essential progress depend on guessing an unstated referent or catching a single preposition. Characters experienced in diplomacy should recognize routine techniques automatically. The dramatic act is forcing precision in public, accepting ambiguity for strategic reasons, or choosing not to spend political capital on clarification.

Documents and seals are different from conversation. A valid seal binds the noble to the exact petition text and to the intentions stated in it. Changing the text can release existing seals. A noble who promises to sign later has not signed.

## Clue density

The three-clue rule applies to revelation reliability. It does not imply that every encounter should contain the same number of clues.

This court uses functional density.

- **Procedural hubs** such as the Argent Canopy and Amber Quill Archive carry more clues because they expose rules, cross-house compromises, and routes into several negotiations.
- **Broad political events** such as the Masque carry many clues because multiple bargains become public at once.
- **Focused house encounters** carry fewer clues when their dramatic strength comes from sustained negotiation around one coherent interest.

The original completed source ranged from five to eight clues per encounter and averaged about 6.9. The second-look density pass now ranges from six to ten and averages about 7.9. An encounter should gain a clue because it offers a distinct actionable conclusion, not because another encounter has a larger count. Perfectly regular density is treated as a construction smell.

## The political objective

The delegation must leave the Crown Conclave with:

- seven valid seals;
- seasonal balance;
- a certified petition;
- workable custody and command terms;
- a plan to protect Aurelune while the Sunseed is absent; and
- enough time to reach Orison before the Pall becomes permanent.

Only the first three are strict legal requirements. The others determine whether the victory is usable, honorable, and durable.

A coalition can be legally sufficient and strategically disastrous. The king can be compelled to release the Sunseed under terms that place an Eladrin commander over the delegation, mortgage Orison's future trade, recognize an abusive dynastic claim, or leave Aurelune's border exposed. The delegation may accept such terms. The adventure must preserve that choice and its consequences.

## Coalition state

Track each voting banner through five postures.

### Opposed

The house will not seal the current petition and is willing to spend influence against it.

### Open

The house will negotiate but has not accepted a settlement.

### Declared

The house has publicly announced support for a described policy or draft. This creates reputational force but is not legally binding.

### Sealed

The house has affixed its banner seal to the current exact text.

### Released

The house previously supported or sealed a draft, but a material amendment, failed condition, or lawful withdrawal released it.

A house can move in either direction. The GM should record why. "Failed persuasion" is not a sufficient reason. Posture changes follow concessions, threats, rival alignments, public commitments, changed wording, or events at court.

## Court pressures

Track six campaign pressures without reducing them to a single score.

### Time to Orison

The delegation has six full court days before it must depart. Court Day Seven is the Crown Conclave and the last day on which the root-casket can enter the Crown Passage before the Pall seals the Lantern Road at moonrise. The custody term begins when the Sunseed is uprooted, so an eight- or thirteen-day instrument is not measured from the opening audience. Delay can improve terms, but a late victory leaves less margin for transfer, the march from Orison's western gate, and any correction before the passage closes.

### Royal resistance

Caelir begins publicly opposed. As the coalition grows, he changes method:

1. dignified refusal;
2. alternate aid;
3. private offers to individual houses;
4. procedural challenge;
5. a rival petition with narrower terms; and
6. a final appeal to preserve the Dawnward.

These are not hidden plots. The court expects them.

### Coalition breadth

Seven seals suffice. A broader coalition gives the petition legitimacy, makes certification easier, and limits royal retaliation. It also requires more concessions and creates more owners of the victory.

### Draft stability

Every major amendment risks releasing seals. Early vague language attracts declarations. Late exact language determines whether those declarations survive.

### Dawnward security

The Sunseed's absence weakens Aurelune. The delegation can mitigate that risk through troops, substitute wards, allied guarantees, shortened custody, or a dangerous seasonal rite. Houses Serathiel and Serevin care most, but every noble can use the issue.

### Public credit

The Masque and Conclave determine who appears to have saved Orison, protected Aurelune, humbled the king, preserved the Crown, or sold the realm. Credit affects support because Eladrin political memory is long and ceremonial precedence is material power.

## Binding support

Each house has four distinct political elements.

### Public ground

The reason the house can defend its support before the court.

### Required settlement

A term, act, guarantee, or coalition shape that can secure its seal.

### Red line

A term that makes support impossible unless the delegation changes the surrounding arrangement.

### Spillover

The effect of satisfying the house on other voters, the petition, Orison, or Aurelune.

Every house must have at least two credible routes to support. One may satisfy the house directly. Another may change the coalition so that the house accepts a different settlement. No house should be reducible to a secret password or one skill check.

## The king

King Caelir III is neither a final boss nor a disguised ally.

He believes:

- the Sunseed is first a royal trust for Aurelune;
- Orison's peril is real and morally serious;
- the Great Banners should not normalize compelled custody of crown relics;
- several houses will use the crisis to win unrelated advantages;
- the delegation cannot fully understand the risk to the Dawnward; and
- a king who yields too easily teaches the court to create the next emergency.

He will keep every promise he makes and exploit every ambiguity the delegation accepts. He prefers to defeat the petition, but he also prepares terms under which losing preserves royal dignity and Aurelune's safety.

A strong ending can leave Caelir opposed, persuaded, reconciled, or lawfully compelled. Persuading him is valuable but never required.

## Voting architecture

There are ten Great Banners.

- Eight are ordinary seasonal houses: two Spring, two Summer, two Autumn, and two Winter.
- House Eiral, the Twilight Laurel, occupies the Equinox office and supplies the Lord Chamberlain.
- The Ashen Bough is vacant when the delegation arrives. A public hearing can seat one of two lawful claimants before the Conclave.

The delegation needs seven seals and at least one seal from each of the four seasons. The Equinox and Ashen Bough votes count toward seven but do not satisfy seasonal balance.

The Lord Chamberlain certifies the petition in an official capacity. House Eiral's banner vote is legally distinct from certification. The Chamberlain cannot lawfully refuse a valid text, but can demand exact correction of defects, schedule the certification late, or decline to spend political capital defending a strained interpretation.

## The Ashen Bough

The tenth banner creates a political choice, not a mystery.

House Avarre's last lord died without a natural child. Two claims are openly recognized as legally serious:

- **Lady Mirath Avarre**, his elder sister and designated military regent, argues that a banner cannot pass outside the bloodline while a competent dynast remains. She offers continuity, troops, and an immediate seal. Seating her strengthens the martial houses and weakens reform of inheritance law.
- **Aven Tal**, his adopted mortal-born son, argues that the lord's sealed adoption made him heir before the death. Seating him recognizes intentional kinship over blood and transforms the status of mortal-born nobles throughout Aurelune. He will support the petition, but several houses demand limits on the precedent.

The evidence and law are sufficient for either result. The question is which doctrine and political future the delegation will help establish. The hearing can also remain unresolved, leaving the tenth banner unavailable.

## Encounter registry

The final source is planned around fourteen encounters.

### 1. The Argent Canopy

**Mode:** audience, constitutional briefing, and first alignment.

Caelir refuses the Sunseed. The Lord Chamberlain explains the Concord of Open Hands. The ten banners state their first positions in language that is literally true and politically incomplete.

The delegation can demand a formal calendar, secure standing as recognized petitioners, choose which early approaches to make public, and respond to the king's offer of evacuation magic in place of the artifact.

**Carries forward:** court schedule, public framing, the first royal offer, and which houses believe the delegation will respect Aurelune's interests.

### 2. The Unfurling Branch

**Mode:** reform coalition, mortal standing, and ambitious generosity.

House Ilyrion will support saving Orison if the petition treats the city as a treaty partner rather than a ward of Aurelune. It wants mortal envoys in joint custody and language recognizing Orison's right to refuse later Eladrin direction.

That support helps Aven Tal and alarms Maelith, Orelle, and Thalan. Ilyrion can accept narrower recognition if another house guarantees it publicly.

### 3. The Gallery of Silver Kin

**Mode:** dynastic politics, wardship, and succession.

House Maelith wants custody arrangements that pass through recognized noble persons. It proposes placing an Orison heir or senior envoy under Eladrin fosterage as security for the Sunseed's return.

The house can instead accept a reciprocal hostage, a marriage compact, or a tightly drafted guarantee backed by another banner. Its preferred settlement conflicts with Ilyrion's insistence on political equality.

### 4. The Noon Spear Court

**Mode:** military planning and command authority.

House Serathiel accepts that Orison should be saved but argues that no relic can leave while the eastern border lacks a replacement commander and reserve. It wants an Eladrin military command over the expedition or a binding deployment elsewhere.

The delegation can offer command, allied troops, a shorter term, or a substitute Dawnward plan. Serathiel's seal brings military credibility and creates fear that the rescue will become an Eladrin intervention.

### 5. The Red Rose Pavilion

**Mode:** public honor, satisfaction, and ceremonial coercion.

House Vaudren has a live grievance against Maelith over precedence at the previous equinox. It will not join a coalition that allows Maelith to claim senior authorship without satisfaction.

The delegation can arrange an apology whose exact words matter, adjudicate precedence, accept a formal challenge, or build a coalition in which neither house leads. A private settlement is easier and politically weaker than a public one.

### 6. The Golden Sheaf Exchange

**Mode:** treasury, liability, and the future price of rescue.

House Orelle treats the Sunseed's absence as an insurable public risk. It wants collateral, trade rights in Orison, or a revenue-backed indemnity for damage to Aurelune.

The delegation can offer material security, obtain guarantees from other houses, limit the term, or persuade Orelle that the political value of a successful rescue exceeds direct payment. Every route assigns the cost to someone.

### 7. The Amber Quill Archive

**Mode:** precedent, drafting, and technical truth.

House Namaris maintains the precedents that define custody, return, royal interest, and a named undertaking. It can make the petition enforceable or expose how apparently favorable language fails.

Namaris wants audit rights and a text that cannot be cited to compel unrelated crown treasures later. Its seal is available through narrow drafting, shared oversight, or a broader constitutional settlement with Thalan.

### 8. The Hall of First Frost

**Mode:** constitutional restraint and principled opposition.

House Thalan believes the Concord exists for existential necessity, not sympathetic foreign rescue. It fears the petition more than the temporary loss of the Sunseed.

Thalan can be won by proving the undertaking fits the old standard, narrowing the precedent, preserving a meaningful royal role, or accepting a sunset and formal review. It will oppose language designed to humiliate Caelir even if every practical safeguard is present.

### 9. The Silent Fir Lodge

**Mode:** border security, intelligence assessment, and burden sharing.

House Serevin guards the eastern marches. It does not claim the Gloam Host will certainly attack. It states truthfully that the host can recognize a weakened Dawnward and has forces positioned to exploit one.

Serevin wants replacement troops, substitute warding, or authority to recall the Sunseed if the border breaks. Recall authority can make the Orison expedition fail at the worst possible moment.

### 10. The Twilight Laurel Apartments

**Mode:** agenda, legitimacy, and royal dignity.

House Eiral can vote like any banner, while its head serves as Lord Chamberlain. Eiral wants a coalition that can govern after it wins. It will not spend its banner or office on a seven-house arrangement held together only by private bargains and mutual hatred.

The delegation can secure Eiral through breadth, a public statement of shared purpose, protections for the Crown, or a carefully neutral chairmanship. Eiral is likely to commit late and makes a powerful eighth or ninth seal.

### 11. The Ashen Bough Hearing

**Mode:** succession, public law, and coalition realignment.

The delegation can support Lady Mirath, support Aven Tal, broker a regency and later review, or leave the seat vacant. The court knows the evidence and consequences.

The result changes inheritance doctrine, the balance among seasonal houses, and the easiest routes to seven seals.

### 12. The Masque of Plain Faces

**Mode:** public commitments, reputation, and cross-house bargaining.

At the Masque, every guest wears a transparent mask and speaks beneath portable oath-mirrors. Private understandings can be witnessed, rival houses can challenge their meaning, and the king can offer alternate aid before the whole court.

The Masque is not a mandatory social minigame. It is a high-connectivity encounter where the delegation can convert declarations into public commitments, reconcile incompatible narratives, or deliberately preserve ambiguity.

### 13. The Chamber of the Fourfold Petition

**Mode:** drafting, amendment, certification, and coalition discipline.

The petition becomes exact here. Custody, command, duration, liability, recall, credit, and return must be stated. Every amendment can release a seal whose conditions no longer hold.

The delegation can use a narrow petition, a broad political compact, parallel side covenants, or staged custody. The chamber tests whether the assembled coalition supports the same undertaking rather than seven different imagined bargains.

### 14. The Crown Conclave

**Mode:** final vote, counter-coalition, and binding consequence.

Caelir presents the strongest lawful alternative he can assemble. Houses may reaffirm, condition, or withdraw support according to the exact draft and public record. The Chamberlain certifies or identifies defects.

If the petition succeeds, the final question is not whether the Sunseed leaves. It is who carries it, under whose command, for how long, with what recall power, and what political order follows the rescue.

## Planned topology

The political graph should feel like a court, not a corridor.

- The Argent Canopy is an entry hub that opens several initial approaches.
- The eight seasonal houses form a rivalry-and-dependency ring, allowing lateral movement without repeated return to the throne.
- The Twilight Laurel and Ashen Bough connect otherwise distant blocs.
- The Masque is a public cross-house hub.
- The Petition Chamber is a legal cross-house hub.
- The Crown Conclave has at least three independent routes of approach.

A provisional simple topology uses fourteen encounters:

```text
A  Argent Canopy
B  Unfurling Branch
C  White Hart Gallery
D  Noon Spear Court
E  Red Rose Pavilion
F  Golden Sheaf Exchange
G  Amber Quill Archive
H  Hall of First Frost
I  Silent Fir Lodge
J  Twilight Laurel Apartments
K  Ashen Bough Hearing
L  Masque of Plain Faces
M  Chamber of the Fourfold Petition
N  Crown Conclave
```

The final clue graph will be built for exact necessary-encounter edge connectivity at least three. The intended rhythm is:

```text
opening audience
    -> several houses
    -> lateral bargaining among seasonal rivals
    -> public commitments and succession pressure
    -> exact petition drafting
    -> renewed bargaining after terms become concrete
    -> Crown Conclave
```

The list is not a scene sequence. A delegation may draft early, use the Masque late, ignore the vacant seat, win the Chamberlain first, or enter the Conclave with more seals than it needs.

## Planned revelation architecture

The authoritative source now contains fifteen necessary and three optional revelations. Each has at least three clues from at least three source encounters; the present range is three to eight clues and three to eight sources.

The three-clue rule is a revelation-level reliability floor, not an encounter-density target. The current source ranges from six to ten clues per encounter and averages about 7.9. Density follows function: procedural hubs carry more cross-court leverage, while focused house negotiations carry fewer, more concentrated conclusions. Fifteen second-look additions deepen non-route coalition truths without changing traversal.

### Banner opportunities

Ten revelations explain actionable routes to a valid seal from each Great Banner. These are not revelations of hidden motives. They establish specific settlements the delegation can pursue and bind.

### Procedural leverage

Planned procedural revelations include:

1. the Concord can compel joint custody without transferring ownership;
2. seasonal balance is independent of the seven-seal count;
3. the Chamberlain must certify a valid text but controls the cost and timing of correction;
4. public declarations can be made politically expensive to abandon without becoming legal seals;
5. the Ashen Bough can be seated before the Conclave;
6. substitute wards and allied deployments can materially protect Aurelune during the loan;
7. side covenants can preserve house-specific bargains without overloading the central petition; and
8. the king's counter-petition can be defeated without persuading him personally.

These conclusions should tell the delegation what can be done. They should never function as secret keys required to understand the adventure.

## Court-day rhythm

The adventure runs on court days rather than a fixed scene order.

### Court Day One: Refusal

The king refuses. The court states its opening positions. The delegation gains standing and chooses its first public frame.

### Court Day Two: Prices

Private settlements become possible. The king offers evacuation aid and begins distinguishing "saving Orison" from "lending the Sunseed."

### Court Day Three: Rivalries

The first declarations provoke counter-demands. The Ashen Bough hearing opens. Houses begin asking who will control the coalition.

### Court Day Four: Public meaning

The Masque turns private bargains into public narratives. Caelir offers narrower aid to peel away sympathetic houses.

### Court Day Five: Exact language

The Petition Chamber forces custody, command, liability, and recall into words. Seals become possible and fragile.

### Court Day Six: Counter-coalition

The king and opposed houses make final offers. Border risk, precedent, and Orison's remaining time sharpen every concession.

### Court Day Seven: Conclave

The final text is debated, certified, and voted. A late victory can still carry operational costs on the road to Orison.

The GM may compress or expand days. The clock should create political pressure, not prohibit exploration.

## Outcome families

Outcomes are descriptive, not scores.

### Royal relief

The delegation accepts Caelir's alternate aid without securing the Sunseed. Many lives are saved, but Orison is abandoned or permanently altered by the Pall.

### Compelled loan

Seven banners force release of the Sunseed under a legally valid but narrow or burdensome settlement. Orison can be saved, and the coalition's costs follow the expedition.

### Royal concord

Seven or more seasonally balanced banners create a petition strong enough to compel the Crown, then Caelir joins after negotiating real concessions. The settlement gains royal cooperation without pretending that assent, rather than the seals, created the leverage.

### Broad concord

A wide coalition protects Aurelune, preserves meaningful royal participation, and gives Orison workable political independence. This requires more than seven seals and usually leaves several houses with less than they wanted.

### Partisan victory

The petition succeeds as the instrument of one dominant bloc: reformist, martial, mercantile, dynastic, or constitutional. The Sunseed leaves, but the rescue becomes part of that bloc's program.

### Constitutional rupture

The delegation wins through humiliating procedure, a contested certification, or a succession settlement the losing houses reject. The law holds and the court fractures around it.

### Failure with consequence

The petition fails or becomes too late. The delegation can still secure evacuation, military aid, future alliance, or a changed succession. Political failure does not reset the court.

## Session 5 stress-test findings

Six complete coalition routes now test the political architecture rather than only the clue topology.

- A reformist bare majority can succeed through Ilyrion, Maelith, Vaudren, Namaris, Serevin, Eiral, and an Aven-led Ashen Bough.
- A martial coalition can succeed through Maelith, Serathiel, Orelle, Thalan, Serevin, Eiral, and a Mirath regency.
- A mercantile-constitutional coalition can succeed without either military banner.
- Seven of the eight seasonal houses can compel the Crown without House Eiral or the Ashen Bough and then negotiate voluntary royal joinder.
- All nine occupied banners can form a broad concord without deciding the Ashen succession for convenience.
- A material recall contradiction can release four of seven seals and still be corrected through exact reaffirmation rather than arbitrary failure.

The tests confirm that a house's political function is not identical to its vote. Defenses can be purchased without military-house seals; liability can be assigned without Orelle; exact drafting remains possible without Namaris; and certification remains independent of Eiral's banner. Excluding a specialist raises cost and uncertainty rather than removing the associated function from play.

The tests also produced two source clarifications. The finale now names **royal concord** as distinct from both compelled loan and the Crown's separate relief instrument. A scope-limited Ashen regency now states how corrections, material amendments, changed undertakings, and extended terms affect its voting authority.

## Development constraints

Later sessions must preserve the following decisions.

- The adventure is not and does not contain a conventional mystery.
- The oath is real; court members do not secretly lie.
- The oath does not make them honest.
- Seven of ten banners, seasonal balance, and Chamberlain certification are the legal path.
- The king can be compelled without being exposed as evil or secretly persuaded.
- Every house has more than one route to support.
- No route is free of spillover.
- The final petition's exact wording matters.
- The delegation may win a coalition whose political cost it dislikes.
- The completed graph should be large, nonlinear, and at least three-edge-connected.
- Encounters should normally carry five to eight clues, with several conclusions pointing beyond the local house.
- Clues communicate actionable political leverage, available settlements, and procedural consequences; they are not fragments of a concealed solution.

## Session 6 demonstration findings

The Grey Lantern Delegation playthrough exercises one seven-seasonal-banner royal concord rather than touring every authored possibility.

- The final majority is Maelith, Serathiel, Vaudren, Orelle, Namaris, Thalan, and Serevin.
- Ilyrion is approached first and remains a principled supporter of the rescue while opposing the final status settlement.
- House Eiral remains politically unsealed, while Theron Eiral certifies the petition in office.
- The Ashen Bough remains vacant by an explicit opening decision.
- The delegation revisits Namaris after the Masque and Thalan before the Conclave because public commitments and royal joinder change the value of those encounters.
- A broad royal safety phrase releases four dependent seals. The corrected purpose-limited authority is reaffirmed rather than treated as an inconsequential clerical fix.
- The journal corrects an inaccurate report that Vaudren's seal remained attached, preserving the original operation while excluding it from the current projection.
- Certification precedes royal joinder. Caelir joins a binding petition in exchange for an eight-day term, shared credit, and explicit bounded protective authority.

The playthrough establishes all eighteen revelations while leaving thirty-seven clues unseen, including fifteen later alternatives, and never visiting the Twilight Laurel Apartments or Ashen Bough Hearing. The example therefore demonstrates both clue redundancy and political optionality rather than exhaustive completion.

## Voice and coherence pass

The final prose revision treated the completed political architecture as fixed. It changed no encounter, revelation, clue identifier, graph edge, petition rule, coalition test, journal action, or ending.

The revision concentrated on four coherence problems.

1. **Repeated abstractions.** Recurring explanatory terms such as *settlement*, *available*, *political cost*, and formulaic “not this but that” constructions were reduced where scene action could carry the meaning.
2. **Undifferentiated court speech.** Each principal actor now favors a distinct cadence and a distinct lawful evasion: universal principle, technical kinship, capability stated as forecast, courtesy preserving accusation, selective accounting, selective precedent, historical sequence, conditional silence, or exact division of offices.
3. **Construction language in play.** Encounter openings and encounter headings now present rooms, ceremonies, documents, and choices before explaining their design function. Procedural hubs remain information-rich without sounding like reports about their own topology.
4. **Narrative alignment.** The Grey Lantern Delegation, full playthrough, journal notes, consequences, and generated play summary now use the same motives, concessions, and terminology. The append-only correction, released seals, Eiral certification, vacant Ashen Bough, and royal joinder remain unchanged.

The finished voice remains formal because Aurelune is a high court, but formality is no longer uniform. Courtesy, precision, and beauty are instruments of contest rather than a shared narrator's mannerism.
## Second-look clue-density findings

The original encounter totals were already irregular, but most of that variation belonged to route revelations. The court-wide conclusions that make a coalition governable remained comparatively thin. The second look therefore preserved every existing clue and added fifteen independent discoveries to five non-route truths: joint custody without alienation, seasonal balance, substitute security, compatible side covenants, and Orison's retained agency.

The additions use each institution's own evidence: royal dockets, kinship surety, war-table markers, account threads, adverse indexes, constitutional precedent, watch orders, official files, clause matrices, and the seasonal call. All fifteen revelations lack encounter destinations, so the seventy-two route edges and exact edge connectivity six remain unchanged. The 158-event journal also remains unchanged.
