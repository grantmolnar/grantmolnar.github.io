# Petition Clause Matrix: The Concord of Aurelune

This document is a table-facing drafting aid for the Chamber of the Fourfold Petition and Crown Conclave. It does not define one correct petition. It records the political effects that follow when the delegation chooses one form over another.

## Living-text use

Read the matrix as a map of where banner recognitions touch. A compatible pair shares one line of the petition's seasonal light. An incompatible pair is not merely a drafting defect: it reveals that two houses still imagine different custodians, commands, terms, or political authors. Resolve the relation before polishing the sentence.
## Core articles

| Article | Available forms | Houses helped | Houses pressured | Operational risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Undertaking | Destroy the Pall; preserve Orison; restore natural day; prevent permanent Shadowfell dominion | Ilyrion prefers a preserved polity; Serathiel prefers an executable objective; Thalan prefers a narrow emergency finding | The Crown can call evacuation preservation if the noun remains vague | A narrow objective may end custody before the city is stable; a broad objective may authorize coercive action |
| Custody | Exclusive Crown; delegation; joint royal and mortal; rotating by stage | Maelith accepts answerable persons; Ilyrion requires meaningful mortal authority; Thalan prefers preserved Crown interest | Caelir resists possession beyond the Crown; Serathiel dislikes custody that obstructs command | Nominal joint custody may leave one custodian powerless at the point of use |
| Command | Delegation; royal; Serathiel; divided; purpose-limited royal veto | Serathiel requires coherent command; Ilyrion accepts operational command that does not reach civil government; Eiral values conflict resolution | Serevin resists Serathiel control of the marches; Vaudren resists command being treated as authorship | Divided authority can produce delay or contradictory orders |
| Term | Six days; thirteen days; completion with an outside date; staged term | Thalan and Serevin prefer a shorter exposure; Serathiel and Orison prefer enough time to complete the work | Caelir offers six days to peel away security-minded houses | The Sunseed may be recalled after the Pall breaks but before natural day returns |
| Recall | None; Crown; Serevin; multi-party; objective breach trigger | Serevin needs a lawful response to real invasion; Thalan prefers measurable limits; Namaris prefers named verifiers | Ilyrion and Orison resist revocation at political convenience | A slow trigger may fail Aurelune; a unilateral trigger may doom Orison |
| Liability | Orison; Crown; Orelle insurance; shared banners; allied guarantors; fault-based split | Orelle needs assigned risk; Namaris needs audit; Ilyrion accepts broad guarantees better than trade subordination | Orison may reject permanent privileges or unlimited debt | Unfunded guarantees collapse during the expedition; harsh collateral survives the rescue |
| Return | Delivery to royal officer; replanting beneath the throne; restored Dawnward; delivery plus verification | Namaris and Orelle prefer auditable completion; Maelith prefers named answerable custodians | The delegation may lose control before the Dawnward is actually restored | Risk can fall into the gap between delivery, replanting, and restored function |
| Credit | Royal gift; court concord; named house leadership; neutral service; no sole author | Vaudren needs visible satisfaction; Eiral prefers neutral or shared authorship; Thalan prefers no humiliation | Maelith and the Crown resist formulas that diminish their office | Credit disputes can release a seal without changing operational language |
| Precedent | General; emergency-only; relic-specific; express noncitation; later review | Thalan and Namaris prefer a narrow sunset; Ilyrion and Aven prefer durable recognition | Caelir resists a general right to compel Crown relics | A nonprecedential clause may preserve the rescue while postponing promised reform indefinitely |
| Orison status | Ward; protected ally; treaty partner; participant only for the undertaking; unspecified | Ilyrion and Aven require real agency; Maelith can accept personal surety separated from sovereign status | Orelle wants reachable guarantees; Thalan resists emergency constitutional transformation | Status language may either enable later domination or fail to protect Orison from redirection |

## Compatible packages

These packages are examples, not prescribed routes.

### Narrow instrument with broad associated covenants

The Crown instrument states the undertaking, joint custody, command, term, objective recall, liability, and return. Separate instruments carry Orison recognition, personal surety, credit, deployment, and finance.

**Advantages:** isolates amendments, limits relic precedent, and lets houses rely on specialized bargains.  
**Weaknesses:** depends on the associated covenants remaining public, enforceable, and consistent.

### Broad concord instrument

The petition itself states Orison's political standing, neutral authorship, shared defense, joint custody, and constitutional sunset.

**Advantages:** makes the political settlement difficult to disaggregate after victory.  
**Weaknesses:** almost every amendment becomes material to several seals.

### Security-first instrument

The petition uses a short or staged term, royal protective veto, eastern deployments, objective recall, and bounded liability. Orison receives civil autonomy and consent to redirection but little expedition command.

**Advantages:** holds Serathiel, Serevin, Orelle, Thalan, and possibly Caelir.  
**Weaknesses:** may save Orison under an Eladrin military structure whose time limit is too short.

### Sovereignty-first instrument

Orison is a treaty partner and mortal co-custodian. The delegation commands the expedition, while the Crown may prevent damage to the Sunseed or uses outside the undertaking. Surety and liability sit in associated instruments.

**Advantages:** preserves Orison's agency and supports Ilyrion and Aven.  
**Weaknesses:** requires strong answers for military defense, personal responsibility, and precedent.

### Royal-concord instrument

Seven or more seasonally balanced banners retain a petition capable of compelling release while negotiating Caelir's voluntary joinder. The Crown receives real authority, credit, or term concessions without replacing joint custody with the separate royal instrument.

**Advantages:** gains royal cooperation while preserving the coalition's legal leverage.  
**Weaknesses:** late royal concessions can release several seals at once and may narrow Orison's authority or the operational term.

## Amendment effects

| Change | Default effect |
|---|---|
| Correct numbering or cross-reference without changed scope | Chamberlain reads the correction; seals normally remain unless a banner demands reaffirmation |
| Alter one article's legal effect | Releases seals whose stated conditions depend on that article; other seals remain unless broadly reserved |
| Replace or materially change an associated covenant | Releases subscribers and any banner that made the covenant a condition of its seal |
| Change the named undertaking | Releases every seal; this is a new petition |
| Replace joint custody with exclusive custody | Releases every seal attached to the prior custody structure |
| Narrow or extend the outside term | Releases houses that made the term or Dawnward exposure a condition |
| Add a new guarantor without changing shares | Requires the guarantor's assent; need not release unrelated seals |
| Replace objective recall with unilateral recall | Releases Ilyrion, Orison-aligned support, and any house that relied on the objective trigger |
| Amend a petition sealed by a scope-limited regent | Corrections within the authorized undertaking may be reaffirmed; a changed undertaking, unauthorized covenant, or term beyond the office requires renewed authority |

## Final audit questions

Before the Conclave, the delegation should be able to answer each question by pointing to text.

1. What exact condition ends the undertaking?
2. Who physically possesses the Sunseed at each stage?
3. Who commands the expedition, and what authority is excluded from that command?
4. Who may stop or redirect use of the Sunseed?
5. What measurable event can trigger recall, and who verifies it?
6. What happens if the Pall breaks before natural day returns?
7. Which losses are compensable, and who values them?
8. What act constitutes return?
9. Which associated instruments are conditions of particular seals?
10. What may Orison refuse without violating the command article?
11. Which house receives public credit, and which house loses it?
12. What later court may cite this instrument, and for what proposition?
13. Does every temporary banner-holder still possess authority to seal the final text and term?
