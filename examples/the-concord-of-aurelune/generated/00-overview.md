## Synopsis

The Shadowfell army that raised the Pall has been driven from Orison, but its siege-work now feeds itself. At moonrise after the seventh court day, it will close the last Fey road into the city. A paving stone carried from Orison remembers noon beneath Aurelune's Sunseed, proving the relic can restore the sky if it crosses in time. King Caelir III refuses the proposed loan. Seven of the ten Great Banners, with all four seasons represented, can compel joint custody for one named undertaking. Orison's petitioners must win those seals and make their rival bargains govern one exact instrument before the road closes.

## Premise

A defeated Shadowfell siege-work is closing around Orison. The Sunseed can restore the city's sky, but it must enter the Crown Passage before moonrise after the seventh court day. In Aurelune's high court, recognized speakers cannot knowingly lie; the adventurers must win seven seasonally balanced banners and draft one undertaking their incompatible truths can obey.

## Explanation

There is no concealed culprit or secret vote. The Pall, the Sunseed test, the deadline, the voting rule, the king's opposition, and the available settlements are public. Orison's Lantern Road compact guarantees an emergency audience and passage into Aurelune; it grants neither treaty equality nor command of the rescue. The adventure assumes only that Orison has commissioned the adventurers to petition and negotiate beside Envoy Mara Venn; their identities, history, internal offices, and preferred coalition remain open. The court is assembling a coalition, not filling offices. The Canopy turns its leaves toward recognized speakers; plaques change category when the court recognizes a relation; oath-mirrors retain abandoned promises; and banner seals attach only to exact text. No recognized speaker can knowingly assert what is false, so omission, narrowed answers, selective precedent, and arranged context remain political weapons. Play turns on making rival houses truthfully recognize one shared undertaking, then preserving enough room inside it for Orison and the Crown to act. Every bargain changes which banners can stand together when the Sunseed rises.
