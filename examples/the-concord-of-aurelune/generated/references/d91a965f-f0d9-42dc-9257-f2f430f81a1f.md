# The Oath of the Unbroken Word

**Reference ID:** `d91a965f-f0d9-42dc-9257-f2f430f81a1f`  
**Kind:** Other  
**Aliases:** the Unbroken Word, the truth oath  
**Tags:** oath, court, truthful deception

## Summary

Aurelune's court oath forbids knowing falsehood and bad-faith sealing while leaving omission, narrow scope, and selective context as political weapons.

## Reference Material

## The leaves turn toward the speaker

Every sovereign, peer, officer, advocate, and recognized petitioner speaking beneath the Argent Canopy swears not to assert what they believe false, deny what they believe true, or seal words they do not intend to bind them. A knowing violation takes the speaker's court voice for a year and causes the court's mirrors to repeat the broken sentence. The sanction is real; the court does not secretly lie around it.

## Truth without completeness

The Oath of the Unbroken Word does not require relevance, completeness, cooperation, ordinary-language scope where precedent supplies a narrower one, or a stable future intention that has not been promised. Omission, selected categories, convenient ignorance, accurate but incomplete precedent, and answers to narrower questions remain lawful techniques.

## What mirrors and seals preserve

Oath-mirrors can preserve an exact public declaration and the question left unanswered. A declaration binds reputation and witnesses, not the final vote. The oath forbids a speaker from sealing words they do not intend to obey; it does not keep a material amendment from releasing a prior seal or make several incompatible private meanings into one governable instrument.
