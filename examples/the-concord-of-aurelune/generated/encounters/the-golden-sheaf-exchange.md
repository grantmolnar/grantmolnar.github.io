# The Golden Sheaf Exchange

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** autumn, banner, liability

Orelle prices the absent Sunseed in grain, wards, villages, and political credit, then asks who signs each loss.

## Opening View

A black grain drops into Mirelle Orelle's palm. She weighs it, glances at three open ledgers, and asks who promised the expense without naming its bearer. Above the orchard table hang sheaves marked Loss, Capital, Advantage—wardstones in one, soldiers in another, bright roads and privileges among the rest. Mirelle returns the grain to none of them. It remains in her hand, waiting for a named account.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

The Golden Sheaf Exchange occupies an orchard held in perpetual late afternoon. Every obligation spoken beneath its branches appears as a grain of gold on a hanging sheaf. The grain grows heavier with the cost. It turns black when no bearer has been named. The magic reveals no secret price. It only refuses to let generosity remain weightless.

Mirelle lays the black grain from her palm across three open books. It will not settle into Loss, Capital, or Advantage until someone names what is consumed, what remains valuable, and what advantage is being purchased under the name of compensation.

Mirelle opens at three million crowns. Every number is defensible. The total includes reusable wardstones, a reserve returned if no breach occurs, and ten years of exclusive trade privileges valued as though Orison had already granted them. Asked whether all three million would be lost after an uneventful return, she says no. Unasked, she lets the court repeat the larger figure.

> A gift paid by an unconsulted border village is not a gift. It is an invoice delivered by someone with better manners.

Mirelle does not require Orison to pay every line. She requires the coalition to stop calling a line free because the payer is absent.

## What is being priced

The Exchange can produce a complete schedule before the audience ends. Its principal entries are openly available.

- temporary ward lattices around the weakened Dawnward;
- an eastern reserve for Warden Serevin;
- transport and containment for the Sunseed;
- insurance against verified damage to Aurelune;
- delayed seasonal tolls while the passages are unstable;
- expedition provisions and replacement healers; and
- any indemnity, collateral, or trade rights pledged to secure the above.

The schedule can shrink without erasing the risk. Joint custody is cheaper than transfer of ownership. A capped indemnity is cheaper than a general guarantee. A thirteen-day term is more expensive than six days, but the six-day term may make the Orison undertaking fail. Reusable wardstones are capital rather than loss. A reserve posted by several banners requires less profit than one carried by Orelle alone.

Mirelle will discuss each distinction. She does not volunteer the distinction that harms her preferred bargain.

## House Orelle's seal

Orelle can seal through several materially different settlements.

### Orison bears the risk

Orison grants Orelle privileged docks, collection rights on fey-road commerce, and a revenue-backed indemnity for verified Dawnward losses. This is Mirelle's easiest settlement. It finances the security package immediately and burdens a city already facing ruin. Ilyrion opposes any version that can be described as purchasing rescue through dependence.

### Allied guarantors bear the risk

Two or more banners, allied cities, or wealthy estates guarantee a capped sum. Orison remains liable for loss caused by misuse of the Sunseed, but not for ordinary defensive preparations in Aurelune. This route is compatible with Ilyrion and Maelith if the guarantors are named persons rather than Orison's treasury in disguise.

### The coalition shares the risk

Participating banners subscribe to short-term ward bonds. Orelle supplies procurement and accepts a lower return because the undertaking becomes a court-wide public work. This favors a broad concord and makes Eiral more receptive. It also gives every subscribing house a plausible claim to authorship.

### The Crown bears a defined share

The petition records that maintaining the Dawnward is an existing royal obligation. The Crown pays ordinary defense costs; the coalition pays only incremental costs caused by the Sunseed's absence. Caelir will resist the characterization, but he cannot truthfully call all Dawnward expense a foreign liability.

### The political return replaces part of the financial return

Orelle accepts a smaller indemnity in exchange for nonexclusive access to Orison's reconstruction contracts and visible credit for financing the Dawnward. This is less predatory than permanent trade privilege and still changes Orison's post-crisis economy.

## Negotiating the schedule

Mark every line as loss, capital, advantage, or disputed. Mirelle defends each classification.

Useful direct questions include:

- Which items remain Orelle property after the Sunseed returns?
- Which costs would Aurelune incur even if the Sunseed never left?
- Which sums are reserves returned if no breach occurs?
- Which projected values depend on Orelle receiving a monopoly rather than reimbursement?
- What maximum payment would satisfy the house if the Dawnward suffers no verified damage?
- Will Orelle accept several guarantors whose obligations are separate and capped?

Mirelle welcomes a precise audit. She objects when the petitioners call every margin theft. In her view, profit is the part of a promise that remains after enthusiasm has gone home.

## Coalition consequences

### Ilyrion

Saelira can defend a guarantee funded by allied states, court revenue, or participating banners. She will not defend exclusive trade rights extracted from Orison as the price of rescue. A separate reconstruction compact with ordinary competition is acceptable.

### Maelith

Othalan prefers named estates and persons to abstract public credit. His house can become a guarantor, but he will expect the guarantee to strengthen his personal-surety argument elsewhere.

### Serathiel and Serevin

The Exchange can finance both an eastern reserve and substitute warding without giving Serathiel expedition command. It can also finance Serathiel's preferred command structure. Money resolves the resource objection, not the authority dispute.

### Namaris

Pelion can reduce the schedule by defining **compensable loss** as verified, incremental, and unrecovered damage. Orelle accepts the definition if the audit process is funded and the cap is real.

### The Crown

Caelir's evacuation offer appears cheaper because most losses fall on Orison: abandoned property, displaced citizens, and a city left beneath the Pall. Mirelle will confirm that the royal plan does not eliminate cost. It changes whose ledger recognizes it.

## Entries that need names

### Choose the liability base

Indemnify all verified harm, only incremental Dawnward harm, only loss caused by misuse, or a fixed maximum regardless of cause. Each version changes Orelle's price and Namaris's drafting burden.

### Name the payers

Orison, allied guarantors, participating banners, the Crown, or a mixed consortium can bear the obligation. An unnamed promise becomes black grain on the sheaf and does not secure Orelle's seal.

### Authorize procurement

Wardstones and reserve contracts must begin before the fourth court day to support a thirteen-day loan. Authorize procurement before the final vote to make the security package credible, at the cost of money and political capital if the petition fails.

### Decide whether compensation purchases influence

Reimbursement can remain reimbursement, or it can include trade access, reconstruction authority, ceremonial credit, or future fiscal oversight. Mirelle prices these separately. The petitioners must do the same.

## How the Sheaf may leave the exchange

### The bill has names

The petitioners leave with a capped, auditable liability schedule; named payers; procurement authority for the full Dawnward package; and Orelle either declared or ready to seal once the draft incorporates those terms.

### Financing that changes the peace

- Orelle declares immediately in exchange for privileged access to Orison's docks.
- The coalition shares the cost, but every subscribing banner acquires a claim on public credit.
- The Crown pays ordinary defense costs only after the petitioners accept royal co-custody and a narrower undertaking.
- Procurement begins before the coalition is stable, creating sunk costs that royal advocates use to push a compromise.

### The black grain remains

Contest the existence of risk rather than allocating it. Orelle remains open, publishes the three-million-crown figure without the petitioners' classifications, and lets the king cite it as the uncontested price of the loan.

## Carry into the ledgers

- the definition and cap of compensable loss;
- the named guarantors or revenue source;
- whether Orison grants trade or reconstruction privileges;
- which security contracts begin before certification;
- whether the coalition treats ordinary Dawnward defense as a Crown cost; and
- whether Orelle has declared, sealed a side covenant, or merely priced the options.

## On a return

The Exchange remains useful whenever command, term, recall, or custody changes. Mirelle recalculates openly. A more cautious draft lowers some costs and may make the undertaking unusable. A broader coalition lowers the required financial return and raises the number of nobles entitled to say they paid for victory.
