# The Crown Conclave

**Role:** necessary, end  
**Tags:** finale, court, vote

The Sunseed rises among four seasonal shadows, forcing each banner to decide whether its public reason can inhabit the coalition that will carry it.

## Opening View

The glass root opens. The Sunseed rises close enough to warm the first row of hands and casts one shadow toward each season. Theron calls Spring before counting any seal. The Ashen place remains in the circle; Caelir's complete six-day instrument lies beside the coalition's petition. Every banner may still withdraw truthfully. The king looks from the texts to their bearers. "Which reasons," he asks, "will survive possession?"

## Encounter Material

## Situation

On Court Day Seven the Argent Canopy descends until its silver leaves form a circle around the throne. The glass root opens. The Sunseed rises into view: a fruit no larger than a clenched hand, bright enough to throw four shadows, one toward each season.

The banner standards stand around it. If the Ashen Bough remains vacant, its empty place is not removed. If Eiral has withheld its seal, the Twilight Laurel still faces the throne. The court has spent six days speaking about custody. Now the thing to be carried is close enough to warm every hand in the room.

No argument arrives as a surprise. The Sunseed does not count seals; it throws the coalition's named powers into four seasonal shadows and exposes where two banners still imagine different custodians, commands, or returns. The Conclave tests which declarations, covenants, and compromises survive proximity to the relic and the king's complete alternative. A banner may reaffirm, withdraw, or join the Crown. Its explanation must be true; generosity, completeness, and courage remain optional.

## How the Sunseed reads the coalition

### 1. The seasonal call

Theron calls Spring, Summer, Autumn, and Winter before he counts seals. Eiral and the Ashen Bough are called afterward. The order makes seasonal balance visible before the count.

An eighth unseasoned seal cannot cure a missing season. Bargaining remains open until the final call.

### 2. The petition is read

Pelion Namaris reads the exact Crown instrument and identifies every associated instrument by title and parties. He does not summarize away difficult clauses. Any house may ask that a definition or linked covenant be read in full.

Mara Venn or another authorized representative of Orison states whether the named undertaking is accepted. Orison's acceptance is not a legal substitute for seven banners. It demonstrates that the coalition has not secured the Sunseed for an undertaking the city itself rejects.

### 3. Certification or defect

Theron either certifies the petition or identifies each defect. His reasons are reviewable against the Concord. Political objections do not become defects merely because the Chamberlain shares them.

A narrow contradiction, failed cross-reference, or omitted verifier may be corrected in recess. Banners affected by the correction reaffirm. A changed undertaking, exclusive-custody substitution, or new liability scheme requires a new instrument and releases the existing seals.

Caelir may argue that a clause is dangerous, unprecedented, or dishonorable. He may not command the Chamberlain to misstate its validity.

### 4. The declaration record

The Master of Mirrors reads each relevant declaration beside the house's present posture. A house may withdraw. It must then say which condition failed, which fact changed, or which public purpose it now ranks above the earlier one.

The Conclave turns inconsistency into authorship. A banner may leave the coalition under its own name.

### 5. The royal instrument of relief

Caelir presents a complete alternative.

- The Sunseed travels for six days under exclusive Crown custody and royal command.
- Orison's representatives are consulted and may refuse civil cooperation, but they have no authority over the relic.
- The Crown assumes liability for royal misuse and funds immediate evacuation corridors.
- Lesser relics protect selected districts if the Pall cannot be broken within the term.
- The Sunseed returns automatically upon the term's end or a defined Dawnward failure.
- The Crown claims the undertaking as royal relief rather than compelled joint custody.

Caelir states that this instrument saves every life and district his officers can reach without surrendering the trust placed in the Crown. He does not claim that it is as likely to restore Orison's sky as the longer joint-custody plan. He does not claim that Orison governs its own rescue.

Houses may support the royal instrument and withdraw from the petition without speaking falsely. The king's strongest argument is that their declared purposes can be served without the constitutional and military risks of the coalition's text.

### 6. Final questions

Each banner may ask one question of Orison's petitioners, one of the Crown, and one of another house. Refusal is an answer. Keep the questions on text and declared commitment, not concealed motive.

Useful final questions include:

- "Which act by the royal custodian is beyond the purpose-limited veto?"
- "What happens on the seventh day if the Pall is broken but natural day has not returned?"
- "Which guarantor pays first if the eastern ward fails without an invasion?"
- "May Orison refuse a use of the Sunseed against its civil authorities?"
- "Will House Eiral seal if the Crown does not join?"
- "Will this house preserve its deployment after another house receives public credit?"
- "Does the Ashen Bough's authority extend through the entire custody term?"

Answer by pointing to the instrument. An answer that depends on an unwritten assurance is an invitation to amend or withdraw.

### 7. The seals are called

Each banner is called once. It may seal, reaffirm, withdraw, or abstain. A conditional seal is not a seal. A house may first demand that an already permitted correction be made; it may not rewrite the undertaking during its vote and pretend the other seals remain attached.

When seven valid seals including all four seasons stand on the same certified text, the Concord binds the Crown. Caelir's personal assent is unnecessary.

If eight or more banners seal, the court treats the petition as a broad act even if the king remains opposed. If Caelir joins voluntarily, he does so after certification and before transfer. His joinder strengthens the political settlement; it is not the source of its validity.

## Holding the pattern of banners together

Use only the pressure that fits the coalition actually assembled.

### Reformist coalition

Ilyrion and an Aven-led Ashen Bough emphasize Orison's equality. Caelir offers evacuation and royal relief that save lives while removing mortal co-authority. Thalan and Maelith ask whether emergency recognition has become a general inheritance and sovereignty rule.

The coalition holds if recognition is real, the relic petition remains bounded, and personal surety does not become wardship by another name.

### Martial coalition

Serathiel, Serevin, and a Mirath-led Ashen Bough emphasize command and defense. Caelir's exclusive expedition appears close to their stated objectives. Ilyrion or Vaudren may refuse to ratify a rescue that becomes solely a Crown military act.

The coalition holds if command is coherent, Orison retains civil authority and consent to redirection, and the border package survives without unrestricted recall.

### Mercantile coalition

Orelle and Namaris anchor liability, audit, and return. The king offers Crown-funded relief with lower exposure. Reformers ask whether Orison is being saved into debt; military houses ask whether valuation procedures will delay action.

The coalition holds if liability is bounded, funding is executable, and repayment does not purchase domination.

### Constitutional coalition

Thalan, Namaris, and Eiral emphasize narrow precedent, exact process, and royal dignity. Caelir invites them to accept voluntary royal relief and avoid compulsion entirely. Other houses may suspect that the undertaking has been narrowed until it cannot save the city.

The coalition holds if the emergency finding is genuine, the operational term remains usable, and restraint does not become disguised refusal.

### Bare majority

With exactly seven seals, every withdrawal is decisive. The king addresses each house separately and offers it a true route to part of its public purpose. The petitioners cannot answer by claiming the houses are secretly bribed or lying. They must show why the joint undertaking better satisfies the grounds they chose to make public.

### Broad concord

With eight or more seals, the king attacks the text rather than peeling one house: too many owners, authorities, and promises. A broad coalition must show a real conflict-resolution structure and common purpose. It is the coalition most likely to receive voluntary royal joinder.

## Royal conduct on defeat

Caelir honors a valid Concord. He meant to win.

If compelled, he says:

> The court has placed its hands upon a trust that was mine to guard. I will not call lawful hands thieves. I will require them to carry what they have taken responsibility for.

He then performs every duty the instrument assigns him. He interprets every ambiguity in favor of the Crown and Aurelune. A clean petition produces stern cooperation. A humiliating petition produces exact obedience without generosity.

If persuaded or reconciled, Caelir joins after certification and names the undertaking a concord between Crown, banners, and Orison. The conflict remains in the record.

## Outcomes

### Broad concord

At least eight banners seal, the security package is credible, Orison holds meaningful authority, the text preserves a real Crown interest, and no single bloc owns the rescue. Caelir may join voluntarily. The Sunseed leaves with the best chance of preserving both realms, though many houses acquire durable claims on the settlement.

### Royal concord

Seven or more seasonally balanced banners certify a petition strong enough to compel the Crown, then Caelir joins after negotiating real changes to command, term, credit, custody, or royal dignity. His joinder improves cooperation and lets the undertaking be presented as reconciliation rather than defeat. The seals remain the source of the coalition's leverage, and the negotiated concessions remain binding costs rather than ceremonial language.

### Compelled loan

Seven banners and all four seasons seal a valid petition over Caelir's opposition. The Sunseed leaves. Every burden in the text travels with it: command limits, guarantors, recall triggers, liability, public credit, and the outside date.

### Partisan victory

A reformist, martial, mercantile, dynastic, or constitutional bloc supplies the decisive structure. The petition succeeds and the rescue becomes evidence for that bloc's program. Other houses comply while preparing the next contest.

### Constitutional rupture

The text is valid, but the coalition humiliates the Crown, seats a contested claimant solely to manufacture a vote, or forces certification through the most aggressive lawful reading. The Sunseed leaves under law. The court fractures around who will bear the precedent.

### Royal relief

The petition loses support or the petitioners choose the Crown instrument. The Sunseed may still travel under exclusive royal control, or evacuation and lesser relics may save many lives. Orison's sky is less likely to be restored, and the Crown defines the undertaking.

### Failure with consequence

No valid instrument survives before departure becomes impossible. The court still implements any evacuation, border, recognition, succession, or alliance agreements the petitioners secured. Orison suffers the Pall, but political play has changed Aurelune rather than resetting it.

## Transfer of the Sunseed

On a successful petition, the glass root divides according to the custody article. A royal custodian takes one handle of the root-casket. Each other named custodian receives the authority and physical access stated in the text. If the petition created only nominal joint custody, the ceremony makes that failure visible immediately.

Theron reads the undertaking, term, recall trigger, and act of return. Each custodian answers with the exact promise the oath will enforce. Trust is not among the required answers.

The root-casket enters the Crown Passage before moonrise. The passage reaches Orison's western gate; from there, one road still remembers the way to the Pall's center. The custody term begins when the Sunseed leaves the throne root, not when the court week began. A late correction, six-day term, recall threshold, command compromise, or unfinished deployment is now an operational fact rather than court rhetoric.

## What leaves the throne hall

- the final certified petition and associated instruments;
- the banners that sealed, withdrew, or joined the Crown instrument;
- whether Caelir was compelled, reconciled, or voluntarily joined;
- the Sunseed's named custodians and command structure;
- the outside date and exact recall trigger;
- the active border, liability, surety, recognition, and credit obligations;
- the Ashen Bough settlement and its duration; and
- the public account of who saved Orison, protected Aurelune, or accepted the cost of failure.

## If the court is reconvened

The Conclave ends the court campaign; its consequences remain. Later play may bring the adventurers back to answer for loss, delayed return, a recalled relic, an inheritance precedent, unpaid guarantees, or a rescue that succeeded under politically intolerable terms.
