# The Ashen Bough Hearing

**Role:** optional  
**Tags:** succession, banner, hearing

A succession hearing may supply an eighth banner, but only by making the coalition own the doctrine that seats it.

## Opening View

Theron names one duty Vaelor Avarre left behind. An ember leaf opens above the empty Ashen chair, hangs between Mirath's marshal's baton and Aven Tal's sealed adoption, then falls when neither claimant owns it. Advocates begin arranging a military regency, a child's inheritance, and a term ending with the Sunseed's return. The gray bough waits for someone to claim tomorrow morning.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

The Ashen Bough hangs above an empty chair in a round court open to the evening sky. Its gray branches kindle an ember-colored leaf for each recognized obligation of House Avarre; a leaf falls when that obligation has no owner.

Theron places three instruments beneath the branch: Vaelor's blood genealogy, Mirath's three regency commissions, and Aven Tal's sealed adoption. The adoption names Aven child for household, estate, and personal obligations; no presentation to the Crown names him bearer of the Great Banner. Mirath points to the missing public act. Aven points to duties the court already enforces. An ember leaf falls over each obligation neither claimant will concede.

Mirath denies that Aven is Vaelor's **natural heir**. Aven denies that Mirath was named **successor**. Neither statement touches the other's claim.

The court may seat either one, impose a bounded regency, divide duties without a vote, or leave the chair empty. The danger is not a hidden correct answer. It is an answer the court will use long after Orison's sky has returned.

## Procedure

Theron marks two thresholds on the hearing slate: six of the nine sitting banners, including two seasons, for a final succession judgment; five for a regency limited to the Sunseed undertaking. Fewer leaves the chair empty. The king may speak and bargain but casts no Great Banner vote.

The petitioners vote only if they already represent a banner. Otherwise the emergency petition gives them standing to question the claimants, broker a remedy, recruit the required houses, and decide whether the tenth vote is worth the doctrine that creates it.

## Lady Mirath Avarre

Mirath argues that a Great Banner is a public office before it is property. Vaelor's private adoption transferred household obligations and estate rights. It did not give the court a known bearer able to command troops, answer ancient compacts, or defend the seasonal order.

Her political position is martial but not merely Serathiel's.

- She will seal a petition with coherent expedition command and real eastern defense.
- She accepts divided command if one officer has authority during immediate danger.
- She supports a temporary regency that ends when the Sunseed returns and does not brand Aven's adoption invalid.
- She opposes language treating every adopted heir as automatically entitled to a public banner without court acknowledgment.

Mirath's truthful deception lies in public-law nouns. She speaks of **the banner's heir** when she means the person entitled to its office, then lets listeners hear **Vaelor's heir** in every respect.

## Aven Tal

Aven argues that Aurelune cannot celebrate chosen kinship in private law and deny it at the point where rank becomes consequential. Vaelor bound him to the house's debts, ancestral duties, and mourning rites. The court should not accept those burdens and refuse the banner because the chosen child was mortal-born.

His political position is reformist but not identical to Ilyrion's.

- He will seal a petition naming Orison a political partner and giving a mortal co-custodian meaningful authority.
- He does not require the emergency petition to establish a general right of mortal inheritance.
- He can accept a temporary Mirath regency if his adoption remains valid, his ultimate claim is calendared for full judgment, and the current petition does not subordinate Orison.
- He opposes any settlement stating that only blood can carry a Great Banner.

Aven's truthful deception lies in private-law nouns. He calls himself Vaelor's **heir** without adding that the court has never decided whether the term carries the public office.

## Lawful outcomes

### Seat Mirath as banner-holder

Six banners recognize blood continuity and public capacity as sufficient. Mirath receives the tenth vote and can seal the Sunseed petition. Serathiel and Maelith are strengthened. Ilyrion and Aven demand language making clear that adoption itself remains valid.

### Seat Aven as banner-holder

Six banners hold that the sealed adoption carries the house's public obligations. Aven receives the tenth vote and favors Orison's equality. Ilyrion is strengthened. Maelith and Thalan seek language limiting the precedent to a fully executed adoption by a reigning banner-holder.

### Appoint Mirath as emergency regent

Five banners appoint Mirath only through return of the Sunseed or expiration of the petition. She may cast the Ashen Bough during that term. Aven's inheritance claim remains undecided and receives a scheduled hearing afterward. This is Maelith and Thalan's easiest compromise.

Aven accepts only if the order does not call Mirath **heir**, preserves the adoption's validity, and prevents the emergency from becoming indefinite.

### Appoint a conditional regency

Mirath becomes regent and may cast the banner only on the Sunseed petition and associated security covenants. She cannot use the office to alter the estate or succession record. This is narrower and makes the Ashen Bough almost a single-purpose vote.

A scope-limited regency must state how later drafting affects its authority. Corrections that preserve the named undertaking, authorized covenants, and outside term remain within the order. A material amendment may release the Ashen seal and require Mirath to reaffirm. A changed undertaking, a new use of the Sunseed, or a term extending beyond the regency requires renewed authority from the succession court before the banner can seal again.

### Establish joint stewardship

Mirath commands the house's troops; Aven administers the estate and personal obligations. Neither may cast the banner until the full succession judgment. This preserves both claims, reduces immediate conflict, and leaves the coalition with only nine possible votes.

### Leave the seat vacant

The court declines to exploit a succession crisis for the petition's convenience. Neither claimant becomes an enemy merely because the petitioners choose another route to seven seals. The unresolved doctrine remains for later.

## Public examination

Require each claimant to state the exact conditions under which they would cast the banner. Those answers are declarations rather than seals. They become part of the hearing record and make later withdrawal politically expensive.

Useful questions include:

- Do you deny the validity of the adoption instrument, or only its power to transfer the banner?
- Would you accept an emergency regency that expressly preserves the later claim?
- Which command structure is sufficient for your seal, and which merely preferable?
- Does Orison's equality need to appear in the central petition, or may a guaranteed side compact suffice?
- Will you support the Sunseed undertaking if the court recognizes the other claimant?

The last question matters. Mirath may support the petition through military assistance even if Aven is seated. Aven may support Orison's petitioners and Ilyrion even if Mirath becomes regent. The succession result does not have to reduce either person to a defeated obstacle.

## Coalition effects

### Maelith

Othalan supports a bounded Mirath regency because it supplies a named noble guarantor without deciding inheritance under pressure. He can also accept Aven if a limiting doctrine preserves formal acknowledgment for future banners.

### Serathiel

Mirath's seal strengthens a martial coalition. Aven can still accept coherent command if a mortal custodian retains authority over changes to the undertaking.

### Ilyrion

Aven's seating is a public victory for chosen kinship and mortal standing. Saelira can tolerate Mirath's regency if the order preserves Aven's adoption and the petition recognizes Orison independently.

### Thalan

Eirath prefers an emergency regency to a permanent doctrinal judgment made because the petitioners want one more vote. She can support either final claimant after full argument and dislikes pretending urgency has answered the larger question.

### Eiral

House Eiral supports any remedy adopted through its stated threshold, written without insulting the losing claim, and integrated into a governable coalition. A claimant seated by a razor-thin arrangement of immediate beneficiaries makes the Ashen Bough a liability rather than a stabilizing eighth vote.

## Judgments the petitioners must choose to own

### Decide whether the tenth vote is needed

Already have a plausible route to seven. Pursuing the hearing can broaden the coalition, reshape it, or consume the time needed to reconcile existing bargains.

### Choose final judgment or emergency relief

A final decision creates durable law and requires six banners. A regency is easier, narrower, and vulnerable to the charge that the petitioners manufactured a temporary voter.

### Broker terms between the claimants

Preserve Aven's adoption, limit Mirath's office, schedule later judgment, divide household functions, and define whether the banner may vote on matters beyond the Sunseed.

### Demand public coalition prices

Requiring exact declarations helps at the Masque and gives the king less room to buy the eventual bearer privately. It also binds the petitioners to treat the claimant's stated ground seriously.

## How the hearing may close

### A lawful bearer with a bounded doctrine

The court adopts a clear lawful remedy, the Ashen Bough becomes either a usable vote or an intentionally unavailable one, and neither claimant is falsely declared fraudulent. The result creates an explicit alignment rather than a hidden resentment.

### A vote that outlives the emergency

- Mirath becomes regent and the petition adopts a stronger military package.
- Aven is seated and the court accepts a limiting inheritance doctrine to hold Thalan and Maelith.
- The hearing preserves both claims but consumes a public appointment and yields no vote.
- The Ashen Bough can vote only on the Sunseed petition, making later amendments especially sensitive.

### The branch remains unowned

Treat the hearing as a search for the real heir and accuse one claimant of lying. Both answer the accusation precisely, the court closes ranks around its lawful ambiguity, and the seat remains vacant for lack of a proposed remedy.

## Enter in the succession record

- whether the Ashen Bough is occupied and by whom;
- whether the result is final succession or temporary regency;
- the doctrine stated about adoption, blood, acknowledgment, and public office;
- the scope and end date of any regent's voting authority;
- each claimant's public condition for supporting the Sunseed petition; and
- which houses spent influence to produce the result.

## On a return

The hearing may be reopened only for a material breach of the adopted order or after the emergency term ends. Both claimants remain political actors regardless of who holds the banner. Judgment does not remove the losing claimant from court.
