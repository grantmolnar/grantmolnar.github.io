# The Masque of Plain Faces

**Role:** optional  
**Tags:** court, public, commitment

Oath-mirrors give private conditions authors and let every abandoned promise keep its witness until dawn.

## Opening View

The oath-mirror repeats a courtier's declaration before she lowers her clear mask. It preserves her posture, her witnesses, and the question she declined. Four seasonal doors admit banners in order; Caelir enters barefaced. Servants carry separate tablets for recognition, surety, credit, troops, and precedence. Nothing spoken is yet a seal. By dawn, every promise abandoned here will still possess its author's name.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

The Masque of Plain Faces opens on the fourth evening in a gallery joined to all four seasonal gardens. Every guest wears clear glass. Etiquette requires each speaker to address the mask rather than the face behind it, allowing a courtier to insist—truthfully—that a remark was made to an office, a house, or a public role and not to the person who heard it.

Oath-mirrors stand on silver tripods. When a recognized voice makes a formal declaration, the nearest mirror repeats the exact words once and keeps their reflection until dawn. It records language, posture, witnesses, and the questions declined. It cannot record intent.

The Masque is where private terms acquire owners. A house may declare support, name a sufficient condition, subscribe to a side covenant, challenge another house's account, or offer a rival settlement. No declaration is a seal. Every declaration gives the court a sentence to place beside a later withdrawal.

King Caelir attends barefaced. Caelir attends barefaced. The Crown is legally never masked.

## What the Masque changes

Before the Masque, a house can give each rival a different true fragment. A witnessed declaration forces the fragments to coexist in public.

Use the event to:

- turn negotiated terms into declarations other houses can rely upon;
- establish a shared purpose broad enough for Eiral without erasing each house's separate ground;
- resolve Vaudren's demand for public satisfaction;
- replace Maelith's preferred fosterage with a witnessed personal bond;
- assemble Orelle's guarantor consortium and Serevin's border package;
- docket side covenants as one coordinated settlement; and
- force the king's alternatives to stand beside the exact undertaking they would replace.

Use the Masque to make two or three decisive bargains legible to the entire court; it need not carry every negotiation.

## The order of the evening

### The Procession of Seasons

Each banner enters through its seasonal garden and names the public ground on which it is prepared to act. Eiral and the Ashen Bough enter last because neither satisfies seasonal balance. The procession makes a missing season impossible to hide behind a high seal count.

A house may change its stated ground from the opening audience, but the Master of Mirrors asks whether the earlier statement was false. The truthful answer is almost always no. The court then hears that the house has changed its policy, not discovered a new fact.

Ask a house to name one condition sufficient for a declaration. It may refuse. It may not answer with a condition it knows would still be insufficient.

### The Floor of Satisfaction

Any recognized participant may ask another to acknowledge a public act, accept a correction, or state that an old grievance will not govern the petition. The challenged party may answer, decline, or demand reciprocal language.

This is the natural place to settle Vaudren and Maelith. Several settlements are available.

- Othalan acknowledges that Vaudren's office was omitted from the equinox procession and that the omission diminished its public precedence. He need not confess malicious intent.
- Vaudren accepts a neutral ruling that precedence remains unresolved and agrees not to treat Maelith sponsorship as authorship.
- Both houses appoint champions to a formal contest whose result determines ceremonial order but not political merit.
- The petitioners create a neutral public office for Vaudren within the undertaking, such as Keeper of the Returning Dawn, and Maelith states that the office is not subordinate to its surety role.

A private apology offered elsewhere does not satisfy Vaudren unless the public record changes. A public formula that says nothing meaningful does not survive direct questions beneath the mirrors.

### The Tables of Subscription

Five long tables receive associated instruments: recognition, surety, command, finance, and border defense. A covenant placed on a table is read aloud in summary. Its complete text remains available for inspection. Houses may subscribe, guarantee, witness, or merely acknowledge receipt.

This procedure can create a coordinated package without forcing every bargain into the central petition.

- Ilyrion's Equal Dawn compact can recognize Orison as a treaty partner.
- Maelith can accept named personal guarantors or a reciprocal bond instead of detention.
- Orelle can divide liability among several banners with capped shares.
- Serathiel and Serevin can allocate expedition command, eastern command, reserves, and ward funding.
- Vaudren can receive neutral credit without making the rescue its private triumph.

The Chamberlain warns that public association does not cure contradiction. A side covenant promising mortal authority cannot coexist with a petition granting exclusive royal discretion over every use of the Sunseed. The Masque can expose such conflicts; only the Petition Chamber can resolve them.

### The Declaration of Common Purpose

A coalition approaching seven seals may state one undertaking before the assembled court. The declaration should be broad enough to include the coalition and narrow enough that its members mean the same event.

Examples include:

- restore natural day to Orison while preserving Aurelune's defensibility;
- destroy the Pall without placing Orison under foreign civil rule;
- conduct a joint emergency custody of the Sunseed and return it upon restoration of the sky; or
- preserve both realms against a permanent Shadowfell expansion.

Theron Eiral asks each participating house whether the declaration describes the undertaking it intends to support. A house may answer yes while preserving a separate reason. A qualification enters the public coalition whether invited or not.

Eiral does not require affection. It requires a statement under which later disputes can be adjudicated without pretending the houses joined different ventures.

## The king's open alternatives

Caelir ordered a six-day Crown expedition studied on the first morning. By the Masque, the coalition's public work has supplied what his officers lacked: a passage schedule, a command scheme, a priced border replacement, and a return protocol. He cannot appropriate private promises, but he can assemble public plans into a lawful rival instrument. He offers four alternatives before the oath-mirrors.

### Evacuation without the Sunseed

Royal gates, healers, and roads can remove perhaps one-third of Orison's people. Caelir calls this an immediate plan to save lives. He does not call it a plan to preserve Orison.

This offer appeals to Ilyrion's compassion, Orelle's preference for bounded exposure, and Thalan's resistance to compelled relic custody. Accepting the gates need not end the petition unless the petitioners or a house say that it does.

### A royal expedition

Caelir offers to send the Sunseed under exclusive Crown custody and royal command for six days. The expedition will attempt to break the Pall and will retreat when the term ends or the Dawnward reaches a named threshold.

This can satisfy Serathiel, Maelith, and Serevin while defeating joint custody, Orison's co-authority, and a thirteen-day operational plan. Caelir truthfully describes it as an offer to send the Sunseed. He does not describe it as the undertaking Orison requested.

### Lesser relics

The Crown can provide artifacts sufficient to preserve districts, open evacuation corridors, or prevent the Pall from advancing for a short time. None can restore the sky. Caelir states exactly what each can do.

### Voluntary joinder later

If the coalition becomes broad, protects the Dawnward, and leaves the Crown a meaningful role, Caelir will consider joining an instrument he presently opposes. He does not promise to join. He promises to consider the exact final text rather than refuse it solely because the Concord compelled its drafting.

The king's strategy is not to expose secret divisions. It is to offer each house enough of its public purpose that withdrawing from joint custody can be defended honestly.

## Truthful pressure

The Masque rewards exact questions. Useful questions include:

- "When you say you support saving Orison, do you include preservation of the city and restoration of its sky?"
- "Is this condition sufficient for your declaration, or merely necessary?"
- "Will your declaration remain if the named side covenant is adopted unchanged?"
- "Does your support depend on who receives public credit, or only on the credit article itself?"
- "Which authority in the royal expedition may Orison lawfully refuse?"
- "Do you intend your border pledge to survive if another house commands the expedition?"

A courtier may decline to answer. A refusal beneath an oath-mirror is itself a public political act. Questions delimit commitments the petitioners may later draft; repeated traps merely teach the court to narrow its answers.

## Ways to use the mirrors

### Stage a broad concord

Bring eight or more houses into the Declaration of Common Purpose, adopts neutral credit, and places the security and recognition bargains on separate tables. This strengthens Eiral and makes royal joinder plausible. It also forces the coalition's contradictions into view early.

### Harden a seven-seal bloc

Ask only its intended seven to declare sufficient conditions and defend them publicly. This can produce a disciplined majority. Excluded houses and the king then know exactly which member to peel away.

### Build a reformist public narrative

Center Orison's equality, Aven Tal's claim, and mortal co-custody. The Masque can make those terms politically difficult to strip from the draft. Maelith, Thalan, and the Crown will insist that emergency recognition not silently become general constitutional law.

### Build a security compact

Publicly funds wards, deploys reserves, defines command, and accepts objective recall. Serathiel, Serevin, Orelle, and Thalan become easier to hold. Ilyrion and Orison may find themselves joining an undertaking whose military structure was settled without them.

### Preserve deliberate ambiguity

Decline a common declaration and keep several bargains private until drafting. This prevents premature rupture and forfeits the Masque's strongest protection against later reinterpretation.

## What may remain at dawn

### Conditions become public property

The coalition leaves with a witnessed common purpose, two or more adopted associated instruments, a clear seasonal count, and at least seven houses that have publicly named sufficient conditions for support. The king's alternatives remain available but no longer appear to satisfy the same undertaking.

### A declaration too expensive to soften

- Vaudren receives satisfaction in a form Maelith treats as a lasting loss of precedence.
- Maelith accepts a personal bond only after several party members or allied rulers become answerable guarantors.
- Orelle's consortium spreads liability across houses that demand later influence over the expedition.
- Serevin declares on the strength of deployments that constrain Serathiel's preferred command.
- Eiral accepts the common purpose only after partisan language and sole authorship are removed.
- The petitioners accept royal evacuation while continuing the petition; lives are saved, and Caelir gains public credit for the only aid already underway.

### The king supplies the only coherent story

Treat declarations as disguised seals and accuse withdrawing houses of breaking the oath. The Master of Mirrors states that no false assertion has occurred. Several houses narrow their language, the king's alternatives become the clearest public plan, and the coalition enters drafting with fewer reliable commitments than it had in private.

## What the mirrors keep

- each house's exact declared ground and sufficient conditions;
- the seasonal composition visible in the procession;
- every associated instrument adopted, subscribed, or merely acknowledged;
- the public settlement, if any, between Vaudren and Maelith;
- whether the coalition stated a common purpose and which qualifications entered it;
- which royal alternatives particular houses described as acceptable; and
- any public promise of troops, funds, wards, personal surety, or mortal authority.

## Returning before dawn

The oath-mirrors preserve declarations only until dawn, but certified transcripts remain in the Amber Quill Archive. The Masque itself is not repeated. Later public corrections occur beneath the Argent Canopy or during the Crown Conclave, where the earlier words are read beside the correction.
