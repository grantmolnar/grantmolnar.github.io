# The Argent Canopy

**Role:** necessary, start  
**Tags:** court, opening, procedure

Caelir's refusal leaves lawful nouns open; beneath the listening Canopy, Orison must name the standing, purpose, and calendar around which banners can gather.

## Opening View

Mara lays Orison's black-frosted stone beneath the throne. The Sunseed warms it; a market-stall shadow opens across the marble, sharp as noon. Ten banners watch. Four seasonal aisles stand empty. Theron has left seven seal lines beside a calendar ending at moonrise. Caelir waits until the frost is water. "I refuse this undertaking," he says. He does not refuse the rescue.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

The Argent Canopy hangs above the throne hall: a living crown of silver leaves, each turned toward whoever presently holds the right to speak. Beneath King Caelir III's throne, the Sunseed burns inside a root of clear glass. Its light climbs through the floor and lays the same mild dawn across every face, kind and cruel alike.

Orison's emergency delegation is received on the first morning after the Pall completes its outer ring around the city. The Shadowfell host that kindled it has been driven away; the siege-work has continued without its makers. Mara Venn has brought a paving stone from Orison's noon market. Beneath the Sunseed it warms, sheds black frost, and casts the sharp shadow it cast before the sky failed. Aurelune's Dawnward keepers have repeated the test. Caelir accepts that the relic can restore Orison's day.

Seven court days remain before the Pall closes the Lantern Road and begins feeding itself. The Crown Passage can still carry the Sunseed to Orison's western gate before the last open road to the Pall's center closes at moonrise. The loan term begins when the relic is uprooted; the road deadline determines whether the undertaking can begin at all.

Caelir also knows that uprooting the Sunseed will thin the Dawnward, disturb the seasonal roads, and teach every neighboring realm that sufficient need may put a royal trust to a vote. He refuses with a sentence polished for the record:

> I will not place the Sunseed beyond the custody of my Crown for the undertaking you have proposed.

The sentence leaves travel, joint custody, and a different undertaking open. Half the court hears the doors.

Lord Chamberlain Theron Eiral reads the Concord of Open Hands. Seven Great Banners may compel joint custody of a royal relic for one named undertaking. Spring, Summer, Autumn, and Winter must all stand among them. The petition must preserve a royal interest and state custody, command, term, liability, recall, and return. Theron must certify a valid instrument and must refuse an inconsistent one.

No one lowers their voice. As each recognized speaker answers, a band of leaves turns toward that voice and remains there until the court accepts a different relation among Crown, banner, relic, and city. The hall begins the week already shaped by incompatible truths.

## The voices beneath the Canopy

The first positions arrive like blades laid on the table.

- **Caelir:** “I can save those whom my roads can carry. I will not promise a city where I can promise lives.”
- **Theron Eiral, in office:** “Seven, with every season. The number and the balance are separate questions.”
- **Saelira Ilyrion:** “Do not rescue a people into ownership.”
- **Othalan Maelith:** “Name the person who will answer when the city cannot.”
- **Ysandre Serathiel:** “Compassion has given us a destination. It has not given us command.”
- **Avar Vaudren:** “Before we save Orison, perhaps someone will decide who is permitted to be seen saving it.”
- **Mirelle Orelle:** “Whose village is paying for the generosity presently admired in this hall?”
- **Pelion Namaris:** “Which sentence should a hostile court quote from us in a hundred years?”
- **Eirath Thalan:** “Need may be urgent without being constitutional.”
- **Caldus Serevin:** “What stands east when the Sunseed does not?”
- **Mirath Avarre:** “A banner cannot be inherited by wishing blood irrelevant.”
- **Aven Tal:** “Nor by pretending an adoption was a household decoration.”

Every sentence is defensible. None gives away the bargain.

## What the first audience establishes

The campaign's machinery is public. Before the audience ends, plain questions establish:

- seven seals are required, and the four-season requirement is independent of the count;
- Theron's duty to certify is not House Eiral's decision to seal;
- the Lantern Road compact guarantees Orison an emergency audience and a usable Crown Passage, not treaty equality;
- the Sunseed must enter the passage before moonrise after Court Day Seven, while the custody term begins only when the relic is uprooted;
- declarations bind reputation and witnesses, not the final vote;
- the Ashen Bough can be seated only if its hearing enters the calendar now;
- Caelir may file a narrower royal instrument or join a certified Concord later;
- Crown title can survive joint possession and use; and
- house bargains may sit in indexed side covenants, provided they do not contradict the central petition.

The doors are public; preference hides in the nouns.

## Choices made before leaving the hall

### Standing

Orison's sealed emergency mandate and Lantern Road compact secure recognized-petitioner status, not treaty equality. Choose the entry: agents of an independent polity, royal guests seeking relief, or joint petitioners with an Eladrin sponsor.

- **Sovereign-petitioner standing** strengthens Ilyrion and Aven Tal, but makes Maelith's preferred wardship settlement harder to present as benign. It gives Orison a voice in this proceeding without deciding the broader status Ilyrion wants.
- **Royal-guest standing** gives the petitioners protection and access, but allows Caelir to frame every concession as generosity to dependents.
- **Joint sponsorship** accelerates access to one house and lets that house claim early authorship.

Theron permits all three. He dips the pen after the petitioners choose the noun.

### Public purpose

Give the court one sentence describing the work. Saving Orison, restoring natural day, preserving the seasonal cycle, resisting Shadowfell expansion, and honoring an alliance lead to different bargains. Every house will quote this sentence when the draft asks it to accept an inconvenient term.

### The royal offer

Caelir opens evacuation gates immediately and orders his officers to prepare a six-day Crown expedition as a fallback. That expedition cannot become executable until the court has costed a substitute Dawnward, opened the passages, and settled command; the same public work that strengthens the petition may also strengthen the king's rival instrument. Accepting the gates does not waive the Sunseed petition unless Orison's petitioners say it does. Refusing them preserves bargaining clarity but costs mortal lives if the court fails. Accepting while continuing the petition is possible, though the king will truthfully describe Orison's petitioners as having accepted his plan.

### Calendar

Request an accelerated hearing calendar, public appointments, private consultations, or an early drafting session. The Chamberlain grants time according to constitutional priority rather than courtesy. A formal Ashen Bough hearing must be requested now or it cannot be concluded before the Conclave without displacing another public proceeding.

## Opening strategies

### Announce a broad concord

Seek eight or more seals, preserve a royal role, and frame the rescue as a shared defense of natural order. This attracts Eiral and Thalan, but gives reformers and martial houses less partisan credit.

### Build a minimum coalition quickly

Approach the easiest seven banners and plan to settle inconsistencies in drafting. This conserves time and invites the king to attack the coalition at its weakest seams.

### Lead with Orison's equality

Make political recognition part of the rescue from the beginning. Ilyrion and Aven become natural allies. Maelith, Orelle, and Thalan require careful separation between sovereign status and enforceable security.

### Lead with Aurelune's security

Begin by funding wards, reserves, and return guarantees. Serathiel, Serevin, Orelle, and Thalan become more accessible. Orison may be rescued under terms that turn it into the object of an Eladrin military undertaking.

### Accept the king's frame, then narrow it

Treat exclusive royal command as a starting point and bargain for joint custody, purpose-limited vetoes, or a mortal co-custodian. This can produce a voluntary royal joinder, but it makes a cleanly independent petition harder.

## Where the first audience can end

### The court opens cleanly

The petitioners gain recognized standing, a usable calendar, an unambiguous statement of the Concord, and at least three houses willing to receive immediate proposals. The royal evacuation offer remains available without being treated as settlement.

### Access purchased with position

- The petitioners gain access by accepting one house as sponsor; that house claims precedence over the coalition.
- The petitioners secure an accelerated calendar by making all appointments public; later compromises become harder to describe differently to different houses.
- The petitioners accept evacuation gates; lives are saved, but Caelir gains a truthful public claim that his plan is already in operation.

### The king leaves with the only public plan

Refuse to state standing or purpose and spend the audience contesting the king's motives. The court still opens. The petitioners leave with fewer appointments, while Caelir's evacuation instrument becomes the only plan in the hall that already has roads, officers, and a timetable.

## Enter in the public record

- the noun used for Orison's standing;
- the public purpose announced beneath the Canopy;
- whether evacuation gates were accepted;
- whether the Ashen Bough hearing was calendared;
- which house, if any, became the first sponsor; and
- whether the petitioners publicly committed to a broad concord or a seven-seal victory.

## Returning beneath the Canopy

The Canopy remains available for formal declarations, royal counteroffers, and changes to the public calendar. Returning here is never neutral. A statement made beneath the silver leaves is heard as a bid to define the whole court's business.
