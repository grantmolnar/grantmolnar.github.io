# The Hall of First Frost

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** winter, banner, constitution

Thalan asks what necessity permits today without becoming a standing license against the Crown tomorrow.

## Opening View

Ice gathers beneath Eirath Thalan's words and makes an old compelled loan visible underfoot: late return, fallen keep, unpaid taxes, a later petition refused. The lines touch, separate, and touch again as she names them. Fresh frost leaves spaces for substitute wards, an outside date, measured failure. Eirath looks down through three generations. "Orison may die. Tell me what that permits—and what it does not."

## Encounter Material

## Situation

The Hall of First Frost is paved in clear ice that thickens whenever a constitutional claim is spoken. Each argument becomes another layer beneath the floor. Older claims remain visible, distorted by everything said above them and never quite gone.

Duchess Eirath Thalan waits over the record of every compelled royal loan. She accepts the Pall, the measurements, and Orison's peril. She disputes the move from catastrophe to constitutional necessity.

Eirath recites the Briar Compulsion: a late return, a fallen keep, taxes refused, an ambitious later citation, a king who died unreconciled with his court. Every event belongs in the history. She begins without sorting cause from coincidence or opportunism. The oath does not sort it for her.

A direct question makes her do so:

> Which of those consequences do you claim were caused by the compelled custody rather than by the war already underway?

The keep might have fallen regardless. The tax refusal was opportunistic. The later citation failed. Eirath concedes each point and keeps the one she means: a lawful victory can still leave a wound between Crown and court, and wounds become precedent when no one names them.

## Thalan's constitutional case

House Thalan asks Orison's petitioners to satisfy four propositions.

### The necessity belongs partly to Aurelune

Sympathy for a foreign city is not enough. The petition must explain why the Pall threatens the natural cycle, Aurelune's treaties, or the eastern balance against the Shadowfell. Serevin's border assessment and Ilyrion's alliance claim can both support this finding for different reasons.

### The instrument remains exceptional

The Sunseed, the Pall, Orison, and the maximum term must be named. The court should not create a general doctrine that seven banners may seize any useful Crown relic whenever a foreign emergency is grave.

### The Crown retains a meaningful office

Title alone is insufficient if every royal custodian is ceremonial. Eirath accepts royal co-custody, authority to prevent damage to the Sunseed, participation in the recall process, and the right to state a dissenting constitutional rationale in the final instrument.

### Victory is not written as humiliation

The petition may compel Caelir. It need not describe him as faithless, defeated, or unworthy of trust. Thalan treats language as institutional action. A preamble celebrating the court's triumph can lose her seal even when the operative clauses are cautious.

## Routes to Thalan's seal

### Narrow necessity

Adopt a recital that the Pall threatens Orison, the cycle of seasons, and Aurelune's eastern security; limits custody to thirteen days or completion; preserves Crown title and co-custody; and includes a relic-specific sunset. This is Thalan's direct route.

### Voluntary royal joinder

Assemble seven seals on acceptable terms and offers Caelir a dignified way to join the petition. If he joins, Eirath can tolerate broader recognition or command provisions because the Crown has consented to them rather than suffered them all by compulsion. The king need not endorse the court's reasoning.

### Emergency action followed by full review

The petition remains narrow, while a scheduled constitutional convention later addresses Orison's status, adopted heirs, and the future scope of the Concord. Ilyrion and Aven receive a real forum rather than an unenforceable promise. Thalan refuses to decide those matters through a seven-day crisis.

### A more cautious petition

A six-day term, stronger royal control, or a restrictive recall article can secure Thalan quickly. Each may make the rescue less usable. Eirath does not treat operational failure as a constitutional virtue, but she will not conceal the tradeoff.

## The necessity record

Build the record in the hall. Eirath asks each factual proposition to be stated without rhetorical surplus.

- At moonrise after Court Day Seven, the Pall closes the Lantern Road and becomes self-sustaining.
- The noon-market stone and Dawnward test show that the Sunseed can restore natural day to Orison.
- Removing it weakens but does not extinguish the Dawnward.
- The Gloam Host can observe that weakness.
- Substitute wards and reserves can reduce the risk.
- The Crown's evacuation plan preserves some people and not the city.
- No other known relic can accomplish the same undertaking within the time available.

The last proposition is not a hidden discovery. Caelir and every house accept it. The dispute concerns the legal and moral conclusion drawn from it.

## Recall and emergency power

Eirath opposes unilateral recall by Serevin or the Crown. She will accept a defined breach trigger confirmed by multiple custodians. She also accepts immediate suspension of a use that would visibly damage the Sunseed, provided suspension cannot be converted into a change of undertaking.

This compromise helps Serevin, Namaris, and Eiral. It frustrates any house seeking complete discretion.

## Seasonal balance

The Hall preserves the oldest surviving Concord text. It states the four-season requirement separately from the seven-seal count. Eirath will not entertain an argument that urgency waives the distinction. The Ashen Bough and Eiral may increase the total and cannot replace a missing seasonal seal.

## Claims that need owners

### Choose the theory of necessity

Ground necessity in alliance, natural order, common defense, or some combination. A broad theory attracts Ilyrion and Eiral. A narrow military theory attracts Serevin and Serathiel. The chosen theory will be quoted at the Conclave.

### Preserve or minimize royal authority

Offer royal title, co-custody, damage prevention, recall participation, and public framing separately. Decide which powers are operative and which are courteous.

### Separate reform from rescue

Place recognition and inheritance reform in side covenants or a scheduled later convention. Doing so makes Thalan easier and asks reformers to trust a future proceeding.

### Invite royal joinder

Offering Caelir a path into the final text may strengthen the outcome. It also gives him leverage to demand revisions after the coalition has assembled seven seals.

## How First Frost may join the season call

### Necessity without a general license

Thalan accepts a stated theory of necessity, the Crown retains a meaningful but bounded role, and the petition gains a sunset or review structure. Eirath becomes open to sealing rather than merely ceasing opposition.

### Restraint that limits the rescue

- Recognition reform moves to a later convention sponsored now by named banners.
- The king receives a purpose-limited veto over damage and redirection.
- The petitioners promise not to characterize the result as a defeat for the Crown, limiting Vaudren's appetite for public triumph.
- The term shortens unless the security package is fully funded.

### Compassion without a constitutional finding

Argue only that saving Orison is morally right and treats constitutional caution as cowardice. Thalan remains opposed, supplies the king with a disciplined public argument, and makes any Winter coalition depend on Serevin alone.

## Freeze into the record

- the petition's theory of necessity;
- the scope of the relic-specific sunset;
- the Crown's retained powers;
- whether reform is inside the emergency instrument or calendared later;
- whether Caelir has been offered voluntary joinder; and
- the exact objective standard for recall or emergency suspension.

## On a return

Thalan revisits her support whenever the undertaking expands, the term lengthens, or the final rhetoric changes. She is easier to win than to keep if the coalition treats cautious language as disposable once her Winter seal is secured.
