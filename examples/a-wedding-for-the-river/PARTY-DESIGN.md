# Party Design: The Four Open Hands

The demonstrated party consists of four free witnesses appointed by the House of Open Measure. None is kin to Tomas Vale, Neris Reed-in-Rain, or any principal household. None begins as the couple's confidant. Their legitimacy comes from useful competence, public accountability, and the fact that no local family can plausibly claim them.

The party is designed to make four different forms of overreach tempting. Each witness can help the wedding by using a professional method well; each can also turn that method into a substitute for the couple's judgment.

## Adel Berren

**Function:** Road notary, copyist, and examiner of contradictory terms.

**What Adel brings:** Adel has spent twelve years carrying contracts between villages that share roads but not weights, calendars, or inheritance customs. He can compare documents without assuming that identical words carry identical consequences. He carries paired wax tablets, colored thread for marking disputed clauses, a traveling seal press, blank witness leaves, chalk, and a small folding rule.

**Why the House chose him:** Mera Quill has used Adel twice when a bridge charter and a grazing compact contained honest but incompatible readings. He is not a member of the House and cannot certify alone. He is known for preserving minority statements instead of quietly regularizing them.

**Opening position:** The compact should be rewritten until Tomas and Neris can give the same plain-language account of every duty. Adel initially treats their different household descriptions as evidence that the work is unfinished.

**Private fear:** He fears signing a beautiful ambiguity that later becomes a weapon. He once copied a widow's right to remain “while the household endured”; the heirs later argued that her husband's death ended the household. Adel has never forgiven himself for recognizing the phrase and leaving it untested.

**Red line:** No witness may record acceptance that was only proposed, implied, or reported by an absent speaker.

**Temptation:** Adel may mistake one vocabulary for one agreement. He can pressure Tomas and Neris toward a single household label even when the operational duties are shared and both descriptions are honestly retained.

**How he argues:** He asks who may act, what event triggers the duty, what is excluded, how refusal is recorded, and which sentence survives an unfriendly reader ten years later.

**Table voice:** Patient, dry, and exact. “That is a generous intention. I am asking what someone is allowed to do on a wet Tuesday when you are both elsewhere.”

**Growth demanded:** Adel must learn that a stable record can preserve a real difference rather than eliminate it. His best ending signs one set of duties beside two household descriptions without treating the difference as a defect.

## Kest Larch

**Function:** Itinerant bridgewright, flood surveyor, and practical safety witness.

**What Kest brings:** Kest knows temporary platforms, water loads, accessible routes, warning signals, rope work, crowd movement, and the failure modes of structures assembled under ceremonial optimism. She carries sounding cord, pegs, a plumb bob, waxed canvas, two hand bells, a knife, spare rush lashings, and a notebook full of waterlines.

**Why the House chose her:** Kest repaired a Concord ferry landing after both banks blamed one another for a collapse. Her report assigned separate failures to rotten timber, an unmarked change in current, and a handoff nobody had authority to make. Rain-at-Noon knows the report but has never met her.

**Opening position:** The ceremony should be moved to the old wheel yard, where the ground, shelter, and evacuation route are easiest to control. Kest regards the flood meadow as a needless exposure until she understands what the shared wet-and-dry site must communicate.

**Private fear:** She fears preventable injury disguised as tradition. She also knows that a builder can seize control simply by declaring every disagreement a safety issue.

**Red line:** No person is placed on footing they cannot test, leave, or refuse. No boat, mat, bridge, or procession carries symbolic meaning that depends on concealed physical risk.

**Temptation:** Kest may decide the site before the principals have chosen what the site must show. She can solve the wrong ceremony perfectly.

**How she argues:** She asks where weight goes, who crosses, what changes in rain, how a person exits, which labor is actually available, and what remains valid after relocation.

**Table voice:** Blunt without contempt. “You may call it a threshold after it has a safe return. Before that, it is a boat with witnesses watching.”

**Growth demanded:** Kest must build for meanings chosen by others rather than replacing meaning with safety. Her best ending creates a movable six-function ceremony whose fallback does not silently alter household standing or departure.

## Sella Broth

**Function:** Caravan quartermaster, cook, and witness of particular hospitality.

**What Sella brings:** Sella has fed pilgrims, soldiers, mourners, debtors, children, and people who would not share a table but could share a kitchen. She can count labor, trace food sources, arrange lodging, identify who has been treated as a representative rather than a guest, and convert abundance into named obligations. She carries tally cords, spice packets, a tasting spoon, guest tokens, wax labels, and a ledger divided by person rather than household.

**Why the House chose her:** Pella Nine-Spoons requested an outside quartermaster after discovering that the preliminary feast list counted “river guests” as one dietary category. Sella is passing through Cresswater with a caravan whose next departure leaves after the joining light.

**Opening position:** Nearly every conflict will soften once people are fed early, seated well, and given useful work. Sella is correct often enough to become dangerous when a grievance cannot be cooked around.

**Private fear:** She fears a public gathering becoming cruel through small omissions nobody intended. Her former caravan master once called an injured drover ungrateful for missing a feast held up a stair he could not climb.

**Red line:** Attendance, eating, dancing, lodging, or accepting a gift may never be entered as assent to a household term.

**Temptation:** Sella may use comfort to hurry people past an unresolved obligation. She can mistake a successful feast for evidence that the underlying settlement is accepted.

**How she argues:** She asks the person's name, what they actually take, who provides it, who performs the labor, where they can sit, and what happens if they abstain.

**Table voice:** Warm, brisk, and merciless toward collective nouns. “Which river guest? The one who cannot leave running water, the one who cannot sleep near it, or the child who eats only toasted oats?”

**Growth demanded:** Sella must let some discomfort remain visible. Her best ending serves a generous feast while preserving named abstentions, separate grievance statements, and one deliberately empty response token.

## Oren Chalk

**Function:** Funeral singer, lay mediator, and examiner of public meaning.

**What Oren brings:** Oren travels between small shrines that cannot keep a resident musician. He can teach responsive speech, hear when one rhythm has become accompaniment, distinguish silence from refusal, and ask what an object or gesture will mean from the back row. He carries a narrow reed flute, a frame drum, mourning chalk, blank song leaves, and a cord marked in four-, six-, and twelve-pulse lengths.

**Why the House chose him:** Keeper Ansel Rook asked for a witness who understands that ceremony can tell a public lie even when every sentence is technically true. Oren has officiated funerals where two households gave separate accounts of the dead without forcing reconciliation.

**Opening position:** The two traditions should be woven into one memorable form whose beauty helps the households recognize themselves in the marriage. Oren is drawn to the joining measure before he knows whether Tomas and Neris want it to symbolize unity, alternation, or simple coordination.

**Private fear:** He fears a rite so procedural that it cannot carry grief, joy, or affection. He also fears that people will remember the quarrel and forget the couple.

**Red line:** Music, repetition, crowd response, and emotional momentum may never supply consent or complete a promise on someone's behalf.

**Temptation:** Oren may stage reconciliation that has not occurred. He can turn Agnes and Serein's separate truthful statements into a duet because it sounds better.

**How he argues:** He asks what the audience will think happened, who leads, where correction can interrupt, what silence means, and whether the plain paraphrase remains true after the poetry ends.

**Table voice:** Gentle, attentive, and unexpectedly firm about performance. “A harmony is not two voices if one can stop and nobody notices.”

**Growth demanded:** Oren must accept formal roughness where it protects truth. His best ending keeps the old flood statements separate, permits asymmetric names, and gives the joining measure a limited task: carrying the couple from two approaches to one listening place, not proving that every difference has vanished.

## Party tensions

### One record against two descriptions

Adel wants one stable household formula. Oren understands why the families need publicly legible language, but he is more willing to preserve parallel forms. The dispute turns when both witnesses recognize that the ceremony can read two descriptions followed by one shared operational schedule.

### Safety against represented meaning

Kest prefers the old wheel yard. Oren and Sella see why a mixed wet-and-dry site matters to guests and household recognition. The party must define the six functions before Kest can build a safe form that does not erase them.

### Comfort against obligation

Sella wants food available early and grievances bounded tightly. Adel insists that a comfortable interval cannot be recorded as settlement. The final feast succeeds only when both are right: guests are fed before public obligations are named, and the record states exactly which obligations remain open.

### Beauty against consent

Oren can make the ceremony feel complete before its terms are complete. Adel can make the terms complete while draining the ceremony of voice. Kest and Sella repeatedly force them back to observable acts, particular people, and the couple's corrections.

## Demonstration-route commitments

The canonical playthrough begins with six commitments:

1. Tomas and Neris remain the only principals who can accept, revise, postpone, or end the marriage.
2. A witness group may observe, draft, reserve resources, and carry proposals; it may not accept for absent principals.
3. The household-description disagreement may remain if shared duties are clear and both descriptions are recorded without hierarchy.
4. No gift, meal, response, dance, seat, or attendance is treated as consent to another matter.
5. The ceremony will preserve six observable functions and a safe fallback rather than require one perfect site or waterline.
6. The old flood may produce a new warning practice and separate true statements without requiring forgiveness, apology, or a joint performance.
