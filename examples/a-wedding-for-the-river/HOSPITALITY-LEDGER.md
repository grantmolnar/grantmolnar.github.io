# Hospitality Ledger: A Wedding for the River

This is the Session 3 operating aid for the Market of Seven Baskets. It converts broad claims about “the Vales,” “river guests,” “the elders,” or “both families” into named people, promised work, and visible limits.

Use it as a worksheet rather than a canonical settlement. Tomas, Neris, affected guests, hosts, cooks, gift givers, and grievance stewards retain the relevant decisions.

## The seven baskets

| Basket | Question | Complete when | Common false shortcut |
|---|---|---|---|
| Names | Who is actually invited or accompanying someone? | Every uncertain attendee has a named confirmer | Counting households as though both lists include the same people |
| Food | What will this guest eat or abstain from, and where did it come from? | Source, taker, cook, eater, and obligation are known | “River food,” “mortal food,” or anonymous ethical substitution |
| Labor | Who performs each task, when do they stop, and who replaces them? | Worker, shift, fallback, and limit are named | Assigning Tomas, Neris, the party, or “both families” to everything |
| Seating | What does each place allow a particular person to see, hear, reach, or avoid? | Functional seats are assigned before precedence seats | Treating honor as more important than hearing, footing, or mobility |
| Lodging | Where can this named guest safely and comfortably sleep? | Host, route, bedding, access, and night responsibility are accepted | Sorting all river guests to the willows and all mortals to the mill |
| Gifts | What is offered, why, and how does the receiver understand it? | Giver and receiver state compatible meaning or a chosen transformation | Assuming an object has one hidden universal symbolism |
| Grievances | What unfinished obligation may be carried into the feast, and how? | Scope, parties, steward, response, and later hearing are bounded | Public accusation, compulsory apology, or pretending silence is peace |

## Food and source plan

### Immediate trout problem

Twelve live trout from the protected spawning reach must reach the upper reed gate before noon. Record:

- who keeps the tank wet and aerated;
- who repairs or replaces the cracked cart wheel;
- who clears the market route;
- who verifies release at the permitted gate; and
- who enters the mistaken order and its cost in the ledger.

Saving the trout prevents waste and a closed-reach violation. Pella’s expense and Brin’s responsibility remain.

### Kitchen constraint

Pella can serve forty-two guests one hot course at once. Up to fifty can be fed through two seatings, a cold course, or additional cooks. Every ceremonial delay should identify the dish, worker, or seating it affects.

## Seating options

### Hinge table

Two long tables meet at Tomas and Neris. Agnes and Serein receive equal sight lines without being forced together. This is the easiest plan for hearing the ceremony and preserving different household groups along the arms.

### Open square

Four short tables surround the fountain. It has no head and makes grievance speakers visible. It costs more aisle space and requires careful accessible routes.

### Two first courses, one final table

The households begin in familiar groups, then join for gifts and sweets. This makes joining an explicit act but may be read as a divided feast if the transition is not clearly announced.

### Named mixed tables

Guests sit by actual duty, work, care, gift, or guardianship relationships. Do not alternate bodies merely to make the room look reconciled.

Record the functional reason for every contested seat. Honor never overrides hearing, footing, mobility, guardianship, stewardship, or service access.

## Declared-gift register

For each gift, record:

1. material and maker;
2. source or prior history;
3. intended honor;
4. receiver’s paraphrase;
5. accepted form: as made, transformed, private, deferred, or refused;
6. person authorized to alter it; and
7. public wording, if any.

| Gift | Intended honor | Conflicting reading | Available status or transformation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agnes’s veil | Lineage and remembrance | Concealed breath and mourning | Canopy, compact wrapping, shared threshold, alternate remembrance, refusal |
| Orren’s iron ring | Durable labor and kin recognition | Captivity without outlet | Open spiral, paired clasps, rush-bound gap, threshold fitting, refusal |
| Mara’s handstone | Steadiness and provision | Fixed course | Water-bowl base, shared table object, paired moving object, private gift |

No surprise gift enters the public ceremony unless both Tomas and Neris consent to the surprise itself.

## Bounded grievance procedure

### Submission

- Rain-at-Noon and Mera receive entries by noon.
- Each entry names speaker, respondent, duty, requested next act, steward, and later hearing date if needed.
- Character attacks, claims over the couple, surprise testimony, and complex historical disputes are excluded.
- The old flood remains a separate hearing unless Agnes and Serein both request one narrow public obligation.

### Public interval

1. Arrival cups and road bread are already available.
2. Pella sets twelve minutes and no more than four entries.
3. Rain states that the rite concerns unfinished obligation, not a verdict on character.
4. The speaker names the duty and requested act in one breath.
5. The respondent may acknowledge, state discharge, contest while accepting a hearing, or decline public reply while accepting private stewardship.
6. The steward places the marked stone in water. Washing carries the obligation honestly into the meal; it does not resolve it.
7. At time, remaining entries stay in the ledger and the first shared course begins.

### Abstention

A blank reed token marks: **present, not participating in this act**.

It may apply to:

- grievance interval;
- a specific course;
- toast;
- gift;
- dance; or
- other named custom.

It does not imply rejection of the couple or absence from the wedding.

## Recoverable-cost sheet

If postponement is discussed, Pella separates:

- **recoverable:** preserved food, returned ingredients, reusable platforms, stored gifts;
- **redistributable:** cooked food, flowers, temporary lodging, hired labor already present;
- **committed:** deposits, travel, lost work shifts, market closures;
- **irrecoverable:** spoiled food, damaged goods, missed seasonal light, public disappointment.

Postponement costs money, labor, travel, and a year’s distinctive joining light. It does not threaten the town or river with supernatural punishment.

## Minimum Session 3 result

The market remains successful if it produces:

- a named guest register rather than household totals;
- an honestly sourced menu and a live plan for the protected trout;
- assigned labor that does not consume the couple;
- functional seating and lodging;
- a declared status for each major gift;
- a bounded grievance interval and abstention mechanism; and
- a recoverable-cost account suitable for a real postponement decision.
