# Ceremony Ledger: A Wedding for the River

This is the Session 4 operating aid for the Shrine of the Open Door, Reedwright’s Yard, and Bridge of Three Tunes. Use it to convert selected terms into public acts that can be heard, seen, crossed, revised, and moved when weather changes.

The ceremony is ready when the six functions remain legible without depending on any single inherited object, exact waterline, or compulsory response.

## Six-function worksheet

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| Household standing |  |  | Boat, keys, threshold, spoken duties, or another chosen form | Entry, retained standing, reciprocal duties, and limits are stated | Act implies transfer, permission, or duty not in the compact |
| Names |  |  | Announcement and registry entry | Current answered names match the chosen record treatment | Replacement or invented name appears without consent |
| Exchange |  |  | Declared object or act | Giver states intention; receiver states acceptance and limits | Surprise, attached duty, or changed meaning is not understood |
| Promises |  |  | Paired vows, image plus plain speech, separate duty vows, or compact affirmation | Both can paraphrase endurance, revision, and exclusions | Poetry conceals consequence or one person cannot repeat the duty |
| Departure |  |  | Boat exit, bridge crossing, ford crossing, or shared threshold | Couple leaves the ceremony together by present choice | Route divides them or implies a residence decision they did not make |

Do not fill a box merely because a family has prepared the object. “No public exchange” and “one household description remains unresolved” are valid entries.

## Vow design

### Distinguish four kinds of duration

| Duration type | Question | Example use |
|---|---|---|
| Continuing | What remains owed without a new ceremony? | Mutual care, truthful warning, respect for retained standing |
| Renewed | What is reviewed after a season, year, changed course, or changed work? | Work schedules, water-correspondent office, extended absence arrangements |
| Interrupted | What pauses during danger, incapacity, travel, or conflicting stewardship? | Personal warning delivery, household labor, residence expectations |
| Released | What ends through discharge, replacement, separation, or mutual revision? | A particular office, exclusive task, temporary guardianship, marriage itself |

### Vow structures

**Plain paired vows**

- Best for clarity and public hearing.
- Each partner names chosen marriage, recognized dwellings or households, principal duties, retained limits, and right to revise.
- Risk: legal density if too many clauses are spoken.

**Image followed by plain speech**

- Preserves poetic language such as wheel, water, current, shelter, or open door.
- The speaker immediately states what the image requires in ordinary life.
- Risk: guests remember the image and forget the limiting explanation.

**Separate duty vows**

- Places care, adoption, residence, warning, and endurance at different ceremonial acts.
- Best when objects and family offices already carry distinct functions.
- Risk: length and repeated consent fatigue.

**Compact affirmation**

- Mera reads a concise record summary; each partner affirms it and adds one personal promise.
- Best for rain, limited hearing endurance, or a ceremony with many physical acts.
- Risk: the ceremony may feel administrative unless personal roles and objects remain present.

### Plain-speech test

For every duty-bearing line, ask both partners:

1. What does this require next week?
2. What happens if one partner lives elsewhere for a season?
3. What happens during illness or incapacity?
4. What event requires a new agreement?
5. What does this line explicitly not transfer or decide?
6. Can the marriage end without declaring that the original promise was a lie?

A line may remain poetic if both partners give compatible answers and the record preserves the operational terms.

## Threshold boat checklist

- [ ] Boat lies perpendicular to the bank and has no departure route downriver.
- [ ] Both landings are stable and visible to the same witnesses.
- [ ] Water is shallow enough for the rehearsed users.
- [ ] Handholds and a dry bypass exist.
- [ ] Tomas enters under his own power after consent is established.
- [ ] Duties and reciprocal river obligations are spoken exactly as recorded.
- [ ] Tomas can correct or refuse a changed duty.
- [ ] Neris enters only if the couple chooses a paired threshold.
- [ ] Both leave toward the chosen shared departure route.
- [ ] A named steward controls the boat and does not pull either person into it.

The boat may be omitted. Its function is entry, receipt, reciprocity, and exit—not proof of courage or surrender.

## Minimum Session 4 result

Before the full rehearsal, the GM should be able to answer:

1. What exact public acts satisfy the six functions?
2. Which names are spoken and how are later bynames recorded?
3. What vow structure is chosen, and what do its duties mean in ordinary life?
4. Which objects are public, transformed, private, deferred, or refused?
5. Which site is primary, which is fallback, and what triggers relocation?
6. Where do Agnes, Deep-Reed, Noll, Lilt, Aunt Sal, the couple, musicians, and witnesses stand or move?
7. How does water enter and leave the ceremony site?
8. What does the threshold boat do, if used, and how does the couple leave it?
9. Who leads each musical section, where do the rhythms join, and where is silence mandatory?
10. What remains unresolved, and what exact event requires a pause?

If these questions have honest answers, the ceremony is ready to be stressed by rain, old injuries, late work, and actual rehearsal. It does not need to look perfectly unified.
