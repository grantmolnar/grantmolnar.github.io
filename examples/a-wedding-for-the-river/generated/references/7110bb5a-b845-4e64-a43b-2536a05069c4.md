# Vale Mill and Hearth

## Summary

A working Cresswater mill, multigenerational Vale home, and leased water operation where every wedding promise must survive workers, tenants, debt, flood signals, and an ordinary week.

## Reference Material

## Wheel, hearth, and lease

Vale Mill and Hearth is a workplace before it is a family emblem. The wheelhouse shakes flour from the rafters; the bake room begins before dawn; six workers and four tenant households order their weeks around the sluice, stones, deliveries, and account books. The Vales own buildings and machinery, while the right to divert Cresswater belongs to a renewable Concord lease with separate signatures and remedies.

## The eastern rooms

The eastern rooms are Tomas and Neris’s intended ordinary dwelling, with a common room, yard door, runnel steps, and space for current ledgers and river tools. Full domestic welcome does not transfer mill ownership, wages, debt, succession, or the water lease. The willow house remains another recognized dwelling, and no count of nights determines loyalty or abandonment.

## Where promises become shifts

At the mill, promises become keys, shifts, warning thresholds, closure authority, care instructions, and somebody awake beside the wheel. Property, operation, household authority, and water compliance remain distinct. The place remembers the old flood in high-water cuts and a jammed lower gate without settling blame. A workable compact must protect people before machinery and continue when Tomas or Neris is absent.
