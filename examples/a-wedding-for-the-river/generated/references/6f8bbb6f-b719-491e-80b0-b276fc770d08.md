# The House of Open Measure

## Summary

The Concord of Banks witness office that records where unlike households touch, preserving consent, authority, refusal, limits, and endings without reconciling the households or deciding for them.

## Reference Material

## Two doors, one record

The House of Open Measure is the Concord of Banks office for agreements where mortal settlements meet old households of river, wood, road, and hill. Its local seat has two doors and no privileged entrance. The House does not make customs interchangeable. It records the exact places where they touch and writes for the unfriendly absent reader who might otherwise enlarge a promise.

## What survives the absent reader

A sound folio separates stable identity, current answered names, parentage, dwellings, household standing, property, labor, care, warnings, gifts, abstentions, open clauses, and the authority behind each assent. Free witnesses observe or verify the acts; a resident recorder preserves filings and drafts the resulting forms. Refusal, postponement, asymmetry, and a named later hearing are recordable outcomes rather than procedural failures.

## The seal and its edge

The House may receive paired readings, commission or recognize independent witnesses, preserve revisions, and certify only what qualified witnesses observed. Its seal opens doors and earns answers. It confers no judgment over love, household, mill, river, marriage, or grief. A recorder who accepted the filings and drafts the folio cannot also become the independent witness who certifies it.
