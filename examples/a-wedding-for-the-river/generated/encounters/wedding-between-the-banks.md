# The Wedding Between the Banks

**Role:** necessary, end  
**Tags:** finale, ceremony, consent, aftermath

At joining light, Tomas and Neris choose, revise, postpone, or refuse each public act. The witnesses sign only the marriage, boundaries, abstentions, and open lines they saw.

## Opening View

By the time the joining light touches the wheel, the wedding has already refused several of its own beautiful ideas. Gold runs from the wet spokes toward the willow bank, over dry planks, reed mats, and grass darkening at the edges. The chosen gifts wait uncovered. Beyond the red cord, rejected and unfinished things remain visible. Mera opens the folio to blank lines. Tomas and Neris stand apart, and no one—not kin, musician, witness, or river—gives either of them the first word.

## Encounter Material

## Situation
The final encounter is the wedding, not the GM's preferred settlement. Build it from what Tomas and Neris accepted: site and fallback, household descriptions, names, adoption duties, gifts, guest positions, grievance limits, music, food sources, open clauses, and visible abstentions. Do not restore a discarded custom because the last scene looks plain without it.

Once each year, the low sun passes through the alder gap and strikes the wet mill wheel. Gold crosses the yard toward the willow bank. Both households have built the day around that light. It is not a spell. If it passes, the feast can be eaten, the folio sealed, and another wedding chosen. A lost year is a cost, not a voice.

Begin with empty signature lines and the last private repetition. Then play the six acts one by one. At each act, ask who speaks, what is offered, what may be corrected, who abstains, and what the witnesses can actually see.

## Before the guests are seated

Read the rehearsal record. Put the visible pressure board back in play with its final marks.

- **Water** may preserve the primary site, require modular changes, or trigger the announced fallback.
- **Work** determines which optional elements are complete. Remove decoration, extended procession, public gifts, and dance before removing access, consent, or clear duties.
- **Guests** arrive as particular people with confirmed choices. Their household does not answer for appetite, lodging, seating, response, or abstention.
- **Open obligations** remain corded in red until they are settled, omitted, or named as boundaries.

Do not invent new pressure. Use the consequences already earned. A wet meadow matters if the plan depended on it. A late iron fitting matters if the exchange still uses it. An abstention matters because a named person is absent from a named act, not because harmony lost a point.

### The last private repetition

Before public entry, a witness meets Tomas and Neris separately. Ask each:

1. Do you choose to marry today?
2. What household standing and duties do you understand yourself to accept?
3. What name or names will be spoken and recorded?
4. Which exchange, promises, and open clauses do you accept?
5. Has rehearsal, weather, family conduct, or changed wording altered any answer?
6. Do you want a private pause, revision, postponement, or end to the process?

Then bring them together for reciprocal paraphrase. Each explains the other's principal duties and the boundary the ceremony must not imply. A partner may correct the paraphrase. The witnesses may compare it with the folio. Nobody may treat public anticipation as an answer.

If either person does not choose the wedding, stop the ceremony. The House protects the decision without demanding an accusation or revealing private reasons. Guests may be disappointed, food may already be cooked, and the joining light may pass. Those are costs, not counterarguments.

## The ceremony in six functions

The order may vary, but each function needs an observable act, the couple's stated meaning, and an honest place in the record. Use the chosen full or shortened order. The sections below describe what to play, not mandatory wording.

### 1. Consent

Ansel calls for complete silence. Music stops. Gifts remain down. Household speakers do not stand behind the couple as though lending answers.

Ask Tomas and Neris separately in the public form they approved. Each answers audibly and in words an ordinary listener can paraphrase. If either changes wording, hesitates, requests privacy, or corrects the officiant, pause. Let the silence stand; do not cover it with music or reassuring laughter.

Possible consequences from prior play:

- a relative answers too soon and must be told that family assent is not consent;
- rain makes the first position inaudible and the party must move before repeating the question;
- the joining light arrives before the answer and passes during a pause;
- one partner accepts marriage but not a newly added household phrase; or
- a guest interprets silence as refusal although the couple agreed that the answer follows a breath-count.

Protect the silence, restate approved language, move the audience, or take the couple aside. Do not decide that love is obvious enough.

### 2. Household standing

Read the household description or descriptions exactly. If Tomas and Neris retained different descriptions—one household in two dwellings and two households joined by marriage—read both, followed by the shared operational terms. Do not force a single label when the duties are compatible and the difference is honestly preserved.

If Tomas accepts river adoption, Serein or another named speaker states the bounded duties. A second speaker states the river household's reciprocal duties. Tomas may correct any altered phrase before accepting. The public limit should name what adoption does **not** transfer: Vale kinship, mill property, unrestricted labor, custody, or future children.

Neris's mortal household standing must likewise be specific. Recognition at the eastern rooms need not confer surname, property, debt, mill succession, or permission-based access to the willow house. Mara, Agnes, or another accepted speaker may state the welcome; Neris states what she receives.

Possible outcomes include:

- both forms of standing are accepted;
- marriage proceeds while river adoption is deferred;
- Neris accepts domestic standing but declines a lineage phrase;
- Tomas accepts warning duties but not a broad kinship formula;
- one household abstains from recognition while the couple retains the chosen duties; or
- an unready clause forces postponement because the public act would imply more than either partner accepted.

### 3. Names

The announcer speaks only the forms authorized in rehearsal. Mera's folio separates stable identity, current answered name, parentage, byname history, dwellings, and household standing.

Neris may be announced as **Neris Reed-in-Rain**, child of Serein-at-Alderfall, also dwelling at Vale Mill, without becoming “Neris Vale.” Tomas remains **Tomas Vale** unless he later receives and accepts a river byname. A changing name is not an unstable identity, and asymmetry is not an unfinished marriage.

Guests may repeat, remain silent, or use an authorized short form. A congregational response cannot create a name the person did not choose. If an announcer misspeaks, stop and correct the record before proceeding. The correction is part of the ceremony's honesty, not an embarrassment to hide.

### 4. Exchange

Present only objects or acts whose intended honor and received meaning were declared. The giver says what the gift is for. The receiver accepts, transforms, defers, keeps private, or refuses.

Use earlier decisions:

- Agnes's veil may become canopy, compact wrapping, memorial backdrop, threshold cloth, or a private heirloom. It does not cover Neris unless Neris unexpectedly and freely chooses that use after understanding it.
- Orren's iron may be an open spiral, paired clasps, a rush-bound gap, threshold fitting, or a withheld gift. From the audience's angle, its opening should remain visible.
- Mara's handstone may support the water bowl or become an ordinary household object without promising immobility.
- Aunt Sal's cradle ribbon may be omitted or repurposed rather than imply a promise of children.
- Jory's salt box carries named labor, credit, and provision rather than serving as an anonymous substitute.
- The threshold boat, if used, establishes entry, reciprocal duty, correction, and exit. It does not carry Tomas away or test whether he will surrender himself to the current.

A receiver may refuse in public if that possibility was protected. The gift steward removes the object without turning refusal into accusation. A withheld gift may be painful and still more honest than an object made to lie.

### 5. Promises

Use the vow structure the couple selected. Poetry may frame the promises, but duty-bearing language is spoken in silence and survives plain paraphrase.

State continuing duties, renewed duties, interruption or incapacity provisions, correction procedures, and any conditions for release or later hearing. Keep personal promises distinct from enforceable household obligations. If the phrase “while this river runs” appears, the ordinary-language clause must explain endurance through change rather than fixed course or impossible permanence.

Mera reads every open clause at the place agreed. Examples include:

- household description remains dual while duties are shared;
- future-child standing is not decided;
- inheritance remains governed by existing deeds;
- one care signatory has not yet accepted;
- the old flood goes to a later hearing; or
- river adoption is deferred although the marriage proceeds.

An open clause is not a whispered defect. It tells guests what the ceremony does not settle.

### 6. Departure

Silence remains until Tomas and Neris choose the first shared step. Music may answer after the choice; it may not move them into it.

At the joining measure, Iven may reach the shared pulse first. Moss does not enter beneath him. He waits for the river phrase's own lead cue, and the ceremony waits too. That held entrance is visible evidence that two parts can meet without one becoming accompaniment, even under the pressure of a gathered crowd.

The departure should show the future they actually chose. They might:

- leave the threshold boat together over a shared bridge;
- take two approaches into one feast route;
- walk first to the common table and later to the eastern rooms;
- pause at both household markers before choosing the next step;
- leave by a neutral path toward the House of Open Measure to sign; or
- depart separately for a private interval before rejoining the feast, if that was their agreed form.

Do not let the route silently settle the one-household or two-household disagreement. A path ending at the mill may look like absorption into Vale property; a boat pointing downstream may look like removal from the mill. Use a route whose stated meaning matches the record.

If weather interrupts the departure, pause and use the fallback route. A muddy hem or awkward musical join can become part of the memory. Unsafe footing does not become romantic because the scene is nearly over.

## Pressure during the ceremony

Let pressure interrupt only where it reveals something precise.

### Rain and relocation

At Water 1, execute named chair and route changes. At Water 2, shorten movement or begin the announced relocation. At Water 3, pause between functions if possible and move to the fallback. Repeat any function whose witnesses lost sight or hearing; do not repeat a private choice merely for spectacle if it was already observed clearly.

The shortened order preserves all six functions. It may omit procession, dance, decoration, public grievance, extended exchanges, and many family responses. The ceremony is not less valid because the weather exposed what was optional.

After the first guests sit, a fresh mud line may cross the planned aisle. Dessa's relocation marks let the witnesses move one station at a time and ask the useful question: which accepted act depended on that exact patch of earth? Usually the answer is none. The route changes; the represented marriage does not.

### Delayed preparation

A late object or unfinished ornament does not delay the function unless no alternate act exists. Use the declared intention, a spoken exchange, or omission. A late handrail, inaccessible route, missing witness page, or unassigned consent pause is different: pause until the necessary support exists or change site and scope.

### Guest friction

Use visible abstention rather than surprise rebellion. Agnes may attend but withhold the veil. Serein may witness consent but abstain from Vale household language. Aunt Sal may refuse a river response while serving the meal. A guest may leave before grievance, decline a dance, or sit apart from an elder.

The couple may be hurt. The witnesses record participation accurately. They do not coerce applause or call attendance unanimous recognition.

### Unresolved obligations

A grievance that exceeds the bounded form moves to later hearing. A disputed duty is not repaired by reading it faster. If an open matter is outside the six functions, record and proceed. If it changes consent, household standing, promises, or another represented choice, return to the couple privately.

## The old flood at the wedding

Use only the rehearsal outcome.

- In a **joint warning practice**, Agnes rings the paired bell pattern and Serein calls the river threshold. The act honors Perrin by making the future handoff intelligible; it does not announce forgiveness.
- With **separate true statements**, each woman speaks her approved sentence and Mera records both.
- With **protocol but no testimony**, Rain or Mara displays the paired signal board without naming the old dispute.
- With a **later hearing**, Mera names the date, stewards, evidence packet, and narrow questions.
- With **no joint act**, leave the absence visible. Do not improvise reconciliation because guests expect one.

Perrin's death belongs to Agnes, Serein, Rain, Mara, Tomas, and others in different ways. The wedding may improve a warning system without consuming their grief as ceremony material.

## Feast and bounded grievance

The feast begins at Pella's promised cue even if some obligations remain open. Credit sources, crews, cooks, carriers, and substitutions. Guests use their declared courses and abstentions.

If the public grievance interval remains part of the day, serve food first. Rain and Mera admit no more than the agreed number of one-breath entries. Each names speaker, respondent, duty, next act, steward, and later hearing. Character attacks, surprise testimony, claims over the couple, and the full old-flood dispute do not enter.

The wedding need not end with every obligation discharged. It needs an end to the interval so celebration is not held hostage to compulsory reconciliation.

## The witnesses' record

After the departure or at the chosen signing point, Mera opens the folio. Each witness signs only what they observed and understood. The record contains separate fields for:

1. **Persons and identities:** stable marks, current answered names, parentage, and authorized references.
2. **Consent:** separate and public choices, pauses, revisions, and whether consent remained present through the represented terms.
3. **Household standing:** each accepted description and its operational duties.
4. **Property and office boundaries:** mill property, operation, water compliance, succession, and marital representation.
5. **Exchange:** public objects or acts, declared meanings, acceptance, transformation, deferral, or refusal.
6. **Promises:** enforceable duties, personal promises kept outside the compact, correction procedures, and release or later-hearing terms.
7. **Departure:** the observed act by which the couple left together or otherwise completed their chosen form.
8. **Open matters:** unresolved clauses, withheld recognition, abstentions, and assigned hearings.
9. **Weather and adaptation:** site used, relocation, omitted elements, and any function repeated for observation.
10. **Certification:** the exact scope of what the witnesses can attest.

The folio has no field for “both families agreed.” Name each household speaker and each abstention instead. Attendance at the ceremony or feast is not assent to every act.

### Certification forms

Choose the narrowest honest form:

**Cross-bank marriage certified.** All six functions were observed, the terms were understood and chosen, and any open matters do not contradict the marriage represented.

**Marriage certified with stated boundaries.** The couple married under clear terms while one or more matters—such as household description, later care signatories, inheritance clarification, or the old-flood hearing—remain expressly open.

**Marriage certified; river adoption deferred.** Consent, mortal and chosen household standing, names, exchange, promises, and departure were observed, but river adoption was omitted or left for later acceptance. Do not record duties that were not undertaken.

**Ceremony witnessed without House certification.** The couple completed a personally meaningful rite, but the witnesses could not attest one or more represented functions. State what was observed and why certification was withheld without declaring the relationship unreal.

**Voluntary postponement recorded.** Tomas, Neris, or both declined to proceed under the current terms. Record the decision, protected privacy, any preparations preserved, and any requested next hearing. Do not imply breach or abandonment unless a separate agreement establishes it.

**Process ended without marriage.** Either person may decide not to marry now or later. The House records the end of its commission and any property, gift, or hearing matters that remain. Affection in the premise does not remove this freedom.

Individual witnesses may sign narrower observations if they disagree about certification. Mera preserves the difference rather than manufacturing a unanimous panel.

## Late discoveries and the smallest affected scope

A late fact does not automatically return the ceremony to its beginning. Name what it changes.

- If it changes **consent or authority**, pause that function. Question the affected principal privately and return the term to the actual authority holder. Continue unrelated functions only if Tomas and Neris still choose to proceed.
- If it changes **safety or access**, adapt before continuing. Omit the object, move the station, reduce attendance, or use the fallback.
- If it changes **gift, hospitality, or performance meaning**, declare the corrected meaning and let the receiver accept, transform, privatize, defer, refuse, or omit it.
- If it is **historical corroboration**, append it to the record or a later hearing. Reopen a completed function only when the evidence contradicts a material claim somebody actually made.

After certification, the House adds an append-only correction. A witness may narrow or correct their attestation without pretending the earlier page never existed. Such a correction does not by itself dissolve the marriage.

## Authority at the threshold

When a refusal or objection occurs, ask what the speaker controls.

Agnes may withhold the veil, her testimony, attendance, or personal recognition. Mara may withhold operational changes or duties within her office. Serein may withhold adoption and reciprocal river-household commitments. Orren may withhold the ring. Pella may reduce what the market can safely provide. Ansel may decline to officiate wording he cannot read. Dessa may close an unsafe structure. Iven and Moss may refuse music they cannot honestly lead. Guests may abstain from food, grievance, gift, response, or dance.

None of these decisions answers for Tomas or Neris. Conversely, the couple cannot bind somebody else's property, office, labor, household, or testimony by marrying. The witnesses cannot repair that gap through an eloquent certification.

If one household cooperates and the other does not, preserve the personal marriage and exact accepted standing while leaving the absent household term open. If both households withhold recognition, Tomas and Neris may still hold a personal ceremony; the House may record what occurred without issuing cross-bank household certification. If witnesses disagree, each signs a narrower scope.

Before closing any function, say aloud: who accepted, within what authority, what was refused or left open, and whether the principals still choose the marriage under that state.

## Mixed outcomes

The ending need not satisfy every guest or settle every inherited dispute.

### Recognized across both banks

The six functions are clear, both household speakers accept the record, and the chosen feast becomes genuine celebration. Old injuries may remain. Recognition means the represented marriage is understood, not that every relative feels the same emotion.

### One marriage, two descriptions

Tomas and Neris accept the same residence, care, warning, property, and departure duties while retaining different descriptions of household structure. Mera records both descriptions beside one operational schedule. Some guests call this inelegant. The witnesses may call it accurate.

### Marriage with a boundary

One issue is assigned to later hearing or excluded from the marriage. The wedding succeeds because the limit is named. The red cord remains tied around the copied clause until the hearing is complete.

### Marriage without adoption

Tomas and Neris marry, but Tomas does not enter river kinship today. The river household may be disappointed or may prefer a later rite. Water obligations continue through the lease and any duties separately accepted; marriage does not manufacture them.

### One household abstains

A family withholds recognition, gift, response, or feast attendance while lawful witnesses observe the couple's choice. Record the exact abstention. The relational cost is real and may shape the later aftermath, but it does not retroactively prove coercion or error.

### Postponed by the couple

The couple chooses more time. Pella serves the food as a gathering rather than wasting it; gifts return to their declared custodians; the House seals the current draft; and the joining light passes. Some guests will call the day a failure. The witness office has done its work.

### Ceremony without certification

The couple proceeds despite a function the witnesses cannot attest. Let the ceremony keep its meaning for those who accept it. State the institutional dispute exactly; do not pronounce on whether love exists.

## Closing the encounter
Do not call the result simply beautiful. Name the thing the table made.

Say where Tomas and Neris stood, which names were spoken, what duty crossed the bank, which gift changed office, which line remained red-corded, who abstained, where the water sent the route, and what first step the couple chose after the final silence.

Ask each player character to read the line they sign and the claim they leave blank. End on a handled thing: wet gold on the wheel; the opened iron clasp; the veil above, not over, a face; an empty abstention token beside a full bowl; two household descriptions on one page; or joining light passing over an unsigned folio.

The adventure ends with a made thing—a certified marriage, a bounded marriage, deferred adoption, a ceremony outside House certification, a postponement, or an honest end to the commission. Success is not smoothness. It is a choice no longer hidden inside somebody else's words.
