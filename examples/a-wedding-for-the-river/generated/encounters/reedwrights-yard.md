# The Reedwright's Yard

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** site, capacity, craft, procession

Dessa Reedwright prices the metaphors: safe footing, clean water return, visible stations, named labor, and a fallback ready before the rain decides for them.

## Opening View

Dessa Reedwright has put Agnes Vale’s chair on a floating panel and ordered three apprentices to walk across it. The panel rolls. The apprentices catch the chair. Around them, fish gates lean against planks, a ceremonial boat is tied sideways, and four handsome plans flap beneath a board painted METAPHORS, UNPRICED. Dessa draws her measuring cord through the mud, looks from the chair to the witnesses, and asks the only question she considers fit for poetry: “What, exactly, is meant to carry her?”

## Encounter Material

## Situation
The **Reedwright's Yard** occupies a shelf of packed clay below the market bridge. Fish gates lean against plank stacks. Reed panels float in troughs while apprentices jump on them one at a time. A small ceremonial boat is tied sideways between two posts. Four sketches for “one place for both households” flap beneath Dessa's painted judgment: **METAPHORS, UNPRICED**.

One sketch puts every river guest in ankle-deep water. One lifts the Vales onto a dais Agnes cannot hear from. One sends Tomas away in the boat. The last draws a beautiful spiral with no opening wide enough for Deep-Reed's chair. Dessa Reedwright can build any meaning the couple chooses, provided somebody tells her its weight, route, capacity, labor, and return.

Bring Dessa the shrine's six acts and the market's named guests. She turns them into wet and dry footing, sight lines, handrails, rill intake, clean return, a stationary threshold, work lanes, musician positions, and a stripped fallback. The site works when every needed person can see or hear the act that concerns them, no one must risk injury to prove trust, and rain can move the ceremony without changing who receives whom.

Lumber cannot reconcile a family. Reeds cannot decide where home is. They can, however, expose a false answer before fifty guests stand on it.

## Dessa Reedwright
Dessa is compact, sun-browned, and missing the tip of one finger to a winter lashing. She tests a platform by standing on it while somebody else shakes the frame. Custom earns her respect when it admits weight, depth, distance, labor, and bodies. Ask lumber to settle an unspoken quarrel and she writes **METAPHOR** where another builder would write a price.

She wants one modular build that can move among meadow, wheel yard, ford, and shrine rise. She also wants twelve workers named where other people would write “the river provided.”

Dessa accepts asymmetry, linked platforms, separate routes, ordinary handrails, a stationary boat, smaller scale, and decoration cut for access or fallback. She refuses stagnant display water, risk called trust, chairs treated as blemishes, untested capacity, blocked work lanes, and metaphor changes delivered after the last peg is driven.

Dessa prices speech as readily as timber:

> “Tell me what the bridge means after you tell me who crosses it.”

> “Water may be sacred. It is still heavy.”

> “I can build one place. I cannot build one consequence from four promises.”

## The yard inventory

Dessa has enough material for one full site and one stripped fallback, not two elaborate sites.

- eighteen woven reed mats, each safe for four standing adults or two chairs;
- twelve dry plank sections, each six feet long;
- six shallow rill trays with pegged couplings;
- four low bridge sections with handrail sockets;
- two backed basin chairs, one already promised to Deep-Reed;
- eight removable handrail posts;
- one open threshold boat;
- two broad turning platforms suitable for chairs, musicians, or exchange tables;
- sixteen lantern stakes;
- one compact chest stand;
- three water-return screens to keep flowers, wax, food, and dropped objects out of the channel; and
- enough spare reed and cord for repair, not for an unplanned second grand aisle.

A crew of eight can erect the meadow design in four hours on dry ground. Moving it to the mill yard takes two hours if the modules are labeled and the route remains clear. Moving it to the shrine rise takes three because the rill water must be lifted from the runnel and returned downhill. The willow-bank design requires less material but admits fewer dry chairs. The old ford can be made usable quickly, but guests will stand in separated groups and the ceremony must be shorter.

These limits create decisions rather than punishment. Decorative arches, extra lanterns, and matched railings consume the same labor needed for a dry turning space or fallback labeling.

## The site survey

Dessa asks the party to choose a primary site only after walking all four candidates with the guest register and six-function order.

### Flood meadow

**Capacity:** up to fifty seated or sixty standing in fair weather.

**Strengths:** broad sight lines, room for two approaches, space for tables and musicians, easy access from market and mill, and enough open sky for the joining light.

**Weaknesses:** overnight rills appear unpredictably; ground softens under chairs; shade is limited; the exact ford edge changes; and every long route becomes harder for Agnes, Deep-Reed, and small children.

**Best use:** paired household areas joined by a central threshold, with modular rills crossing raised mats. The meadow makes the whole wedding visible but needs the strongest rain plan.

### Old wheel yard

**Capacity:** forty-two seated if one mill access lane remains open; forty-eight with some guests standing.

**Strengths:** existing stone runnel, dry packed ground, nearby eastern rooms, kitchen access, familiar mortal acoustics, and a visible wheel for the joining light.

**Weaknesses:** grinding noise, work traffic, narrow western entrance, risk that river guests appear merely accommodated inside Vale property, and no room for two long processions.

**Best use:** compact-first or threshold order. The eastern runnel can feed shallow rills and return water without pumps. Ask Neris what would make the yard a shared site instead of the mill receiving her household as visitors.

### Willow bank

**Capacity:** thirty-six comfortable witnesses, fewer if many dry chairs are required.

**Strengths:** living water, shade, existing platforms, strong river-household recognition, and an easy stationary boat threshold.

**Weaknesses:** roots, narrow approaches, limited kitchen access, poor dry turning space, and a risk that Tomas’s adoption visually overwhelms Neris’s entry into mortal household standing.

**Best use:** smaller ceremony with overflow witnesses on the market bridge or opposite bank. The Vale canopy, keys, or household act must remain clearly visible.

### Shrine rise and old ford

**Capacity:** thirty seated at the shrine, additional standing witnesses along the ford.

**Strengths:** neutral ground, dry shelter, existing runnel, clear officiant acoustics, and the fastest rain fallback.

**Weaknesses:** limited feast space, divided sight lines, little room for dance, and an uphill approach for several guests.

**Best use:** shortened compact-first order in hard rain, or separate consent and record at the shrine followed by a brief departure crossing at the ford.

No site is neutral. Name whose comfort, recognition, and labor each choice improves, and whose it spends. Then compensate with a shorter route, visible household act, carried announcement, second witnessing position, or private exchange.

## Wet and dry ground in one ceremony

The core construction is not a half-wet compromise surface. It is a sequence of adjacent surfaces whose use is explicit.

Dessa’s preferred module uses:

- dry plank paths for chairs, canes, shoes, and wheeled carts;
- low reed mats over firm ground for standing witnesses;
- shallow flowing rills no deeper than two fingers;
- basin chairs for guests who want moving water without unstable footing;
- broad crossings where the couple and attendants can turn without stepping backward; and
- screens at each return point to prevent wax, petals, pins, and food from entering the channel.

River guests differ in how much water they need. Deep-Reed needs feet in moving water but a dry backed chair. Lilt-before-Storm needs audible flow and can remain dry. Serein prefers to stand in a rill for the adoption act but does not require the entire ceremony there. Neris can cross dry ground for several minutes if the route and purpose are chosen rather than imposed. Mortal guests also differ. Aunt Sal wants the wet route with a handrail. Agnes wants stable dry ground and a clear view. Noll Ferry wants both dry footing and easy retreat from crowding.

The plan therefore marks **water positions**, **dry positions**, and **choice positions** rather than “river side” and “mortal side.” Guests can move only if they wish. Nobody is assigned to water by ancestry.

## The rill test

Assemble one rill section before choosing the whole plan.

1. Draw water from an allowed point above the site.
2. Run it through two trays and beneath one bridge section.
3. Place dry mats on both sides.
4. Test the flow with leaves, then remove every leaf at the return screen.
5. Have Agnes’s chair, Deep-Reed’s basin chair, a child, and a person carrying the compact chest pass or remain nearby.
6. Tip one tray, obstruct one return, and shift one mat to see what fails visibly.

Success demonstrates that one place can contain moving water and dry ground without pretending every person shares one surface. A partial failure identifies labor or capacity: a sagging mat, inaudible water, splashed hem, blocked chair turn, or return screen that needs a steward.

## The six-function plan

Dessa redraws the ceremony by function rather than prop. Place six visible stations or cues.

### Consent station

Both partners must be seen and heard without family members standing between them and the witnesses. The station needs no object. It needs quiet, clear sight, and a pause route if either answer changes.

At the meadow, place consent near the center before the household platforms. At the wheel yard, stop the wheel or move beyond its noise. At the willow bank, use the broad dry platform rather than forcing Tomas into the boat before he has publicly chosen the marriage.

### Household-standing station

This station may include keys, the threshold boat, a shared doorway frame, or spoken duties. It must allow retained standing to be visible as well as entry. If Tomas enters the boat, the route by which he leaves it should already be in place. If Neris accepts Vale household standing beneath the veil canopy, she must remain visible and uncovered.

### Name station

The announcer, Mera, and the couple need a clear speaking triangle. The record should not be read from behind the couple as though names are being assigned to them. A small water bowl and dry stone can hold witness marks, but neither is mandatory.

### Exchange station

Every accepted object needs a stable surface, correct giver, safe approach, and route away if refused or deferred. An empty place is useful. It makes a withheld gift visible without requiring explanation beyond the receiver’s stated choice.

### Promise station

The speakers need quiet and enough time for the chosen vow structure. The musicians should see Ansel’s cue but not play beneath words merely to maintain atmosphere. In rain, this station moves under roof first.

### Departure station

The departure path begins where the last promise ends and reaches a place the couple can occupy together. It cannot terminate at two separate family platforms. If the boat is used, it is placed perpendicular to the bank and secured at both ends so entry and exit occur before the same witnesses.

Dessa draws sight-line cones from each station to Agnes, Deep-Reed, the free witnesses, and overflow guests. Not everyone must see every hand movement. The six certifiable acts must each have enough direct witnesses that the record is credible.

## The threshold boat

The boat is seven feet long, broad-bottomed, and open at both ends. It draws less than a foot of water when Tomas stands inside. It is not fitted for travel and has no oars. The absence of oars is useful evidence: nobody planned a departure downriver.

The standard adoption sequence is:

1. The boat is tied perpendicular to the bank between two stable landings.
2. Tomas states that he enters under his own power to accept the bounded duties already recorded.
3. He steps in without being pulled or covered.
4. Neris or Serein receives him by naming the duties and the river household’s reciprocal obligations.
5. Tomas may refuse or correct any duty stated differently from the compact.
6. Neris joins him only if the couple chooses a paired threshold.
7. Both step out toward the shared departure route.

Alternatives include placing the open iron spiral on the boat’s side, using the boat only to hold the water bowl and adoption record, or omitting it entirely in favor of a bridge crossing. The boat is not valid merely because it is old custom. It is useful if it makes entry, receipt, reciprocity, and exit legible.

Safety requires handholds, shallow water, a dry bypass, and one named boat steward. Tomas should rehearse once in ordinary clothes. A surprise balance test is not a loyalty test.

## Procession patterns

The spatial plan must match the musical plan. Dessa offers three patterns.

### Paired approaches

The Vales and river household approach from different directions. Tomas and Neris may walk with their present households, alone, or already together. The approaches meet at the consent station. This makes both backgrounds visible but requires room and synchronized timing.

### One shared route with alternating offices

All guests enter by the same safe path. Different people lead portions: Mara at the mill gate, Rain at the first rill, Pella at the guest turn, Ansel at the shrine marker. This reduces distance and avoids a cultural parade, but household recognition must be carried through announced offices and acts.

### Gathered witnesses, moving couple

Guests are seated before the ceremony. Tomas and Neris move among six stations while families remain visible around them. This is accessible and weather-resistant. It can make the couple feel inspected unless they choose the movement and have a private pause route.

No procession is mandatory. In poor weather the couple can begin together at the central station and let names, objects, music, and speakers carry the distinctions.

## Labor and office assignment

Construction creates ceremonial offices that can satisfy real desires for recognition.

- Orren can lead bridge pegging and serve as threshold-boat steward.
- Mara can assign mill hands and certify that the work does not disrupt wheel safety.
- Aunt Sal can supervise handrails and guide guests over the wet route.
- Brin can label modules and must answer for any invented label that sends a guest wrong.
- Elsin can mark sight lines, test chest routes, and stop adults from calling her “the future miller” while she works.
- Rain can verify intake and return points and post water changes.
- Pella can confirm table and kitchen routes remain open.
- Noll Ferry can test crowd exits from the perspective of someone who needs quick air.

Do not spend the couple's eve hauling every module. Reassign labor before exhaustion begins making decisions for them.

## Yard complications

1. **The beautiful spiral.** A family sketch becomes popular until a chair cannot turn through it. Preserve the spiral as lantern pattern or discard it.
2. **The dry rill.** The first test is too quiet for Lilt-before-Storm. Increasing flow splashes Agnes’s route. A separate audible spillway solves the need better than widening every channel.
3. **The ceremonial depth.** Serein remembers the adoption boat in deeper water. Tomas can accept the same duty in shallower depth; the witnesses must distinguish symbolic intensity from actual requirement.
4. **The uncredited crew.** Guests praise the river for the platforms while workers are still carrying them. Dessa stops work until names, wages, and feast seats are entered.
5. **The blocked mill lane.** A grand approach prevents flour carts from moving. Shorten it, move the wheel schedule, or choose another site.
6. **The moved peg.** Children relocate a lantern stake and expose that everyone has been using decoration as a navigation marker. Replace it with a labeled structural cue.
7. **The high water rehearsal.** A sudden release raises the test trough. The modules hold, but one return screen clogs, proving the need for a named water steward.

## Decisions that travel forward

Record:

- primary and fallback sites;
- capacity at each site and who may witness from overflow positions;
- the position of each of the six functions;
- wet, dry, and choice positions for named guests;
- rill intake, return, screen, and water steward;
- handrails, chairs, turning spaces, and retreat routes;
- whether and how the threshold boat is used;
- the procession pattern and joining point;
- module labels, labor assignments, movement time, and the deadline for relocation; and
- which decorations are sacrificed first if weather or labor worsens.

Take the plan to the Bridge of Three Tunes for timing and cues. Test it at the Flood Meadow with actual guest stand-ins, objects, and a simulated site change.

## If the yard is skipped or reached late

Use a known safe fixed site. The shrine rise and old wheel yard can each support a stripped ceremony without a moving rill, threshold boat, elaborate ramps, or late structural improvisation. Mark wet and dry standing places with existing ground, mats, benches, and spoken office rather than unstable devices.

Dessa's absence removes craft capacity, not ceremonial meaning. Consent, names, promises, and departure remain possible. Household standing and exchange must be made legible through words and accepted acts instead of architecture. The couple takes its first shared step on a route they have inspected.

A late safety discovery can stop an object, route, or structure immediately. It cannot dictate the marriage. Move the affected function, omit the device, reduce attendance, or relocate. Do not ask a last-minute roll to determine whether a visibly unsafe platform somehow holds.

## Failure forward

Bad planning produces wet clothes, separated audiences, a delayed start, omitted decoration, tired workers, or an improvised ceremony at the old ford. A site change may reduce the guest count or move one exchange private. None of these outcomes invalidates freely chosen promises.

The physical plan becomes unacceptable only when it prevents a principal from choosing, hearing, moving safely, or understanding what the ceremony publicly claims. Even then, Dessa can strip the design to dry shelter, one moving-water basin, six visible functions, and a shared departure. A smaller honest place is better than an impressive structure whose bodies contradict its words.
