# The Flood Meadow and Old Ford

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** rehearsal, weather, family-history, contingency

At the old ford, chairs, weather, work, guests, and the memory of Perrin's flood test whether the planned ceremony still says what the couple chose.

## Opening View

The meadow was one meadow at breakfast. Now water runs through the planned aisle, leaving three wooden markers on either bank and forcing the procession to cross the new stream twice. Chairs, serving boards, rolled awnings, and red cord stand wherever yesterday’s plan abandoned them. Near the old ford, Rain-at-Noon holds a cracked bell. Agnes Vale faces her across the water. Between them, half pressed into the mud, lies a small carved signal token. Neither woman reaches for it.

## Encounter Material

## Situation
The **flood meadow** is the broadest ground neither household can call its own. In dry weather it holds hayricks, fairs, wrestling, and the yearly line of light between mill wheel and alder bend. After rain, old channels return as silver cuts through the grass. Some vanish within an hour. Others place yesterday's path under a handspan of water.

That makes the meadow the right rehearsal ground. Every choice from house, mill, willows, market, shrine, yard, and bridge must now bear chairs, canes, children, serving trays, fatigue, weather, hearing, unfinished work, and people who stand somewhere other than the diagram. The six plain markers are easy to move. The duties they represent are not.

Run the rehearsal on the afternoon before the wedding. It introduces no saboteur and unveils no secret curse. It shows what the current design depends on while there is still time to rotate the veil canopy, widen a route, shorten a vow, change a signal, move the site, leave a clause open, or postpone.

The old ford also holds the surviving measures of Perrin Vale's death: water cuts in the wheelhouse, Rain-at-Noon's cracked bell, a lost north path, bell time, river marks, and a warning nobody had promised to receive. The witnesses are not asked to settle grief. They are asked to build the next handoff so it reaches somebody before the yard becomes water.

## Who arrives and what each person is watching

**Dessa Reedwright** has brought labeled planks, rolled mats, stakes, handrails, and a small crew. She watches loads, turning room, water return, and whether anybody is asking one piece of lumber to solve two incompatible decisions.

**Keeper Ansel Rook** carries six flat markers: consent, household, names, exchange, promises, and departure. He watches whether each function remains intelligible after props and poetry are removed.

**Mera Quill** has the current compact, a blank witness folio, a weather sheet, and red cord for any clause that must not be read as settled. She watches who answers, who paraphrases, and who is being treated as present merely because someone placed a chair for them.

**Pella Nine-Spoons** has the feast clock. She watches the route from kitchen to tables, the point at which grievance must end, and every ceremony delay that threatens to convert warm food into a second argument.

**Iven Crossbow** and **Moss-in-Evening** watch sight lines and silence. A joining measure that cannot be heard at the fallback site is not ready. Music that carries people across a consent pause is worse than no music.

**Rain-at-Noon** carries the cracked handbell from the old flood. **Agnes Vale** brings the memorial bowl and, if still offered publicly, the transformed veil. **Serein-at-Alderfall** brings the river depth staff. None of the three is required to reconcile with another. Each has agreed to test the new warning handoff.

Tomas and Neris arrive separately. Let the process bring them together by choice; do not begin by treating engagement as completed union.

## The visible pressure board

Place four headings where everyone can see them. Use chalk, stones, cards, or marks on Mera's weather sheet. Pressure never advances in secret. State what changed, who can respond, and what will happen if nobody does.

### Water

| Mark | What everyone can see | Practical consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **0 — Grass** | Ground is firm; old channels are only darker lines | Primary meadow plan remains available |
| **1 — Threads** | Water appears in the lowest channels; mats begin to wick | Move vulnerable chairs and verify every return path |
| **2 — Crossings** | One planned dry route is wet or soft; the rill no longer has a single obvious edge | Use modular detours, shorten movement, or prepare relocation |
| **3 — Relocate** | A required dry route, chair position, boat landing, or witness sight line is unsafe | Pause and move to the chosen fallback; do not improvise safety by social pressure |

Choose the starting mark from the established forecast. If no forecast was established, announce one before rehearsal or roll openly: clear shift, light rain, steady rain, or upstream rain already entering the meadow. Water advances only when rain, upstream flow, or a blocked return visibly justifies it. Good drainage, removing decoration from a channel, opening a return, or relocating a module can prevent an advance. Nobody lowers the mark by insisting that a wet route is symbolically important.

### Work

Write every unfinished item as a named task with a responsible person and a latest useful time. Typical tasks are the west handrail, compact chest cover, open iron fitting, chair backs, rill screen, food awnings, name cards, and bridge lashings.

The crew has **four substantial work turns** before dusk. One turn completes one major task or two minor tasks if the decision is already made. The adventurers may help, recruit named labor, purchase a ready substitute, or remove a task by simplifying the ceremony. Debate without a decision consumes the turn only when people are genuinely waiting on it.

The work board is not a punishment for visiting encounters in an unexpected order. If the party arrives late, reduce decoration and optional movement before reducing consent, access, or a named duty.

### Guests

List particular people whose participation is not yet workable. Do not write “river side” or “Vale side.” A guest entry has four fields:

- the person;
- the difficulty or choice;
- the steward who will ask rather than assume; and
- the visible accommodation, abstention, or refusal.

Agnes may need a quieter position. Deep-Reed needs a backed dry chair with moving water at the feet. Noll Ferry needs an open-air retreat. Lilt-before-Storm needs audible water if asked to witness river standing. Aunt Sal needs a handrail. A guest who has not confirmed an accommodation remains an open entry; the party may not close it by assigning what seems culturally appropriate.

Guest pressure produces separate seating, smaller attendance, delayed entry, or visible abstention. It does not produce automatic insult. The cost comes from who cannot participate in which function, not from a hidden etiquette score.

### Open obligations

Place one red cord around every term that Tomas or Neris cannot yet paraphrase, every gift whose public use lacks the receiver's acceptance, every grievance without a steward, and every office without a named person. Some cords can remain through the wedding if the record assigns a later hearing or declares the boundary. A cord becomes a certification problem only when the ceremony would imply an answer the couple has not given.

Keep four states distinct:

- **ready:** the affected people can state the term and the act;
- **conditional:** the term is accepted only if a named condition occurs;
- **open:** the matter is honestly assigned to later hearing or left outside the marriage;
- **unready:** the ceremony would represent a choice that one principal has not made.

Only the last state requires revision, omission, postponement, or refusal of certification.

## Rehearsal pass one: the empty ceremony

Before guests, gifts, boat, veil, or music enter, Ansel places the six markers in the chosen order. Tomas and Neris walk the route in ordinary clothes and speak only plain language.

At each marker ask:

1. What can an observer see?
2. What do Tomas and Neris say the act means?
3. Who may interrupt if the meaning changes?
4. What remains if rain removes this site or prop?

This pass catches elegance legible only to its designers. If Tomas enters the boat before consent, move the boat. If Neris's household standing depends on the veil, separate recognition from cloth. If music starts the departure for them, restore a silent choice before the first shared step.

Children, helpers, or wind may move decorative markers between passes. That is not sabotage; it is a legibility test. If a child can turn **names** into **exchange** without an adult noticing, the ceremony needs clearer offices.

## Rehearsal pass two: principals and corrections

Bring in Ansel, Mera, household speakers, gift givers, and musicians. Run the exact words or plain paraphrases for consent, household standing, names, exchange, promises, and departure.

Useful interruptions include:

- Serein adds a duty to the adoption language that Tomas did not accept;
- Agnes uses “daughter of Vale” where Neris accepted household recognition but not replacement kinship language;
- Orren presents the open iron with a flourish that makes it look closed from the audience's angle;
- a name announcer shortens Neris's name without authorization;
- a personal promise is read as though it were enforceable household duty;
- the musicians enter one measure before a correction is complete; or
- Mera discovers that the couple agreed on duties but still use different household descriptions.

The witnesses may pause, ask, compare, and propose. The affected principal decides. Correct with a new sentence, changed angle, removed object, separate description, or explicit open clause.

After this pass, question Tomas and Neris separately. Ask what the rehearsal made clearer, what became less acceptable, and whether they still choose the wedding under the current terms. Rehearsal is new information. Earlier consent does not answer for them now.

Keep one plain promise marker beside the route. When water or access forces a change, write the first wording, the interrupted method, the revised duty, and the people who accepted the revision. The marker should show a promise enduring through changed words and footing instead of proving itself by exact repetition.

## Rehearsal pass three: bodies, service, and weather

Add representative guests, chairs, the compact chest, any threshold boat, accepted gifts, grievance stewards, food carriers, and both musical routes. Do not require the full guest list; choose the people whose needs stress different parts of the plan.

Run the procession or approaches at walking speed. Then test:

- whether Agnes can hear the consent and names;
- whether Deep-Reed can see the household act without leaving the supported chair;
- whether Noll can withdraw without crossing the couple's path;
- whether Aunt Sal can use the wet route or choose the dry one without embarrassment;
- whether Pella's carriers can reach the feast without crossing the compact or rill;
- whether the gift steward can remove a refused or deferred object without spectacle;
- whether the boat has a dry bypass and a visible exit;
- whether both tunes remain audible without filling required silences; and
- whether the witnesses can observe all six functions from their actual positions.

Advance water only when the fiction warrants it. At Water 1, move named chairs and inspect the return. At Water 2, require a real decision: shorten movement, move modules, or commit to the fallback. At Water 3, relocation is not a test of courage. The witnesses and builders pause the rehearsal.

Deep-Reed provides the sharpest relocation test. Two helpers can move her backed chair quickly and still fail her if they leave the shallow moving basin behind. Dry support and current at her feet form one chosen accommodation. Noll Ferry, Agnes, Aunt Sal, and Lilt-before-Storm each have different things that must move, may change, or may be omitted.

A relocation rehearsal is valuable even if the meadow remains dry. Dessa can call an announced false threshold and give the crew one work turn to move only what the shortened order requires. Time the transfer by bells rather than minutes if the setting does not use clocks. Anything that cannot be moved must be either site-independent or optional.

## Rehearsal pass four: the old flood handoff

The old flood is not a surprise revelation if the party has visited the mill, willow house, or market. The meadow lets them put the surviving measures in one place.

Set five evidence stations:

1. **The north alder:** the first warning braid was hung where the river household expected a watcher, but the mill's night workers had no assigned reason to inspect it.
2. **The depth staff:** the second warning named current depth at the alder bend without converting it to the wheelhouse's carved yard marks.
3. **Pella's slate:** delivery notations place rising water on the mill road before the yard bell sounded, but they do not show that anybody at the wheel understood the river sign.
4. **Rain's borrowed bell:** the first warning in mortal bell time arrived while evacuation was still possible but after the main wheel was already endangered.
5. **Perrin's tool line:** abandoned wedges and the jammed lower gate show that Perrin sent others away and remained after he could have left, trying to save the wheel.

These facts support several true statements at once:

- the river household warned earlier than Agnes heard a bell;
- the early warnings were not translated into the mill's working measure;
- the mill lacked a named watcher and acknowledgment;
- Rain's bell still arrived in time for people to leave;
- Perrin chose to remain; and
- no single admission settles grief, duty, or blame.

Do not ask for a verdict on who killed Perrin. Ask what handoff would have made the danger intelligible before his final choice.

The old packet contains a carved token marked **mill gate received**. It was never used. That small absence names the broken joint more precisely than another accusation: somebody knew how to send, but nobody had accepted the duties to receive, repeat, and escalate. Rain may grieve the silence; Agnes may grieve Perrin's choice. The token requires neither woman to become the other's verdict.

### Building the new handoff

A complete protocol needs six parts:

1. **paired thresholds:** each river depth or sign is matched to a carved mill mark and bell pattern;
2. **named watchers:** at least one mill worker and one river keeper per watch period;
3. **acknowledgment:** the receiving side answers the warning so the sender knows it was understood;
4. **authority:** a named person may stop the wheel and open the lower sluice without waiting for Tomas or Neris;
5. **evacuation language:** one signal means leave people, animals, and tools; it does not mean “save the wheel if you can”; and
6. **review:** false alarms and missed signs go to a mixed hearing, not a loyalty test.

Do not make Tomas and Neris the protocol's only translators. Their marriage may establish duties; the protocol must still work when both are absent, asleep, injured, quarreling, or downstream.

### Honest outcomes for Agnes and Serein

Any of these outcomes is honest:

- **A joint warning practice:** Agnes rings the mill pattern while Serein calls the paired river measure. They need not discuss Perrin publicly.
- **Separate true statements:** each woman states what she now recognizes, and Mera records both without synthesizing them into false unanimity.
- **A protocol without testimony:** both approve the new handoff but decline any ceremony reference to the flood.
- **A later hearing:** the evidence and questions are sealed for a separate meeting after the wedding.
- **No joint act:** one or both refuse. Rain, Mara, and named keepers can still implement the protocol if they accept its duties.

Forced forgiveness worsens the rehearsal. If the witnesses make certification or family honor depend on an apology, Agnes or Serein may withdraw from the joint act, sit apart, withhold a gift, or request a stewarded later hearing. The wedding remains possible.

## Complications that find the weak joint

Use only complications that follow from the current plan. Announce the practical problem and let the party choose what to preserve.

- A plank sinks at one corner because decoration blocked the return channel.
- A late awning crew can cover either the food tables or the witness station before rain, not both.
- Brin has accurately labeled six guest tokens but placed them on the wrong chairs.
- Orren's iron arrives unfinished at the public angle; it is open in the hand and apparently closed from the crowd.
- A grievance speaker brings a character accusation instead of a named obligation.
- One musician can hear the joining cue at the meadow but not at the mill-yard fallback.
- Agnes's seat has the best lineage prominence and the worst acoustics.
- Neris realizes that the chosen departure path visually ends at the mill, though she intended it to lead first to a shared meal and only later to the eastern rooms.

A failed response changes scope, participation, cost, or record. It does not reveal an unknown taboo that invalidates previous choices.

## Rehearsal close

Before dusk, Mera asks the witnesses to state the final readiness aloud. Record:

- primary site and exact relocation trigger;
- fallback site and shortened order;
- the observable act for each of the six functions;
- current Water mark and unfinished work;
- every named access position and steward;
- accepted, transformed, private, deferred, and refused gifts;
- grievance entries and feast-start cue;
- the old flood's place in the ceremony, if any;
- ready, conditional, open, and unready clauses;
- who has authority to pause for consent, safety, changed wording, or changed duty; and
- what the witnesses presently expect they can certify.

Then complete another separate consent check with Tomas and Neris. Either may request revision or postponement without accusing the other. If they proceed with an open clause, ask them to say what the ceremony must **not** imply.

## If rehearsal is skipped, shortened, or divided

The rehearsal is scheduled but not compulsory. If the party misses it, weather or refusal prevents it, or the couple wants privacy, conduct a short site walk immediately before guests are seated.

The minimum walk does four things:

1. Tomas and Neris state the six functions and their correction points without relying on props.
2. Dessa or another competent observer declares the actual route and surfaces safe, or the ceremony moves to a known fallback.
3. Named guests test hearing, seating, footing, service, and abstention positions.
4. Tomas and Neris answer the private consent questions again after seeing the actual design.

Without the fourth rehearsal pass, the old flood remains open. Do not improvise a public reconciliation or warning protocol at the wedding. State separate truths, assign a later hearing, or omit the old-flood act. The witnesses may certify the marriage while declining to claim that the handoff was repaired.

A divided rehearsal is viable. One group may test the fallback while another conducts private repetition, provided actual time and NPC location are tracked. Rejoin the results before public consent. No group may assume that a safe route establishes accepted meaning or that a private answer authorizes an absent household.

## Failure forward

The final venue may shift to the mill yard, willow platforms, old ford, or shrine rise. The veil may stay folded. The iron may remain in Orren's hand. One elder may sit apart. The joining tune may become a single answer after silence. A grievance may move to later hearing. A costly decoration may be abandoned so that an access route can be finished.

A weak rehearsal should make the wedding plainer, smaller, more divided, or less certifiable—not secretly doomed. Its best result is not perfect agreement. It is a plan whose remaining risks are known by the people who must choose under them.
