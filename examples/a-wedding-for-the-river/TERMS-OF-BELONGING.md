# Terms of Belonging: A Wedding for the River

This document is a Session 2 operating aid for the House of Open Measure, the two household visits, and any later consent check. It does not prescribe one correct compact. It separates the decisions that Tomas and Neris must make from the recognitions their families may offer or withhold.

## The central live disagreement

Tomas and Neris agree on affection, the eastern rooms, continued river work, bounded adoption duties, separate mill and water property, changing river names, and the absence of default claims over future children.

They do not initially agree on the public description of their household:

- **Tomas:** one new household whose shared dwelling is at the mill, with a second dwelling available when river duty calls.
- **Neris:** two continuing households joined by marriage, with the eastern rooms and willow house both remaining true homes.

Do not solve this by declaring the descriptions equivalent. They may produce the same schedule while recognizing different continuities. The couple may choose either description, invent another, or preserve both descriptions beside one shared list of duties.

## Private consent sequence

Run this sequence without relatives present. Repeat a shortened form before the ceremony.

1. **Marriage now:** Does the person freely wish to marry at the joining light? “Not yet” is sufficient and needs no public accusation.
2. **Positive terms:** What does the person want the compact to establish?
3. **Limits:** What will the person not permit the compact to establish?
4. **Unknowns:** What does the person not yet understand well enough to promise?
5. **Reciprocal paraphrase:** Can each partner accurately describe one duty and one retained limit belonging to the other?
6. **Repeated choice:** After draft language is offered, does each partner adopt it in the other's presence?

Record only what is needed to certify free choice. Private reasons remain private unless their speaker authorizes disclosure.

## Bounded river adoption

Tomas may accept all, some, or none of these duties:

1. recognize agreed warning signals;
2. stop the wheel and open the lower sluice at the agreed threshold;
3. ring the bell and aid evacuation;
4. enforce spawning closures among people under his authority;
5. relay warnings when doing so does not abandon immediate dependents;
6. accept rescue and mixed hearing before force in a water dispute.

The river household's reciprocal duties are equally material:

1. provide recognizable signals and, when possible, named messengers;
2. share current readings relevant to mill operation;
3. aid all endangered residents, not only river kin;
4. provide recovery care after service or injury;
5. hear claims that a warning was late, unclear, or impossible to obey;
6. avoid loyalty tests through concealed information.

Adoption does not transfer property, impose unrestricted labor, cancel mortal kinship, assign future children, or require dangerous water entry beyond Tomas's ability.

## Name record

A workable record distinguishes identity, current name, residence, and household recognition:

> Neris, child of Serein-at-Alderfall, entered under her stable witness mark, presently answering to Reed-in-Rain, dwelling at the House Beneath the Willows and also in the eastern rooms of Vale Mill.

Future accepted bynames append to the chain. “Neris of Vale Mill” may describe residence. “Neris Vale” is not used unless she later chooses it. Tomas receives no ceremonial river byname merely for symmetry.

## Named care rather than household custody

The compact should never say only that “both families will care for them.” Assign people and acts.

Possible signatories include:

- Mara: mill dwelling, food accounts, notice, and protection of the survivor's room;
- Serein: river-born medical knowledge, warning, shelter, and current access;
- Orren: rescue, transport, repairs, and craft teaching;
- Agnes: household memory and short-term care while able;
- Rain-at-Noon: warning lore and emergency relay, explicitly not routine childcare;
- Tomas and Neris: each other's first ordinary care and prior written directions.

A future child may be entered in both the mortal registry and river care ledger without either entry deciding inheritance, permanent residence, apprenticeship, immersion, or final household standing.

## Principal concessions and limits

| Principal | Can concede | Will not concede |
|---|---|---|
| Tomas | Two recognized homes; exact flood duties; revised work schedules; no ceremonial river byname | Property transfer through adoption; vague unrestricted service; treating Neris's river duty as marital absence |
| Neris | Eastern rooms as the ordinary shared dwelling; stable registry mark; household and mill-water roles | Replacement surname; permission to return to the willows; becoming sole translator or living lease |
| Agnes | Any transformed use of the veil; another explicit remembrance of the Vale dead | Treating the veil and Perrin as shameful obstacles to be hidden |
| Mara | Domestic authority, a water-correspondent role, worker-council participation, dwelling rights | Automatic ownership, debt, succession, or displacement of Elsin |
| Elsin | Neris teaching current marks; ceremonial “wheel” language carefully limited | Being displayed as proof that succession is settled or that she must inherit |
| Serein | Residence mainly at the mill; retained Vale kinship and property; bounded and reciprocal duties | Decorative adoption without operational answerability |
| Orren | Opening, dividing, relocating, or rush-binding the ring | Erasing his labor by discarding or secretly replacing it |
| Rain-at-Noon | Separate hearing for the flood; bounded wedding grievances; shared conversion protocol | A staged reconciliation or claim that no river warning was given |

## Honest incomplete outcomes

The Session 2 material remains successful if it produces any of the following:

- a complete set of belonging clauses;
- practical duties with two preserved descriptions of the household;
- marriage without river adoption, if Neris freely accepts that result;
- adoption deferred to a later hearing;
- a care clause deferred because no named adults have accepted it;
- a wedding postponed by either partner;
- a ceremony the couple chooses to hold without House certification.

The witnesses fail only when they treat family assent as the couple's consent, conceal a known contradiction, or certify language they cannot honestly paraphrase.
