# Design Notes: A Wedding for the River

## Design objective

This is an intimate constructive social adventure. It is not a mystery with a wedding painted over it, not a diplomatic crisis in miniature, and not a story in which outsiders teach two benighted cultures to compromise.

The governing question is:

**Can neutral witnesses help Tomas and Neris make one intelligible marriage without choosing it for them or pretending that every difference has disappeared?**

Every major encounter provides:

1. a concrete preparation whose meanings differ across households;
2. people whose intentions are better than their assumptions;
3. a practical choice among preservation, transformation, substitution, abstention, and explicit refusal;
4. a decision that belongs finally to Tomas, Neris, or another affected principal; and
5. a consequence visible in the final ceremony.

## Governing voice standard

The adventure speaks through handled things. Cloth remembers bodies. Iron remembers force and whether it can open. Flour marks work already underway. Reeds admit weight or fail under it. Bells matter only when somebody has agreed to hear them. Food carries source, labor, temperature, and a guest's free choice. Ink preserves both a promise and the temptation to overstate it.

Use each principal's working measure:

- **Tomas:** gates, shifts, repairs, notices, and the next ordinary week.
- **Neris:** channels, answerable movement, offices, changed names, and whether a warning reaches the bank.
- **Agnes:** household sequence, the dead, cloth, hearing, and what welcome remembers.
- **Mara:** accounts, workers, keys, liability, succession, and the empty shift somebody must fill.
- **Elsin:** chalk, ratios, apprenticeship, and correction when adults use her future as a settled fact.
- **Serein:** thresholds, reciprocal duties, recognized signals, and the distance an answer must travel.
- **Orren:** heat, hammer marks, hinges, openings, and labor that should be corrected rather than erased.
- **Rain-at-Noon:** signals, handoffs, named debts, dry humor, and grief that refuses choreography.
- **Pella:** names, sources, pots, seats, beds, workers, temperature, and who carries the basket.
- **Ansel:** six acts, ordinary verbs, paraphrase, red cord, and the clause that remains open.
- **Dessa:** weight, depth, turning room, tested capacity, clean return, and credited labor.
- **Iven:** entries, downbeats, crowd movement, and silence held as a measure.
- **Moss:** turns, breath, changing leadership, bodies in motion, and a bank found without a straight line.
- **Mera:** bearers, limits, refusal, endings, and what an absent reader may try to claim.

Avoid abstract agreement where a key, chair, signal, bowl, route, signature, or duty can show the point. Do not make every wise character sound like Mera. Let people reach accuracy through their own craft.

## Why the adventurers belong

The party’s outsider status is not a concession. It is the qualification.

Under the Concord of Banks, free witnesses must have no local kinship, debt, water lease, inheritance claim, or blood grievance. They can ask questions that family members cannot ask without appearing to maneuver for advantage. Their commission authorizes inquiry, comparison, pauses, proposed language, and certification. It does not authorize command.

This gives adventurers credible access without presuming friendship. Their general competence matters because preparations are physical as well as verbal: they may inspect a mill lease, return protected fish, test a platform, reconstruct a flood warning, conduct private interviews, redirect a procession, or improvise a ceremony in rain.

## Why the contradiction appears now

The late discovery is a consequence of the Concord's protective filing procedure, not authorial delay. Each household files a sealed reading copy. The central formula is known, but the marginal account of what it confers and requires remains sealed until the free-witness period opens. Paired opening prevents strategic revision after one side sees the other's interpretation. It also allows sincere incompatibility to surface three days before the ceremony.

Mera is essential but deliberately not sufficient. She receives both filings, maintains the record, and drafts the folio. That involvement disqualifies her from acting as the independent field witness who certifies her own work. The party therefore occupies a real institutional office rather than a task an available NPC has inexplicably declined.

## The old flood is the causal engine, not a hidden answer

Perrin Vale's death explains why both households chose the same formula. The Vales want a marriage that receives Neris into the mill without surrendering the household, its dead, or Elsin's future. The river kin want a marriage that makes Tomas answerable to warnings that once stopped at the wheelhouse threshold. Both hear repair in the same words. Their readings are opposed because their memories assign different unfinished duties.

The flood should be legible from the opening and increasingly concrete across the mill, willow house, market, and rehearsal. It is not a final twist and does not conceal a verdict. Its dramatic function is to bind the compact, veil, adoption, warning duties, and elder disagreement into one causal history. Its practical product is a better handoff; emotional reconciliation remains optional.

## No villain and no hidden correct culture

Nobody secretly opposes the marriage. Do not add sabotage, a jealous former lover, a concealed inheritance plot, or an evil river sovereign merely to create conventional momentum.

Conflict comes from four sources:

- shared words with different consequences;
- gifts whose intended honor and received meaning diverge;
- limited time, labor, space, food, and attention;
- real family interests that do not disappear because the couple is in love.

Neither bank owns the default. Mortal inheritance practice is not automatically practical; river custom is not automatically mystical wisdom. Every custom should be evaluated by the function it serves and the people it obligates.

## Consent is active and revisable

Question Tomas and Neris together and separately. They may agree on affection while disagreeing on residence, names, duties, children, property, ceremony, or postponement. Clearer meanings may change either answer.

A postponed wedding can be a successful outcome. So can a marriage with an unresolved clause. Certification is not a reward for keeping the timetable; it is an honest statement of what the witnesses observed.

## Belonging is plural and operational

Do not allow “dual residence,” “shared household,” or “river adoption” to function as conciliatory labels without content. Ask who holds a key, who may leave without permission, who answers a warning, who signs a closure, who retains a room after a death, and who owes care when one partner cannot speak.

Tomas and Neris may agree on every practical duty while retaining different descriptions of their household. That difference is not necessarily an obstacle to marriage. It becomes an obstacle only if one description silently controls a consequence the other person has not accepted.

The adventure should preserve distinctions among:

- affection and household standing;
- residence and permission;
- property and domestic authority;
- office and marital representation;
- kinship and custody;
- care and ownership;
- stable identity and current name; and
- inherited expectation and named consent.

## Private consent is a procedure, not a check

Consent should not collapse into one question at the beginning or one roll at the end. The House of Open Measure uses separate interviews, explicit terms and limits, reciprocal paraphrase, and repeated choice after proposed language is available.

A character who changes an answer has supplied useful information. A character who says “not yet” need not produce a villain. A character may keep private reasons private while still allowing the witnesses to record whether the choice was free, conditional, deferred, or refused.

Do not use compelled truth, thought reading, or supernatural punishment as substitutes for understandable commitments. The relevant evidence is whether Tomas and Neris can state what they and the other person are agreeing to, revise it without retaliation, and repeat the choice when the ceremony arrives.

## Future care must name people and acts

“Both families will care for them” is not an operational promise. It hides the exact conflict the witnesses are meant to expose.

Care clauses should name who offers shelter, food, notice, rescue, transport, teaching, medical knowledge, guardianship, or survivor dwelling rights. Help creates gratitude and perhaps reciprocal duties; it does not automatically create custody, inheritance, or authority over an incapacitated person.

The couple need not decide whether they will have children. They do need to prevent broad household language from assigning hypothetical children at birth. A mortal registry and a river care ledger may both recognize a child while leaving residence, apprenticeship, naming, immersion, inheritance, and later household standing to separate decisions.

## Hospitality is particular and consent-bearing

Household identity may explain who has invitation authority, but it does not determine appetite, mobility, lodging, comfort, ritual participation, or assent. Replace every statement about what “river guests,” “the Vales,” “elders,” or “both families” require with named people wherever the affected people can answer.

Accommodation is not a symbolic prize. A prestigious seat that prevents Agnes from hearing is worse than an ordinary chair that lets her witness the vows. A willow bed is not generous to Noll Ferry merely because he is river-born. A guest may attend while abstaining from a course, grievance, toast, gift, or dance. Presence should never be used to manufacture consent to a custom.

Food source must name labor and obligation as well as ingredient. Sea catch is not automatically innocent, river fish is not automatically forbidden, and a substitution does not become virtuous by erasing the people who supplied it.

## Grievances need scope, stewardship, and an ending

The pre-feast grievance custom should remain a practical disclosure of unfinished obligation. It is not a generalized truth session and should never become a mechanism for forcing apology or public reconciliation.

A usable grievance names speaker, respondent, duty, requested next act, steward, and later hearing. It excludes character attacks, surprise testimony, claims over the couple, and disputes too complex for a brief public statement. The rite should have food before it, a visible time limit, a private route, and a fixed point at which the meal begins even if obligations remain unresolved.

## Ceremony functions must be observable and movable

Consent, household standing, names, exchange, promises, and departure are functions rather than mandatory props. Every chosen form should answer three questions: what would an observer see, what do Tomas and Neris say it means, and what survives if rain moves the ceremony to another site?

Do not let an object complete a function merely by appearing. The boat does not establish adoption unless entry, reciprocal duties, correction, and exit are legible. The veil does not establish Vale standing unless Neris accepts the stated recognition. Music does not establish joining if one musician or one partner merely disappears into the background.

A shortened rain ceremony may remove procession, dance, decoration, and public exchanges while preserving all six functions. This is adaptation, not a lesser ending.

## Names and vows need not become symmetrical

A mixed marriage does not require matching surnames, matching bynames, parallel gifts, or mirrored household acts. Neris may retain a changing river name while Tomas retains a stable Vale name. Tomas may enter river kinship without receiving a ceremonial byname. The record should hold stable identity, current answered name, accepted byname history, parentage, dwellings, and household standing in separate fields.

Poetic vows are welcome only after each partner can paraphrase continuing, renewed, interrupted, and released duties. A personal promise may remain outside the enforceable compact. An unresolved clause may remain visible. Symmetry and completeness are not substitutes for consent.

## Movement and music do not manufacture assent

Processions, congregational responses, dances, and joining tunes should invite participation rather than use bodies to imply agreement. Guests may remain seated, silent, or visibly abstaining. Duty-bearing words require silence. The first shared departure step belongs to Tomas and Neris, not to a musical cue that moves them before they choose.

Two musical traditions need distinct leadership, not merely simultaneous sound. A shared pulse can be a genuine office, but it should not be called equal participation while one melody receives all recognition. The score should state who leads, who answers, where the measures join, and how the structure changes at each fallback site.

## Revelations are affordances

Most revelations in this adventure do not expose concealed guilt. They establish what is possible:

- an heirloom can change use without being discarded;
- adoption can add duties without erasing kinship;
- a record can preserve changing names;
- wet and dry ground can share one site;
- two rhythms can meet without one becoming background;
- disagreement can remain visible inside a valid compact.

Use material examples, records, demonstrations, and candid statements as clues. Discovery opens choices instead of merely explaining the past.

## Comedy without condescension

The comedy comes from capable people making earnest preparations through incomplete models of one another. The protected trout, the iron ring, the stubborn veil, invented cousin-customs, moving rehearsal markers, and incompatible dance counts are comic. Neither household is the joke.

Use consequences smaller and more personal than civic catastrophe: a grandmother feels excluded, a gift is withheld, a course goes cold, an aisle floods, a tune falls apart, or a witness cannot honestly sign one clause.

## The ceremony is the state display

Every prior decision appears in the finale through visible components:

- who stands on wet or dry ground;
- what names are spoken and recorded;
- how Tomas enters river kinship;
- how Neris enters or retains mortal household standing;
- what happens to veil, iron, stone, boat, and compact;
- how grievances are received;
- what the musicians do at the joining point;
- which foods are served and credited;
- what remains unresolved; and
- how the couple departs together.

Do not summarize these choices into a generic “beautiful ceremony.” Play the six functions and let the chosen form reveal the adventure’s history.

## Pressure must be public and negotiable

Weather, unfinished work, guest friction, and unresolved obligations create pressure only when the table can see the cause and consequence. Announce changes. Let players trade decoration, scale, route, labor, or public participation for safety and clarity. Never convert a clock into authority over consent.

Pressure should expose what the current design depends on. Rain reveals whether the ceremony has a fallback. Late work reveals whether an object is functional or ornamental. Guest friction reveals whether accommodation was confirmed by name. An open clause reveals whether the ceremony can preserve a boundary. None of these should introduce an unknown taboo after the party has made a reasonable choice.

## Rehearsal is new consent evidence

The full rehearsal is not merely logistical. Tomas and Neris hear relatives state duties, see how symbols appear from the crowd, walk the actual routes, and experience the difference between the drafted ceremony and the ceremony other people intend to perform.

Question them separately after rehearsal and again before public consent. A changed answer is not inconsistency to overcome. It is evidence that the process clarified what the choice would mean.

## The old flood should improve a handoff, not consume grief

The surviving evidence supports several truths: early river warnings existed, they were not translated into mill measures, evacuation remained possible when mortal bell time arrived, and Perrin chose to remain at the wheel. Do not collapse these truths into one verdict.

The useful product is a paired warning protocol with thresholds, watchers, acknowledgment, emergency authority, evacuation language, and review. Agnes and Serein may practice it together, state separate truths, approve it without testimony, assign a later hearing, or refuse a joint act. Forgiveness is not a ceremonial resource the party must extract.

## Certification is scoped observation

The witness folio records persons, consent, household standing, property and office boundaries, exchange, promises, departure, open matters, adaptation, and certification. It does not contain a field for universal family assent.

Witnesses may certify a marriage with explicit boundaries, certify the marriage while deferring adoption, observe a ceremony without institutional certification, record postponement, or sign different scopes. They should state exactly what they observed and exactly what they refuse to claim. Certification is not a prize for producing the most unified tableau.

## Failure-forward rule

Failure changes recognition, trust, participation, cost, preparation, or record before it ends the marriage.

Examples:

- A mishandled veil causes Agnes to withhold it, making mortal lineage recognition explicit rather than ending the wedding.
- A failed ring discussion leads Orren to keep the iron, forcing another exchange.
- Late food corrections reduce the feast or split courses.
- A poor rehearsal produces separate processions that meet only at the final threshold.
- Rain moves the ceremony to a less inclusive site.
- One unresolved clause is assigned to later hearing.
- The couple postpones freely.
- The witnesses refuse certification while the couple proceeds.

Never punish a reasonable choice with a custom revealed only afterward.

## Calendar, clue flow, authority, and ceremony state are separate

A known place is not necessarily understood, an understood option is not necessarily accepted, and an accepted personal promise is not necessarily a household commitment. Track the calendar, clue graph, authority graph, and ceremony state separately.

All six preparation encounters are physically available after the opening. Rehearsal and wedding occur on the calendar whether or not every earlier encounter was visited. Clues provide affordances, precedent, and corroboration. They should never be interpreted as magical access locks.

## Authority is bounded and named

Every consequential answer needs a speaker and a sphere. Tomas and Neris control their own marriage, names, personal promises, and duties within their authority. Other people control their own property, office, labor, testimony, participation, household commitment, or safety judgment. Witnesses control only the scope of their certification.

A refusal should state what is withheld. Do not let “the family,” “the river,” “the shrine,” or “the market” become imaginary unanimous actors. Internal division is playable information.

## Split parties preserve location and acceptance

Divided witnesses may observe, ask, reserve reversible resources, draft, and carry offers. They may not accept for an absent principal. Track where traveling NPCs are and how messages move. On reunion distinguish observed, proposed, accepted, conditional, refused, contested, and unasked terms.

A witness signs only what they directly observed or explicitly verified later. Different scopes are allowed.

## Late discoveries reopen the smallest scope

Classify a late fact by whether it changes consent or authority, safety or access, gift or performance meaning, or historical corroboration. Pause and repeat only the affected function. Adapt unsafe elements. Transform or omit misunderstood gifts. Append corroboration to the record or later hearing.

Never reveal an unknown taboo after a reasonable decision and declare the whole wedding spoiled. The adventure's tension comes from explicit costs and boundaries, not retrospective traps.

## Necessary encounters are not compulsory scenes

In the authoring model, all nine encounters remain necessary so each stays discoverable and structurally integrated. At the table, players may skip any preparation encounter or the rehearsal. The consequence is narrower evidence, smaller scale, deferred duties, withheld recognition, or reduced certification—not a blocked finale.

## Demonstration proves methods can overreach

The Four Open Hands are not neutral because they lack opinions. They are neutral because their authority is narrow and their methods remain answerable. Adel can flatten a real difference into one vocabulary; Kest can convert safety judgment into control; Sella can use comfort to hurry obligation; Oren can stage harmony before it exists. The playthrough succeeds when each witness narrows a useful competence rather than abandoning it.

## A successful ending may remain formally rough

The demonstrated wedding preserves two household descriptions, asymmetric names, an empty abstention token, separate old-flood statements, different household signatures, and witness-specific scopes. These are not unfinished drafting artifacts. They are the visible form of consent and authority that were not manufactured into unanimity.

## The journal is part of the ethical machinery

Append-only correction matters especially in a social adventure. Adel's overstatement about Agnes's veil proposal is not silently edited away. The record shows the premature claim, the reason it was voided, and the narrower conditional statement. This prevents the witnesses' summary from becoming stronger than the people they observed.

## Aftermath tests offices rather than romance

The first spawning closure, hard freeze, flood warning, illness, and next joining light test the practical terms. They should not reveal hidden customs or prove that one household description was secretly correct. The durable question is whether named people give notice, exercise bounded authority, preserve correction, and honor the couple's movement between dwellings.
