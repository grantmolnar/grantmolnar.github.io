# Full Playthrough: The Four Open Hands

The Four Open Hands take one workable route through *A Wedding for the River*. Across three table sessions they visit all nine encounters, divide twice, spot fifty-nine of seventy-two clues, explicitly miss one useful old-flood clue, correct a split-party overstatement through the append-only journal, and certify a marriage whose shared duties retain two honest household descriptions. The other twelve unseen clues are redundancy the route never needs, not opportunities the witnesses knowingly fail.

The route is:

**House of Open Measure -> Vale Mill / House Beneath the Willows -> House of Open Measure -> Market / Shrine -> Reedwright's Yard / Bridge of Three Tunes -> Flood Meadow -> Wedding Between the Banks.**

The household scenes and both pairs of preparation scenes run in parallel. The journal follows table order; it never implies that one witness occupies two places at once.

## Session One: The Same Words, Two Marriages

### The House of Open Measure

The Four Open Hands arrive expecting to witness a compact whose ordinary terms are already agreed. Instead, Mera Quill opens the two sealed household readings, places them beneath paired stones, and asks Tomas Vale and Neris Reed-in-Rain to explain the three ceremonial lines: “Tomas enters the house of Neris. Neris keeps the wheel. Each bears the other’s name before water and flame.”

Mera explains why the contradiction appears now. Each household filed its marginal reading six weeks earlier, but the House opens paired readings together only when the free-witness period begins. Neither side was allowed to revise its interpretation after seeing the other’s. Tomas and Neris approved their intention to marry and many ordinary arrangements; they never approved either margin. Mera handled both filings and will draft the final folio, so she can advise but cannot certify her own record.

Tomas says the eastern rooms will be their shared home, with river duties taking either spouse elsewhere when needed. Neris says the eastern rooms and willow house will both remain homes, neither subordinate to the other. Tomas describes one household in two dwellings. Neris describes two continuing households joined by marriage. Both think the other has agreed to the practical life they discussed. Neither realizes the public phrase will be heard differently by both families.

Adel immediately proposes suspending the commission until the sentence can be replaced by one definition. Mera refuses the premise without refusing the concern. The House certifies who accepted what; it does not require every cross-bank agreement to adopt one metaphysics of household. She asks the witnesses first to determine whether the different descriptions conceal incompatible powers or merely preserve different recognitions.

The party conducts the private consent sequence. Kest waits outside both interviews because her presence makes questions about physical safety feel like instructions. Sella asks each principal what ordinary day they are choosing, not what ceremony they want. Oren asks each to paraphrase the other's current position without defending it. Adel records the answers separately.

Tomas chooses marriage now if mill property, debt, and succession remain outside marriage. He accepts exact river duties and does not require Neris to abandon the willow house. Neris chooses marriage now if adoption creates real reciprocal obligations, her names remain hers, and neither family acquires default custody over a sick spouse or hypothetical child. Each can state the other's answer accurately. Neither accepts a single household label.

The witnesses inspect the House's deed books, prior dual-household compact, alias chain, care ledger, source-declaration form, old adoption diagram, grievance ledger, and crossed-out household columns. Their first findings open procedures; none settles the marriage:

- marriage need not transfer the mill or water lease;
- river adoption can add duties without erasing Vale kinship;
- changing river names can be preserved through a stable witness mark and appended name chain;
- food, lodging, access, and participation must be assigned to named guests rather than “mortals” and “river folk”;
- care must name people and duties rather than grant custody to a household; and
- the threshold boat is supposed to permit entry, correction, and return, not carry Tomas away.

The household-description question remains open. The party divides before the market closes. Adel and Sella go with Tomas to the mill. Kest and Oren go with Neris beneath the willows. Each group may observe, ask, and draft proposals. Neither may report a household's willingness as acceptance by Tomas or Neris.

**State on departure:** both principals presently choose marriage; household description unresolved; property, names, adoption, care, hospitality, grievances, and threshold meaning identified as separate scopes; witnesses divided with explicit reporting limits.

### Vale Mill and Hearth — Adel and Sella

The mill is running too loudly for Agnes Vale to hear ordinary speech. She brings the white flax veil into the wheelhouse because nobody has yet told her that the room makes communication impossible. Sella moves the conversation to the flour loft, seats Agnes where she can see every speaker, and asks what the veil is meant to give.

Agnes answers that it places Neris among the Vale dead and living. She wore it at her wedding and laid it over Perrin after the flood. When Adel asks whether Neris must cover her face, Agnes says the face is not the point; the kept object is. She would accept the veil over the compact chest, above a threshold, or as a memorial cloth if Neris names the Vale dead rather than receiving Tomas as though he began at the wedding.

Mara Vale approaches the marriage through work. She separates four authorities that everyone else has been calling “the mill”: deed and machinery, mill operation and wages, domestic rooms and household purchasing, and water compliance under the Concord lease. Neris may hold a domestic key, attend worker councils, sign water notices, and stop the wheel under an agreed spawning or flood threshold without receiving ownership, debt, wage authority, or succession. Mara wants Elsin's apprenticeship protected explicitly.

Elsin refuses to be discussed as the person displaced by a symmetrical settlement. She wants to know whether Neris will teach her to read water notices and whether Tomas will still keep the freeze watch. When Sella asks who would care for whom during illness, Elsin names tasks: Mara controls accounts, Tomas can lift and carry, Agnes sits night watches, and Elsin can run messages but cannot be sole carer. The room's abstract language improves immediately.

Adel finds the high-water cuts from Perrin's flood and notes that three warnings were received at different places with no named person responsible for relaying them. He does not yet know the river household's account, but the marks already contradict a single-betrayal story.

The stone gift causes the first real refusal. Mara has commissioned a smooth handstone cut from a retired millstone. She intends steadiness and shared work. Tomas admits that Neris heard “the wheel fixes your course.” Adel records the gift as proposed, not accepted. Sella asks whether it could support a water bowl at the exchange station, carrying Mara's labor without requiring Neris to hold it or wear it. Mara agrees to propose that use.

Before leaving, Adel drafts that Agnes has accepted a canopy use for the veil. Sella corrects him: Agnes accepted several possible transformations if Neris recognizes the household dead; she did not accept a final use. Adel marks the statement conditional.

**State on departure:** Neris can receive domestic and water-compliance authority without property or debt; Elsin's apprenticeship remains protected; Vale care is assigned by task; the handstone-as-bowl-support is proposed; the veil's intention is understood but its use remains conditional; the old flood already appears to involve failed relay.

### House Beneath the Willows — Kest and Oren

Neris's household receives the witnesses in ankle-deep running water beneath living willow roots. Kest spends several minutes measuring exits before Serein-at-Alderfall asks whether she plans to certify the floor. Kest answers that she plans to know who cannot stand in it. Deep-Reed, an elder who travels in a basin chair, approves the distinction.

Serein defines adoption through six duties rather than general obedience: Tomas must answer recognized warnings, stop the wheel and open the lower sluice at the agreed threshold, ring the mortal bell and clear the yard, honor spawning closures, relay downstream warnings when able, and appear at a mixed hearing before using force in a water dispute. The river household must in return issue recognizable warnings, preserve an exit, rescue within its capacity, provide evidence at hearings, respect Tomas's bodily limits, and give the mill enough notice to perform duties honestly.

Kest tests every signal. One relies on hearing a willow chime from inside the wheelhouse, which Agnes cannot do and machinery may mask for everyone. Rain-at-Noon produces the borrowed hand bell used during Perrin's flood. Its handle is split; the warning had no designated mortal receiver and no fallback when the bell-bearer was swept downstream. Kest proposes three redundant signals and a named handoff rather than a ceremonial promise that “the mill will answer.”

Orren Underbridge presents a closed iron ring. He forged it because iron holds under effort. Neris explains that closed metal around the body signifies captivity beneath the current. Orren does not defend the shape once he understands the received meaning, but he refuses to replace his work with a reed token chosen by someone else. Kest shows how the band can be opened into a visible spiral and bound across the gap with rush, so the iron remains worked and the opening remains legible. Orren agrees to attempt the transformation and to let Neris decide whether to accept it.

Oren asks Neris how she wishes to be announced. She keeps Reed-in-Rain as her current answered name, her parentage, and the House's stable witness mark. “Of Vale Mill” may describe a dwelling. “Neris Vale” does not. Tomas receives no invented river byname. Oren initially suggests a paired naming response in which both receive a new marriage epithet. Neris declines. He records the asymmetry rather than improving it.

Rain-at-Noon explains the river grievance custom. It is not license to insult the couple; it names an obligation and the person asked to answer it. He wants the old flood spoken because both banks still train children through incompatible stories. Serein wants Agnes to admit the mill ignored a warning. Oren asks whether either woman must reconcile for the wedding to proceed. Rain says no. A new warning practice matters more than a public apology, although separate true statements may belong in the record.

Deep-Reed lists three river guests with incompatible needs: one must remain within sound of running water, one cannot sleep beside it, and one cannot cross a narrow flexible mat. Kest sends the exact chair width and turning radius to Sella through a market runner.

**State on departure:** adoption duties and reciprocal river duties are bounded; the warning system needs three signals and named receivers; Orren will propose an open spiral; Neris's name chain is exact and asymmetric; the old flood need not become reconciliation; three river guests require particular accommodations.

### Return to the House of Open Measure

The groups return separately with the split-party correction already visible in the journal. Adel had written that Agnes “accepted the veil as a canopy”; Sella caught that the choice remained conditional on Neris naming the Vale dead. The overstatement remains as a voided operation, followed by the narrower note. Mera uses the pair to remind everyone that a proposal carried by witnesses is not acceptance by an absent principal.

Tomas and Neris hear every proposal in one another's presence. They accept the separation of mill authorities, Neris's domestic key and water-correspondent role, Elsin's protected apprenticeship, the stable name chain, and the six adoption duties with reciprocal river obligations. They accept that care will be assigned to named adults. They do not yet accept the veil, ring, handstone, site, music, or exact vow form.

On household structure, each repeats the other's position accurately. Adel asks whether the different labels authorize different acts. After testing absence, care, keys, work, warnings, and return, the answer is not yet. Tomas still believes they are making one household in two dwellings. Neris still believes they are joining two households. The party records the difference as live but not presently operationally contradictory.

**Session close:** the couple continue to choose marriage; household description remains dual; household powers are separated; adoption and care modules are accepted in principle; all ceremony objects and public forms remain proposed.

## Session Two: Seven Baskets, Six Functions

### Market of Seven Baskets — Adel and Sella

Pella Nine-Spoons has arranged seven baskets across the market floor. The first contains a guest list that says “twenty-three river folk.” Sella crosses out the category and starts again by name. By midday, the wedding has fifty-two likely attendees, six uncertain attendees, four visible abstentions, and three people whose attendance depends on access or lodging.

The protected trout are still alive in a tub. Brin Quickwater bought them after hearing that river guests prefer food from running water. Sella makes him carry the tub back to the spawning channel with Jory Salt and two children who helped catch them. The return is not humiliation; Brin must name the wrong assumption, the source he failed to check, and the labor required to repair it. The children receive payment for the original catch and the return.

Food becomes a source ledger rather than a cultural menu: barley loaves from Vale grain, smoked eel from a licensed downstream weir, lentils, orchard fruit, toasted oats, herb broth, and Jory's salt box with named credit for miners, carters, and market cooks. Eating before the grievance interval becomes explicit. Nobody must remain hungry to prove seriousness.

Sella builds four table forms and chooses a broken-ring arrangement with two openings, one near running water and one on firm dry ground. Deep-Reed receives a broad level route and turning place. The guest who cannot sleep beside water takes Pella's upper room. The guest who must hear water takes the covered stall by the fountain. Agnes receives a seat where she can see speakers and has a younger person assigned to repeat public words in writing.

The gift basket forces declarations. Aunt Sal's cradle ribbon is deferred because neither couple nor witness will let a fertility wish imply an undertaking. The veil, iron spiral, handstone, and salt box are listed with intended honor, proposed use, receiver, and right of refusal. No object enters the ceremony merely because it arrived.

The grievance basket receives more entries than the twelve-minute interval can hold. Pella limits the public interval to four one-breath obligations. Others may be heard privately, deferred, or represented by visible abstention. Sella initially suggests omitting the empty response token because it will make the feast look divided. Adel insists it remain. Attendance and comfort cannot be entered as consent.

While reviewing delivery marks, the party misses Pella's old flood notation showing that a warning cart was rerouted after the north path washed out. The missed clue would have strengthened the handoff reconstruction. It is not the only route to that conclusion.

**State on departure:** named guest count, food sources, labor, access, lodging, gift declarations, four public grievances, private hearings, and visible abstentions are recorded; protected trout returned; one old-flood clue missed.

### Shrine of the Open Door — Kest and Oren

Keeper Ansel Rook refuses to begin with vows. He places six blank tablets on the floor: consent, household standing, names, exchange, promises, and departure. If a proposed ceremony cannot show all six and pause at each, it is not ready no matter how moving its language sounds.

Oren proposes image followed by plain speech. Tomas may speak of wheel and return; Neris may speak of current and answer. Each image must be followed by an ordinary-language clause stating the duty, interruption rule, correction procedure, and any open matter. Music stops during duty-bearing speech.

The name function preserves asymmetry. Neris is announced by current answered name, parentage, witness mark, and dwelling. Tomas remains Tomas Vale. Guests may repeat an authorized short form or remain silent. No crowd response creates a surname or byname.

The archive offers the decisive precedent. One respected compact records two household descriptions beside one schedule of duties. Another leaves an open clause visible under red cord. Ansel says the House can certify an honest boundary more readily than a false unanimity.

Kest tests the meeting, compact-first, and threshold orders for relocation. The threshold order offers the clearest physical logic but risks making the boat look like removal. The group retains it only if the boat is stationary, both partners can enter and leave, and the departure goes toward a neutral signing path rather than directly to mill or downstream water.

The pair carries four proposed decisions back to the principals: six visible functions, image followed by plain speech, two household descriptions followed by one duty schedule, and a threshold order with a neutral departure. Tomas and Neris accept all four as rehearsal forms, not final ceremony state.

**State on departure:** six-function rubric accepted; image-plus-plain vows selected for rehearsal; household descriptions may remain parallel; threshold order selected conditionally; pause and correction rules explicit.

### Reedwright's Yard — Kest and Sella

Dessa Reedwright has enough material for one generous site or two modest ones. Kest still favors the old wheel yard. Sella lays out the guest map and shows why the flood meadow can represent both banks without placing every body on wet ground. Dessa proposes raised dry mats around a narrow flowing rill, with the water entering and returning visibly rather than becoming a decorative basin.

Deep-Reed's measurements change the north approach. The original mat is too narrow and turns too sharply. Kest removes one ornamental screen, widens the platform, and assigns a firm side route that reaches the same household-standing station. The adjustment changes access, not ceremonial rank.

The threshold boat is lashed at four points in water shallow enough for an adult to step out. A dry bypass runs alongside it. Tomas and Neris may enter, speak the adoption terms, correct them, and step back out. Nobody is carried away. The boat remains available only if both test it at rehearsal.

The veil receives a physical proposal: stretched above the compact table as a narrow canopy with both ends visible, then lowered after the ceremony to wrap the witness folio. Neris can recognize the Vale dead in plain speech without wearing funeral dress. The iron spiral receives a rush-bound gap wide enough to remain visible from the rear seats. Mara's handstone supports the water bowl at the exchange station.

The site plan remains modular. If the water rises above the second meadow peg or the mat flexes under Deep-Reed's chair, the ceremony moves to shrine rise with the same six stations and no claim that relocation changes the marriage.

**State on departure:** flood meadow selected provisionally; raised dry mats and visible rill form one site; accessible side route added; threshold boat made stationary and optional; veil canopy, open iron, and handstone support ready for rehearsal; shrine rise is the exact fallback.

### Bridge of Three Tunes — Adel and Oren

Iven Crossbow can play the four-step Mill Road. Moss-in-Evening can lead the six-step Reed Turn. Each musician initially assumes the other will ornament the dominant tune. Oren makes them exchange leadership rather than accompaniment.

The procession will use simultaneous approaches. The Mill Road leads four pulses; the Reed Turn leads six; both arrive at the joining place after twelve. The Joining Measure begins only after Tomas and Neris have chosen to continue. It does not begin consent or complete a promise.

Adel insists on six silence cues. Before consent, household standing, names, exchange, promises, and departure, the music stops. The next cue begins only after the responsible person has spoken and any correction has been accepted. Guests may walk, stand, tap, remain seated, or abstain. No dance is required.

Oren wants Agnes and Serein's old-flood statements set over alternating chords. Adel asks whether either woman has agreed to perform with the other. Neither has. Oren removes music from the statements entirely. Their roughness remains visible.

The musicians test the joining point at bridge, meadow, yard, and shrine rise. Their arrangement can move without turning one tune into background. The party sends the score to Dessa with exact station lengths rather than assuming musicians can adapt around the built route.

**State on departure:** both traditions lead; joining occurs after twelve pulses; six silent cues protect the ceremony functions; participation is optional; old-flood statements remain unscored and separate; score can move to fallback site.

### House comparison before rehearsal

All four witnesses compare records with Tomas and Neris. The couple accepts the provisional site, threshold boat, six cues, image-plus-plain vows, gift uses, guest plan, and bounded grievance procedure for rehearsal. Neris accepts the veil-canopy proposal if she may name Perrin and the Vale dead in her own words. Agnes has not yet accepted that exact use. Tomas accepts the open iron proposal if Orren confirms the gap remains intended. Orren has not yet presented the finished object.

Adel now accepts that one operational schedule may accompany two household descriptions. He still marks the difference as an open clause rather than pretending it is settled.

**Session close:** all six functions have testable forms; site and fallback are defined; hospitality is particular; gifts remain subject to final declaration and acceptance; household description remains openly dual; rehearsal must test access, water, object visibility, warning handoff, and separate old-flood statements.

## Session Three: The Joining Light

### Flood Meadow and Old Ford

The visible pressure board begins at water one, work one, guests one, and open obligations two. Overnight rain has raised the rill but not crossed the relocation peg. Orren's spiral is finished. Two market benches are late. Agnes has not yet approved the final veil use. Serein and Agnes have each prepared a statement about Perrin's flood and refuse to read them together.

The first rehearsal pass uses only six markers. Tomas and Neris walk the sequence without relatives, gifts, music, or poetry. Both can stop at every function and explain what happens. Kest discovers that the threshold boat's south rope shortens its safe exit when weighted. Dessa relashes it and marks the dry bypass more clearly.

The second pass restores principals and corrections. Neris repeats Tomas's household description; Tomas repeats hers. The record reads both and then the shared duties. Nobody is asked to choose a superior label. Orren presents the iron spiral and says he offers endurance under effort without closure around the wearer. Neris accepts the intention and the open form.

The veil returns to the corrected journal entry. Agnes has offered several transformations but approved no final placement. The party now asks the missing question in front of Agnes and Neris.

Neris says she will stand beneath the veil only while it remains above the compact table, not over her body. She will name Perrin and the Vale dead, receive the object as a household trust, and permit it to wrap the final folio afterward. Agnes asks whether the river household will hear Perrin's name without answering it. Neris says yes. Agnes accepts the use. The corrected acceptance enters as a new event.

The third pass restores bodies, service, and weather. Deep-Reed uses the widened side route. Agnes can see the written repetitions. The guest who needs running water can remain beside the rill without blocking the dry route. A sudden shower proves that the veil canopy sheds water onto the name station. Kest and Dessa rotate it, preserving the function while changing the angle. Water rises to the second peg, then falls. Relocation remains available but is not required.

The fourth pass reconstructs the old flood. The Vale high-water cuts show when the wheelhouse learned danger. Rain's broken bell shows the attempted river warning. Pella's missed delivery notation is absent, but the witnesses still establish that no person held the whole handoff: the first river signal had no named mortal receiver, the mill's response depended on a bell inaudible in the wheelhouse, the north path washed out, and nobody had authority to switch to a second signal.

Kest proposes a future protocol with three signals: willow flags by daylight, paired hand bells at mill and willow house, and a runner chain through the market after dark. Mara, Rain-at-Noon, Elsin, and Jory Salt accept named roles. The protocol works if Agnes and Serein are absent.

Agnes reads one sentence: “The mill did not know soon enough, and we trained our anger as though knowing late were the same as refusing.” Serein reads another: “The river sent a warning no mortal office had promised to receive, and we trained our grief as though sending were the same as handing over.” They do not apologize, embrace, or perform together. Oren leaves the silence unfilled.

At rehearsal close, pressure stands at water one, work zero, guests zero, and open obligations one. The remaining open matter is household description. It does not contradict the shared duties. Tomas and Neris repeat their private choice and decide to proceed.

**State on departure:** all six functions work at meadow and shrine fallback; gifts accepted in declared forms; access tested; warning protocol accepted by named hands; old-flood accounts remain separate; household description remains dual; couple chooses to proceed.

### The Wedding Between the Banks

Before seating, Pella serves bread, broth, fruit, and toasted oats. The grievance interval will occur after the ceremony and before the main feast. The empty abstention token remains visible. Two benches arrive late and are placed at the outer dry table without moving Deep-Reed's route.

Mera conducts the final private repetition separately. Tomas and Neris each choose marriage, the current terms, the right to correct, and the right to stop. Neither changes the household description. Both authorize the shared operational schedule.

The ceremony begins with simultaneous approaches. Iven leads the Mill Road. Moss leads the Reed Turn. Four and six meet after twelve pulses. The music stops.

At consent, Tomas and Neris answer in their own words. No family responds for them.

At household standing, Adel reads both descriptions:

- Tomas Vale understands the marriage as one household in two dwellings.
- Neris Reed-in-Rain understands the marriage as two continuing households joined by marriage.

He then reads the shared residence, access, care, property, water, absence, and hearing duties. Tomas accepts bounded river adoption. Serein states the six duties. Rain-at-Noon states the reciprocal river obligations. Tomas corrects “enter dangerous water” to “assist within his ability and preserve a route for trained rescue,” and the correction is accepted. Agnes remains silent during the river-household response. Serein remains silent when Mara welcomes Neris into the eastern rooms. Their abstentions are recorded as particular, not as vetoes.

At names, Oren announces Neris Reed-in-Rain, child of Serein-at-Alderfall, identified by her stable witness mark and dwelling also at Vale Mill. Tomas remains Tomas Vale. No new surname or byname is created. Guests use the authorized short forms or remain silent.

At exchange, each giver declares an intention. Agnes offers the veil as trust in the Vale dead and living; Neris accepts it as canopy and folio wrapping, not as dress. Orren offers worked iron that endures effort while remaining open; Neris and Tomas accept the spiral clasp. Mara offers the handstone as the labor beneath a shared vessel; it supports the water bowl and passes afterward into the eastern rooms as an ordinary household object. Jory's salt box enters the feast ledger rather than the marriage compact. The cradle ribbon remains with Aunt Sal.

At promises, Tomas speaks of a wheel that can stop without ceasing to be a mill. He then states the ordinary duties: shared dwelling, free access to both homes, work notice, care, warning response, correction, and hearing. Neris speaks of a current that changes banks without becoming unanswerable. She states the same duties in her own order and adds the stable name chain. Each paraphrases the other's operative promise. Mera reads three open matters: the household description remains dual; future-child standing is not decided; mill succession remains governed by the Vale deed and river standing does not alter it.

At departure, Tomas and Neris enter the stationary threshold boat, hear the adoption duties once more, and step out together onto the neutral path toward the House of Open Measure. The Joining Measure begins only after they choose the step. They sign the folio before turning toward the common table. No road in the ceremony declares either home the only home.

The bounded grievance interval follows food. Four obligations are spoken. Agnes asks the river household to preserve Perrin's name in the later flood hearing. Rain asks the mill to post the new warning protocol before the next storm. Deep-Reed asks the market to retain the widened route at future Concord gatherings. Elsin asks Tomas not to assign away his freeze watch without telling her. Each receives a named answer. Other grievances remain private or deferred.

The Four Open Hands sign **marriage certified with stated boundaries**. Their scopes are entered:

- Adel signs consent, property separation, household terms, open clauses, and record corrections.
- Kest signs access, site adaptation, threshold safety, warning duties, and the practical limits of adoption.
- Sella signs hospitality, named care, gift declarations, abstentions, and grievance procedure.
- Oren signs names, public meaning, vow paraphrase, silence cues, and departure.

Mera signs the House certification. Agnes signs the Vale household welcome but not the river-adoption recognition line. Serein signs the river adoption and reciprocal obligations but not the one-household description. Mara, Rain-at-Noon, Elsin, Jory, Dessa, Ansel, Iven, Moss, Orren, and Pella sign only the offices or duties they actually accepted.

The wedding is recognized across both banks as a marriage with boundaries. It does not end the argument over what a household is. It makes that argument less able to control keys, water, care, names, property, or the couple's movement.

**Final state:** Tomas and Neris are married and cross-bank certification is issued with stated boundaries; bounded river adoption is accepted; the same operational schedule carries two household descriptions; gifts are transformed and declared; abstentions remain visible; the old flood yields a new warning protocol and separate true statements rather than reconciliation; future-child standing and any later river byname remain open.

## What this route demonstrates

### Split parties without transferred acceptance

The household and preparation encounters are resolved in parallel. Each group reports observations, proposals, conditions, and actual acceptances. The canopy overstatement is not quietly repaired. It is corrected through the append-only journal, and Agnes's final acceptance occurs only after Neris states the received use in her presence.

### A complete route that is not a compulsory route

The Four Open Hands visit all nine encounters because this demonstration tests every subsystem. The adventure does not require that itinerary. Session 6 fallbacks would permit a shorter circuit, a skipped craft encounter, a reduced rehearsal, one-sided household participation, or postponement.

### A missed clue that changes confidence, not possibility

The party misses Pella's old delivery marks. They still reconstruct the old flood through the Vale high-water cuts and Rain's borrowed bell. The missing clue would add detail about the washed-out north route; it does not contain the only explanation or only means of building a new warning handoff.

### A late fact that reopens only one scope

Deep-Reed's chair width changes the side route, not household standing. The veil's conditional acceptance reopens the exchange function, not consent, names, vows, or the entire wedding. Rain changes the canopy angle, not the meaning Neris and Agnes accepted.

### NPC control remains bounded

Agnes controls her veil, memory, welcome, and signature. Serein controls river-household adoption and recognition. Mara controls mill offices and the Vale deed within her authority. Craftspeople control their labor and safety. Musicians control performance. Mera and the player characters control only what they can honestly certify. Tomas and Neris alone decide whether to marry.

### Constructive success without unanimity

The folio closes with two household descriptions, an open name-chain, different family signatures, and one empty response token beside the feast bowls. Agnes and Serein have not forgiven on command. Tomas and Neris have not discovered that one of them secretly used the correct word for home. The mill wheel still stops for water, and the river still asks the mill to hear farther than its gate.

What changed is smaller and more durable. Keys, warnings, care, property, gifts, names, routes, and refusals now belong to people who can answer for them. The wedding succeeds because its roughness has owners.
