# Wedding Ledger: A Wedding for the River

## Causal spine

1. **Perrin's flood leaves two unfinished duties.** The Vales remember a man who stayed at the wheel after the bell; the river household remembers warnings that never became a named mortal handoff.
2. **Tomas and Neris solve ordinary life more easily than household language.** They agree on rooms, work, water, property, and much of care, then let their elders choose public words because neither wants the wedding to reject a grieving household.
3. **The sealed readings expose opposed repairs.** Both households use the same old formula. Vale margins make Neris part of the mill household; river margins make Tomas part of the current's duty.
4. **Preparations make the disagreement bodily.** Veil, iron, stone, fish, names, seats, signals, music, and routes reveal who would bear each interpretation.
5. **The wedding displays chosen scope rather than synthesized harmony.** Tomas and Neris may accept duties, retain different descriptions, transform gifts, preserve abstentions, defer adoption, postpone, or proceed beyond what the House can certify.

## Filing timeline

- Six weeks before the wedding, each household lodges a sealed reading copy with Mera.
- Tomas and Neris have signed their intention to marry and approved many ordinary arrangements, not either household's marginal claims.
- Three evenings before the wedding, the free-witness period begins and the paired readings are opened together.
- Mera may advise and draft but may not independently certify the record she received and will prepare.
- Rehearsal and wedding remain scheduled; postponement remains costly but safe.

## Terms of belonging

### Residence

- The eastern rooms are a shared dwelling with equal domestic authority.
- The willow house remains a recognized dwelling, not a permitted visit.
- No fixed count of nights determines loyalty or abandonment.
- The unresolved descriptive fork is one household in two dwellings versus two households joined by marriage.

### Mill and water

- Property, operating authority, domestic authority, water compliance, and succession remain separate.
- Neris may serve as a water correspondent without becoming owner, debtor, or universal manager.
- Tomas may accept water duties without speaking for the whole river household.

### Bounded adoption

Tomas may accept recognized signals, wheel stoppage, sluice opening, evacuation, spawning enforcement, warning relay, rescue, and mixed hearing. The river household must reciprocate with intelligible signals, relevant readings, inclusive rescue, recovery care, error hearings, and no concealed loyalty tests.

Adoption does not transfer property, cancel Vale kinship, impose unrestricted labor, require dangerous water entry beyond Tomas’s ability, or assign future children.

### Names

- Neris retains a stable witness identity and an append-only chain of accepted bynames.
- Parentage and dwellings may be recorded separately.
- “Of Vale Mill” may describe residence; “Neris Vale” is not presently chosen.
- Tomas remains Tomas Vale and receives no invented wedding byname.

### Care and future children

- The conscious person chooses where care occurs.
- Prior directions and the spouse’s judgment govern incapacity except in immediate survival emergencies.
- Helping with care creates gratitude and reciprocal aid, not custody or property.
- Future children may appear in both the mortal registry and river care ledger without either entry deciding permanent standing.
- Shelter, food, rescue, teaching, notice, guardianship, immersion, apprenticeship, inheritance, and residence must be assigned separately.

## Private consent record

For each unsettled subject, record:

1. what the person positively wants;
2. what the person can accept;
3. what the person will not accept;
4. what the person does not yet understand;
5. whether the other partner can paraphrase the duty and retained limit; and
6. whether the person repeats the choice after language is drafted.

Use “free,” “conditional,” “not yet,” or “refused.” Do not require private reasons for postponement to be entered in the public compact.

## Hospitality decisions

### Guests and access

- Build the guest register by name; household affiliation cannot settle attendance, lodging, diet, access, or participation.
- Agnes needs low noise, stable seating, and sight of speakers rather than ceremonial precedence.
- Deep-Reed needs a dry backed chair with moving water at her feet.
- Noll Ferry needs dry, quiet lodging; Lilt-before-Storm needs audible water rather than wet bedding.
- A guest may attend while abstaining from a named course, grievance, toast, gift, or dance.

### Food, source, and labor

- Return the twelve protected trout to the upper reed gate before noon.
- Name growers, gatherers, catchers, traders, cooks, carriers, and obligations for every course.
- Jory Salt’s sea catch remains paid and credited labor, not an anonymous ethical substitution.
- Pella can serve forty-two people one hot course at once; fifty requires split service, cold food, or more cooks.
- Every task names worker, shift, replacement, and stopping point; Tomas and Neris are not default labor.

### Gifts

Every public gift requires material, maker, source, intended honor, receiver paraphrase, status, authorized alteration, and public wording. Status may be accepted as made, transformed, private, deferred, or refused.

### Grievances

Rain-at-Noon and Mera receive entries by noon. No more than four public entries fit a twelve-minute interval. Each names a duty and next act; responses may acknowledge, state discharge, contest with hearing, or decline public reply with private stewardship. The old flood stays outside the interval unless Agnes and Serein jointly request a narrow statement.

## Six certifiable functions

1. Free and separately verified consent.
2. Known household standing and duties.
3. Recorded and recognized names.
4. An exchange whose meaning is declared.
5. Promises whose endurance and limits are intelligible.
6. A public departure together by the couple’s choice.

## Rehearsal and wedding decisions

### Visible pressure

- Water uses four public marks: Grass, Threads, Crossings, and Relocate.
- Unfinished work is a named task list with four substantial work turns before dusk.
- Guest pressure records particular people, stewards, accommodations, abstentions, and refusals.
- Open obligations are ready, conditional, open for later, or unready; only unready terms require revision, omission, postponement, or withheld certification.
- Pressure changes only through visible fictional causes and never overrides consent.

### Four rehearsal passes

1. Empty ceremony: walk consent, household standing, names, exchange, promises, and departure without props.
2. Principals and corrections: restore actual speakers, wording, gifts, and music; record every changed term.
3. Bodies, service, and weather: test access, food routes, gift withdrawal, boat safety, music, and announced relocation.
4. Old flood handoff: place alder braid, depth staff, delivery slate, borrowed bell, and Perrin's tool line in one chronology.

After the second pass and at rehearsal close, question Tomas and Neris separately. Rehearsal is new information.

A future protocol requires paired thresholds, named watchers, receiving-side acknowledgment, authority to stop the wheel, unambiguous evacuation, and mixed review.
