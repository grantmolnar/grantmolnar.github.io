# Witness Quickstart

Use this sheet to begin the demonstrated party or to build a similar free-witness group.

## The commission

You may:

- question principals and household speakers;
- compare meanings and consequences;
- inspect records, sites, gifts, food, routes, and warning systems;
- draft proposals and preserve alternatives;
- pause a represented function when consent, authority, safety, or meaning is unclear; and
- sign only the scope you can honestly attest.

You may not:

- accept for an absent person;
- turn family assent into the couple's consent;
- treat attendance, food, music, gifts, or silence as assent;
- require one household description when shared duties are otherwise clear;
- command another person's property, labor, office, testimony, or participation; or
- decide whether Tomas and Neris marry.

## The Four Open Hands

| Witness | Primary lens | Useful question | Temptation to resist |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adel Berren | wording, authority, record | Who may do what, when, and under which limit? | forcing one vocabulary to prove one agreement |
| Kest Larch | safety, access, handoff | Where does weight, water, warning, and exit go? | solving the site before learning what it must mean |
| Sella Broth | hospitality, labor, particular needs | Which named person needs this, and who provides it? | using comfort to hurry past an obligation |
| Oren Chalk | ceremony, voice, public meaning | What will the back row think happened? | staging harmony or consent through performance |

## Opening questions for Tomas and Neris

Ask separately:

1. Do you choose marriage now?
2. What ordinary life do you believe you are choosing?
3. Which duties do you accept?
4. Which powers do you refuse to transfer?
5. What may change later without ending the marriage?
6. What would make you pause today?

Then ask each to paraphrase the other's answer. A correct paraphrase is not acceptance; it proves understanding.

## Split-party report

For every divided scene, record:

- who was physically present;
- what was observed;
- what was proposed;
- what was accepted, by whom, and in whose presence;
- what remained conditional, refused, contested, or unasked; and
- what exact question must be carried back.

Do not write “the household agrees” when one person offered a possibility.

## Ceremony state

Track each function separately:

| Function | Accepted form | Pause condition | Fallback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consent |  | either principal cannot freely repeat the choice | private repetition or postponement |
| Household standing |  | speaker adds authority or duty not accepted | read narrower term or defer that standing |
| Names |  | unauthorized surname, byname, or crowd response | correct publicly and continue |
| Exchange |  | intention or received meaning differs | transform, defer, withdraw, or refuse object |
| Promises |  | poetry cannot be paraphrased into accepted duties | use plain clauses or postpone promise function |
| Departure |  | route implies a household destination not chosen, or becomes unsafe | use neutral or fallback route |

## Four pressures

Advance only for visible causes.

- **Water:** current or footing changes.
- **Work:** required construction, food, object, or record is unfinished.
- **Guests:** named access, lodging, service, or participation is unresolved.
- **Open obligations:** a represented duty, grievance, gift meaning, or authority remains unclear.

Pressure creates choices and narrower forms. It never supplies consent.

## Final witness scopes

A witness may sign all or part of:

- consent;
- household standing;
- names;
- property and authority limits;
- adoption and reciprocal duties;
- care;
- exchange and gift declarations;
- promises and open clauses;
- access, site, and adaptation;
- hospitality and abstention;
- departure; and
- overall certification.

Differences among witness scopes remain in the record. Unanimity is not required.
