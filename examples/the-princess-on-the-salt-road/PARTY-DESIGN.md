# Party Design: The Narrow Shield

The Narrow Shield is the household name for the four people Princess Ianthe keeps close enough to hear an order without a herald. It is not an elite formation. Aulon’s ceremonial guard can ring a royal litter with forty bronze shields; Kallias controls most of them before the adventure begins. The Narrow Shield performs the smaller and harder work: seeing which hand reaches for a cup, deciding whether a crowd is cover or killing ground, carrying an unwelcome answer back to the principal, and standing near enough that obedience cannot be mistaken for possession.

The characters are system-neutral and deliberately incomplete. Each counts danger differently—spear lengths, false trails, deniable facts, or bodily capacity—and each can mistake one professional measure for the whole duty. None is the company's conscience. Their disagreements should alter marching order, witnesses, custody, route, and the meaning of success rather than remain private characterization between encounters.

## Damon Keryx

**Function:** Senior household shield, formation leader, and reader of armed intent.

**What he brings:** Damon knows how soldiers close a road, how cavalry prepares a seizure without announcing one, how to move a principal through a crowd, and how long a small rear guard can hold a threshold before “holding” becomes dying in place. He carries a broad Aulonite shield without royal devices, a short spear, a peace cord, two chalk colors for route marks, and King Leandros's old order naming the household guard answerable to Ianthe in her own person.

**Prior connection:** Damon served Leandros during the winter campaign and returned with half a company. He was on the palace stair when Kallias's men arrested the council, but Ianthe ordered him not to begin a battle inside the women's court. He obeyed. He has not decided whether that obedience saved the household or surrendered the city.

**Opening position:** Ianthe must remain within reach of at least two sworn guards until Myrine assumes custody. Damon distrusts route division, civilian auxiliaries, and any bargain that asks the bodyguards to step away before the legal transfer is complete.

**Private fear:** He fears that Ianthe chose him because she knows he will obey even when the order is tactically wrong. He also fears becoming the man who says “for your safety” while doing exactly what Kallias intends to do.

**Red line:** No claimant may move Ianthe out of sight under the language of private examination, medical necessity, kinship, or temporary protection unless she names the terms herself before a witness.

**Temptation:** Damon may preserve physical custody by overruling Ianthe's decision, blocking her public appearance, or refusing a route split that would materially improve the expedition's chances.

**How he argues:** He names distance, fields of fire, numbers, and the point after which retreat becomes impossible. When someone says a witness or treaty makes violence unlikely, Damon asks who is close enough to strike before the witness can speak.

**Table voice:** Brief, formal with Ianthe, and plain with everyone else. He does not threaten people he expects to fight. “I heard the claim. Name the hands nearest her when she answers.”

**Growth demanded:** Damon must learn that the last duty of a bodyguard may be to release physical custody into a lawful arrangement the principal has chosen. His best ending protects the free crossing rather than treating continued possession as proof of loyalty.

## Phaedra Neris

**Function:** Royal courier, outrider, signal reader, and architect of route division.

**What she brings:** Phaedra knows every public milepost between Aulon and Myrine, courier badges, remount customs, shutter codes, false-trail craft, and how professional pursuers distinguish a decoy from discarded clothing. She carries two courier mantles, a bronze seal case padded to imitate its weight when empty, lamp-black, wax tablets, a compact bow, and three route messages written in Ianthe's hand but left unsealed until their destination is chosen.

**Prior connection:** Phaedra carried Ianthe's letters to Serathiel for seven years and knows how often maternal concern arrived as instructions about where Ianthe should live. She also carried Kallias's legitimate wartime dispatches before the coup. Dorion knows her face, pace, and habit of changing horses before a mount is fully blown.

**Opening position:** The escort survives by making the pursuit solve more than one credible problem. Phaedra wants the bronze seal, an identified household rider, or a public order to travel separately from Ianthe as soon as the Narrow Shield leaves Aulon.

**Private fear:** She fears that she values a clever division more than the people assigned to each branch. She has sent riders into bad country because their route bought time for someone more important. She also fears that Dorion will read every stratagem because she learned the trade from the same courier service he commands.

**Red line:** A decoy must know the danger, carry something genuinely worth pursuing, and possess a fallback. No servant, villager, or page is a disposable false princess.

**Temptation:** Phaedra may turn public trust into signal material, using a truthful witness or royal seal order chiefly for the trail it creates rather than for the obligation it imposes.

**How she argues:** She asks what the pursuer will believe, what physical evidence supports that belief, and when it can be corrected. She distinguishes hiding from misdirection: hiding removes information; misdirection spends a true fact in the wrong place.

**Table voice:** Fast, amused when frightened, and professionally disrespectful toward vague plans. “That is not a decoy. It is a cloak asking cavalry to be stupid.”

**Growth demanded:** Phaedra must accept that the public account cannot always be sacrificed to tactical surprise. Her best work sends the seal away from Ianthe while preserving exact witnesses, recognition signs, and a route by which the divided groups can answer for one another.

## Thaleia Ormon

**Function:** Assembly clerk, household advocate, and keeper of voluntary witness.

**What she brings:** Thaleia knows Aulon's gate charters, Myrine's petitioner law, emergency protector proclamations, the difference between custody and assent, and the forms by which testimony survives after soldiers control the room. She carries blank witness leaves, thread for bundling duplicate records, the Assembly's last valid roll, a narrow writing case, and copies of the decree by which Ianthe appointed each member of the Narrow Shield.

**Prior connection:** Thaleia recorded the Assembly session Kallias closed. She saw citizens who opposed the crown object to the arrests and royalists applaud them because they expected Kallias to protect Ianthe. Her father is among the detained councilors. Kallias has offered to release him if Thaleia certifies that grief made Ianthe unfit to direct her own household.

**Opening position:** Ianthe should appear before named witnesses whenever doing so does not create an immediate killing ground. A hidden princess may survive while Kallias establishes that she was abducted, manipulated, or never present.

**Private fear:** She fears that procedure makes other people stand still where she can record what happens to them. She also fears that her father's imprisonment has made every bargain look like corruption, including bargains that might save villages or wounded companions.

**Red line:** No one may speak Ianthe's petition for her, alter her words into a stronger legal claim, or present silence under armed custody as assent.

**Temptation:** Thaleia may pursue a perfect public record at the expense of speed, concealment, or the bodily safety that makes later speech possible.

**How she argues:** She asks who can deny a fact tomorrow, what document or living person will answer them, and whether the proposed protection leaves Ianthe free to revoke it. When someone promises legality, she asks which official act creates it.

**Table voice:** Deliberate, courteous, and sharper when another person treats wording as ornament. “You have described possession. I asked for the moment she consented.”

**Growth demanded:** Thaleia must use incomplete evidence decisively. Her best ending accepts that a free petition may be protected by hurried witnesses, divided records, and guards who cannot all stand inside the sanctuary hall.

## Melia Varo

**Function:** Household physician, poison reader, and guardian of Ianthe's capacity to choose.

**What she brings:** Melia can inspect tack, food, wounds, fatigue, and the physical signs of coercion; stabilize a rider long enough to move; judge whether someone can speak freely; and distinguish a useful rest from delay disguised as care. She carries antidotes, bitter charcoal, bandages, a folding cup, wax for sealing opened bottles, a small knife, and a physician's token recognized in Aulon, the Reed Villages, and Myrine.

**Prior connection:** Melia treated Serathiel during her last year in Aulon and Ianthe through the fever that killed Leandros. She knows the king died of disease and has little patience for conspiracies that make his death more useful than it was. Serathiel once offered Melia a place at the White Cypress court. Melia refused because the offer treated Ianthe's household as an extension of maternal custody.

**Opening position:** The expedition must preserve Ianthe's consciousness, speech, and ability to understand each transfer of protection. Melia accepts concealment, force, and temporary separation, but not a “rescue” completed while the principal is drugged, delirious, or unable to refuse.

**Private fear:** She fears becoming another custodian who calls obedience treatment. A physician can stop a journey, exclude witnesses, or decide that a patient is not fit to answer, all while speaking the language of necessity. She also fears that one missed inspection will make every later political question irrelevant.

**Red line:** No sedative, restraint, or medical transfer may be used to manufacture apparent calm or assent. If Ianthe cannot speak, the bodyguards preserve her life and waits; it does not substitute another voice.

**Temptation:** Melia may keep Ianthe from a dangerous public act after the danger has become hers to choose. She may also spend scarce time protecting every injured person because triage feels like betrayal.

**How she argues:** She asks what condition the proposed plan requires from the body, what failure looks like before it becomes fatal, and who benefits if Ianthe is too exhausted or wounded to speak. She distinguishes pain from incapacity and risk from coercion.

**Table voice:** Warm until someone uses medical language dishonestly. “She is tired, Captain. Tired is not transferred custody.”

**Growth demanded:** Melia must accept that preserving capacity does not mean preventing every injury or distress. Her best ending protects Ianthe through the white stones, then lets the petition proceed in Ianthe's exact voice despite pain, fear, and an imperfect medical setting.

## Company tensions

### Formation against division

Damon wants at least two sworn guards beside Ianthe. Phaedra wants a credible seal detail far enough away to divide pursuit. The disagreement is not solved by declaring one of them correct. A viable split must state who carries the principal, seal, records, medical kit, recognition sign, rendezvous, and fallback.

### Secrecy against witness

Phaedra treats information as terrain. Thaleia treats it as future custody. A public gate declaration may be tactically expensive and politically protective; a perfect concealed route may leave the bodyguards unable to answer Kallias's abduction story.

### Care against urgency

Melia can identify when rest, inspection, or treatment changes the odds. She can also consume the road with precautions. Damon is willing to move wounded companions sooner; Phaedra is willing to leave a coherent rear route; Thaleia asks what account survives if the wounded are abandoned.

### Physical custody against agency

All four are sworn to protect Ianthe, but each can turn a professional competence into a reason to overrule her. The bodyguard compact requires plain counsel, a real emergency exception, and obedience once immediate danger permits Ianthe to decide.

## Demonstration-route commitments

The canonical playthrough uses these starting commitments:

1. Ianthe remains the principal. The Narrow Shield will not cross a legal threshold, accept elven wardship, or sign a protector proclamation in her name.
2. The bronze seal may travel separately, but only with two named companions, a genuine mission, a recognition sign, and a fallback.
3. No civilian serves as a disposable decoy. People who aid the escort receive warning, a choice, and a stated obligation in return.
4. Dorion may be fought, bargained with, or joined temporarily against assassins. Cooperation does not transfer custody.
5. At Myrine, preserving Ianthe's free petition outranks keeping every bodyguard beside her or carrying the seal aboard the Sea-Lark.
