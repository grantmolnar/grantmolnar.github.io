# Design Notes: The Princess on the Salt Road

## Design objective

This is a bodyguard adventure, not an abduction story told from the abductors' side and not a mystery with a road painted over it.

The governing question is:

**How do the bodyguards preserve Ianthe's life, freedom, and political agency when different forms of safety compete?**

Every major encounter should therefore provide:

1. an immediate protection problem with more than one credible answer;
2. a choice among speed, concealment, witnesses, force, and obligation;
3. an action Ianthe can take as principal rather than commentary she supplies from behind the shield line;
4. an adversary objective more precise than “win the encounter”; and
5. a consequence that changes the remaining road.

## Governing prose and imagery

The adventure speaks through materials that carry authority from one person to another: bronze, wax, dyed cloth, tack, road boards, sluice water, clay signal strips, white stones, ink, rope, and chain. Use the image first, then reveal the obligation inside it. A cut girth is an assassination attempt, a courier ledger is both witness and trail, and a rising chain is both machinery and deadline.

Abstract language must acquire an address before the sentence ends:

- **Safety** names who can leave, refuse, or end the arrangement.
- **Custody** names body, law, witnesses, destination, duration, and release.
- **Authority** names the act ordered, its limit, and who answers afterward.
- **Witness** names where the person stands and what danger the testimony creates.
- **Delay** names a bell, water state, horse, household, or lost political opportunity.
- **Neutrality** names guards, lanes, records, substitutions, and visible restraint.

The road should not become uniformly lyrical. Myrto notices stain and concealment; Thessa notices horses, pages, and the hour line; Demea notices missing hulls and occupied roofs; Mara notices what water does to a boat; Phorbas distinguishes what he saw from what the harbor must judge. Myrine becomes numinous only because the white stones and bells remain usable under pressure.

## Ianthe's compact governs protection

Ianthe's compact is the adventure's ethical and procedural center:

> Counsel me plainly. Obey when I decide. Do not make me safe by making me absent.

State her reasons, let the bodyguards contest them, and preserve the practical consequences of her final choices. In immediate danger, they may move her without debate. Repeated deception, drugs, binding, or silence may preserve her life while transferring authorship and trust away from the compact.

Ianthe contributes law, road custom, knowledge of the royal household, and her own aims. She rides and can defend herself modestly. Tactical formations, assassin searches, aqueduct repair, reed piloting, and sustained pursuit remain the bodyguards' work.

## The conflict is public

King Leandros's death is not a murder mystery. Kallias's coup, Ianthe's refusal, Dorion's orders, the Ash Knives' republican logic, and Naevan's extraction offer are all discoverable early and should become explicit in play.

Uncertainty comes from:

- which route remains usable;
- how quickly Dorion can concentrate force;
- whether a stranger is a pursuer, assassin, opportunist, or frightened ally;
- what Ianthe is willing to concede;
- which communities will bear retaliation;
- what witnesses believe they saw; and
- whether physical survival preserves or destroys political freedom.

## Route topology

```text
                         Gate of Horns
                        /             House of Blue Lamps --- Dry Aqueduct --- House at Three Cypresses
                        \             /            |                                     Cypress Gate          Red Bridge    Reed Villages
                                                   \            /
                                                    Beacon Hill
                                                         |
                                                   Myrine Harbor
```

The diagram is conceptual rather than restrictive. Cross-ridge paths permit switching among exits; the seal and princess may travel separately; the Red Bridge and Reed Villages can be approached in sequence; and later clues identify fallback roads.

The route is braided twice:

- **Leaving Aulon:** public speed, concealed hardship, or sacred restraint.
- **Reaching the coast:** mounted speed through state infrastructure or slower cover through communities already paying for aid.

Beacon Hill converts physical route into public meaning. Myrine Harbor converts public meaning into a legal procedure under immediate threat.

## The clue graph is not the road

Adventure Graph records discoverability and corroboration, not literal movement. A lead found at Beacon Hill may confirm what happened at the Gate of Horns because that confirmation matters to Myrine's inquiry and Aulon's public account. It does not create a route back to the gate. Use `GM-OPERATING-SHEET.md` for physical movement; the maintained corpus tests cover representative route combinations.

The same distinction governs necessity. An encounter marked necessary remains discoverable, independently supported, and structurally integrated. It is not a scene every playthrough must visit. The courier house, either middle road, and Beacon Hill can be bypassed when plans and geography support it. Skipping an encounter changes resources, information, witnesses, or reception rather than invalidating the road.

## Custody is not a single condition

The adventure distinguishes at least three forms of control:

- **Physical custody:** who can move, restrain, shelter, or expose Ianthe at this moment.
- **Legal custody:** whose law claims authority over her movement and whose witnesses will recognize that claim.
- **Political authorship:** whether Ianthe can still choose and publicly state what her survival means.

Dorion, Naevan, and Myrine can each keep Ianthe physically safe. Dorion calls that recovery, Naevan calls it kin-protection, and Myrine calls it sanctuary. Each arrangement preserves different choices and closes others. A bodyguard success should therefore name which custody changed and whether Ianthe accepted it.

## Pursuit follows information and infrastructure

Dorion does not advance through a secret universal clock. His pursuit improves when he learns the route and when remounts, road posts, or beacon signals let him act on that knowledge. It worsens when decoys, broken relays, divided routes, or contrary witnesses make his information unreliable.

Use the open posture in `PURSUIT-AND-CUSTODY.md`: **Unfixed**, **Named**, **Closing**, and **Contact**. State the posture and the fiction supporting it. The escort can trade time for ambiguity, destroy infrastructure at a civilian cost, or accept public exposure for testimony without guessing at an invisible score.

## Divided routes obey adversary knowledge

When the escort divides Ianthe, seal, witnesses, signals, or transport, list what each adversary knows before assigning danger. Dorion follows evidence through roads and relays. An Ash Knife cell acts where it has preparation or information. A named NPC cannot occupy two routes at once. Unlocated groups may still face terrain, frightened authorities, or independently established threats, but do not move the principal danger to whichever group seems most dramatically convenient.

Every split records a leader, route, rendezvous, recognition sign, and fallback. Resolve actions in causal order: a bridge group does not benefit a reed group from a signal or sluice change that has not yet occurred.

## Protection modes

The adventure should repeatedly support at least five protective modes:

- **Shield:** physically interpose, hold ground, extract under attack.
- **Screen:** conceal identity, route, numbers, or the seal.
- **Witness:** make coercion or unlawful force visible to people whose testimony matters.
- **Negotiate:** exploit the fact that Dorion, Myrine, Naevan, and local communities want different outcomes.
- **Divide:** split people, seal, mounts, signals, or adversary attention.

No one mode should dominate every encounter. Public witness is powerful at the Gate of Horns and Myrine, dangerous at the aqueduct, and morally constrained at the Cypress Gate. Force is useful at the Red Bridge and potentially disastrous at the white stones.

## Infrastructure is shared consequence

The Red Bridge and Reed Villages are not independent set pieces. The bridge's Heron Sluice controls the water used by village boats, fields, and household walkways. The same signal line that concentrates Dorion's pursuit can warn Beacon Hill about assassins or a damaged descent. The same remount stable that carries Ianthe quickly can carry every rider behind her.

Whenever the escort damages infrastructure, identify the exact function affected. Cutting a signal lamp, jamming a road barrier, scattering horses, closing a sluice, and demolishing an arch have different tactical and civic consequences. “Sabotage the bridge” should never resolve as a generic pursuit penalty.

## Capacity makes protection concrete

The Reed Villages force the escort to state what must arrive together. Narrow skiffs can hide Ianthe with only a few protectors. Broad punts can carry a stronger formation or witnesses. The ferry barge can move horses or threatened households, but is slow and visible. Community aid therefore becomes a capacity decision rather than a moral surcharge applied after the route is chosen.

When the escort divides, record the principal or object, leader, route, rendezvous, recognition sign, and fallback for each group. Threats follow adversary information. Decoys should remain credible until evidence disproves them.

## Combat has a road objective

Middle-road combat should concern a crossing, horse, signal, sluice, boat chain, prisoner, fire, channel fork, or transfer. An encounter ends when that objective is secured, lost, abandoned, or transformed. Dorion, Melas, Sosthenes, Naevan, and the bodyguards may cooperate against assassins, fire, flood, or endangered civilians without reconciling their custody claims.

## Ianthe's decisions are reasoned, not scripted

Separate immediate extraction from principal decisions. In immediate danger, bodyguards may move or interpose before debate. When time permits, the GM states which of Ianthe's ordered objectives is threatened, hears concrete counsel, and then states her choice and reasons. She changes her mind when facts, values, or custody change—not because the authored route needs another scene.

Ianthe may rationally choose public danger for witnesses, surrender to prevent civilian bloodshed, elven custody when no free road remains, or renunciation when succession itself becomes the weapon. She should not expose herself merely to prove courage, and she should not reject a sound protective plan merely to prove independence.

## Failure-forward rule

Failure should change custody, time, route, force, witnesses, or public story before it ends the adventure.

Examples:

- A failed covert exit becomes a pursuit through the aqueduct rather than immediate recapture.
- A broken funeral truce preserves Ianthe's life while costing old-family support.
- Lost remounts force the Reed Villages or late arrival.
- Loss of the seal preserves the petition but weakens later claims.
- Dorion's temporary custody can be escaped, challenged publicly, or converted into a contested arrival.
- Reaching Myrine after the chain rises yields sanctuary without the Sea-Lark's decisive extraction.

Death remains possible, particularly from the Ash Knives, but it should follow visible danger and the bodyguards' choices rather than a concealed escort tax.

## The finale is distributed

Beacon Hill and Myrine Harbor deliberately separate the last approach into several control surfaces. The beacon establishes Myrine's first account; the descents determine who arrives together; the white stones test Ianthe's present freedom; the magistrates create a durable record; the seal embodies state authority; and the Sea-Lark provides immediate extraction. No single seizure, speech, combat victory, or failed roll should settle all of them at once.

This distribution creates failure-forward outcomes that remain legible. The bodyguards can lose the tower and preserve testimony, lose the ship and gain sanctuary, lose the seal and preserve Ianthe's political authorship, or secure her departure while paying with arrest or separation. The GM should state which objective changed rather than translating every result into a generic success or failure.

## Procedure creates tactical terrain

Myrine's sanctuary law is not decorative exposition. Its lanes, weapon conditions, seven stones, four questions, three records, lawful substitutes, and protected transfer create predictable spaces that every faction can use or attack. Dorion can seek examination and contest the seal without behaving irrationally. The Ash Knives can target a witness, registrar, magistrate, record, or route instead of materializing as a final assassin. Bodyguards can interpose, divide, negotiate, screen, or surrender weapons without any one mode becoming mandatory.

Procedure must remain resilient. One injured magistrate, stolen tablet, missing seal, or departed ship changes the finale but does not erase every path to sanctuary. Conversely, entering the harbor does not make Ianthe safe before she speaks and the third judgment is recorded.

## Neutrality requires active decisions

Myrine is neither a passive safe zone nor Kallias's concealed ally. Its officers protect the petition, separate armed claimants, defend harbor works, and preserve treaty credibility. They may inconvenience or arrest the bodyguards while still protecting Ianthe from Dorion. They may accept Dorion's help against assassins while rejecting his custody claim. Neutrality should feel like an institution making constrained public decisions, not an absence of conflict.


## Second-look Voice I and II

Voice I returned the authoritative road to bronze, wax, cloth, horses, water, clay, ink, rope, chain, named officials, and the bodyguards who must own each protective act. Voice II completes the packet-level reconciliation: current operating prose uses **encounter**, **escort**, **bodyguards**, and named route groups, while dated construction records preserve their original `encounter` vocabulary and seventy-nine-clue checkpoint. The current source contains ninety-three clues.

The final source finish removes the last editorial assurances about secret answers and protected outcomes. Leandros's funeral rolls establish the uncontested death. Ianthe's compact records every emergency override and the return of her authority. Roads remain physical even when late evidence changes the public account. Pursuers act on acquired information rather than table omniscience. Bells spend fictional time, and Myrine records compromise as a political choice rather than a lesser score. The Narrow Shield remains one demonstrated escort, not a required composition or route.
