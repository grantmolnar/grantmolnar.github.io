# The House at Three Cypresses

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** escort, rendezvous, negotiation, route-choice

A courier house offers four horses, finite stores, suspect provisions, and rival terms; every preparation gives Dorion another mile.

## Opening View

Four saddled horses stand in a courtyard expecting more riders than they can carry. Thessa Sable has divided the remaining food into named bundles; Eleni records every arrival and departure while a lookout calls the northern ridge. One girth has been restitched. A white-cloth message offers transfer to Dorion, and a leaf-sealed letter offers a road that never reaches Myrine. The stable doors are closing. Every additional preparation costs distance from the pursuit.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

The House at Three Cypresses stands where three roads become one long enough for an ambush and separate again before the coast. The courier house is low limestone, half sunk into the hill, with a springhouse older than its kitchen and a stable cut into the slope. Three black trees show above every ridge. Royal riders once changed horses here without touching the ground. Tonight the pennant hook carries a strip of surrender cloth tied by someone who expects the next traveler to halt before asking whose surrender it is.

**Thessa Sable**, Myrto’s elder sister, keeps the station with a courier’s inventory and a householder’s memory. She has four fresh horses, two tired mares, one mule, road clothes for six, three badges, a covered dispatch cart, barley, water, and names for every family punished after a royal messenger slept beneath her roof. She has no room for a siege and no influence capable of erasing a witness.

Here the escort chooses its shape: rested or moving, disguised or witnessed, united or divided. Every remount, inspection, decoy, evacuation, and negotiation attaches to a person, object, or hour. The house can improve almost any plan, never all of them, before Dorion’s information becomes a road beneath his horses.
## Arrival by route

Carry the consequences of the chosen exit into the first view of the house.

### From the Gate of Horns

The escort arrives mounted, comparatively fresh, and already named. Citizens and gate soldiers saw Ianthe choose the road. That testimony weakens Kallias's abduction story, but Dorion knows which ridge to ride. Unless the escort created a decoy or delayed the gate report, begin with the pursuit **Named** and close enough that a herald can reach the house during the first substantial preparation.

Thessa has heard the public horn sequence. She opens the stable immediately and asks which witnesses can be trusted to repeat what they saw. Her concern is not whether Ianthe is legitimate. It is whether the road can carry the truth faster than cavalry.

### From the Dry Aqueduct

The escort arrives on foot, dirty with lime, and materially worn. Dorion may still be **Unfixed**, but the escort needs the house more: the fresh horses, dry clothing, food, and chance to tend injuries are difficult to refuse. Mud, wet footprints, a broken maintenance grate, or a captured helper can still name the route.

Thessa does not congratulate them on cleverness. She sends Eleni to close the springhouse drain and asks whether anyone followed the watercourse. The concealment bought a lead, not immunity.

### From the Cypress Gate

The escort arrives slower, accompanied by funeral witnesses or at least protected by their memory. Weapons may still be peace-bound. Dorion probably knows the route, but he must decide whether closing openly on mourners and grave wardens is worth the political cost. Begin the pursuit **Named** but not automatically **Closing**.

Thessa receives the mourners at the front door rather than hiding them in the stable. Ianthe can ask them to carry testimony back toward Aulon, continue toward Myrine, or leave before the station becomes a target. Each choice changes who bears the next consequence.

### Split arrivals

The house can reunite separated bodyguards, Ianthe, and the bronze seal. Do not assume all parts arrive together. A seal-bearing decoy may reach first and discover that the station has been watched. Ianthe may arrive without the fastest rider. The absent part remains an active objective rather than being silently restored between encounters.

## If the rendezvous is skipped or missed

The house serves the road; it does not own it. At a dry well half a mile north, one cypress twig points toward Red Bridge, two crossed reeds toward the Heron levee, and an empty niche warns that the station is watched. A group may continue without entering. Later arrivals find a wax tablet with road, travel window, recognition phrase, and fallback only if the first group spent time leaving one.

Do not force separated groups to wait until everyone converges. The group with Ianthe decides whether the benefits of reunion outweigh a worsening pursuit posture. The seal group, witnesses, or decoy may continue independently under the route-split procedure. If the house has burned or fallen to Dorion, the springhouse drain still reaches the western orchard, and the dry-well marks can direct survivors toward either middle road.

Skipping or missing the house has exact costs: no guaranteed remount allocation, no full household meal or treatment, no controlled discovery of the tampered cordial and tack, and incomplete knowledge of one or both custody offers. It also saves a meaningful part of the road and denies the pursuit a fixed convergence point.

## The household and its claim

Thessa does not greet Ianthe with inherited loyalty. She puts two names on the stable table: **Petran Vos** and **Alea Nym**, arrested yesterday for repairing mounts used by Assembly messengers. Their families have been fined. Before the house spends another horse or hides another rider, Thessa wants them released, the fines canceled, and any damage done here entered in the royal accounts under Ianthe's own seal.

Her daughter **Eleni Sable** is twenty-two, a strong rider, and angry enough to volunteer for any decoy plan before understanding its limits. Eleni knows the Red Bridge signal mast, the first three reed channels, and the faces of most official couriers. She does not know how to survive a cavalry search merely because she rides well. Thessa will let her choose, but not let the bodyguards call an uninformed sacrifice a volunteer.

Ianthe knows both women. Leandros once used the house as a hunting lodge and left the courier staff to sleep in the stable. At seventeen, Ianthe found the arrangement funny. At twenty-seven, she remembers it with shame. She does not ask Thessa for loyalty as though it were free. She can use the bronze seal to issue a compensation warrant, name Petran and Alea as royal couriers, or promise later redress in her own hand. The seal can make the promise legible; it cannot ensure that Ianthe ever regains power to honor it.

This relationship sharpens the escort's central duty. Protecting Ianthe does not make every helper expendable, but neither does Ianthe have the right to spend her bodyguards indefinitely repairing the monarchy's old debts. Thessa asks for a decision, not a confession.

## What the house can provide

The following resources are concrete: expose them to inspection, allocation, alteration, or refusal.

- **Four fresh horses.** All are sound after inspection. One is a broad gray mare suited to an armored rider; one is a fast bay that will not tolerate an unfamiliar cloak flapping behind the saddle.
- **Two tired courier mares.** They can reach the Red Bridge at a controlled pace or carry light riders toward the reeds. Hard riding risks lameness.
- **One pack mule and a covered dispatch cart.** The cart is too slow for the direct road but useful as a visible decoy, casualty wagon, or means of moving a household.
- **Road clothing for six.** Salt merchants, shrine servants, and courier gray are available. The clothes change recognition at distance, not Ianthe's face before anyone who knows her.
- **Three courier badges.** One is current, one belongs to a dead rider whose face is known at the Red Bridge, and one was reported stolen before the coup. The stolen badge can pass a hurried villager and provoke immediate detention at a military post.
- **The signal box.** Shutter plates and lamp glass can send the old royal sequences for safe station, pursuit, false message, and wounded courier. Kallias has changed some official codes; Thessa knows which ones only through yesterday.
- **The hidden loft.** Five people can lie behind the grain wall. Searchers who know courier-house construction will find it given time. It is a pause, not sanctuary.
- **Food, water, and basic treatment.** A short halt restores judgment and catches developing poison or strain. A full sleep spends most of the lead gained in Aulon.

## The tampered remount

The Ash Knives came first under a stolen loyalist token. They cut nearly through the bay’s restitched girth and dosed the sun-marked cordial with enough hellebore to leave Ianthe confused, weak, and visibly dependent within an hour. They do not need a clean death here. A fall, a medical panic, or a custody struggle over a princess who cannot speak creates the opening they want.

The tampering is discoverable without a special ability. The girth has fresh wax where old leather should be dry; the stitching turns the opposite direction from Thessa's work; the cordial smells medicinal beneath the honey. Eleni remembers that the false courier touched both items. A careful tack inspection, tasting protocol, questioning the household, or simply choosing different equipment defeats the attempt.

If the escort misses it, fail forward. The bay's girth parts under stress rather than at a predetermined lethal moment. The cordial sickens Ianthe rather than killing her outright. The escort must then protect an injured or impaired principal, prove the condition was engineered, and decide whether bodily incapacity permits temporary decisions on her behalf. Dorion will help prevent her death and use her incapacity to argue for custody. The Ash Knives may attack during that dispute.

## Ianthe's counsel circle

Before the escort chooses the coast road, Ianthe closes the stable doors, puts the seal where everyone can see it, and requires each person to name four things: the road they recommend, the cost they expect someone else to bear, the point at which they would reverse, and the person authorized to make that reversal. She listens first to whoever has spoken least.

Ianthe does not reward theatrical certainty. When someone calls the bridge defensible, she asks who loses water if it is damaged. When someone proposes division, she asks what true thing the decoy carries and how its riders return. When someone asks for public witness, she asks where the witness stands during the first arrow. When someone asks for rest, she asks what bodily sign will end it.

Her own habit is to separate possession, law, and authorship: “Who can move me? Whose rule claims the right? Who will be believed when I say why I went?” Under fear she becomes more formal, not less. The bodyguards may dispute every answer. Once she decides, she states the order, the reason, and the condition that would make her reconsider.
## Dorion's white-cloth offer

Dorion sends no demand shouted from horseback. A herald approaches beneath white cloth with a tablet listing names, releases, hours, and limits. Dorion offers amnesty for Ianthe’s household escort, release of specified road detainees, medical care, and a public return under escort if Ianthe signs a protector proclamation lasting thirty days. The bronze seal will be deposited in the temple treasury and opened only for orders countersigned by Kallias and two named councilors.

At **Contact**, the herald remains outside the wall while Dorion places riders where the house can see that refusal has a road cost. The herald carries two copies of every term. Dorion initials changes on both tablets, gives one to the house, and states the effective hour before witnesses. He speaks in bounded verbs: *release these people; hold this object; delay until this date; permit these witnesses*. He does not say Kallias is harmless. He says exactly what his warrant allows and asks the escort to compare those terms with the blood, hunger, and uncertainty of the remaining road.

His weakness is the same precision. He cannot promise a free Assembly, withdraw Kallias’s soldiers from the civic buildings, or make a proclamation voluntary after Ianthe enters private custody. When pressed, he says, “I can give you thirty days with names attached. I cannot give you the city you wish had survived dusk.”
### What Dorion may negotiate

Dorion can alter the place and visibility of transfer, permit named witnesses, let the bodyguards retain weapons longer, exchange written copies of the orders, release wounded or low-level detainees within his authority, and agree to a limited conference at the Red Bridge. He can grant a temporary truce to identify assassins, recover casualties, clear civilians, or cross dangerous ground. He can promise that neither side will use the truce to improve a firing position, though both may continue sending messages.

He can also accept a narrower temporary arrangement: Ianthe rides under joint observation to the Red Bridge, where she hears Kallias's terms through envoys while remaining physically beside her bodyguards. Dorion will regard any movement past the bridge toward Myrine as a breach.

### What Dorion cannot concede

Dorion will not permit Ianthe to continue to Myrine, enter elven custody, retain the bronze seal after surrender, command Aulonite outposts against Kallias, or make restoration of the Assembly a precondition. He cannot guarantee that Kallias will stop pressing Ianthe after thirty days. He will not say otherwise when asked directly.

He also will not knowingly stand aside for an assassination. Evidence of Ash Knife activity can produce immediate cooperation. When the threat is contained, he resumes the demand for custody unless the situation has materially changed.

### Ways to answer

The escort may reject the offer, publish it, negotiate for time, propose witnesses, feign acceptance, attempt to turn Dorion against Kallias, or attack. Feigned acceptance can buy a short interval, but Dorion requires visible steps: the seal placed on the table, the carriage admitted, or Ianthe moving toward his line. A bare promise does not freeze him.

Killing the herald or striking under white cloth destroys a useful channel, hardens the public abduction story, and makes Dorion rely on containment rather than parley. Capturing the herald can yield messages; it also tells every road post that the escort treats formal exchange as combat.

## Naevan's leaf-sealed offer

Naevan does not arrive with a banner. A white-cypress leaf, still green at the cut, is pinned to the orchard door, and his voice comes from beyond the wall before his outriders show themselves. He offers a forest road, fresh mounts, healers, and six bows no Aulonite relay can count. Full acceptance places Ianthe beneath Serathiel’s household protection until White Cypress judges the danger ended.

Naevan’s language is hospitable enough to hide a border. He speaks of receiving, sheltering, carrying home, and letting the roads close behind her. He does not threaten Ianthe. He believes kinship creates duties that precede the polis’s failure and that a daughter returned alive may argue about jurisdiction later.

When Ianthe asks the term of wardship, the authority that can end it, and whether Aulonite witnesses may accompany her, Naevan answers honestly and incompletely. “Your mother’s door has never been barred to you.” Ianthe replies, “A door is not an answer. Who holds the key after I cross it?” Limited aid is possible only when destination, duration, numbers, and withdrawal are written before his scouts join the road.
### Limited elven aid

Naevan has authority to offer less than full extraction, but not without a record. If Ianthe writes that she requested aid from her mother's kin, he may:

- lend two outriders as scouts as far as Beacon Hill;
- send one guide and false trail with a seal-bearing decoy;
- carry messages through the reed channels; or
- evacuate two threatened households across the forest boundary.

The written request makes the aid lawful within elven custom and makes Kallias's foreign-influence argument easier to prove. Naevan will not place his people under Aulonite military command, attack Dorion solely for executing a recovery warrant, or enter Myrine Harbor as an armed elven company. He may fight the Ash Knives or defend a threatened household regardless of politics.

Ianthe takes the offer seriously. If the escort is broken, she is poisoned, the Ash Knives have fixed her position, or Myrine has become unreachable, the forest road may preserve the only person who can later contest Kallias. She also understands that accepting it abandons immediate supporters to Kallias's story and may make every later return look like foreign intervention.

## Preparations and meaningful delay

Do not turn the station into an arbitrary menu with two action points. Ask what the escort actually does and tell them when a plan requires a meaningful portion of the road. The following normally consume enough time for the pursuit posture to worsen or for another arrival to occur:

- giving the whole escort a full meal, treatment, and sleep;
- refitting every rider and redistributing armor and packs;
- preparing a convincing cart or mounted decoy;
- sending and waiting for a reply from the Red Bridge or Reed Villages;
- evacuating Thessa's household and records;
- conducting extended negotiations with Dorion or Naevan; or
- searching the surrounding ridges thoroughly for watchers.

Brief counsel, a careful tack inspection, changing a few garments, filling water, reading messages, and issuing one clear order do not each consume a separate interval. Reward coherent delegation. A divided escort can prepare several things at once while accepting reduced protection around Ianthe.

State what the delay means in the fiction: dust on the western ridge, a courier lamp changing, horses heard below the spring, or the shadow of the cypresses shortening toward noon. The pressure should be legible enough that they can choose it.

## Choosing the second half of the road

### Red Bridge

The direct road preserves mounted speed and reaches the only remount post capable of refreshing the whole escort. It also runs through military infrastructure. The bronze seal can command the bridge, but using it leaves an authenticated trail. Dorion can concentrate there if the pursuit is Named or Closing.

Choose Red Bridge when speed, command authority, a public confrontation, or control of the pursuit's horses matters more than concealment.

### Reed Villages

The reed route breaks mounted pursuit, offers boats and local cover, and reaches Naevan. It is slower, exposes households already under punishment, and may leave tired mounts behind. Aid can be purchased with royal promises, personal loyalty, coercion, or evacuation; these are not equivalent.

Choose the Reed Villages when concealment, community support, limited elven aid, or separating Ianthe from the seal matters more than raw speed.

### Division and reversals

The seal, decoy, witnesses, household, and principal may take different roads. A visible seal detail at the Red Bridge can draw Dorion while Ianthe enters the reeds. Ianthe can ride openly while a courier carries the seal through the channels. The escort can send Eleni ahead to change the bridge signal or ask Naevan’s scouts to make a false forest trail.

Every division names command, rendezvous, and fallback. Weak coordination creates the danger; a divided route that preserves those three can exploit the road without an arbitrary penalty.

## Escalation at the house

The house is defensible against a few assassins and untenable against a cavalry company. Its springhouse has a rear drain into the orchard, the stable loft overlooks the road, and the stone kitchen resists fire. The cypresses make smoke visible for miles.

Possible escalations include:

- an Ash Knife shoots during negotiations, hoping each armed side blames the other;
- Dorion's riders close the visible road while leaving the orchard unsealed;
- Eleni discovers the sabotaged tack after a decoy has already departed;
- a wounded courier arrives from the Red Bridge with contradictory signal orders;
- Thessa refuses to abandon her ledgers unless someone accepts responsibility for the arrested hands; or
- Naevan identifies an elven trail sign copied incorrectly by an assassin wearing loyalist colors.

Combat should be about an objective: hold the springhouse exit, protect the horses, keep Ianthe able to speak, capture an assassin, prevent a signal, or make space for departure. Killing every hostile person is neither required nor always possible.

## Outcomes and failure forward

- **Prepared departure:** the escort leaves with chosen mounts, route, messages, and an explicit pursuit posture.
- **Dorion's truce:** both parties move toward Red Bridge under bounded terms while remaining political opponents.
- **Voluntary return:** Ianthe may accept Dorion's custody. The road turns back toward Aulon as a contested escort, with witnesses, the seal, and the proclamation still separable objectives.
- **Forced custody:** Dorion may take physical control without obtaining Ianthe's assent. The bodyguards can pursue the transfer, challenge it before witnesses, free her at the bridge, or protect her from the Ash Knives while seeking another opening.
- **Elven extraction:** Ianthe survives under White Cypress protection. This is a mixed ending if chosen deliberately and an incomplete rescue if imposed on her.
- **Household evacuation:** saving Thessa's people costs time and may lose the fresh mounts, but preserves a courier network and witnesses.
- **Sabotaged departure:** injury or poison changes escort condition and authority; it does not end the road by fiat.
- **Burned station:** the cypresses become a beacon. The escort loses the hidden loft and future courier support, while both Myrine and the pursuit may see where violence occurred.

Before leaving, state aloud: which road each person takes, who carries the seal, what Dorion believes, what Naevan expects, which households have been warned, and the present pursuit posture. Those facts are the inheritance of the next encounter.
