# The Cypress Gate

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** escort, city-exit, sanctuary, assassination

The funeral road leaves Aulon under a peace older than Kallias’s warrant: no arrest, no named feud, and no blade drawn before the last stone lion.

## Opening View

Oil lamps and grave cloth are already passing beneath the Cypress Gate. Every soldier’s sword is tied shut; Kallias’s observers wait beyond the last stone lion, where the funeral truce ends. Old Chares counts names against Leandros’s tablet. One hired mourner gives the response for the dead in the wrong order. Another steadies an oil jar with hands too clean for the work. Demea of the Keys lifts the gate key. “Name the peace you ask us to spend.”

## Encounter Material

## Situation

Mourning lamps move toward Aulon’s western wall while Kallias’s watchers count carts at both ends of the necropolis. Between the inner arch and the last stone lion, no officer may arrest, no enemy may name a blood feud, and no living hand may draw steel. The peace is civic, visible, and still unbroken; Kallias has not yet taught the city what violating it will cost.

Mourning carts already carry grave cloth, oil, and empty offering jars for Leandros’s funeral. Ianthe can travel veiled among household women, ride openly beside her father’s honor cart, or ask the tomb wardens to name her as a mourner under their protection. Each choice makes a different witness. Each also gives an assassin a crowd in which the bodyguards’ fastest answer is forbidden.
## The wardens

**Old Chares Pell** and **Demea of the Keys** keep the gate. Chares served Leandros and wants Ianthe safely gone. Demea believes using a funeral truce for political escape may destroy the custom for everyone who needs it later. Neither accepts a forged claim of mourning. They can be persuaded by truth, obligation, or a concrete plan to preserve the truce.

A public appeal to the wardens produces witnesses from old Aulonite families. It also obliges Ianthe to answer whether she intends to return with foreign soldiers. Her answer is: she intends to return with a free Assembly if Aulon will still have one. She will not promise that no war can follow.

## Protection without drawn steel

The bodyguards may carry bound weapons, walking staves, shields used as bier boards, or tools. Drawing a blade does not summon divine punishment. It breaks a civic peace that everyone present can see. An attacker who forces that choice may win politically even if killed.

The Ash Knives place two members among hired mourners. Their preferred method is not a dramatic charge. One overturns an oil jar and starts a panic; the other reaches Ianthe with a grave needle treated in ordinary poison. Clues include funeral phrases spoken in the wrong order, hands without oil stains, and a mourner who watches the escort instead of the cart.

Kallias's observers will not assist an assassination unless they mistake the bodyguards for the threat. Captain Dorion, if present, will help stop a visible killer and then demand that Ianthe accept his protection. This can produce a tense temporary alignment without making Dorion an ally.

## The old road

Beyond the stone lion, the funeral road bends south through family tombs and abandoned vineyards. Horses can travel it, though carts move slowly. A hidden postern connects east toward the Gate of Horns road. The House at Three Cypresses lies seven miles ahead.

The route's reward is civic legitimacy among old families and a passage Kallias hesitates to profane. Its cost is restraint. Bodyguards who preserve Ianthe by respecting the truce gain witnesses and perhaps tomb-warden guides. Those who save her by turning the necropolis into a battlefield still save her, but Kallias can say the old peace ended under her colors.

## Roads beyond the last lion

The courier house lies seven miles down the funeral road, but the procession need not reach it. Beside the tomb of the Broken Oar, a vineyard cross-road runs east to the Salt Road above Red Bridge. Farther south, the funeral road meets a causeway once used to carry the drowned from flooded farms; guides can enter the Salt Cut there. Warden keys, procession books, and wheel marks disclose both turns.

The old-family witnesses need not remain physically beside Ianthe to matter. A named mourner may continue openly toward the bridge while Ianthe turns into the reeds, or carry a written account back into Aulon while the escort uses a quieter road. Such division protects only what the witness actually saw. It does not let another person complete Ianthe's petition.

Ianthe favors this exit when custom and testimony can prevent broader military violence. She will not deliberately expose a funeral procession merely to acquire sympathetic witnesses. If assassins have fixed the procession or the wardens cannot protect the crowd, she supports leaving the carts, taking a cross-road, or surrendering the political advantage of the truce.

## Words beneath the truce

The funeral peace also limits accusation. A person may name a danger but may not proclaim a blood feud or demand immediate vengeance. Ianthe can state that Kallias has exceeded his warrant, ask the old families to witness her departure, and promise a free convocation. She cannot lawfully call them to arms until the procession passes the last lion. This gives the bodyguards time to read the crowd and forces every faction to speak more carefully than it fights.

If the escort exposes an Ash Knife without drawing steel, the wardens can surround the suspect with bier poles and grave cloth. If the escort breaks the truce first, Chares still opens the road to save Ianthe, but Demea records exactly who drew and why. That record can preserve the truth of an unavoidable act while denying the escort the political benefit of restraint.
