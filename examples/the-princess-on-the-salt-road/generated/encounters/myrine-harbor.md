# Myrine Harbor

**Role:** necessary, end  
**Tags:** escort, harbor, sanctuary, finale

Seven stones separate sanctuary from custody while magistrates, soldiers, assassins, records, seal, and departing ship each follow a different lane.

## Opening View

Whatever reaches Myrine is taken apart at the painted lanes: witnesses, weapons, wounded travelers, horses, claimants, and the bronze seal. The harbor chain rises while league sailors stand ready to cut the Sea-Lark’s lines at the final bell. Registrar Olyra Fen prepares three duplicate tablets; Kallias’s advocate has already demanded Ianthe’s private examination. Captain Eudora Pell leaves one lane open for an unbound petitioner with empty hands. Then she points to the armed escort. “Who crosses with her?”

## Encounter Material

## Situation

Myrine is neutral because hundreds of hands perform neutrality where everyone can see. Chain crews count links at the water gate. Guards separate armed claimants into painted lanes. League sailors stand by lines they will cut at the final bell. Fish sellers shout around petition clerks who preserve duplicate tablets under oiled cloth. Nothing here is safe merely because it is called sanctuary.

At the upper spear court, Captain Eudora is already assigning armed arrivals to painted lanes and naming which protectors may approach the seven stones. Ianthe must cross unbound and answer in her own voice. Her bodyguards may remain outside, surrender weapons, accept peace bindings, or seek a narrow exception tied to a named danger. Dorion may file his warrant. Niko may object. Neither may answer for her.

The harbor chain is already rising. Before the final bell, the Sea-Lark can receive a lawful petitioner, a modest escort, and selected witnesses—not every ally, horse, refugee, prisoner, and disputed symbol gathered on the road. The finale separates what pursuit tried to bind together: Ianthe’s life, free petition, seal, public record, ship, witnesses, and bodyguards. Each has its own lane, custodian, and failure point.
## Arrival without a beacon account

Ianthe can reach Myrine without visiting Beacon Hill. Riders arrive by the lower Salt Road at the spear court. Boats may land at the fish stairs, chainhouse, or league quay according to draft and control of the harbor mouth. The absence of an authenticated advance signal does not bar sanctuary. It means Myrine begins from uncertainty.

Captain Eudora first separates armed groups, confirms identity through direct witnesses or Dorion's acknowledgment, and asks whether Ianthe is presently able to speak. The coercion inquiry may consume part of the chain clock, and an armed bodyguard exception is harder to obtain before the threat is identified. An escort that saved time by bypassing the tower may still reach the stones sooner than one that fought for the beacon.

Fragmented arrivals remain fragmented. A seal group at the chainhouse, witnesses at the fish stairs, and Ianthe at the spear court do not merge because the finale has begun. Resolve communication, travel, and adversary knowledge across the harbor. One group's success can prepare another objective without completing it.

## Harbor geography

### The upper road and spear court

The Salt Steps enter a rectangular court outside the sanctuary line. Myrine guards can receive surrendered weapons here, separate armed companies, search litters, and hold people under harbor peace. White-painted channels in the paving create three lanes: petitioners and witnesses, disarmed escorts, and armed claimants awaiting judgment.

The court is outside sanctuary. Dorion may lawfully stand here, present Kallias's warrant, ask for Ianthe's private examination, or demand return of the bronze seal. The bodyguards may also stand here armed if they do not threaten the line. The Ash Knives use the confusion of lanes, uniforms, and wounded arrivals to approach under a plausible identity.

### The white stones

Seven broad white stones cross a shallow rain channel between the spear court and the sanctuary steps. They are not magical. Their force comes from treaty, ritual, witnesses, and Myrine's willingness to defend the completed procedure.

The petitioner crosses unbound and without a drawn weapon. A peace-bound weapon may remain sheathed only if the harbor captain grants an exception. No claimant may pull, carry, surround, or speak over the petitioner on the stones. Medical support may walk beside someone who can move under their own direction. If Ianthe cannot speak, sanctuary can protect her body temporarily, but the political petition remains incomplete.

### The sanctuary hall

The hall is open on three sides so no private transfer can be disguised as public judgment. A waist-high cedar rail separates the magistrates and registrar from the petition floor. Three bronze tablets stand ready for names, feared parties, requested remedy, and witness attestations. The tablets are copied after the hearing and one copy travels with the petitioner.

The hall has two exits: back to the spear court and down the **League Walk** toward the Sea-Lark. Once the third magistrate records the petition, Myrine guards close both exits against hostile claimants and escort Ianthe according to the remedy granted.

### The League Walk

A roofed colonnade runs from the sanctuary hall to the league quay. It remains within Myrine's harbor peace but not within the white-stone petition line. Sailors, officials, and recognized sanctuary escorts can use it. Armed Aulonite companies cannot enter without Captain Eudora's order.

The League Walk is broad enough for formation but crowded with cargo. An assassin can hide among sailcloth, petitioners, wounded sailors, or chain messengers. The bodyguards may accompany Ianthe here after surrendering or peace-binding weapons, or after winning a limited exception.

### The chainhouse and ropewalk

The Ropewalk Track from Beacon Hill reaches the chainhouse west of the sanctuary. The chain crew controls the rising barrier, towboats, and emergency harbor gate. Sabotage here can trap the Sea-Lark, release the chain dangerously, or make the harbor believe an armed company is attacking its defenses.

The bronze seal may arrive here separately. Dorion may send a small recovery group. The Ash Knives may attack the chain to delay departure or create a distraction. Protecting the chainhouse does not complete sanctuary, and completing sanctuary does not automatically save the ship.

### The league quay and Sea-Lark

The Sea-Lark is a lean trireme carrying a league envoy, thirty marines, a skeleton rowing complement, and sealed dispatches for three coastal cities. It is not Ianthe's private vessel. Captain **Helike Varro** agreed to receive a lawful Myrine sanctuary transfer before the final chain bell. She will not let rival armed companies board, wait indefinitely while the harbor fights, or make her ship the instrument of an unrecorded abduction.

The ship can leave without Ianthe if remaining would endanger the league mission. It can also remain after the nominal departure moment if Myrine formally commandeers it under treaty, but that requires all three magistrates and makes Myrine an overt participant rather than a neutral sanctuary host.

## The chain clock

Use the bells and visible chain position rather than a concealed timer.

### Before the first bell

The gangplank is down, cargo nets remain open, and the magistrates can conduct a careful hearing. The bodyguards have time to negotiate weapon handling, identify witnesses, and search the immediate route if they arrived with a substantial lead.

### First bell: clear the channel

Small craft are pulled from the departure lane. Cargo nets rise. Captain Helike sends a league officer to the sanctuary hall for the magistrates' likely disposition. Ianthe can still petition and board normally, but a prolonged coercion inquiry or fight at the white stones now matters.

### Second bell: draw the ship in

The gangplank is withdrawn or kept by a single quick-release line. Spring lines hold the Sea-Lark near the quay. Boarding requires a direct harbor order, a boat, or access through the chainhouse side. Sanctuary remains available without qualification.

### Final bell: chain at the pulley

The departure channel opens and the Sea-Lark begins to move. Ianthe can still gain sanctuary in Myrine. She cannot simply run aboard. The magistrates may ask the captain to hold, formally commandeer transport, arrange another vessel, or keep Ianthe under city protection.

Ring each bell when a meaningful choice, conflict, or delay consumes the interval: inquiry, repair, treatment, searching, fighting, route transfer, or negotiation. Table discussion alone spends no bell.

## The three magistrates

### Damara Keos, treaty magistrate

Damara wears a plain blue mantle and treats sanctuary as a promise Myrine must be able to repeat to every signatory after the spears are lowered. She asks who restrained Ianthe, who spoke for her, whether she can leave the escort that brought her, and what exact remedy she seeks.

Damara does not require a spotless road. She requires present freedom and an honest record of earlier coercion. Good intentions do not erase a binding, drug, lie, or forced movement; neither does one wrongful bodyguard act convert Kallias’s custody into consent.

Her sentences separate act from authority: “Tell me what was done. Then tell me who claimed the right to do it.”
### Simo Arcast, harbor magistrate

Simo is a former chainmaster with two missing fingers. He listens for abstractions that have forgotten where the spear points are. His questions concern lanes, numbers, weapon reach, ship movement, and what formation remains when the petition begins.

He grants narrow exceptions readily when the threat and protector are named. He refuses “all our weapons are necessary” because the harbor cannot defend a shape no one has defined. Simo may assign one bodyguard to Ianthe, another to the chainhouse, and a third outside the stones, or accept Dorion’s aid against an assassin without admitting Dorion’s claim.

“The chain has one job,” he says. “Give each sword one.”
### Lysa Meren, civic magistrate

Lysa listens for the people absent from official documents: gate witnesses, fined households, released pilots, stable hands, and the companions left outside the sanctuary lane. She cares about what Aulon will be able to say happened after every claimant has issued a proclamation.

She asks what Ianthe intends to do with protection. A free Assembly, renunciation, neutral mediation, and simple refuge are all possible. Lysa resists every argument that reduces a civic crisis to household custody.

Her questions join public meaning to human bearers: “Who can contradict this tomorrow, and where will they sleep tonight?”
### Substitution procedure

The compact requires three recorded judgments, not the survival or uninterrupted presence of these exact three people. If a magistrate is wounded, recuses for conflict, or is cut off by violence, the registrar can summon a standing substitute from the chainmaster, league factor, and sanctuary warden in that order. The substitution must be named publicly and entered before the replacement hears Ianthe.

This prevents one knife or barricade from ending the legal route while preserving the need for three independent officials.

## Other principals

### Captain Eudora Pell, harbor guard

Eudora commands twenty-four spears, six archers, and the chain gates. She can separate companies, accept weapons, peace-bind selected escorts, grant a limited exception, and arrest anyone who turns the petitioner lane into a battlefield. She cannot decide the petition or award the bronze seal.

Eudora wants a formation she can draw with chalk: one principal protector, one rear commander, one liaison, one fallback. She accepts unusual plans when every hand has an address. She refuses a crowd of self-appointed guardians because no one can tell which movement is protection and which is seizure.

“Name the lane you hold,” she says. “Then stay out of the others.”
### Registrar Olyra Fen

Olyra keeps the tablets, duplicate cords, witness order, and four formal questions. People overlook her because she carries no spear and therefore fail to notice that the third record cannot exist without hands, ink, clean surfaces, and a person who knows where the first two copies went.

If Olyra is injured, any magistrate can appoint a deputy in public, but substitution costs time and introduces another chain of testimony. Olyra insists that Ianthe choose the remedy’s words before stepping onto the stones. Advisers may prepare forms. They may not feed answers across the line.

Her voice is sequential: “Name. Choice. Fear. Remedy. Then three judgments, and not before.”
### Captain Helike Varro of the Sea-Lark

Helike commands a league trireme, not a floating court. Her order permits her to carry a person lawfully placed under Myrine sanctuary. It does not authorize her to settle Aulon’s constitution or load every armed ally the crisis has gathered.

She measures promises in gangway width, rowing benches, tide, line tension, and the number of strangers her crew can watch after dark. Ianthe, a modest escort, the seal, and named witnesses may fit. Horses, refugees, prisoners, and rival companies do not.

Helike will hold for a visible petitioner already crossing the stones or a formal harbor order. She will not hold for smoke on Beacon Hill and a promise that the important person is coming. “Bring me law I can carry,” she says, “before the water carries me.”
### Captain Dorion Vey

Dorion enters the spear court unarmed and deposits Kallias’s warrant where the magistrates can read its limits. He asks that Ianthe answer outside the immediate presence of her bodyguards. Before anyone moves, he asks Damara to write the duration, sight lines, witnesses, and return point into both copies of the court order.

His preferred result is voluntary return with the seal. His acceptable result is delay beyond the Sea-Lark followed by contested sanctuary inside a city Kallias can pressure. His minimum is the seal and a record that Myrine did not declare the entire emergency order unlawful.

Dorion defends the stones from the Ash Knives because his order begins with a living princess. When the threat ends, he resumes the custody demand at the exact clause where it was interrupted. Once the third judgment is recorded, he stops physical recovery within sanctuary. He may contest the seal, seek the bodyguards outside, preserve Kallias’s objections, or withdraw. “My warrant ends at your judgment,” he tells Damara. “My argument does not.”
### Niko Sarn, advocate for Kallias

If Niko reaches Myrine, he turns the whole road into an evidentiary objection: concealment, divided escort, elven scouts, coerced witnesses, broken peace bindings, and any moment when a bodyguard acted after danger passed. His strongest material is conduct the escort truly cannot defend. His weakest is pretending the assassins do not exist after Myrine has captured them.

Niko may file questions and documentary objections. He may not answer across the white stones. He becomes useful against forged signals, unauthorized soldiers, or a claim Dorion’s actual conduct contradicts.

He speaks as a man trying to keep emergency power inside a sentence with a period. “I can defend a hard decree,” he says. “I cannot defend an endless one.”
## The sanctuary procedure

The procedure is designed to be played, not summarized as one check.

### 1. Establish identity

Ianthe may establish identity through the bronze seal, recognition by a magistrate, two credible witnesses, royal household tokens, or uncontested public acknowledgment by Dorion. The seal is powerful evidence but not required. A counterfeit or absent seal delays identity only when every human witness is also disputed.

### 2. Separate immediate claimants

Captain Eudora places bodyguards, Dorion's soldiers, elven agents, and other armed parties in distinct lanes. Ianthe names who may remain within speaking distance before she crosses. She may request one or more bodyguards as protection. Eudora decides weapon conditions, not whether the relationship is politically legitimate.

### 3. Cross freely

Ianthe crosses the seven stones unbound, without a drawn weapon, and without physical direction from a claimant. She can pause, turn back, call someone forward, or dismiss an escort. A bodyguard may interpose against an attack without invalidating the petition; dragging her onward after the threat ends may do so.

### 4. Speak the petition

Olyra asks four questions:

1. “Name yourself before Myrine.”
2. “By whose choice have you come to these stones?”
3. “Whose power or violence do you fear?”
4. “What protection or judgment do you ask of this polis?”

Ianthe answers in her own words. Her intended default petition is:

> “I am Ianthe Aulonid. I came by my own choice, aided by companions I charged to guard me. I fear the unlawful command of Strategos Kallias, the knives of those who would end my house by murder, and any protection that denies me the right to leave it. I ask sanctuary, passage from his immediate reach, and neutral witness while Aulon summons a free Assembly.”

She may alter this according to play. She can name bodyguard wrongdoing, renounce succession, ask only sanctuary, accuse Dorion personally, accept Myrine mediation, or declare an elven claim. The remedy shapes the aftermath.

### 5. Record three judgments

Each magistrate states whether they heard an identified petitioner speak freely and whether Myrine grants the requested protection within its law. They need not agree on Aulon's constitution. The third affirmative record begins sanctuary.

A magistrate may ask a short clarifying question but cannot turn the stones into a trial of every road event. Objections and witness testimony follow after the immediate protection decision unless they bear directly on present coercion or identity.

### 6. Transfer under sanctuary

Once sanctuary begins, Captain Eudora controls the physical route to the hall, city lodging, or Sea-Lark. Ianthe chooses among remedies Myrine lawfully offers. Dorion and the bodyguards may contest property, arrests, and escort status outside the protected transfer, but cannot lawfully seize her.

## Weapon handling and bodyguard access

The bodyguards have four principal options.

**Remain armed outside.** They hold the spear court or road while Ianthe crosses with Myrine guards. This preserves combat strength against Dorion or assassins but separates them from the moment of petition and may leave Ianthe exposed to threats already inside.

**Surrender weapons.** Myrine racks and tags each weapon. Disarmed bodyguards can accompany Ianthe through the hall and League Walk. Weapons are returned when the immediate crisis ends unless the owner is arrested for a separate offense.

**Peace-bind weapons.** Waxed cord and Myrine seals bind selected weapons into sheaths. Drawing breaks the seal and creates visible evidence of necessity or violation. Eudora permits this for a small number of named protectors when the threat is credible.

**Win a limited exception.** One or two bodyguards may carry ready weapons within a defined zone if Eudora believes an assassin is present and the plan names fields of responsibility. The exception never authorizes force against Myrine, Dorion's nonattacking envoys, or legal objections.

Concealed weapons can preserve surprise against assassins and destroy trust if discovered. The choice should remain available with its real consequences.

## The bronze seal

The seal authenticates Ianthe, opens Aulonite stores, commands certain road posts, and carries enormous symbolic value. It does not own her voice.

Possible dispositions include:

- carried openly by Ianthe across the stones;
- deposited with the magistrates pending adjudication;
- carried separately to the Sea-Lark;
- entrusted to a bodyguard who remains outside;
- surrendered to Dorion in exchange for concessions or safe passage;
- hidden in Myrine or among village witnesses; or
- lost, stolen, or destroyed during the finale.

Dorion may recover the seal after sanctuary without recovering Ianthe. Myrine may refuse to let the seal aboard if possession is violently disputed. Ianthe may voluntarily relinquish it to separate her personal safety from the royal claim. Every disposition changes the political campaign after the road.

## Ash Knife plans

The Ash Knives know that the sanctuary procedure creates predictable moments when Ianthe must be visible, relatively unencumbered, and separated from armed companions. They exploit procedure rather than ignoring it.

### The bloodied witness

An assassin arrives in village clothes with a genuine road injury and a story of helping Ianthe. The hidden weapon is a thin ceramic blade undetectable by a casual metal search. The assassin seeks a place in the petitioner lane and strikes as Ianthe turns to name witnesses.

### The registrar's ink

A clerk's assistant replaces one ink cake with a contact poison intended for Olyra or Ianthe's fingers when she marks the tablet. The immediate objective may be incapacitation rather than instant death, allowing a second assassin to attack during confusion. Earlier clues about poison, false loyalist tokens, or tampered cordial should make this method legible.

### The spear-court panic

An agent in Dorion's colors fires or throws from behind the Aulonite lane, hoping Myrine guards answer with force and break the hearing into a battle. Another assassin then approaches from the League Walk or fish market. The bodyguards can preserve the petition by identifying the true objective rather than simply winning the melee.

### The chainhouse attack

The assassins threaten the Sea-Lark or release a chain component to draw Eudora's strongest fighters away. This can be a real attempt to strand Ianthe even if no killer reaches the stones. The escort must decide whether sanctuary without extraction is acceptable.

### The third record

If Ianthe survives her statement, an assassin targets a magistrate, Olyra, or the bronze tablet before the third judgment is authenticated. The substitution procedure and duplicate records prevent a single attack from ending the legal route, but delay may cost the ship.

The Ash Knives continue to prefer Ianthe's death after sanctuary and before boarding. Recognition changes the political consequences of murder, not their objective.

## Dorion's harbor strategies

Dorion makes every action answerable to the deposited warrant. He does not improvise a secret power when a public clause will not carry him.

**Demand private examination.** He argues that armed companions may be scripting Ianthe's answers. Myrine may grant a brief, visible separation. Ianthe can use it to confirm or qualify the bodyguards' account.

**Contest the seal, not the petitioner.** Dorion deposits his warrant and asks Myrine to hold the seal as Aulonite state property. This can succeed even when Ianthe receives sanctuary.

**Offer a delay bargain.** He offers release of village sureties, amnesty for helpers, or recognition of sanctuary in exchange for the Sea-Lark's departure without Ianthe and a formal mediation period. Ianthe may accept if civilian costs or immediate bloodshed matter more than rapid extraction.

**Accept a protected loss.** Once the third judgment is recorded, Dorion may publicly acknowledge Myrine's custody while denying Ianthe's political allegations. This preserves him as a future interlocutor and prevents desperate soldiers from beginning a war.

**Arrest the bodyguards outside.** Myrine's sanctuary for Ianthe does not automatically pardon road violence, theft, or deaths. Dorion may ask Myrine to hold the bodyguards, arrest them on Aulonite ground above the white stones, or exchange their freedom for the seal. The escort's own survival remains a separate objective.

## Objective-driven finale frames

### Seven stones under fire

Ianthe crosses while an attack develops from the spear court, fish market, or upper road. Bodyguards must protect her freedom of movement, not merely keep enemies distant. The objective ends when she reaches the hall, retreats by choice, is physically seized, or can no longer speak.

### Three records

The petition is spoken, but assassins or political actors target magistrates, registrar, tablets, and substitutes. The escort protects a legal process distributed among people and objects. Success may cost the Sea-Lark or leave fighters outside.

### The divided harbor

One group protects Ianthe, one contests the seal at the chainhouse, and one secures boarding. Dorion acts against the seal, the Ash Knives act against life or record, and Myrine acts against uncontrolled violence. Each group needs a named fallback if another objective fails.

### Hold the spear court

Ianthe and disarmed companions enter sanctuary while armed bodyguards hold Dorion's soldiers or assassins outside the line. The bodyguards may win the principal's safety and lose their own freedom. Dorion can be an opponent, temporary ally, or both across successive moments.

### Sanctuary without a ship

The Sea-Lark begins departure while the petition remains incomplete or the chainhouse is attacked. The escort must choose whether to rush extraction, compel a formal delay, finish the strongest legal procedure, or divide. Full success is possible but should require preserved preparation, witnesses, and control of separate routes.

### A joint defense no one owns

Dorion, Myrine, bodyguards, and perhaps Naevan's limited scouts identify an Ash Knife attack. They form a temporary defense with incompatible custody claims. After the attack, the formation must dissolve without anyone treating tactical position as Ianthe's consent.

## Mixed-success endings

### Petition, seal, ship, and escort

Ianthe completes the petition, retains or lawfully deposits the seal, boards the Sea-Lark, and takes at least one chosen bodyguard and useful witnesses. This is the broadest success. It does not crown her. It gives her distance, neutral recognition, and a political campaign.

### Sanctuary with the seal lost

Dorion, thieves, or confusion return the bronze seal to Kallias while Ianthe's petition succeeds. Kallias controls a powerful instrument of state and can question orders issued in her name. Ianthe retains her voice and Myrine's record.

### Sanctuary after the Sea-Lark sails

Ianthe is safe in Myrine but remains close to Aulon's diplomatic pressure. The bodyguards must help secure lodging, alternate transport, public support, and protection from later attempts. This is not failure disguised as success; it is a less decisive safety with a richer political aftermath.

### Ianthe sails while bodyguards remain

The bodyguards hold the road, accept arrest, are wounded, or cannot all fit aboard. Ianthe chooses whether to leave them under Myrine's law, bargain with the seal, or miss the ship rather than abandon them. Their compact gives her the right to decide; it does not require emotional indifference.

### Petition completed under contested escort conduct

Myrine records that Ianthe came freely overall but that particular bodyguards deceived, restrained, or endangered her. Sanctuary succeeds while the sworn relationship changes. Ianthe may dismiss, forgive, censure, or retain them under new terms.

### Dorion recovers the seal and public legitimacy collapses

Dorion gains the state object but is forced by witnesses to admit Ianthe travelled by choice and faced assassination. Kallias can govern with the seal while finding a voluntary-looking protector proclamation much harder to obtain.

### Voluntary mediation

Ianthe accepts Myrine custody without immediate sailing in exchange for released sureties, withdrawal of troops, or a fixed Assembly summons. This can preserve more lives and give Kallias time. Record it as Ianthe's political choice, with both gains and concessions named.

### Renunciation

Ianthe freely renounces succession while asking sanctuary and a free Assembly. The Ash Knives lose their stated justification for killing an heir but may still regard her as a dangerous symbol. Kallias loses the royal assent he sought. Royalist allies may feel betrayed even while her agency is fully preserved.

### Elven extraction at the threshold

If the harbor becomes untenable and Naevan remains available, Ianthe may choose the White Cypress road rather than petition. She survives under foreign protection and concedes much of the political story she wanted. The bodyguards may accompany her, refuse, or ensure the choice is recorded as hers.

### Dorion's recovery under witnesses

Ianthe accepts or is forced into Dorion's custody before the third record, but Myrine, village, and road witnesses preserve evidence that any later assent is contested. Her physical loss does not erase the road's public record or end the political conflict.

### The dead heir

If the Ash Knives kill Ianthe after visible warning and failed protection, resolve what survives: the seal, the magistrates' record, witnesses to Kallias's conduct, the bodyguards, and Dorion's response. Her death may destroy the succession, create a martyr, discredit Kallias, vindicate republican terror to some, and bind the survivors to consequences. It should never erase the road that produced it.

## Immediate aftermath

Once the urgent objectives resolve, ask and record:

- Where is Ianthe physically, and whose law protects or restrains her?
- Did she speak a free petition, and what exact remedy did she request?
- Who holds the bronze seal?
- Did the Sea-Lark depart with her, without her, or not at all?
- Which bodyguards remain free, wounded, arrested, missing, or dismissed?
- Which road communities paid costs, received protection, or gained witnesses?
- What did Dorion publicly acknowledge?
- What account will reach Aulon first?
- Did Ianthe's relationship with the bodyguards survive the methods used to protect her?

End the road when these answers are stable enough to state; later political consequences belong to aftermath.
