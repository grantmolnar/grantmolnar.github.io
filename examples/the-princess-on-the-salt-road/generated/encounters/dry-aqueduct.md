# The Dry Aqueduct

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** escort, city-exit, stealth, hazard

Beneath the baths, lime throats, old water, and a single order of march divide people from horses and force authority to travel as weight.

## Opening View

Once Myrto’s false cistern stone closes, the passage permits only one order of march. Shields turn, packs scrape, and the bronze seal pulls one cloak out of line. Fresh wax shines in a worker’s niche beside a severed maintenance cord. Farther down, a lamp crosses the broken settling bowl and disappears. Overhead, city voices pass without slowing. Ahead, the measured drip becomes a run as pressure gathers behind the swollen lower sluice.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

The old southeast aqueduct once carried spring water beneath Aulon’s wall and into the public baths. A raised channel replaced it; an earthquake opened the lower course; magistrates sealed the maintenance mouth rather than pay masons to make the hidden damage honest. Lime workers, smugglers, and children have used pieces of it ever since. The wall remembers traffic the city no longer records.

The route begins behind Myrto’s cistern or beneath the bath furnace and ends in an olive cutting two miles outside the wall. No horse passes. Rigid armor catches at the lime throat; packs travel ahead or behind. A bodyguard who turns to fight may block everyone she protects. Before entry, decide what weight survives the crawl, what authority takes another road, and whether Ianthe or the seal should draw the first search.
## The channel

The aqueduct has three practical sections.

**The lime throat** is narrow enough that travelers move one at a time. Loose stone above it makes noise travel farther than people. A rear guard cannot see the front. Establish marching order and signals before entering; this is bodyguard work, not bookkeeping.

**The broken bowl** is a circular settling chamber with a collapsed roof. Moonlight reaches it through fig roots. **Pela the Limeburner** and two cousins use the chamber to store untaxed lamp oil. Pela will guide the escort, demand payment, ask Ianthe to pardon an imprisoned brother, or sell the route later depending on how she is treated. She is not secretly loyal to either side.

**The lower sluice** is closed by a swollen wooden gate. Opening it clears the exit but releases old water from a side reservoir. The escort can raise it slowly, break it, divert the water through a maintenance crack, or climb the collapse above. A careful repair costs time. A violent opening sends muddy water into the bath drains and tells Aulon where the fugitives went.

## Threats in confined ground

Dorion's soldiers will not enter blindly in force. If they discover the route, they occupy both mouths, send two shielded scouts, and offer terms through the stone. Their aim is to make the bodyguards choose between surrender and exposing Ianthe to a dangerous climb.

The Ash Knives can exploit the same geometry with fewer people. One attacker in front and one behind can turn an escort into separated pairs. Give attentive characters signs: fresh wax on a wall niche, a maintenance cord cut at shoulder height, or a voice repeating a password with the wrong bath-house accent.

Ianthe accepts being placed in the middle of the order. She objects if every competent fighter goes ahead and leaves her with whoever happens to be weakest. She can help hold a lamp, remember old water marks, and speak to Pela without requiring a bodyguard to impersonate her judgment.

## Exiting the channel

The olive cutting provides concealment but no mounts. The Gate of Horns road lies east; the Cypress road lies west; the House at Three Cypresses is six miles south over low ridges. The escort can steal or buy farm animals, continue on foot, rendezvous with a decoy group, or send a fast runner ahead.

The aqueduct's principal reward is an uncertain lead. Dorion may not know which exit was used. Its principal cost is physical: reduced equipment, fatigue, separation, and delayed access to the mounted road. Do not convert those costs into a single test. Let the confined order make them decide what protection means without a clean shield wall.

## Choosing beyond the olive cutting

Water marks, shepherd cairns, and Pela’s knowledge reveal three continuations. The south ridge reaches the House at Three Cypresses. An eastern sheep track drops to the Salt Road north of Red Bridge. The cracked feeder channel runs southwest to the Heron levee above the Reed Villages. It cannot take carts and punishes armor, but it preserves the aqueduct’s ambiguity instead of returning the fugitives to the first obvious road.

Ianthe favors the bridge when the escort can recover mounted speed without giving Dorion immediate control of the remounts. She favors the reeds when pursuit remains unfixed, the principal and seal must stay divided, or the escort has already sacrificed too much equipment to contest a military post. She does not choose the slowest road merely because it is hidden; the second-sunset deadline remains one of the facts she weighs.

An escort that bypasses the courier house receives no automatic meal, fresh tack, or warning about the station's sabotaged cordial. It may reach the middle road earlier and with less complete information.

## If the route closes

A blocked mouth should create a custody problem, not a dead end. Pela knows a vertical furnace shaft that returns to the bath quarter, while the broken bowl opens onto a fig-root climb outside the wall. Either fallback separates the escort from cached equipment and may place Ianthe in public without warning. Dorion can also offer to withdraw from one mouth if the bodyguards surrender the seal or one named escort. He keeps literal terms and exploits anything left unstated.

Ianthe will consider caching the seal, sending it with Pela, or using it to bargain for a clear exit. She asks who will be endangered by each choice and whether the escort can later prove that any transfer was voluntary. The aqueduct should leave the group with a changed arrangement of people and objects, not merely fewer resources.
