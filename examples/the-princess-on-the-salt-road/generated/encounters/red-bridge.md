# The Red Bridge

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** escort, bridge, pursuit, military

At the mounted road’s hinge, remounts, signal shutters, waterworks, and the bronze seal wait for signatures while pursuit closes from the north.

## Opening View

Before the road reaches its first arch, the Red Bridge is moving horses, signals, and water. White shutters open toward Beacon Hill; the Heron Sluice wheel jars the western pier. Sergeant Melas Orro halts the remounts at a red line while Engineer Ione Marr holds the brake. Kallias’s writ and the older road code lie beneath the same wax box. A northern lookout lifts his horn. Melas sets the crossing register beside the line and waits for a signature.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

The Red Bridge is the last place on the Salt Road where one signed order can still outrun rumor. The western post has kept every horse saddled, every shutter dark, the wagon barrier half-ready, and the Heron Sluice at its ordinary mark while two incompatible writs wait in one wax box. A decision here can refresh Ianthe, refresh Dorion, warn Myrine, strand a company, or spend the villages’ water.

Nothing at the bridge fails alone. Stone, horseflesh, rope, shutters, and moving water carry the consequence into another person’s road.

Sergeant **Melas Orro** commands twelve road soldiers, two grooms, a signal apprentice, and the engineer **Ione Marr**. On his table lie two orders that fit the same wax box and point in opposite directions. Kallias’s writ commands detention, seal recovery, and immediate report. The older road code requires remounts and passage for the royal house unless a court has entered judgment. Kallias has named protection, not judgment. Melas has therefore lowered Leandros’s pennant, refused Kallias’s white, and kept every horse saddled but unissued.

Melas will not let the loudest claimant become lawful by arriving first. Give him a procedure he can repeat at the inquiry, a person who will sign it, and a limit on what happens after the bridge. He can stand inside that.
## Inheritance from the road

Do not reset the escort when it reaches the bridge. Carry forward the facts named at the House at Three Cypresses.

- **Unfixed pursuit:** the garrison may know that someone is moving south without knowing which group carries Ianthe. The escort can approach as couriers, travelers, mourners, or a divided column before Melas commits the signal mast.
- **Named pursuit:** Melas has a description, a likely road, or an authenticated seal report. He still has not necessarily accepted Kallias's abduction account. He asks Ianthe to speak where his soldiers can hear her.
- **Closing pursuit:** a bridge herald, dust line, or signal from the northern ridge announces Dorion within the scene. Any extended inspection, negotiation, or redistribution of horses gives him time to occupy the north approach.
- **Contact:** Dorion's riders are already visible or share the bridge precinct under a truce. The question is not whether he appears but who controls the deck, stables, mast, and lower works when the truce ends.

Also establish who carries the bronze seal, which mounts are sound, whether the Ash Knife sabotage at the rendezvous was discovered, what Dorion believes was agreed, and whether Eleni, Naevan’s scouts, witnesses, or household evacuees accompany the escort. Each changes what can be attempted without splitting the protection detail further.

## Arrival without the courier house

An escort that bypassed or missed the House at Three Cypresses can still read the bridge before entering it. The north toll board lists remount reservations, the signal shutters are visible from the last rise, fish carts mark the turn toward the Heron towpath, and travelers can identify whether Dorion's orders arrived ahead. Melas asks who commands the group and what authority or witnesses support its account; he does not require a token from Thessa.

Do not grant resources the escort did not obtain. Direct arrivals may be hungry, on poor mounts, without signal tools, and unaware of Dorion's precise settlement boundary. They also reach the post earlier and may find its officers less prepared. The sabotaged road mare at the bridge is an independent Ash Knife operation; the station's tampered tack and cordial do not follow an escort that never used them.

If the bridge is closed beyond practical seizure, the lower ledge reaches the Heron towpath and Reed Villages. A longer gorge path reaches the eastern ridge toward Beacon Hill on foot. Bypassing the bridge deck loses remounts and public command of the post; it does not end the journey.

## The bridge precinct

Use the precinct as five connected objective areas rather than a blank battlefield.

### The north approach

The Salt Road descends between two cut-stone walls before opening onto the bridge. A cavalry company can form here, but cannot pass more than three abreast onto the deck. A milestone shrine gives witnesses and heralds a place to stand outside immediate weapon reach. Dorion prefers to establish his line here because he can contain the crossing without appearing to storm a royal post.

### The bridge deck

The deck is one hundred and twenty paces long, broad enough for two wagons to pass with care. Waist-high parapets offer cover from the gorge but little protection from either approach. A red line at the midpoint marks the boundary between the old Aulon road district and Myrine's protected trade corridor. The line has no sanctuary force, but it gives Melas a customary place for parleys, transfers, and crossing truces.

Two capstan housings near the western end control a retractable wagon barrier. Closing it stops horses and carts while leaving a narrow pedestrian gap. Ione can jam the barrier in either position if given several uninterrupted minutes.

### The stable court

The stable lies behind the western guardhouse. It holds:

- six cavalry remounts reserved under Dorion's changing-horse order;
- four postal horses fit for a hard ride to Beacon Hill;
- two steady road mares suitable for Ianthe, a wounded rider, or a conspicuous decoy;
- one mule and a light repair cart; and
- saddles, courier cloaks, feed, water, and three sealed dispatch tubes.

The escort cannot simply take every animal at full speed. Six riders can change mounts in a few minutes if the stable remains orderly. Refitting armor, transferring packs, disguising horses, or selecting animals for a decoy consumes meaningful time. A frightened or wounded horse is not restored because the escort reached a remount station.

The stable has two exits: the road gate and a narrow manure lane descending toward the lower works. The lane can pass people and one led horse at a time. It is an excellent escape route until panic, fire, or a fallen animal blocks it.

### The signal mast

The mast has three shutter lamps and a dayboard. It can send a brief code to Beacon Hill in less than a minute when visibility is clear. The relevant signals are:

- **one amber opening:** an authenticated royal command or bearer has reached the bridge;
- **two white openings:** the princess is believed under coercion and armed recovery is requested;
- **one black opening:** assassins, sabotage, or an uncontrolled armed threat is present;
- **amber over black:** the authenticated traveler has departed by the lower road or reedward route; and
- **no confirmation:** the bridge refuses to authenticate any report.

The mast reports what the operator sends, not objective truth. Melas normally requires two witnesses for a political signal. The bronze seal can compel a stable requisition, but its impression also gives the signal apprentice grounds to send the amber authentication unless the escort controls, persuades, or disables the mast.

Destroying the lamps denies immediate confirmation. It also prevents the bridge from warning Beacon Hill about Ash Knife activity, a damaged descent, or a civilian evacuation.

### The lower works

Stone stairs inside the western abutment descend to maintenance ropes, the gorge inspection ledge, and the **Heron Sluice**. The ledge provides a hazardous crossing beneath the deck for people carrying light equipment. A sure-footed detail can move Ianthe across without entering the garrison court, but not quickly, not mounted, and not while under unopposed fire from above.

The sluice diverts Kestros water into the upper reed channels. Its ordinary setting keeps the village channels navigable and the lower fields wet. Closing it for several hours drops the hidden channels enough to strand boats and expose footprints. Opening it fully produces a fast surge that can speed a prepared boat downstream, sweep an unprepared one into pilings, and flood the lowest homes and seed beds. Breaking the mechanism threatens an entire season's water.

The bridge and villages therefore share one piece of infrastructure. Sabotage is not a clean way to inconvenience pursuit.

## Melas Orro and the bridge hearing

Melas holds the hearing in the stable court because every claimant must stand beside the horses, signal shutters, and sluice accounts affected by the decision. He permits one speaker for Ianthe, one for Kallias, and one for the road post. Ione Marr may interrupt any promise that spends bridge stone or water without naming the repair.

Melas asks three questions in order: *What authority do you present? What exactly do you ask this post to do? Who answers when that act is reviewed?* He is unimpressed by broad allegiance. A lawful order that says “secure the bridge” tells him less than an irregular order that names six horses, one hour, and a receipt.

His table voice is patient until someone pretends infrastructure has no owner: “You may borrow the road. You may not borrow the villages beneath it without saying their names.” A decision supported by witnesses, a bounded requisition, and a plan for the pursuit can move him. A demand to choose the true government cannot.
## Commanding the post with the bronze seal

The bronze seal remains powerful here because the bridge is a royal road installation. Pressing it into the requisition clay opens the stable stores and authorizes up to six remounts, road food, dispatch materials, and one signal. Kallias's later emergency writ remains in force.

Using it creates three consequences:

- the impression proves that the true seal reached the bridge;
- Melas must record who presented it and what was taken; and
- Beacon Hill may receive an authenticated route signal unless the mast is neutralized.

The escort can exploit the gap between object and person. A decoy bearing the seal can requisition horses and draw the amber report while Ianthe descends to the reed towpath. Ianthe can speak publicly while another rider takes the seal south. A bodyguard can surrender the seal to Melas under terms that deny both Dorion and Kallias immediate possession.

The seal solves supply. It does not solve custody.

## Dorion at the crossing

Dorion arrives according to the pursuit posture, but he never treats the bridge as a stage for a grand charge. He counts horses, watches the signal mast, reads Melas’s formation, and asks who has seen Ianthe since dawn. His first goal is to learn whether the principal or the bronze seal is actually present. His second is to keep the post usable after the encounter.

At the red line painted across the north approach, Dorion offers exact exchanges: a witnessed inspection for passage, a treaty chest for the seal, named prisoners for a delayed departure, shared defense against an identified assassin, or a fixed interval before he follows. He can concede road space, time, and individual releases. He cannot recognize Ianthe’s right to convene the Assembly while Kallias holds emergency command.

Dorion does not confuse cooperation with surrender. After a falling counterweight or Ash Knife attack, he restates the unresolved claim before anyone moves. “We saved the same life. We have not agreed whose order governs the next mile.”
## Ash Knives in the mechanism

Three Ash Knife agents have prepared the bridge because conflict between soldiers and bodyguards gives them the confusion they need.

- **Kyras Pell**, posing as a replacement groom, has marked one steady mare as “princess-safe.” A thin glass barb beneath the saddle flap carries a fast paralytic. It is meant to leave Ianthe conscious enough to fall badly and unable to speak clearly.
- **Tara Venn**, the signal apprentice's supposed cousin, has replaced one shutter pin. A hard pull on the amber lamp will release the counterweight into the stable court, panicking the horses and drawing every guard's eye upward.
- **Olos Rhem** waits on the inspection ledge beneath the third arch with a short bow and a salt-glass arrowhead. His shot is reserved for the instant Ianthe stands exposed at the midpoint or mounts the marked mare.

Their plan is modular. Discovering one agent does not automatically reveal the others, but it gives the escort grounds to demand an assassin truce and inspect the bridge. The agents retreat rather than fight to the death if the kill becomes impossible. Capturing one can prove the Ash Knives are exploiting both factions, but does not reconcile Dorion and Ianthe.

Signs include a groom who does not know the feed bins, fresh tool scratches on the mast housing, a maintenance rope tied with a city rather than bridge knot, and salt-glass dust below the parapet. Let competent protection procedures find danger before initiative is rolled.

## Protection decisions

### Take the fast horses

Securing four or more postal or cavalry remounts preserves the best chance of reaching Beacon Hill before the second sunset. It usually requires the seal, Melas's consent, or seizure of the stable. Leaving Dorion's six reserved mounts intact allows a Closing pursuit to remain Closing after the escort departs. Scattering, disabling, or taking them can reduce pursuit, but creates stranded soldiers and a clear act against Aulonite infrastructure.

### Hold neutrality

The escort can persuade Melas to close the stable to everyone for one travel window, record Ianthe's free choice, and permit passage on the escort's existing mounts. This preserves the bridge, denies Dorion a fresh change, and avoids a royal requisition signal. It gives up the escort's own fastest option.

### Use the lower works

The inspection ledge and towpath can bypass a blocked deck or carry Ianthe toward the Reed Villages. The escort must decide who remains above to distract, negotiate, or carry the seal. Armor, wounded people, and frightened companions make the crossing slower. Falling, missile fire, or an opened sluice can turn a clever route into a rescue problem while the other objectives remain active.

### Divide the road

The bridge is ideal for a planned division:

- a seal-bearing rider takes fresh horses and the visible south road;
- Ianthe descends with a pilot or small guard toward the reeds;
- witnesses remain with Melas to preserve the record;
- Eleni or another courier carries a message to Beacon Hill; and
- heavier companions hold the bridge or follow once the pursuit commits.

Require a named rendezvous, signal, and fallback. If the escort does not provide them, uncertainty should arise from communication and timing rather than arbitrary separation.

### Damage the system

The escort can burn the stable, lame horses, cut the mast, jam the wagon barrier, drop part of the parapet, or destroy the sluice. Each has a different consequence. Do not treat “sabotage the bridge” as one abstract action.

- **Stable damage** slows everyone and may kill animals or trap grooms.
- **Mast damage** denies confirmation and emergency warnings.
- **Barrier damage** controls the deck without stopping the lower route.
- **Sluice damage** changes channel travel and harms the villages.
- **Structural demolition** can stop cavalry for days, kill people on the bridge, isolate trade, and make Ianthe appear willing to cripple the country she seeks to represent.

Ianthe may accept limited sabotage when it protects lives and preserves a route. She argues hard against destroying the bridge merely because it is efficient.

## Encounter frames

Use combat only when objectives collide and negotiation, stealth, or procedure has failed or been deliberately rejected.

### The falling counterweight

Tara triggers the mast during a hearing or requisition. The counterweight crashes into the stable court, horses break loose, and Olos takes his shot from below. The immediate objectives are to move Ianthe out of the sight line, stop the stampede from blocking the manure lane, prevent the wrong signal from being sent, and locate the shooter. Dorion's soldiers may help with any of these while still trying to close around Ianthe afterward.

### The stable seizure

The escort or Dorion attempts to take the remounts while Melas holds neutrality. Fighting spreads across stalls, the gate, and the narrow lane. Victory is measured by which horses leave sound, who controls the requisition log, and whether Ianthe departs freely—not by clearing every soldier from the map.

### Fire below the bridge

An Ash Knife agent ignites pitch near the lower works or opens the sluice while Ianthe is on the ledge. The escort must choose between pursuing the assassin, operating the gate, rescuing someone swept from the ledge, and preserving the hidden route. The bridge garrison can lower ropes if someone reaches Ione and makes the danger legible.

### Contact at the red line

Dorion and the bodyguards face one another during a crossing truce when an assassin attacks. Both sides have reason to protect Ianthe and preserve witnesses. When the assassin is contained, the tactical positions achieved during the rescue matter. Dorion does not forget his warrant, and the escort does not lose the benefit of having saved his people.

## Ianthe at the bridge

Ianthe understands the temptation to solve the scene by commanding everyone who once served her father. She also knows that forcing Melas to choose under the seal may leave him and his soldiers exposed when Kallias still holds Aulon.

She can:

- state publicly that she travels by choice;
- order a limited requisition and accept the record it creates;
- release Melas from personal loyalty while asking him to preserve neutral road law;
- negotiate a bridge truce with Dorion;
- surrender the seal without surrendering herself;
- authorize compensation for horses, damaged works, or village losses; or
- choose the dangerous lower path to avoid making the garrison fight for her.

Her bodyguards should advise which action best protects her and the road. She decides which political burden she is willing to carry.

## Outcomes and failure forward

- **Fast passage:** the escort leaves on fresh horses. State how many remounts remain for Dorion and what signal Beacon Hill received.
- **Neutral bridge:** Melas records Ianthe's free passage while denying both parties the cavalry reserve. The escort keeps legitimacy and loses some speed.
- **Lower-road escape:** Ianthe reaches the towpath or Reed Villages while a visible group remains above. The pursuit may follow the seal rather than the princess.
- **Dorion controls the deck:** the bodyguards can still hold the lower works, contest transfer, preserve witnesses, or follow Ianthe under the terms of custody.
- **Seal lost:** Kallias or Melas gains the object, but Ianthe's free petition remains possible. A decoy strategy may collapse or become more convincing.
- **Remounts scattered or killed:** the escort and pursuit both slow. The Reed Villages become more valuable, and public anger follows needless cruelty to animals or grooms.
- **Bridge damaged:** record exactly which function failed. Trade, signals, pursuit, and village water do not all change in the same way.
- **Ianthe wounded or paralyzed:** the bodyguards must protect her body and preserve the possibility of later free speech. Myrine will not accept a petition spoken for her.
- **Assassin captured:** Dorion may grant a bounded truce or escort against further attack. He resumes his custody demand once the immediate danger passes.
- **Civil bloodshed:** surviving soldiers, witnesses, and logs carry the story to Beacon Hill. The road does not forget who struck under truce or who protected enemies from murder.

Before departure, state: who crossed by which level, who carries the seal, which horses remain, what the bridge log says, what signal was sent, the Heron Sluice setting, Melas's allegiance or neutrality, Dorion's position, and the current pursuit posture.
