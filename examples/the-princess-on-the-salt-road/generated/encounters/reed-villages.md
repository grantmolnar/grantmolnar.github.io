# The Reed Villages

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** escort, villages, boats, elven-politics

Confiscated boats and punished households can hide Ianthe, carry witnesses, or evacuate civilians, but no channel moves one burden without assigning another.

## Opening View

A skiff drops into the mud before Kallias’s search detail can chain it above the landing. Other villagers follow, reclaiming boats whose confiscation has already put four surety names on Demea Pheros’s board. Mara Seven-Oars reads the upper cut from a painted pole. A supposed royal courier claims the narrowest hull; six elven outriders wait where the channel leaves Aulon. No household lights a lamp. Each boat can carry a principal, a witness, a horse, or a family—but not the whole cost of flight.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

A confiscation board has turned the three Reed Villages into one argument about who may still move. Boats lie above the waterline under guard, two pilots are jailed, four relatives stand as sureties, and every usable hull already belongs to a household that will lack it after Ianthe passes.

The channels can still hide a small company, move a public flotilla, carry horses, scatter pursuit, conceal the seal, or evacuate threatened households. They cannot do all six before the sluice changes, and no choice is paid by “the villages” in the abstract.

Kallias’s search detail has dragged most sound boats onto the confiscation landing, jailed two pilots, and taken four relatives as sureties. Naevan waits beyond the western reed line with six outriders and a road that leaves Aulonite law behind. Dorion may still be at Red Bridge or already bargaining for the channel mouths. An Ash Knife has entered as a loyal courier and selected the narrowest boat because there is nowhere to interpose once Ianthe sits down.

The protection problem is capacity made moral. Every hull assigned to Ianthe is a hull not carrying a witness, wounded rider, horse, or household. Every open flotilla strengthens her public account and tells pursuit which water to close. The bodyguards must decide what the road may ask of people who cannot leave it with them.
## Carry forward the bridge and rendezvous

State the inherited facts before play begins.

- Which road brought the escort: the cypress-house track, the lower works from Red Bridge, the public bridge road, or a split approach.
- Whether the Heron Sluice is at ordinary flow, closed, fully opened, damaged, or unknown.
- The pursuit posture and Dorion's last reliable information.
- Who carries the bronze seal and whether a visible decoy is still active.
- Which mounts, wounded companions, witnesses, prisoners, or evacuees accompany Ianthe.
- What aid Naevan believes Ianthe requested and whether a written request exists.
- Which road communities have already been exposed by the escort's choices.

The villages respond to those facts. A hidden group is not greeted by the same crowd as a royal column. A flooded channel is not merely faster travel. A seal-bearing decoy does not remain useful if everyone in the village is told the truth aloud.

## Arrival without the courier house

An escort may enter the reeds directly from any city exit. Gate travelers turn at the Heron mile-stone; aqueduct travelers follow the cracked feeder channel; funeral-road travelers use the old causeway. Demea and Mara judge arrivals by conduct, named connections, and what danger follows them, not by possession of Thessa's courier token. Naevan can state his own terms in person if the escort never received his offer at the courier house.

Direct arrivals keep whatever time and ambiguity they preserved, but they do not acquire the courier house's food, treatment, remounts, decoy preparation, or complete warning about sabotage. Establish who knows the local channels. Without a pilot, the broad Salt Cut remains obvious, while the Black Thread and Cypress Drain require discovery, persuasion, or following physical signs at a cost in time or exposure.

If the channels become unusable, the Heron levee returns to the Red Bridge and the coastal towpath continues toward Beacon Hill. A blocked boat route changes transport and pursuit; it does not trap the escort in an abstract reed maze.

## The channel network

Use four named routes. A pilot knows all four; a competent outsider can follow the broad cuts but cannot reliably distinguish concealed junctions at night.

### The Salt Cut

The Salt Cut is the broad service channel from the Red Bridge sluice through Kalamai to Vathys. It can carry the ferry barge, horses, and several boats together. It is also visible from the levee road and easy to block with a chain, felled rack, or line of soldiers. A public procession on the Salt Cut produces many witnesses and no meaningful concealment.

### The Black Thread

The Black Thread leaves the basin beneath overhanging reeds and reaches within a mile of Beacon Hill. It is too narrow for the ferry barge and difficult for broad punts when the sluice is low. Two skiffs can pass in single file. Branches look identical, and one wrong turn ends at a mud pocket or household landing.

The Black Thread offers the best concealment for Ianthe, but requires a village pilot and a small protection detail. Heavy armor, horses, a large witness train, and obvious royal baggage do not fit.

### Pilots' Run

Pilots' Run is a straighter eastern channel marked by notches on willow posts. It reaches the hidden landing below Beacon Hill before the Black Thread does and can carry broad punts. The markings are readable to anyone who learns the sequence, which makes the route faster and easier for pursuit to follow.

### The Cypress Drain

The Cypress Drain runs west toward the old forest boundary. It is shallow, half choked, and useless to cavalry. Naevan's outriders can meet travelers at its upper bank and guide them beyond Aulonite jurisdiction. It does not lead to Myrine. Sending a boat or tracks into the drain can support a decoy; accepting the route in earnest changes Ianthe's destination and custody.

## Water and travel windows

The villages track water openly with a painted pole at Vathys. State the current water and what will change it.

### Ordinary flow

The Salt Cut, Pilots' Run, and most of the Black Thread are navigable. Broad boats move steadily, skiffs can hide, and the ferry barge can carry horses. This is the villagers' preferred state.

### Low flow

If the Heron Sluice is closed, the Black Thread becomes patchy within one travel window. Skiffs may be dragged across mud shelves, leaving tracks. The Salt Cut remains passable to light craft but the ferry barge grounds. Pursuing cavalry can use exposed maintenance banks farther into the reeds.

### Flood surge

A fully opened sluice sends a swift pulse through the upper channels. A ready pilot can ride it quickly toward Beacon Hill. Boats tied badly, children on low walkways, and the seed beds at Kalamai are endangered. Soldiers cannot form in the channels, but floating debris and broken racks create hazards. The surge is a chosen event with visible civilian cost, not a free speed bonus.

### Damaged works

If the bridge mechanism failed, water behavior is uncertain but not unknowable. Demea and the pilots can read the pole, debris, and sound of the upper cut. Repair, waiting, or sending a scout can replace uncertainty with information. Damaged infrastructure follows the pole, debris, sound, and earlier sabotage rather than arbitrary weather.

## Boats and capacity

At the start of the encounter, the community possesses the following craft unless earlier events changed them:

- **Two broad fishing punts**, each carrying one pilot and either four armored adults, six lightly equipped adults, or one horse with two handlers.
- **Three narrow skiffs**, each carrying one pilot and two other adults. A skiff can use every branch of the Black Thread.
- **One ferry barge**, carrying up to four horses, twelve adults, or two households with essential goods. It is slow, noisy, and confined to the Salt Cut and Pilots' Run.
- **Six small household coracles**, useful for messages, children, or one lightly equipped adult each. They are poor fighting platforms and cannot be treated as spare military skiffs.

Kallias's soldiers hold one punt, two skiffs, and the barge at the Kalamai watch landing. The villagers have hidden the other punt and one skiff. The remaining two skiffs need their plugs and oars returned from confiscation.

This means the escort can immediately move Ianthe with a small detail, or can first recover enough capacity for the whole company, horses, witnesses, or civilians. Recovery costs time and exposure. Taking every hidden craft leaves households without evacuation or fishing transport.

## People under pressure

### Demea Pheros, headwoman of Vathys

Demea receives claims at the basin steps with the four surety names written on a fish board behind her. She measures every noble promise in boats, roof spaces, children moved before dark, and soldiers likely to return after the escort is gone.

She does not ask whether Ianthe deserves loyalty. She asks what the princess will leave that can still protect a household when Kallias’s riders come back. A written obligation, public witness, evacuation place, released prisoner, or preserved boat is real. Gratitude is not.

Demea’s voice is flat and exact: “A princess needs one hull. A family needs one hull. Tell me which family becomes brave on foot.” She will aid a plan that acknowledges the subtraction and resists any request that treats village suffering as atmosphere around a royal escape.
### Mara Seven-Oars, senior pilot

Mara knows the channels by what touches the hull: reed hiss in the Black Thread, salt under the eastern wind, a double knock where the Cypress Drain narrows, cold water rising before the Heron Sluice opens. She distrusts maps that make every route the same width.

She will pilot Ianthe, a decoy, horses, witnesses, or evacuees, but she insists the escort choose what each boat is for before anyone boards. She names capacity in breathing bodies, not abstract slots, and refuses to overload a skiff because a noble object is called indispensable.

Mara speaks in corrections. “That is not cover; it is silence. That is not a fast channel; it is a channel fast boats can survive.” She respects Phaedra’s routecraft and mocks any plan that has a rendezvous but no tide, recognition sound, or person able to pole the fallback craft.
### Lieutenant Sosthenes Kar

Sosthenes commands eight Aulonite soldiers and four mounted auxiliaries at the Kalamai landing. His order is to deny boats to Ianthe, arrest known royal couriers, and report any foreign armed company. He has not been ordered to burn villages or execute hostages. He is using relatives as sureties because he lacks enough soldiers to search every house.

Sosthenes believes Kallias prevented the polis from collapsing after Leandros's death. He also knows that drowning civilians or handing Ianthe to assassins would destroy that claim. He can be negotiated with, deceived, isolated from his auxiliaries, publicly overruled by Ianthe, or defeated. He will exchange the pilots for the bronze seal only if he believes he can retain it. He may accept a civilian truce, boat inspection, or joint action against the Ash Knives.

### Naevan of the White Cypress

Naevan waits where cultivated reeds give way to white-cypress shade. His six outriders never enter a village together. He has learned that an elven formation can turn protection into evidence before anyone draws a bow.

He offers two things and keeps their names distinct. **Full reception** carries Ianthe to Serathiel’s court under White Cypress authority. **Limited aid** lends scouts, false trails, healing, or an evacuation screen to a Myrine-bound company, then withdraws at a written place. He will not disguise full custody as temporary assistance.

Naevan speaks of kin, doors, and seasons; Ianthe answers with terms, witnesses, and endings. He is most persuasive when Aulonite institutions have plainly failed and most dangerous when everyone lets the warmth of his offer stand in for its jurisdiction.
## The confiscation landing

Kallias's search detail has converted Kalamai's boat landing into a temporary post. A chain secures the barge and confiscated craft. The pilots and sureties are held in a raised grain shed. The mounted auxiliaries sleep in the reed-drying hall. Signal mirrors on the roof can contact the Red Bridge ridge or Beacon Hill on a clear day.

The escort can recover boats and people in several ways.

### Public command

Ianthe can enter the landing openly, state that she travels freely, and order Sosthenes to release the boats and prisoners. The bronze seal strengthens the command but authenticates her position. Soldiers may obey, hesitate, demand that she accept protective custody, or split according to how the escort handles witnesses and weapons.

A public command creates strong testimony against the abduction story. It also moves pursuit at least to **Named** and may make it **Closing** if a signal leaves.

### Exchange or limited settlement

Sosthenes can trade boats, prisoners, or passage for concrete security: the seal placed in neutral hands, Ianthe's written statement, elven outriders remaining west, wounded soldiers released, or the escort using one route while his patrol holds another. He cannot promise Myrine passage, but he can lack the force to stop it and choose not to cause a massacre.

### Covert recovery

The grain shed can be reached by a household walkway, the chain can be opened from below, and hidden coracles can move rescuers behind the landing. Stealth preserves ambiguity until something is discovered. Freed prisoners and missing boats still need somewhere to go.

### Force

The landing is defensible from the levee and vulnerable from the water. Combat objectives include open the chain, free the pilots, prevent a mirror signal, secure enough boats, and keep soldiers from firing into houses. Sosthenes retreats to the levee rather than burn the village if given a path. Mounted auxiliaries may abandon him if they believe Dorion will soon arrive.

## The false loyalist boat

**Philon Vask** presents himself as a courier sent by Myrto. He carries a copied royal token, knows the phrase “blue lamps before dawn,” and offers a narrow skiff already in the water. He claims the Black Thread is closing and urges Ianthe to travel immediately with only one pilot and two guards.

The token is wrong in three ways: the reed binding turns clockwise, the courier tally omits the household mark, and the boat is recorded under a pilot arrested yesterday. Demea or Eleni can identify the errors. A bodyguard who checks the hull finds a waxed seam that can be pulled open from the stern. An accomplice waits at a narrow bend with a reed-colored spear and a fire pot meant to divide rescuers from the sinking boat.

Philon's plan relies on urgency and the idea that a smaller escort is automatically safer. If exposed, he attempts to flee into the reeds or ignite the drying racks, forcing a choice between pursuit and protecting villagers. He does not need to duel the escort. He needs Ianthe separated, afloat, and unable to reach a bank before the second strike.

## Ianthe and the village claim

Ianthe knows that every boat used for her flight is a boat not used for fishing, evacuation, or the families under threat. She does not insist on solving every injustice before moving. She does insist that the costs be named and that people understand whether they are volunteering, bargaining, or being commanded.

She can:

- sign a debt and reparations order under the bronze seal;
- publicly claim responsibility for village aid, protecting households from the abduction narrative while exposing them as supporters;
- order Sosthenes to release prisoners and risk a direct confrontation;
- leave the seal as security while continuing to Myrine;
- authorize Naevan to evacuate two households;
- accept full elven extraction;
- travel hidden with a small guard while sending witnesses or the seal openly; or
- leave immediately, preserving the deadline while accepting that Kallias's reprisals continue.

Her preferred outcome is not “save everyone before sunset.” It is to reach Myrine with enough freedom and public credibility to act against the system producing the reprisals. The bodyguards may reasonably argue that this requires leaving people behind. Demea may reasonably answer that royal strategy always discovers the importance of later reform at the moment villagers need help now.

## Route plans

### Hidden principal

Ianthe, Mara, and two to four bodyguards take skiffs through the Black Thread. The seal, visible riders, or a broad punt travel by Pilots' Run or back toward Red Bridge. This is the strongest screen and the weakest immediate protection formation. It requires trust in the pilot and a clear rendezvous below Beacon Hill.

### Public flotilla

Ianthe travels on a broad punt or the ferry barge with witnesses, released prisoners, and visible village support. The route is slower and easy to follow, but every observer can testify that she directs the movement freely. The bodyguards can form a stronger perimeter and carry wounded people or heavy gear.

### Evacuation convoy

The ferry barge and broad punts carry two threatened households, essential goods, and an escort toward either Naevan's boundary or the eastern landing. This consumes capacity and time. It may preserve the village network, create durable allies, and cause the Sea-Lark window to become doubtful.

### Red Bridge reversal

The escort can move through the upper towpath back to the lower works of Red Bridge, especially if the bridge deck is held by Dorion or a decoy. This route allows sequential play between the two encounters and can recover separated companions, change the sluice, or exploit a pursuit committed to the wrong road.

### Elven extraction

Ianthe enters the Cypress Drain and accepts White Cypress protection. This can be a deliberate mixed ending, a contingency after severe injury, or an imposed custody the bodyguards later contest. It is not secretly the same as reaching Myrine.

## Pursuit in the reeds

Mounted pursuit loses formation at the channels but does not vanish.

- **Unfixed:** search parties watch the Salt Cut and major levees. A hidden pilot route can pass without direct contact, especially if a seal decoy remains credible.
- **Named:** Sosthenes or a signal has identified Ianthe's presence. Broad channels are watched, households questioned, and boats counted. The Black Thread remains usable if the escort controls information and pilots.
- **Closing:** Dorion has reached Red Bridge or the upper levee. He can request the sluice closed, send riders to the eastern landing, or negotiate with Demea. If the bridge signal is unreliable, he must divide his force.
- **Contact:** Dorion or Sosthenes controls one channel exit while the escort controls boats or another bank. Crossing, civilian, witness, and assassin truces are all useful. The geometry favors negotiation because neither side can move a large force without exposing boats and houses.

A beacon report can move pursuit toward the eastern landing. A convincing Cypress Drain decoy can draw riders west. Burning reeds can reveal every boat and harm the community. State what information changed the pursuit rather than advancing it because the scene feels slow.

## Encounter frames

### The boat-chain fight

The escort attempts to free the confiscated craft while Sosthenes's soldiers hold the levee. The objectives are the chain, pilots, signal mirror, and safe channel—not total defeat of the post. Villagers move through the scene, and careless missiles can turn allies into casualties.

### Fire in the drying racks

Philon or another Ash Knife ignites the reed racks to force Ianthe toward a prepared boat and to draw bodyguards away. Wind carries fire toward houses. The escort must protect Ianthe, contain the fire, identify the false courier, and preserve boats. Naevan or Sosthenes may cooperate against the fire without yielding his political position.

### Pursuit at the fork

Boats reach the junction of Black Thread and Pilots' Run as soldiers appear on the levee or in a confiscated punt. The escort can cut a tow rope, switch Ianthe between boats under cover, ground the pursuit, open a household passage, or abandon cargo. The encounter ends when the route becomes uncertain or one side controls the next fork, not when every pursuer is dead.

### The flood pulse

A surge from Red Bridge reaches the basin during movement. Prepared pilots gain speed; unprepared craft strike racks and walkways. The bodyguards may need to rescue civilians, keep Ianthe aboard, recover the seal from another boat, or choose which craft receives the only safe tow line. The event should follow an established sluice decision or known damage, not appear as random punishment.

### Contact with Naevan

Dorion's or Sosthenes's force reaches the Cypress Drain while Ianthe considers extraction. Naevan will defend people who cross into his protection but will not stage an Aulonite ambush. The scene can become a three-way negotiation over the boundary, a race to evacuate households, or a brief fight against an Ash Knife attack designed to make foreign and Aulonite forces blame each other.

## Route splitting procedure

When the escort divides, write or state five facts for each group:

1. **Principal or object:** Ianthe, seal, witnesses, horses, prisoners, civilians, or signal tools.
2. **Leader:** who makes immediate decisions.
3. **Route:** Salt Cut, Black Thread, Pilots' Run, Cypress Drain, levee, or Red Bridge towpath.
4. **Rendezvous and recognition:** where, when, and how the groups identify one another.
5. **Fallback:** what the group does if the route is blocked or the signal fails.

Then apply pressure to the actual groups. Do not teleport bodyguards back around Ianthe when danger appears. A decoy fails only when pursuers gain evidence that identifies it. They act on the information they possess, not the table's complete view.

## Outcomes and failure forward

- **Hidden passage:** Ianthe reaches the eastern landing with a small detail. The abduction story may strengthen unless witnesses or a visible decoy preserve her authorship.
- **Public village escort:** many people see Ianthe choose Myrine. Pursuit is Named or Closing, but Kallias cannot easily describe the movement as private abduction.
- **Recovered boats and prisoners:** the villages retain capacity and testimony. Time, injuries, or exposure may worsen.
- **Households evacuated:** Naevan or the ferry convoy removes people from retaliation. The road loses speed and may gain durable political allies.
- **Seal surrendered or separated:** Ianthe continues without the object while a decoy or neutral custodian changes pursuit.
- **Sosthenes holds Ianthe:** physical custody changes, but the channels, witnesses, and Dorion's warrant remain contestable. Transfer to Red Bridge or Beacon Hill creates another opportunity.
- **Full elven extraction:** Ianthe survives under White Cypress protection and does not petition at Myrine. Record whether she chose freely and whether the bodyguards accompany her.
- **Boats lost:** the escort can proceed on foot by levee, return to Red Bridge, accept elven aid, or arrive late. The route narrows without ending by fiat.
- **Village burned or flooded:** survivors, debts, and blame travel onward. Ianthe may gain sanctuary while beginning her cause with a community she failed or harmed.
- **Ash Knife exposed:** Dorion, Sosthenes, Naevan, and the villages all gain reason for a bounded anti-assassin cooperation. Their custody claims remain unresolved.
- **Ianthe isolated:** a small boat or forced separation becomes an immediate rescue and communication problem. It is not permission to remove her agency indefinitely.

Before leaving the Reed Villages, state: the water state, boat and passenger assignments, who pilots each craft, who carries the seal, which prisoners or households remain, Naevan's role, what signal or witnesses reveal, the rendezvous below Beacon Hill, Dorion's last known position, and the pursuit posture.
