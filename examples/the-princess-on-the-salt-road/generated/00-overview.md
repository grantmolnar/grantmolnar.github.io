## Synopsis

Before midnight, Kallias’s soldiers wrench the Assembly’s bronze speaking rail from its sockets and hang white command lamps over Aulon. Princess Ianthe—half-elven heir to the old royal house—refuses to name him protector and leaves with the city seal beneath her cloak. Her bodyguards have until the second sunset to carry her thirty-six miles to neutral Myrine, where seven white stones, three magistrates, and the waiting Sea-Lark can place her beyond Kallias’s lawful reach. The open Salt Road buys speed with witnesses. The dry aqueduct hides people but strands horses. Funeral peace binds every blade. Bridge remounts may carry rescue or pursuit, and every reed boat taken from the bank is one a household cannot use. Kallias needs Ianthe alive, the Ash Knives need the last heir dead, and her mother’s kin offer a road so safe that she may reach the coast as their ward rather than Aulon’s free petitioner.

## Premise

Princess Ianthe trusts her few household guards to contradict her and still obey. Three nights after King Leandros died, Strategos Kallias occupied the Assembly, gates, mint, and messenger loft, then demanded that Ianthe lend his coup her name. She refused. A neutral league trireme waits at Myrine until the harbor chain rises at the second sunset. Sanctuary begins only when Ianthe crosses seven white stones unbound and speaks before three magistrates in her own voice. Choose which road spends secrecy, which spends time, which households bear the flight, what the bronze seal is permitted to command, and when guarding Ianthe requires releasing control of her.

## Explanation

Aulon balances an old royal house, a citizen Assembly, and a professional strategos elected for war. King Leandros died of fever before physicians, household officers, and Assembly witnesses after the winter campaign; the funeral rolls, sickroom tablets, and public notices agree. Kallias found a divided Assembly, an adult half-elven heir, and an army still answering wartime warrants; he decided that closed gates were safer than an uncontrolled vote. His diagnosis is not wholly false. His remedy is a coup.

Ianthe is twenty-seven, trained in civic law, a practiced rider, and a poor liar. Her mother, Serathiel of the White Cypress, returned to her own people ten years ago after every family appearance became a referendum on foreign rule. Ianthe stayed. She is Aulonite before she is anyone’s elven cousin. The palm-weight bronze seal bears three hills above an open hand. It requisitions horses, opens granaries, and authenticates decrees; it cannot fear, consent, or ask protection. Only Ianthe’s living, uncoerced speech can begin sanctuary. Seal and princess may therefore travel separately.

Kallias sends Captain Dorion Vey under three bounded orders: return Ianthe alive, recover the seal intact, and avoid the public bloodshed that would expose emergency rule as naked tyranny. Kallias wants a protector proclamation that looks voluntary. Dorion never enlarges the warrant in private or pretends it says less than it does. He writes each concession twice, names its witnesses and effective hour, protects Ianthe from assassins because his first order requires a living princess, and resumes the custody demand when the shared danger ends. His exact obedience makes him trustworthy inside a bad warrant and dangerous at its edge.

The Ash Knives have reached the opposite conclusion: neither general nor princess should inherit the crisis. They want Ianthe dead before monarchy can become useful again, and loyalist colors let murder travel inside pursuit. Naevan, Serathiel’s envoy, waits among the Reed Villages with six outriders and a forest road. His protection is real. So is the jurisdiction concealed inside every leaf-sealed promise.

Myrine makes neutrality out of visible labor. Chain crews keep the harbor open. Guards separate armed claimants. A registrar preserves duplicate tablets. Three magistrates ask four plain questions: who are you, whose choice brought you, whom do you fear, and what remedy do you seek? Before the second sunset, the Sea-Lark can carry a lawful petitioner beyond Aulonite reach. Afterward, sanctuary remains, but clean extraction is gone. The road therefore measures time, witnesses, divided custody, public authorship, and the duty to protect a woman who retains the right to decide what her safety is for.
