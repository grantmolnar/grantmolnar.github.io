# The Reed Villages

## Summary

Three linked settlements whose boats, pilots, households, channels, and shared water turn concealment and evacuation into finite civic choices.

## Reference Material

## Three settlements, not one refuge

Kalamai dries mats along the upper channels. Vathys stands on piles around the black basin. Nesos occupies a low island beside the pilots' eastward run and a traveler shrine near harbor water. Together they form a working community of households, pilots, landings, fish boards, drying racks, boats, and obligations rather than a blank place for fugitives to disappear.

## Four named waters

The broad Salt Cut carries large craft, horses, and visible companies. The narrow Black Thread offers concealment to small groups that trust a pilot. Pilots' Run reaches the eastern landing below Beacon Hill quickly enough to be read and followed. The Cypress Drain turns west toward the forest boundary and does not lead to Myrine. The Heron Sluice above the villages changes all four by changing water rather than by changing their names.

## Capacity has owners

A hull used for a principal is unavailable to a witness, wounded traveler, horse, prisoner, child, or household. Pilots possess route knowledge that a map cannot replace, and household craft remain civilian property even when a seal or soldier can compel their use. The villages can support concealment, a public flotilla, route division, evacuation, or testimony only by assigning a visible cost.

Current boats, pilots, sureties, confiscations, water level, fires, households, prisoners, routes, pursuers, aid, testimony, and civilian losses remain live encounter and operations-sheet state.
