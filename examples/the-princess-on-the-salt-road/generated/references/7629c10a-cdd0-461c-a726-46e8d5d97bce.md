# The Gate of Horns

**Reference ID:** `7629c10a-cdd0-461c-a726-46e8d5d97bce`

## Summary

Aulon’s public southern gate, where the royal-house charter, livestock traffic, witnesses, and the mounted Salt Road make passage fast and reviewable.

## Reference Material

## A gate built to be seen

The Gate of Horns opens Aulon directly onto the Salt Road. Paired towers, a broad road court, a livestock wicket, and nearby remount facilities make it the city's fastest mounted exit. Traders, drovers, clerks, soldiers, and citizens can all see how passage is requested and refused; secrecy is the one protection the gate does not offer.

## Charter before command

The older gate charter reserves passage for the royal household until the Assembly performs the civic act required to suspend that privilege. Wartime command may inspect travelers, regulate weapons, and execute a bounded order, but it does not silently become the judgment the charter names. A gate officer therefore needs the authority presented, the exact act requested, and a record fit for later inquiry.

## The straight road

Beyond the towers, the pavement continues toward Red Bridge. The Heron mile-stone marks a reedward turn, while courier and ridge spurs can reach the House at Three Cypresses. The direct road preserves horses, speed, and public testimony; those same surfaces make a departing company easier to name and pursue.

The gate's current doors, guards, orders, witnesses, remounts, traffic, passage decision, and any breach of charter remain live encounter state.
