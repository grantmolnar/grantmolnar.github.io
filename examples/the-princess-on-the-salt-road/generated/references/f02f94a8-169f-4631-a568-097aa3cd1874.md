## Exact obedience inside a bad warrant

Dorion Vey is the captain Kallias trusts with politically dangerous recovery work because he neither enlarges a clause in private nor pretends it says less than it does. He writes concessions twice, names their witnesses and effective hour, and expects every exception to survive later review. That exactness makes him trustworthy inside a bad warrant and dangerous at its edge.

## A living principal, intact evidence, public restraint

The commission that sends Dorion onto the road is framed around a living Ianthe, an intact bronze seal, and avoidance of public bloodshed that would reveal emergency protection as naked coercion. He can negotiate acts he has authority to defend, protect Ianthe from assassins because a dead principal defeats the commission, and acknowledge limits visible in older law or neutral procedure. He does not promise a city, office, or political settlement he cannot give.

## A truce is not consent

Dorion separates shared defense, physical control, written terms, and political assent. Fighting the same assassin does not settle custody; accepting protection from one attack does not ratify Kallias; and a tactical formation does not become Ianthe's choice.
