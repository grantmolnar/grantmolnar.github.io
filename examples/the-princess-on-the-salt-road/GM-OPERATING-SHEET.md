# GM Operating Sheet: The Princess on the Salt Road

## Governing question

**How do the bodyguards preserve Ianthe's life, freedom, and political agency when different forms of safety compete?**

Do not reduce the adventure to hit points, a hidden pursuit clock, or arrival at one map coordinate.

## Table language

Open each scene with a working object or visible condition before naming the abstraction: wax impressions before authority, saddle stitches before assassination, low water before scarcity, white flame before public account, rising links before deadline. Then name the person who bears the cost.

Keep the principal voices distinct:

- Ianthe separates who can move her, whose law claims her, and who authors the result.
- Dorion offers bounded acts he can defend later; he does not promise the city he cannot give.
- Naevan speaks in kinship, doors, and shelter; ask for the key, term, and route back.
- Road officers ask what the order does, who signs it, and what remains usable afterward.
- Village leaders count hulls, roofs, sureties, and returning soldiers.
- Myrine’s officers count lanes, records, bells, weapons, and lawful substitutions.

## Fresh-escort composition

The authoritative adventure supports three to five sworn bodyguards. They may be Aulonite, foreign, or mixed. Record each bodyguard's tie to Ianthe, the protection or civic competency they bring, and one kind of cost they are prepared to own. The group needs no fixed company name and need not reproduce the professions or disagreements in `PARTY-DESIGN.md`.

Adapt Kallias's abduction account to the actual escort. Outsiders become foreign blades, Aulonites become private household retainers outside civic command, and a mixed group becomes foreign instigators with compromised local servants. In every version, the accusation depends on suppressing Ianthe's refusal and treating armed protection as coercion. Let play defeat it through her choices, witnesses, records, signals, and petition.

The Narrow Shield is an optional demonstration company, not a design constraint. Do not require its four members, divided route, seal decoy, clue discoveries, or mixed ending.

## Start-of-encounter declaration

Before opening any encounter after the House of Blue Lamps, state these facts aloud:

1. **Travel window:** departure night, first dawn, first day, first sunset, second dawn, second day, or second sunset.
2. **Deadline confidence:** secure, doubtful, or lost for the Sea-Lark. Sanctuary may remain after the ship is lost.
3. **Pursuit posture:** Unfixed, Named, Closing, or Contact, with the evidence supporting it.
4. **Custody:** physical holder, legal claimant, and author of the public meaning.
5. **Ianthe:** condition, current objective, trust in the bodyguards, and any promise she has made.
6. **Seal:** holder, last authenticated use, and who knows or believes its route.
7. **Escort:** mounts, boats, equipment, wounds, prisoners, witnesses, and civilians.
8. **Divisions:** each group's leader, route, rendezvous, recognition sign, and fallback.
9. **Infrastructure:** remounts, bridge, sluice, channels, beacon, chain, and Sea-Lark as relevant.
10. **Adversary knowledge:** what Dorion, the Ash Knives, Naevan, and Myrine can actually know.

Do not reset these facts between encounters.

## Scene operating loop

### 1. Show the pressure

Describe the visible fact: dust, a changed lamp, a closed post, low water, armed mourners, a bell, a missing boat, or a line of witnesses. Name the pursuit posture when it changes.

### 2. State objectives, not sides

Give every active principal a concrete objective.

- The bodyguards protect a person, route, witness, signal, crossing, or transfer.
- Ianthe preserves one of her ordered objectives.
- Dorion seeks custody, seal, delay, or public legitimacy.
- The Ash Knives seek a killing opportunity, broken record, panic, or stranded principal.
- A local authority protects custom, infrastructure, households, or neutrality.

Temporary cooperation does not erase incompatible custody claims.

### 3. Offer more than one protection mode

At least two should be credible: shield, screen, witness, negotiate, or divide. A solution may combine them. Do not make combat the only way to act on an objective.

### 4. Resolve Ianthe's role

- **Immediate danger:** bodyguards may move, interpose, disarm, or extract without prior debate.
- **Counsel interval:** state Ianthe's objective and reasons; let the bodyguards present facts and costs.
- **Principal decision:** Ianthe decides her route, surrender, petition, renunciation, or accepted custody.

She changes her decision when facts, values, or custody change. She does not veto plans to prove independence or accept danger to preserve an authored scene.

### 5. Resolve the objective

End an encounter when its objective is secured, lost, abandoned, or transformed. Do not require extermination of every hostile participant.

### 6. Record the consequence

Change at least one concrete surface: time, pursuit, custody, route, force, witnesses, infrastructure, public account, trust, or civilian cost.

### 7. Deliver clues by use

- **Forward clue:** disclose a route or coming decision before commitment.
- **Operational clue:** alter tactics or negotiation in the current encounter.
- **Corroborating clue:** preserve evidence for Myrine, Aulon, or later public judgment.

Never gate an essential route behind one check. A failed examination changes time, exposure, or confidence; the only road remains available.

### 8. State the transition

Before moving on, repeat the inherited state that will matter in the next encounter.

## Dorion's warrant discipline

- Put every truce or concession in two copies with named witnesses and an effective hour.
- Dorion protects Ianthe from Ash Knife violence because his first order requires her alive.
- When that threat ends, resume the custody demand at the exact clause where it stopped.
- Do not let him invent private powers outside the deposited warrant.
- After Myrine's third judgment, end physical recovery inside sanctuary; preserve argument, seal claims, and lawful action outside the stones.

## Divided-escort threat allocation

For each adversary, answer in order:

1. Which groups does the adversary know exist?
2. Which person or object does the adversary believe each group carries?
3. What evidence supports the belief?
4. What route and transport can the adversary actually use?
5. Which single group receives the named adversary's personal attention?
6. What lesser force, local obstacle, or independent cell can affect another group?

A named NPC cannot appear in two places simultaneously. The same assassin cannot attack the seal group and Ianthe group unless travel makes that possible. An unlocated group does not become safe from weather, terrain, frightened locals, or a separately established Ash Knife cell.

## Ianthe's decision frame

Ianthe's objectives, in order:

1. Reach Myrine alive and make a free petition before the second sunset.
2. Prevent Kallias from counterfeiting continuity through her voice or seal.
3. Preserve visible Aulonite support and avoid becoming a permanent foreign ward.
4. Avoid civil bloodshed when a slower, costlier, or humiliating route can prevent it.

When objectives conflict, state which one she believes is presently least recoverable. The bodyguards may change her judgment with information, a concrete protection plan, or a credible account of costs. Repetition without new substance does not reopen every decision.

If the bodyguards deceive, drug, bind, or routinely silence her, survival may continue while trust and political authorship pass away from the escort. Record that as a custody consequence, not as an automatic campaign punishment.

## Bypass consequences

### Skip the courier house

Gain time and deny a fixed rendezvous. Lose guaranteed resupply, controlled rest, early sabotage discovery, and complete terms from Dorion and Naevan.

### Skip Beacon Hill

Gain time and avoid a contested tower. Myrine receives no authenticated advance account and begins with stricter separation, identification, and coercion inquiry.

### Miss the Sea-Lark

Lose immediate neutral extraction, not sanctuary itself. Continue with Myrine residence, another ship, arbitration, blockade pressure, elven protection, or negotiated custody.

## Clue timing checklist

Before a route choice, verify that the escort has at least two independent ways to know:

- where each credible route goes;
- its main protection advantage;
- its main cost;
- the current deadline implication; and
- whether using the seal, witnesses, or infrastructure exposes the route.

If the authored clues have not appeared because scenes were skipped, supply the same conclusion through road signs, a map, a guide, physical geography, or an adversary's visible preparations. Preserve the fact; vary the source and cost.

## Failure-forward checklist

On failure, choose the smallest change that follows the fiction:

- posture worsens or route becomes named;
- a mount, boat, signal, or weapon position is lost;
- time becomes doubtful;
- physical custody changes without assent;
- a witness or public account is weakened;
- infrastructure or civilians pay a stated cost;
- Ianthe's trust changes;
- a later objective becomes harder while remaining possible.

Do not use “captured, therefore adventure over” unless the table chooses to end on that custody outcome.

## Final resolution

Answer separately:

- Is Ianthe alive and able to speak?
- Did she act freely, and what did she choose?
- Who has physical and legal custody?
- Who authors the public account?
- Who holds the bronze seal?
- Did the Sea-Lark depart with her?
- Which bodyguards remain free and recognized?
- Which witnesses survive?
- What happened to the road communities and shared infrastructure?

The last answer is not “the princess is safe.” It is a custody, law, and authorship outcome with a human cost.
