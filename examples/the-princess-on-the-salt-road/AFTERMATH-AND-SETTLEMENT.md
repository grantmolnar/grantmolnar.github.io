# Aftermath and Settlement: The Princess on the Salt Road

The road ends when its burdens acquire new keepers. This sheet resolves the first hours and weeks after arrival; it does not replace the mixed-success endings in the Myrine Harbor encounter. Use it only after the table has separately named Ianthe’s life and agency, petition, custody, seal, ship, escort, witnesses, and the households and infrastructure left behind.

## Final objective ledger

| Objective | What success preserves | What loss changes |
|---|---|---|
| Ianthe's life | Any later political or personal choice remains possible | Succession ends in martyrdom, repudiation, or republican triumph depending on the surviving account |
| Capacity and free choice | Sanctuary, mediation, renunciation, or elven protection can be attributed to Ianthe | Other parties may preserve her body while contesting every act performed around her |
| Three Myrine judgments | Kallias cannot recover her without breaking treaty law | Myrine may still shelter her provisionally, but the legal position is slower and easier to contest |
| Bronze seal | Commands, treaties, stores, and public symbols remain available to its holder | Ianthe may retain personal and political legitimacy while losing immediate state capacity |
| Sea-Lark | Immediate neutral extraction and diplomatic movement | Sanctuary continues in Myrine, but blockade, mediation, another ship, or inland protection replaces departure |
| Bodyguard freedom | The escort can continue protecting, witnessing, and organizing | Ianthe may be safe while companions face arrest, exchange, trial, or political abandonment |
| Public witnesses and records | Competing coercion and abduction stories can be answered | The surviving holder of force gains disproportionate authorship of the road |
| Road communities and infrastructure | The escape does not consume the people and systems that made it possible | Fines, arrests, burned homes, lost irrigation, or destroyed crossings become part of Ianthe's first obligation |

Do not combine these into one success score. A petition with a lost seal is not half a petition. A ship lost after sanctuary is not a failed sanctuary. A living princess under unwanted wardship is not the same outcome as a free petitioner.

## First hour

Before ending the final session, record:

1. Ianthe's physical condition and last freely stated decision.
2. Which sanctuary stages are complete and where each record is held.
3. Physical and legal custody of Ianthe.
4. Physical and claimed ownership of the bronze seal.
5. Sea-Lark status, passengers, and those left ashore.
6. Weapon posture and legal status of every bodyguard.
7. Dorion's declared position after the result.
8. Captured, dead, escaped, or unidentified Ash Knives.
9. Named witnesses and where they will sleep that night.
10. Damage to Red Bridge, the Heron Sluice, boats, Beacon Hill, the harbor chain, and the Sea-Lark.
11. Villagers detained, evacuated, injured, fined, or promised aid.
12. Any White Cypress terms Ianthe accepted in writing.

## The first night

### Ianthe aboard the Sea-Lark

The league captain takes Ianthe beyond immediate Aulonite reach but not beyond politics. Her first choices are destination, public letter, and who may speak in her name while she travels. A prudent route sends duplicate petitions to three coastal poleis rather than carrying every record on one ship.

If some bodyguards remain in Myrine, Ianthe must decide whether to delay her wider appeal until they are secure. Doing so honors the escort and may surrender momentum. Proceeding without them may be politically necessary and personally costly.

### Ianthe in Myrine after the ship sails

Myrine assigns a sanctuary residence and separates foreign, Aulonite, and private armed escorts. Dorion may request mediation, surveillance, or bodyguard arrests without receiving Ianthe. Kallias can pressure the harbor economically while avoiding an open treaty breach.

The loss of immediate extraction makes the harbor a continuing political encounter. Another league ship, a public convocation, elven passage, or voluntary mediation may become the next objective.

### Ianthe under White Cypress protection

Naevan's route almost certainly defeats ordinary Aulonite pursuit. The immediate question becomes whether Serathiel treats protection as hospitality, kinship authority, or wardship. Ianthe can demand witnesses, written limits, a destination date, and the right to correspond with Myrine before accepting.

A free written choice makes the outcome politically costly but personally legitimate. An unconscious or coerced transfer preserves her life while leaving the bodyguards with a recovery problem rather than a completed escort.

### Ianthe in Dorion's custody

Record whether she entered voluntarily, was seized physically, or accepted a witnessed settlement. Dorion can prevent immediate assassination and may keep specific promises within his authority. He cannot manufacture free assent merely by keeping Ianthe alive.

Public witnesses can make a protector proclamation unusable, force a visible audience, or preserve a later Myrine petition. A private recovery with the seal and no witnesses is Kallias's strongest outcome.

## The first three days

### Sanctuary and a free Aulonite convocation

If the petition succeeds, Ianthe asks Myrine to host citizens, surviving councilors, royalists, democrats, and military delegates under neutral guard. The first political contest is not coronation. It is whether Aulon can produce a body capable of deliberating without Kallias's soldiers or foreign custody.

The bodyguards' records matter here. They establish that Ianthe directed the journey, accepted or refused protection knowingly, and did not ask Myrine to impose a foreign crown.

### The bronze seal hearing

If the seal is separated from Ianthe, Myrine or another neutral authority may hear competing claims:

- Ianthe claims personal authority as heir and last lawful bearer.
- Dorion claims state property recovered under an emergency warrant.
- Kallias claims the seal must return to the government administering Aulon.
- Road officers may claim temporary lawful custody under receipts or requisition records.

The hearing should not retroactively decide the sanctuary petition. It determines state capacity, not whether Ianthe spoke freely.

### Dorion's choice

Dorion can become one of three kinds of future adversary:

- **Custodial loyalist:** he continues lawful pressure, exchanges prisoners, contests the seal, and refuses to recognize Ianthe's political account.
- **Military intermediary:** he accepts sanctuary as a protected loss and seeks a settlement preventing civil war or foreign intervention.
- **Discredited hunter:** he suppresses Ash Knife evidence, breaks a truce, or attacks Myrine, tying his reputation to Kallias's coercion.

Do not force the third path merely because the bodyguards opposed him. His willingness to defend Ianthe from assassins is part of the adventure's moral geometry.

### The Ash Knife account

Captured agents can expose cells, but their testimony is disputed if obtained by Dorion, royalists, or foreign escorts. The strongest evidence combines physical methods, tokens, separate witnesses, and attacks that endangered both Kallias's people and Ianthe's bodyguards.

If the Ash Knives killed Ianthe, the struggle turns immediately to authorship. Kallias calls the bodyguards negligent or complicit; republicans may celebrate or deny the act; road witnesses decide whether martyrdom strengthens the crown, the Assembly, or neither.

### The road communities' bill

Ianthe's first credible proclamation should name concrete obligations:

- release or hearing for detained pilots, stable hands, couriers, and relatives;
- compensation for burned racks, seized boats, dead horses, and damaged homes;
- repair of the bridge, sluice, channels, beacon, and harbor works where relevant;
- amnesty distinctions between voluntary aid, coercion, ordinary labor, and violence;
- protection for witnesses whose names now appear in public records.

A princess who speaks only of constitutional liberty after villages paid for her route will lose precisely the Aulonite support she sought to preserve.

## Thirty-day political states

### Free petitioner with seal and ship

This is Ianthe's strongest state. She can convene supporters, authenticate treaties, and deny Kallias both custody and the principal instrument of government. Kallias will emphasize elven influence, road violence, and the absence of an Assembly mandate.

### Free petitioner without the seal

Ianthe possesses personal legitimacy and a neutral legal platform but lacks immediate command capacity. This state favors a coalition, public convocation, and negotiated return over unilateral restoration.

### Sanctuary without extraction

Myrine becomes the center of the next adventure. Kallias may blockade trade, Dorion may negotiate, Serathiel may offer a second extraction, and the Ash Knives may seek another attempt in a more public environment.

### Elven ward with an intact claim

Ianthe survives, but every message must answer whether it is hers or Serathiel's. The next objective may be movement from kin-protection to neutral sanctuary rather than return to Aulon.

### Witnessed recovery by Dorion

Ianthe returns to Aulon, but Kallias cannot easily claim voluntary assent if road and Myrine witnesses preserve her refusal. The adventure's sequel becomes an internal custody struggle, public audience, rescue, or Assembly restoration.

### Private recovery with seal and no record

Kallias controls the strongest immediate account. Surviving bodyguards must build a case from absent helpers, damaged infrastructure, and whatever duplicate records escaped. This is defeat without narrative erasure.

### The dead heir

Resolve four questions separately: who killed Ianthe, who is blamed, where the seal lies, and what free statement survives. The Ash Knives may end succession while creating a royal martyr; Kallias may retain government while losing legitimacy; the bodyguards may save the public truth while failing the body.

## Canonical demonstration aftermath

The Narrow Shield playthrough ends as follows:

- Ianthe sails under completed Myrine sanctuary with Melia and Thaleia.
- Damon and Phaedra remain free in Myrine as protected witnesses and recognized bodyguards.
- Myrine holds the bronze seal pending a property hearing.
- Dorion accepts sanctuary while contesting Ianthe's accusations and claims credit for recovering the seal.
- Two reed households receive temporary harbor lodging; three pilots and the bridge witnesses hold independent pieces of the road account.
- Red Bridge and the Heron Sluice remain functional, though the remount reserve is dispersed.
- Naevan possesses a written request for limited aid but no custodial authority.
- The Sea-Lark carries duplicate petition records; Myrine retains the originals and another copy remains with the reed witnesses.

The immediate next adventure is the neutral convocation and seal hearing, not another escape. Ianthe has won the right to act in public without winning the state, the bronze instrument of state, the road communities’ restitution, or the return of every person who guarded her. The chain has risen; the obligations have not.
