# Design Notes: The March on Vossgard

## Design objective

This is an offensive military campaign in the Shadowfell. Its uncertainty comes from command, timing, logistics, allegiance, and force.

The governing question is: **How will the Ash Warrant prosecute the campaign, and what kind of victory will its choices leave behind?**

Every field encounter must therefore provide four things:

1. an immediate military objective that matters without any informational reward;
2. at least two credible methods of attack, negotiation, bypass, or containment;
3. a campaign asset or enemy capability whose status carries forward; and
4. a cost that can be accepted rather than merely avoided through correct deduction.

The opening briefing gives the Warrant enough to choose a plan. Later intelligence changes confidence, timing, authentication, and available means.

## Premise

Count Othmar Voss has ruled the Gloam March for seventy-three years. From Vossgard, he controls the safest Shadowfell crossing within three hundred miles of the material border. His living subjects provide grain, labor, soldiers, and scheduled blood tithes. His dead formations patrol the roads between them. In the last year, he has enlarged the Ashen Gate, reinforced the Iron Causeway, and assembled enough stores to maintain an army on the material side of the crossing.

The Dawn Compact has chosen to attack before that army crosses. Engineers have forced the Ashen Gate open from the material side, and Marshal Aveline Rusk has established a bridgehead around it. The Compact can send troops and supplies through the Gate, but the passage remains narrow, unstable, and vulnerable to counterattack. The invasion cannot become a long occupation unless the army secures roads and local stores inside the Gloam March.

Rusk appoints a small cross-command commission because she cannot personally command every column and ordinary messengers cannot cross contested ground quickly enough. The Ash Warrant authorizes its bearers to redirect companies, requisition limited matériel, receive surrenders, parole prisoners pending hearing, and make bounded battlefield commitments in the Compact's name. Every commitment must name the people covered, the enforcing officer or formation, the resources actually assigned, and the later review that ends or confirms it. The Warrant grants no permanent sovereignty, blanket immunity, or imaginary capacity. Their orders are to break Voss's army, take Vossgard, and prevent his escape.

## Open operational situation

Voss knows the Compact is coming. He intends to trade ground for time, force the invaders to spend strength on prepared positions, threaten the Ashen Gate with mobile troops, and preserve at least one route out of Vossgard. If the Compact advances too slowly, he concentrates his forces. If it advances too quickly, bypassed garrisons strike the supply line. If the Compact alienates the living population, Voss gains guides, informants, and replacement levies. If it protects them credibly, many of those levies become unreliable.

Voss is a tyrant and an enemy commander. The legitimate ambiguity concerns means and aftermath: whether the Compact raids or occupies, whether living collaborators are punished or incorporated, how many soldiers and civilians are spent for speed, and whether the Warrant accepts a narrow decapitation or insists on a durable military settlement.

## War aims

Marshal Rusk gives the Warrant four explicit aims:

1. **Destroy or capture Othmar Voss.** Driving him from one fortress is insufficient if he retains an army and a route back.
2. **Break the field army.** The Compact must not take Vossgard while leaving intact formations behind its supply line.
3. **Preserve the Ashen Gate.** Without the bridgehead, wounded troops, prisoners, and liberated civilians are stranded in the Shadowfell.
4. **Leave a defensible settlement.** The campaign may end in occupation, local self-rule under Compact protection, or an organized withdrawal, but not in an ungoverned vacuum created by a raid.

The Warrant can prioritize these aims differently. A rapid attack may kill Voss while leaving the Gloam March unstable. A methodical encirclement may secure the territory at much greater military cost. A negotiated collapse of the living levies may preserve both strength and legitimacy but gives Voss more time to concentrate his dead troops.

## Player role

The Warrant operates between army scale and encounter scale. Its bearers should not replace the Compact's battalions by personally killing every soldier. Their work decides where those battalions can act and under what conditions.

Typical responsibilities include:

- opening a route before the main column arrives;
- taking or killing an enemy commander during a larger engagement;
- preventing demolition of a bridge, sluice, or depot;
- carrying surrender terms across a contested line;
- choosing which unit receives scarce consecrated weapons or engineers;
- leading a limited strike behind a fixed enemy position;
- holding a decisive point while allied forces complete slower work; and
- committing the Compact to treatment of prisoners and civilians.

The Marshal's Hand can issue orders, but authority is not control. Units may be exhausted, frightened, politically divided, or unable to execute an order without the Warrant first changing the battlefield.

A fresh group does not need the named demonstration's captain, engineer, outrider, and advocate. Across three to five characters, it needs enough authority or reputation to be obeyed, enough mobility to cross contested ground, at least one way to affect fortifications or mass combat, and at least one character willing to own surrender and civilian obligations. Those functions may overlap. The source must not presume the demonstration's exact composition, arguments, route, or settlement.

Detached allied columns operate under bounded orders. Adequate support lets one delay, preserve an objective, or contain a named line; it does not let that command reduce a full encounter, capture its distinctive asset, or resolve its politically contested choice offscreen. Weak support provides reconnaissance and delay. Remote reports should arrive before consequences become irreversible whenever the Warrant can still respond.

## Campaign state

### March turns

The opening council is turn zero. Resolve a major field operation, advance the march turn, announce the threshold reaction, and only then ask the Warrant for its next destination. A full recovery, a failed assault that must be reorganized, or substantial backtracking can advance the clock again. The count and the impending thresholds remain visible.

- **Turn 2:** an unresolved Morcant conducts one announced raid against the supply line, the Gate, or an exposed village. If she is reduced or genuinely contained, she cannot perform that raid.
- **Turn 4:** Voss chooses one surviving outer command for a primary withdrawal, stripping action, or counterstroke. Other positions visibly prepare, but only the chosen encounter executes its primary turn-four action.
- **Turn 6:** Voss issues the general concentration and breakout order. Bypassed mobile forces may answer; reduced forces cannot, and contained forces must first break the named line holding them.

Scouts, refugees, captured traffic, and signal activity announce what is about to move; no threshold silently resolves an encounter. The uncertainty lies in which exposed place Voss chooses and how much force the Warrant has allowed him to preserve.

### Army condition

Track the invading army as **ready**, **strained**, or **spent**.

- Costly frontal assaults, failed operations, lost supply columns, and forced marches can worsen army condition.
- Taking the Iron Causeway intact, organizing support in the Tithe Villages, or opening the Drowned Sluice can improve or stabilize it.
- A spent army can still reach Vossgard, but it cannot simultaneously mount a full assault, secure the rear, and evacuate large numbers of prisoners.

### Population posture

Track the living population as **opposed**, **watchful**, or **cooperative**. This is a consequence of public conduct rather than a persuasion roll made in isolation.

Requisition without payment, collective punishment, abandoned villages, and indiscriminate radiant attacks move the population toward opposition. Credible protection, disciplined troops, enforceable surrender terms, and visible punishment of Compact abuses move it toward cooperation.

Population posture affects guides, food, casualty reports, defections among living levies, and the feasibility of holding the Gloam March after Voss falls.

### Bypassed forces

Each field position may be **reduced**, **contained**, or **bypassed**.

- A reduced position no longer acts as an organized enemy force.
- A contained position remains dangerous but is fixed by assigned Compact troops holding a named road, gate, or interdiction line. The assigned formation is unavailable elsewhere, and containment must be broken before the enemy can move in strength.
- A bypassed position preserves tempo but can raid supply, reinforce Vossgard, or compel the Compact to detach troops later.

Bypass must be a valid strategic choice, not disguised failure. Its cost should appear later in a form the Warrant can still answer.

### Encirclement

Vossgard has three known military exits:

1. the abbey ridge road;
2. the bell road and its field columns; and
3. the Drowned Sluice beneath the fortress.

Controlling the Red Abbey, Black Bell Redoubt, and Drowned Sluice closes those exits respectively. With all three closed, Voss cannot take an organized force, prisoners, or stores out by road. He must fight, surrender, sortie, or abandon the army for a visible personal flight through the consecrated line. With one or two open, he can organize a conventional breakout if the final battle turns against him. Personal flight is a tactical pursuit, not a hidden fourth geographic route, and produces an incomplete victory even when the fortress and army fall.

## Encounter registry

### The Ashen Gate

**Function:** entry encounter, bridgehead, supply source, command hub, and evacuation route.

The Compact holds a crescent of earthworks around a vertical wound of grey light. Everything entering or leaving the campaign passes through one crowded strip of road: troops, wagons, prisoners, casualties, replacement horses, consecrated ammunition, and messages from the material side.

The opening problem is allocation. The Iron Causeway requests engineers and the heavy column. The Tithe Villages request disciplined infantry before irregular troops begin foraging. Scouts at the Thorn Barrows report the night riders assembling. Rusk can reinforce two demands adequately or all three poorly.

The encounter should establish the war aims, Voss's known dispositions, the campaign tracks, and the Warrant's authority. It should also make the first choice consequential without making one route obviously correct.

### The Iron Causeway

**Function:** direct advance, siege-train route, prepared defense, and rear-area threat if bypassed.

A raised stone road crosses a plain of grasping shadow and enters a fortified bridge complex. Voss's living engineers have prepared demolitions beneath the central span. Dead heavy infantry hold the parapets, while a living garrison commands the mechanisms and can still surrender.

The Warrant may lead a direct breach, reach the demolition chambers from below, negotiate with the living commander under fire, or bypass the road and leave a force to contain it. Taking the causeway intact allows the Compact's heaviest engines to reach Vossgard. Destroying it may win the position quickly while committing the final campaign to lighter means.

### The Tithe Villages

**Function:** population policy, local supply, living levies, and the political character of the offensive.

Several close-set villages occupy the only ground in the Gloam March where ordinary crops grow reliably. Voss's officials have ordered grain burned and households moved toward Vossgard. Some reeves want Compact protection. Others expect the invaders to plunder and would rather obey the ruler they know.

The military problem is immediate: prevent removal or destruction of supplies while Voss's levy officers gather conscripts and hostages. The Warrant can distribute troops, arm local resistance, bargain for neutrality, conduct a rapid seizure, or pass through and accept what Voss takes with him.

This encounter should determine population posture more strongly than any single later scene. It must also avoid presenting civilians as a puzzle whose correct answer is hidden. Their fears and interests should be stated directly.

### The Thorn Barrows

**Function:** fast flank, cavalry contest, grave terrain, and control of lateral movement.

A chain of ancient burial mounds forms the fastest route between the Ashen Gate and Vossgard's eastern approaches. Voss's night riders use the barrows as remount stations and rally points. The route is shorter than the causeway but cannot carry the full siege train.

The Warrant may hunt the rider commander, hold a barrow gap while Compact infantry crosses, destroy or capture the shadow mounts, or force the riders toward another column. Success gives the Compact operational mobility and reduces Voss's ability to punish bypasses. Failure leaves the army vulnerable whenever it shifts laterally.

### The Red Abbey

**Function:** blood logistics, prisoners, vampire heavy troops, and the northwestern exit from Vossgard.

The abbey is a fortified depot built around a desecrated cloister. It stores preserved blood, houses prisoners selected for later feeding, and supports Voss's vampire knights. Its ridge road reaches Vossgard without crossing the lower plain.

The Warrant knows what the depot is before arriving. The choices concern how to take it: storm the walls before the knights deploy, cut the ridge and force a sortie, exchange safe conduct for the living garrison, or destroy the stores at the cost of prisoners and useful supplies.

Holding the abbey closes one escape route, denies Voss his best reserve, and may provide consecrated ground for the final assault.

### The Black Bell Redoubt

**Function:** enemy command and control, field reserve, main-road approach, and a second escape route.

A black iron bell hangs in an open tower above a fortified signal camp. Its sound carries farther in the Shadowfell than flags or horns and gives Voss's dead formations simple timed orders. Living couriers and officers interpret the more complicated commands.

The Warrant can silence the bell, seize it and send false timings, destroy the command staff, or force the reserve into battle away from Vossgard. The enemy understands the redoubt's importance and will counterattack rather than abandon it cheaply.

Controlling the redoubt disrupts coordinated reinforcement, opens the central road, and closes Voss's most practical overland breakout route.

### The Drowned Sluice

**Function:** lower supply route, engineering objective, flooded approach, and final escape route.

A military canal once carried stone and grain directly beneath Vossgard. Voss flooded it, chained its gates, and stationed drowned troops in the lock chambers. Compact engineers can reopen it if they reach three mechanisms and keep them intact.

The Warrant may clear the locks in sequence, divert water through occupied low ground, bring boats overland, or destroy the outer gate and accept an uncontrolled flood. Opening the sluice gives the Compact a protected supply route and a lower assault path. Ruining it may still deny Voss an escape but costs the army the same route.

### Vossgard

**Function:** terminal encounter, siege, encirclement test, command decision, and campaign settlement.

Vossgard is a compact fortress rather than a labyrinth. Its important military features are known from border surveys and deserter accounts: the outer wall, the bell gate, the abbey ridge postern, the lower sluice, the garrison court, the prisoner yards, and the inner keep where Voss maintains his coffin vault.

The finale should not depend on finding a secret entrance or learning a special method of killing Voss. The Compact has consecrated weapons, sunlight rites, stakes, fire, and soldiers trained to use them. The challenge is creating the battlefield on which those means can reach him before he escapes or spends the prisoners protecting his withdrawal.

The final operation should have simultaneous demands:

- open or hold an assault route;
- prevent a breakout through any unsecured exit;
- keep enough force on the walls to prevent relief from bypassed garrisons;
- reach Voss and deny him repeated recovery;
- protect or evacuate prisoners; and
- decide who governs the Gloam March after the fortress falls.

## Topology

The clue-derived directed source plan is:

```text
Ashen Gate          -> Iron Causeway, Tithe Villages, Thorn Barrows
Iron Causeway       -> Tithe Villages, Red Abbey, Black Bell Redoubt
Tithe Villages      -> Thorn Barrows, Red Abbey, Drowned Sluice
Thorn Barrows       -> Iron Causeway, Black Bell Redoubt, Drowned Sluice
Red Abbey           -> Iron Causeway, Drowned Sluice, Vossgard
Black Bell Redoubt  -> Tithe Villages, Red Abbey, Vossgard
Drowned Sluice      -> Thorn Barrows, Black Bell Redoubt, Vossgard
```

Every nonterminal encounter points to exactly three distinct destination encounters before non-spatial operational clues are counted. The simple undirected projection contains nineteen edges. Its exact global edge connectivity is three, witnessed at the degree-three Ashen Gate and Vossgard.

The topology creates two triangular belts:

- the Iron Causeway, Tithe Villages, and Thorn Barrows form the first belt;
- the Red Abbey, Black Bell Redoubt, and Drowned Sluice form the second; and
- each second-belt encounter provides an independent approach to Vossgard.

The cross-connections support changes of plan. A Warrant that begins on the causeway can turn toward the villages, abbey, or bell. A Warrant that begins in the villages can shift toward the barrows, abbey, or sluice. A Warrant that begins through the barrows can turn back toward the causeway or advance toward either the bell or sluice.

## Intended campaign rhythms

### Rapid decapitation

The Warrant takes one first-belt route, one second-belt route, and assaults Vossgard in three major operations. Voss is less concentrated, but the supply line and rear are fragile, the army lacks several assets, and at least two escape routes remain open.

### Methodical encirclement

The Warrant reduces or contains most of both belts before the finale. Vossgard is isolated and the political settlement is stronger, but Voss has time to gather defenders, move prisoners, and prepare the fortress.

### Popular collapse

The Warrant prioritizes the Tithe Villages, credible surrender terms, and separation of living levies from vampire cadres. This can break enemy cohesion without destroying every position, but it requires visible restraint and gives Voss time to rely more heavily on dead troops.

### Logistics-first offensive

The Warrant preserves the Iron Causeway and opens the Drowned Sluice. The army arrives at Vossgard in excellent condition with heavy means, but the night riders and bell network may remain capable of striking exposed civilians or the Ashen Gate.

No route should produce every advantage. Each should make a recognizable kind of final battle.

## Pass-two construction decisions

Every position follows a common military template: known situation, named commands, forces, terrain, credible courses of action, strong and costly outcomes, reduction, containment, bypass, carried-forward assets, revisit value, and operational material for the lead graph.

Several campaign-wide decisions are now fixed:

- The Compact's recurring subordinate formations are the Grey Banner under Brigadier Tamsin Holt, the Gatewrights under Colonel Dain Merrow, and the Dawn Lancers under Captain Jessa Vale. Provost Liora Dane handles surrender, requisition, and civil administration.
- The Ashen Gate allocation supports two initial axes adequately or all three weakly. This changes allied exploitation and containment capacity without locking the Warrant into a route.
- Each bypassed field position receives one visible later action derived from its military capability. Bypass is therefore purchased tempo rather than disguised failure or arbitrary punishment.
- Containment closes or fixes a position only by tying down named Compact formations. A contained second-belt encounter can close its corresponding Vossgard exit, but the containing troops cannot join the final assault.
- A complete circuit through all six field positions reaches or exceeds march turn six. Completionism produces superior encirclement and an exhausted, fully anticipated offensive rather than every advantage at no cost.
- Vossgard is now structured as a state-dependent military operation: establish a breach, answer bypassed forces, resolve the living garrison and prisoners, maintain pressure through the inner keep, and issue immediate settlement orders.
- Voss's breakout behavior is explicit. It depends on which of the three known military exits remain open, not on a hidden escape rule.

The authoritative adventure now carries both the operational content and the topology that the original construction passes established.

## Pass-three construction decisions

At the end of pass three, the authoritative source contained eight encounters, twelve required operational conclusions, and thirty-six clues. Every conclusion has exactly three supporting clues from three independent source encounters. Every nonterminal encounter supplies three distinct spatial destinations before its non-spatial campaign leverage is counted.

The clue prose follows five rules:

- Reports establish timing or allocation rather than reveal hidden facts.
- Captured orders remain useful because they authenticate a course of action, not because they expose a surprise.
- Guides and local testimony must be earned through protection, surrender, or battlefield control.
- Engineering clues state what can safely be ordered with preserved infrastructure.
- Political clues name enforceable guarantees, households, units, or administrative capacity.

The exact graph contains nineteen simple edges. Its minimum cut is the three opening edges from the Ashen Gate; Vossgard likewise has degree three through the abbey ridge, bell road, and Underkeep Gate. The first rendered packet is therefore structurally complete before route stress testing begins.

## Pass-four stress-test decisions

The route tests confirm that the existing source supports several recognizable campaigns without amendment. Every tested sequence follows the directed clue graph, every short route reaches a usable fortress approach, and no operational conclusion functions as a concealed key.

The principal pass-four decisions are:

- Initial allocation and Warrant movement remain separate. A supported axis can exploit, delay, or contain, but it does not resolve a position offscreen or prevent the Warrant from changing direction.
- March turns measure major operations and substantial reorganization. Army condition remains a separate track; an early army can be strained, and a late army can remain ready through maneuver, surrender, and supply.
- Containment prevents a named capability only by tying down a named formation. Detached troops do not produce the additional assets of reduction unless those assets are earned in play.
- Cooperative population posture adds food, guides, surrender leverage, stretcher crews, and provisional administration. It never replaces the need to defeat Voss's dead and vampire formations.
- One open exit is sufficient to define a strong final operation. The selected playthrough closes the Bell road and Abbey ridge while leaving the Drowned Sluice open, giving Voss one visible focused breakout.
- A first visit to Vossgard is permitted when it has bounded military objectives. The selected playthrough seizes and holds the outer Bell works, delivers surrender terms, and fixes the fortress before the Warrant turns to the Red Abbey.
- Costly strategic failure remains playable. A damaged campaign can still choose decapitation, denial, prisoner rescue, evacuation, or a limited settlement without a hidden escape or automatic collapse.

The selected demonstration follows Captain Lucan Vey, Gatewright Tala Marrick, outrider Sorin Halvek, and Advocate Maelin Rook as the Ash Warrant. Their fixed route is:

```text
Ashen Gate -> Tithe Villages -> Thorn Barrows -> Black Bell Redoubt
           -> Vossgard -> Red Abbey -> Vossgard
```

The final return occurs on march turn six. The Iron Causeway remains contained, the Drowned Sluice remains bypassed, the population is cooperative, the army is strained, and Voss is fully concentrated around a canal breakout. The append-only journal records the first fortress investment, a corrected field report, remote consequences, and explicit settlement orders.

## Pass-five playthrough decisions

The checked-in playthrough contains 78 raw events across 38 operations and seven visits. Twenty-six clues are spotted, all twelve operational conclusions are established, all seven non-entry encounters become available, and one granary report is corrected through an append-only void operation.

The campaign route is the one selected during pass four:

```text
Ashen Gate -> Tithe Villages -> Thorn Barrows -> Black Bell Redoubt
           -> Vossgard -> Red Abbey -> Vossgard
```

The journal preserves the intended campaign distinctions:

- The Iron Causeway is supported, then contained after Morcant's remote raid. Its battalion and heavy train remain unavailable at the final battle.
- The Tithe Villages, Thorn Barrows, Black Bell Redoubt, and Red Abbey are reduced through different military and political means.
- The Drowned Sluice is knowingly bypassed, announces its breakout preparations, and imposes its cost during the final operation without being retroactively treated as reduced.
- The first Vossgard visit takes and holds the outer Bell works, protects a surrendering levy company, and fixes the fortress. It does not enter the inner keep.
- Drenn's final stand-down is earned through the village terms, Ren's surrender, Abbey prisoners, and direct fortress rolls. It creates the third simultaneous task the strained Compact army could not otherwise support.
- Voss uses the single known open exit. The canal breakout is visible before the final return and is stopped through mobility, command assets, denied blood, and battlefield pressure rather than a hidden vampire weakness.
- Settlement orders are recorded as active campaign consequences: fortress command, prisoner protection, local council formation, Bell control, Causeway containment, canal watch, and Gate evacuation capacity.

The playthrough produces a military victory with the foundations of a durable settlement, not a perfect circuit. The Causeway remains contained, the Sluice remains uncontrolled, Morcant survives without a coherent formation, and the Compact must honor the administrative and judicial obligations it accepted while advancing.
