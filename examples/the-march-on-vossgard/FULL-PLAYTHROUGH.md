# The Ash Warrant: A Full Playthrough

This is one demonstrated campaign, subordinate to `adventure.json`. Its four characters, opening allocation, route, clue discoveries, containment decisions, surrender terms, and settlement are not requirements for a fresh playthrough.

The Ash Warrant crosses the Gloam March by the villages, the barrows, and the Black Bell. It reaches Vossgard, takes the outer works, and turns away before the fortress can consume the army. Only after the Red Abbey falls does the Warrant return for Voss.

```text
Gate -> Villages -> Barrows -> Bell -> Vossgard -> Abbey -> Vossgard
```

Every enemy position and escape road in this account is known before it matters. The uncertainty lies in command: which reports deserve troops, which objectives another officer can hold, how much an incomplete victory is worth, and what the Compact will still possess when Voss is dead.

The first Vossgard operation is a limited investment. The Warrant holds the Bell works, protects a living-company surrender, and fixes the garrison. It then leaves the apparent final target in order to break the blood reserve and close the ridge road. When it returns, the army is strained and the canal is open. Voss's last course of action is visible from the walls.

Twenty-five authored clues remain unseen. The Warrant never enters the Iron Causeway or Drowned Sluice, and the fifteen clues added during the second-look density pass remain fresh-play alternatives rather than retroactive discoveries.

## Session One: What the Warrant Can Promise

### The Ashen Gate

The first carrion bolt crosses the Gate while Marshal Aveline Rusk is naming the three roads.

It falls short of the command mound and opens in the field hospital. Bone splinters scatter through the canvas wards. Ash-dead skirmishers rise from a drainage cut west of the road, not in enough strength to threaten the bridgehead, but close enough to pull the reserve away from the council. A second bolt lands against an iron pylon. The Gate narrows to a blade of grey daylight and then widens again under the artificers' chant.

Rusk does not adjourn.

A map of the Gloam March lies under lead weights on the command table. The Iron Causeway is marked in black, the Tithe Villages in ochre, and the Thorn Barrows in three hooked lines. South of them stand the Red Abbey, the Black Bell Redoubt, and the Drowned Sluice. Vossgard is not concealed behind an unknown symbol. It is drawn at the center of the lower sheet, with the ridge road, bell road, and canal all entering its walls.

Colonel Dain Merrow lays out his causeway table. He needs one Gatewright team under the span and one formation on the parapets. If either moves alone, Standard-Bearer Krail can destroy the bridge. If both move before the defenders complete their next demolition drill, Captain Arven Taal's living engineers can be separated from the grave cohort and the span may survive.

Provost Liora Dane sets three village scout reports beside it. Voss's carts are gathering at Garren, Orra, and Lume, but the columns cannot join until they reach the central crossroads. The removal window is measured in hours. After it closes, the hostages and grain will move toward the Abbey and canal roads under one escort.

Captain Jessa Vale points to the central thorn gap. Morcant can watch the western and eastern gaps from paired remount stations, but she cannot observe that crossing from both at once. A supported screen there will force the night riders to show which barrow they mean to defend.

Tala reads the heavy column manifest rather than the enemy map. The consecrated ram, sunlight mirrors, engineer stores, and bulk ammunition cannot move over the barrow lanes. They require the Causeway intact or the canal opened. She marks the loads that will remain useless at the Gate if Merrow is given enough troops to start but not enough to finish.

Maelin reads Rusk and Dane's occupation-capacity board. It is a count, not a promise of conquest: replacement companies, interpreters, hospital beds, field police, evacuation wagons, and civil officers. The Compact can occupy the March only if the Gate remains stable, a reserve survives, and the army does not commit every administrator as an infantry replacement.

Sorin listens to all of it and says what no one has written on the map.

“If we do not support Vale, Morcant chooses where we pay for it.”

Tala answers without looking up from the manifest. “If we do not support Merrow, we choose to fight a fortress without the train.”

Maelin taps the three village reports. “If we do not move here, Voss chooses which people become part of the fortress.”

Lucan asks Rusk what force can remain uncommitted after two adequate axes. Rusk can leave one mobile reserve at the Gate—smaller than Lucan wants and larger than the campaign will have later. He does not ask for a fourth option.

The Iron Causeway receives Merrow, a Gatewright assault group, and Grey Banner infantry. Their order is to fix the garrison, prevent demolition if feasible, and keep the train east until the galleries are controlled. The Tithe Villages receive Dane, interpreters, wagons, and disciplined infantry. The Ash Warrant goes with them. Vale receives scouts and a reduced cavalry screen in the Barrows, with authority to delay and report but not to accept a decisive battle.

Sorin repeats the last order to make certain it has been heard. Vale is not being told to win. She is being told to keep the road from becoming completely blind.

Maelin writes the campaign policy while the bridgehead troops repel the probe. Requisition will be recorded. Collective punishment is forbidden. Living troops who surrender will be protected from dead cadres and held for later hearing. A guarantee concerning named hostages must include a named unit responsible for rescue, protection, or evacuation. Lucan assigns Dane's field police to enforce the terms, not merely publish them.

The probe dies without drawing the mobile reserve out of position. The Ashen Gate remains stable. The army begins ready. Two axes move. The unsupported third is entered on the campaign board as a risk, not hidden in the margin.

Before leaving, Lucan gives Merrow the Ash Warrant's authority to accept Taal's surrender if it can be made real. Tala objects to the wording “if feasible” in the demolition order. Merrow answers that he knows what an intact bridge is worth and also knows what happens when engineers are ordered to die for a noun.

The causeway operation proceeds while the Ash Warrant marches to the villages. Its supported column may delay demolition or establish a real containment line; it cannot reduce the bridge, win Taal's contested surrender, or capture the heavy route offscreen.

The opening council was turn zero. The village operation will consume turn one. Any threshold reaction will be announced before the next destination is chosen.

### The Tithe Villages

The villages are visible for an hour before the column reaches them. Garren's granaries stand on a low rise. Orra lies behind drainage dikes and a line of black poplars. Lume is marked by smoke.

Blood Provost Tal Vosca has not tried to defend all three. His officials have formed one hostage convoy between Garren and the crossroads, sent grain carts from Orra toward the Abbey road, and left a rear guard at Lume with oil and torches. Two companies of conscripts march among the wagons. Household guards ride the flanks. Ash hounds run beneath the carts, chained to iron rings in the axles.

Dane asks whether the Warrant means to distribute the infantry across all three villages. Lucan refuses. The crossroads is the point from which Vosca can turn separate emergencies into one protected withdrawal. Dane takes and fortifies it. Named companies go to Garren and Orra with orders to protect the granaries, prevent looting, and avoid pursuit. Lucan takes the Warrant against the hostage convoy.

Maelin rides ahead under a white field cloth and reads the protection terms within hearing of the conscripts. She does not tell them they are innocent. She tells them what surrender does: weapons grounded, dead officers separated, wounded treated, accused crimes recorded, families named, and no summary reprisal. Senior Reeve Maela Orr and witnesses from all three villages have already marked the terms. Dane's seal makes them Compact orders rather than battlefield theater.

Vosca sends the ash hounds before the reading is finished.

Sorin cuts the lead chains and drives the hounds into a drainage ditch where they cannot reach the hostage carts. Lucan directs the Grey Banner company against the household guards rather than the conscript block. Tala climbs beneath the first wagon while it is moving and shears the release pins Vosca's men intend to use to overturn it across the road. Maelin remains in the open long enough for one conscript file to lay down its spears.

The convoy stops because its living mass ceases to move as one formation. Some conscripts surrender. Some scatter into the fields. Vosca tries to withdraw with the household guards and a smaller cart carrying selected hostages. Sorin follows him instead of chasing the village officials riding in three other directions.

They catch him at a stone dike south of the crossroads. Vosca puts the hostages between his guard and the Lancers. Lucan refuses a charge. Tala finds where the dike carries an old field culvert and leads the Warrant under the road. Maelin keeps Vosca talking about terms he does not intend to accept. Sorin comes up behind the guard, kills the ash-hound master, and opens a path for the hostages to drop behind the wall.

Vosca dies trying to reach the Abbey road. Three of his clerks surrender with the active blood-tithe register.

The Warrant returns to the crossroads with the hostages and finds the political fight already beginning. Deren Salk's volunteers have seized two homes belonging to levy officials. Hessa Dorn refuses to sign the protection agreement while armed outsiders hold Orra's grain. Maela Orr asks how long the Compact intends to remain after it has eaten what it came to protect.

Maelin does not answer with intentions. She assigns Dane's field police to remove Salk's men from the houses, records the requisition already taken, and names the Grey Banner company that will remain at the crossroads after the Warrant leaves. Lucan gives Orr authority to convene all three reeves and requires Compact officers to attend. Tala orders grain moved away from the outer walls but refuses to send it toward the Ashen Gate; the army will draw from it only under local record.

The shepherds and field hands offer the remount lanes because their households have been protected and because Morcant's riders have been taking fodder from them for years. Teamsters identify the lower tithe road to the Red Abbey, its gate routine, and the time required to move prisoners uphill. Canal workers produce Sila Marr and the labor rolls for the dry approach to the Outer Chain. None of this changes who Voss is. It changes what the Compact can execute.

The blood-tithe register shows collection days, wagon capacities, and the officers who certify delivery. The Abbey is not merely a building with blood in it. It is the center of a known transport system that can be broken by denying carts, crews, and prisoners.

The reeves' witnessed terms are copied for every column. Living troops elsewhere will have a specific precedent to accept or reject.

Near dusk, a runner reports that all three principal granaries are intact. Lucan has the success entered. A second runner arrives before the column departs. Smoke hid the Lume east granary from the first patrol; Vosca's rear guard burned it before withdrawing.

The original report remains in the journal and is voided by a correction. The replacement consequence is narrower and true: the Garren and Orra granaries survive, Lume's east granary burns, most campaign grain is preserved, the hostages are recovered, and the population becomes cooperative.

The correction matters beyond bookkeeping. Tala had already begun assigning surplus grain that did not exist. Dane changes the ration estimate before the next column draws from it. Maelin reads the corrected count to the reeves rather than letting the first report become the official version because it was more convenient.

The session ends with the first public surrender accepted under the Ash Warrant and with the first promise already costing troops, records, and time.

## Session Two: The Flank Chooses Its Moment

### The raid on the Causeway effort

Merrow's message reaches the crossroads before dawn.

Morcant's riders came out of the western barrow lanes while the Gatewrights were moving under the Iron Causeway. They did not attack the bridge. They struck the supply effort behind it, killed draft animals, burned gallery timber, and forced Merrow to choose between finishing the infiltration and preventing Krail from detonating the span during the confusion.

He saved the demolition fight and abandoned the immediate breach.

The causeway is contained, not reduced. One Grey Banner battalion holds the east gate and fixes Taal and Krail inside. The heavy train remains east of the span. The lost wagons and exhausted escorts move the army from ready to strained.

Sorin does not say that he was right. He asks how many hours Morcant's horses have been moving.

The village guides answer. The riders used the remount lanes after the raid. They must change mounts or lose the speed that makes the next attack possible. Vale's screen has watched the central gap, and one of the western stations is receiving more fodder than the others.

Lucan redirects the Warrant east. The campaign does not stop to avenge the raid. Its objective is to remove the mobile formation's ability to choose the next battlefield.

### The Thorn Barrows

The old graves do not resemble a cemetery. They rise as low hills under thorn canopies, joined by lanes worn deeper than the surrounding plain. Riders appear and disappear in them without crossing open ground. Some mounds carry intact seals. Others have been opened and rebuilt as stables for shadow-fed coursers.

Vale holds the central gap with fewer Lancers than Morcant expects. She has spent the night yielding ground slowly enough to appear afraid and quickly enough not to be caught. Morcant watches her from the crown of the middle remount barrow.

The shepherds lead the Warrant along a fodder lane behind the western station. Tala marks the old grave seals before anyone begins breaking stable walls. She will collapse the newer galleries but not the mound itself. Lucan assigns one infantry company to secure handlers and captured mounts. Maelin distributes the village terms to the surrender teams. Sorin goes forward with Vale's best outriders.

Their first strike destroys the fodder stores and opens the pens. Shadow coursers pour into the covered lanes. Morcant answers immediately. She abandons the central gap, not to save the barrow, but to hit the infantry between the western and southern stations while they are managing the loose mounts.

Sorin has expected the turn. He and Vale meet it in the narrow lane where Morcant cannot deploy the full rider line. The fight is short at first and then breaks into moving knots among the thorns. Wight outriders keep riding after their mounts fall. One opened stable wall gives way against an ancient seal, and dead hands begin pressing through the loosened stones.

Tala has to choose between finishing the remount destruction and shoring the old grave. She orders the captured handlers to lead the remaining coursers away, puts the engineer team on the seal, and uses charges only in the modern galleries. The decision costs time and lets part of Morcant's force reach the southern station.

Maelin accepts surrender from dismounted living riders while the wights are still killing them for it. She and Lucan hold a protected pocket around the captured handlers. The surrender is not an intermission; it changes the battle by denying Morcant replacement mounts and guides.

Sorin reaches the southern station as its doors open. He sees Morcant enter the thorns on foot with fewer than a dozen riders and two wights. For a few minutes he believes Vale has closed the far gap. He reports the rider command trapped.

Then a village guide finds cut thorns and dismounted tracks beyond the cairn. Morcant has escaped with a small cadre. Sorin corrects himself before Lucan can build the next order on the wrong conclusion. The journal does not require a second formal void because the overstatement never became a separate durable consequence; it survives in the visit account as the sort of battlefield error the campaign must absorb without turning it into a hidden twist.

Lucan ends the pursuit. Sorin objects until Lucan restates the objective: destroy the remount system, free the Lancers, and preserve the lateral lanes. Morcant's death would be useful. Her continued ability to command ninety mobile riders would be decisive. The first has escaped; the second has not.

Two remount stations are destroyed. The third is captured. The ancient graves remain mostly sealed. Vale's Lancers regain freedom to move between the Gate, villages, and second belt. Morcant survives with scattered dismounted riders who can harass a patrol but cannot choose the campaign's next major action.

Her command kit yields the lateral exit map. It shows the ridge road, bell road, and canal beyond Vossgard's approaches. The map confirms the known strategic problem in operational detail: all three exits can carry a breakout, and the barrow lanes can shift cavalry between them. It does not close any exit by itself.

The captured dispatch route to the Black Bell is more immediately useful. Morcant's riders normally warn Legate Ossa Ren through a narrow southern gap. With the remounts broken and the route in Compact hands, the Warrant can reach the Redoubt before Ren knows the eastern screen has failed.

### The road to the Black Bell

Lucan wants a tower raid and immediate movement toward Vossgard. Tala wants the Bell intact. Sorin wants Ren's field reserve committed where Vale can break it. Maelin wants the living courier staff separated from Bell-Warden Vark and the dead signal cadre.

They combine all four aims into a plan that is more dangerous than any one of them.

Captured rider tack and Morcant's dispatch signs carry a false warning down the southern route: Compact infantry are moving toward the Drowned Sluice, and the Barrows remain contested. Ren sends part of her reserve south to reinforce a threat that does not exist. Vale moves behind them.

The Warrant reaches the Redoubt at the end of the session. The Bell is already sounding recall sequences across the March. Vark has begun closing the tower shutters. Ren's remaining cohorts form outside the command shelter, uncertain whether the rider signal or the tower schedule is current.

Sorin asks Lucan whether they are raiding or taking the position.

Lucan looks at the intact Bell, the living couriers running between dead cohorts, and Vale's dust rising behind the detached reserve.

“Taking it,” he says.

## Session Three: The Fortress Is Not the Campaign

### The Black Bell Redoubt

Tala's assault team climbs the tower while Lucan attacks the command shelter and Maelin opens a surrender lane for the couriers. Sorin leaves the wall fight to meet Vale against the detached reserve.

Bell-Warden Vark is bound into the mechanism by iron tendons and black wire. He continues sounding programmed orders after the tower doors are breached. Each sequence moves grave cohorts on the field below. Tala cannot simply destroy the clapper without bringing the upper chamber down on the living signal staff trapped beneath it.

She seizes the brake wheel, locks the next stroke halfway, and sends engineers into the frame. Vark attacks through the mechanism. He has no plan beyond completing the schedule. The work is close and physical: hold the brake, cut the dead bindings, and preserve enough of the signal system to use afterward.

Maelin reaches the courier room and reads the village terms through a barred door. The staff know what happened to Vosca's surrendered conscripts because copies of the agreement arrived before the Compact did. Some open the door. Others flee toward Ren. The dead signal guards begin killing those who stop running.

Lucan changes the assault objective from capturing Ren's table to holding the corridor between the couriers and the grave cohort. The command packet can burn; the surrender channel cannot be recreated after the people using it are dead.

Outside, Ren realizes that the rider warning was false when Vale strikes the reserve returning from the south. She orders a withdrawal toward the Bell road. Sorin uses the captured remount lanes to place Lancers behind her. The field reserve is not annihilated. It is divided into dead cohorts that continue fighting, living companies that can no longer retreat in order, and officers who must decide whether Voss's fortress can still receive them.

Ren returns to the Redoubt to find Vark destroyed, the Bell under Tala's control, and Maelin holding the surrender corridor with living couriers behind her. She refuses the first demand because it does not name the treatment of her subordinate officers. Maelin amends the custody schedule in front of witnesses. Ren surrenders the active command packet and orders the living reserve companies to ground arms. The dead cohorts do not obey and are destroyed around the tower.

The victory strains the Compact. Vale loses riders breaking the reserve. Tala's engineers are too exhausted to turn the Bell into a perfect instrument. The captured network can send limited authenticated sequences and preserve courier routes, but every use risks confusion with Voss's remaining orders.

The packets make the next choices explicit.

Rane's withdrawal timing shows when the Red Abbey knights will be recalled and how the ridge road will be cleared. The bell-road gate order maps Vossgard's central watches, fallback lines, and relief assignments. Ren's breakout contingency names the Bell road as the only overland route wide enough for a large organized withdrawal. The March command network connects settlements, ration stations, and courier posts that will matter after the battle as much as before it.

The Black Bell is reduced. Its reserve is broken, its dead relay destroyed, and its road closed to an organized breakout. Lucan could now turn the Warrant to the Abbey before Voss finishes concentrating.

Instead, Lucan chooses to press Vossgard once.

The Bell road is open to the Compact now. Every hour gives Voss more time to pull in surviving forces. Lucan believes a bounded attack can seize the outer works, fix the garrison, and test whether Drenn will act on the surrender terms. Tala warns that the heavy train remains behind the contained Causeway. Sorin notes that the ridge and canal exits remain open. Maelin says that Drenn may listen, but Voss can still promise relief, blood, and escape.

Lucan agrees with all three. The first Vossgard operation will not attempt the inner keep.

### First visit to Vossgard

Vossgard rises from the shadow plain like a cliff cut into battlements. The Bell road enters between low outer works before climbing toward the garrison court. The ridge road disappears behind the western wall. Canal vents and lower terraces mark the eastern descent.

Holt's battalions advance under false Bell sequences that delay one dead relief cohort and draw another to the wrong gate. The Warrant takes the outer signal works before Voss can fully reset the command network.

Voss answers with the reserve named at the Ashen Gate: dead infantry from the central barracks, vampire retainers from the inner wall, and living levies under Castellan Lysa Drenn. The living companies are placed where the Compact must either fight through them or stop its advance.

Maelin reads the village protection terms from the captured signal platform. Ren stands beside her under guard and confirms that surrendered officers have not been executed. Drenn does not stand down. She orders one levy company to hold the garrison stair and pulls another away from the vampire retainers before the counterattack closes.

That hesitation creates a narrow surrender at the outer wall. A living company grounds arms while dead infantry advance through it. Lucan commits the Warrant to protecting the surrendering soldiers and the prisoners being moved behind them. The inner gate remains open for several minutes. He does not exploit it.

Tala sees the opportunity and the cost at the same time. Without the siege train, the Compact can force an entry but cannot support a controlled breach or suppress the wall behind it. The ridge road is still bringing Abbey blood and knights toward the fortress. The canal remains open beneath the eastern terraces. If Lucan drives the Warrant into the inner keep now, Voss can leave by two coherent routes while Holt spends the strained army holding a salient.

Sorin wants the canal first. Maelin wants the Abbey. Lucan asks which operation changes both Voss's ability to fight and Drenn's reason to surrender. The Abbey does both: blood, prisoners, vampire knights, and the ridge exit.

The Warrant does not withdraw from Vossgard. Holt fortifies the outer Bell works and keeps the garrison fixed. The captured Bell road becomes a real investment line. Surrender terms remain posted. Accessible prisoners and the grounded levy company move under Compact protection. Voss must commit troops to prevent the salient from expanding.

Then the Warrant leaves the fortress and turns west.

Scouts report activity at the Drowned Sluice as they depart. Lock-Marshal Ors Keln is moving selected prisoners and barges toward the Underkeep Gate. The canal is the one military exit no formation has been assigned to reduce or contain. No one needs to interpret the report. It is the visible price of the route the Warrant has chosen.

The session ends with Vossgard under limited investment, the army strained, the heavy train absent, and the Warrant marching away from the apparent final target because it has defined what the first attack was supposed to accomplish.

## Session Four: Blood, Prisoners, and the Ridge

### The approach to the Red Abbey

The Abbey withdrawal sequence is due before the Warrant can reach the ridge by ordinary march. Tala uses the captured Bell to send it early and in the wrong order. Living quartermaster crews begin loading carts. Dame Halwen Rane receives a recall that tells her to clear the ridge while the fortress packet still orders her to hold it.

Village teamsters lead the Compact up the lower tithe road. Ossa Ren's surrendered couriers authenticate enough of the sequence to delay immediate suspicion. Maelin sends Voln terms before the first attack: formal custody, protection of surrendered living guards, preservation of records, and later hearing. No immunity is promised.

Quartermaster Nera Voln wants to accept. Her authority depends on Voss, but she knows the Abbey cannot survive between the Warrant and Holt's investment. Rane refuses. She takes the vampire knights out onto the ridge to destroy the Warrant before the living garrison can surrender behind her.

Lucan has only a strained army's support. He cannot simultaneously storm the vault, clear the prison yards, and hold the ridge without delegating objectives to people who may fail.

Sorin and Vale take the ridge fight. Tala and a Gatewright detachment go for the blood vault and the postern controls. Maelin, Chaplain Iven Sol, and Dane's field police move to the prison yards. Lucan holds the cloister center with enough infantry to keep these separate efforts from becoming three isolated defeats.

Rane's sortie nearly succeeds because Sorin tries to close the road behind her before Vale has fully fixed the knights. A mounted retainer breaks through toward the cloister. Lucan commits the last local reserve to stop it, leaving Tala without the infantry screen she expected at the vault.

Tala changes the engineering objective. She cannot seize and inventory the depot under those conditions. She can deny it. The vault distribution board identifies which cisterns are assigned to Rane's knights, the Black Bell lieutenants, and Vossgard's inner keep. Tala opens the handling lines into a consecrated waste trench and destroys the lifting machinery. The blood is not recovered for medicine or evidence. It is denied to Voss.

In the prison yards, Voln orders her guards not to resist Maelin's entry. Dead warders continue fighting. Maelin and Sol clear them while the prisoners break their own restraint lines. The rescued prisoners name fortress levy companies, household placements, and officers who resisted feeding and reprisal orders. The testimony makes Drenn's possible stand-down executable at the unit level.

Voln surrenders the outer Abbey and her staff. Maelin places her under formal custody. Several Compact soldiers demand immediate execution when the blood rooms are opened. Maelin refuses. The Warrant has not promised to spare Voln judgment. It has promised that surrender will remain meaningful after it saves prisoners and ends the living defense.

On the ridge, Rane loses the mobility she expected when village guides lead Vale through an old tithe cut behind the mounted line. Sorin kills Rane at the western turn. Most of the vampire knights are destroyed before they can reach Vossgard. A few mounted retainers escape on foot, but the command is broken and the ridge road closes.

The Abbey repair-convoy seals and access instructions could still place a Compact team inside the Causeway galleries. The canal depot records could support a later deliberate Sluice operation. Neither becomes the next objective. The march clock has reached six. Voss is fully concentrated.

The ridge postern plan gives the Warrant a second approach to the fortress. The blood reserve is gone. The prisoners are protected. Voln's surrender and the rescued testimony strengthen Drenn's ability to act. The Abbey becomes a consecrated staging ground rather than a depot feeding Voss.

A scout from Holt arrives before the Warrant rests.

Keln has opened the Underkeep Gate. The escape barges are crewed. Drowned infantry are probing the lower screen. Voss is moving selected prisoners and his surviving vampire cadre toward the dry basin.

Everyone knows what he intends to do.

## Session Five: The open exit

### Return to Vossgard

The Bell road and ridge road are closed. The canal is open.

Lucan has a strained army, which can support two major tasks. He assigns them before the assault begins.

Holt will hold and expand the Bell works, maintaining pressure toward the inner keep. Vale will take the freed Dawn Lancers and captured shadow mounts across the barrow lanes to the lower terraces, screening the canal breakout. There is no third army task for the garrison court and prisoner yards. The Warrant must create one through surrender or accept that the living levies remain part of the battle.

Tala opens the ridge postern with the captured schedule. The Warrant enters Vossgard from two controlled directions while the Bell sounds the authenticated stand-down terms. Voss responds with a visible feint: dead infantry and bloodless vampire survivors concentrate in the sunless hall as if he intends to fight through the inner keep.

Sorin does not believe the keep is irrelevant. He believes the loaded coffin wagons moving east are more urgent. He leaves scouts on the inner route and goes toward the lower ramps.

Maelin and Ossa Ren enter the garrison court under a truce cloth. Drenn has the fortress levy rolls open on the command table. They place living companies beside the households Voss holds, the officers willing to stand down, and the prisoner yards each company can protect. Maelin does not ask Drenn to trust the Compact in the abstract. She names the villages already protected, the surrendered reserve officers still alive, the Abbey prisoners recovered, and the hearing promised to Voln.

Voss sends an order requiring Drenn to close the court and move the prisoners toward the canal as cover. Drenn reads it, looks at the rolls, and tells her companies to ground arms. She orders them to protect the prisoner yards against the dead warders instead.

The stand-down supplies the third task the strained army could not provide. Living garrison troops secure the court, open the inner sally passages to Compact medics, and prevent Voss from taking the full prisoner column into the breakout. They do not become trusted allies in one moment. They become a formation with a lawful order they can execute.

Lucan shifts the Warrant toward the lower terraces.

### The canal breakout

Keln has lowered the canal and opened the dry basin. Three barges wait below the fortress. The first carries Voss, his surviving vampire cadre, and a small group of selected prisoners. The second carries dead infantry and stores. The third is intended to receive retreating troops from the keep. Drowned soldiers hold the terraces and chain mechanisms.

Voss's inner-hall force attacks as the barges begin to move. Holt can either keep pressure on the keep or reinforce Vale at the canal. Lucan keeps him on the first task. If the Bell line collapses, the dead inside the fortress can reopen the central route. Vale must hold the lower screen with the assets the campaign preserved.

The Barrows matter. The Dawn Lancers reach the lower terraces before the lead barge clears the basin because the lateral lanes remain open and the rider formation was reduced. The Villages matter. Sila Marr's labor diagrams show Tala how the launch winches are sequenced, even though the Compact never took the Sluice. The Bell matters. Captured signals delay the third barge's crew and keep one dead cohort marching toward the wrong fallback. The Abbey matters. Voss and his vampires enter the battle without the blood allotment that would let them recover between positions.

None of those assets closes the canal for free.

Tala cannot control the Outer Chain or Sunken Turn from the fortress. She can break the dry-basin launch sequence. Under fire, she places charges on the second winch and severs the mooring system between the first and second barges. The lead barge moves. The second slews across the basin and blocks the third. Drowned infantry climb onto the quay. Vale's screen takes heavy losses holding them away from the launch path.

Sorin reaches the lead barge on a captured shadow mount. The old wound in his hands turns numb as the coursers mass around the basin. He dismounts before the distance distortion becomes worse and crosses the last gap on foot.

Voss fights as the commander described at the Ashen Gate: armored, fast, able to recover from wounds if given blood and time, and determined to preserve a coherent force rather than die for the fortress. He uses the prisoners to delay consecrated fire and orders Keln to flood the lower steps behind him.

Maelin arrives with Drenn's surrendered companies carrying shields, not weapons. They take custody of the prisoners on the barge and open a path for the Warrant. The act is the final proof that the surrender system was military capacity, not moral decoration.

Lucan keeps the Warrant's objective narrow. Stop the lead barge. Keep Voss from reaching the outer channel. Do not pursue Keln into uncontrolled locks while the fortress remains contested.

Tala anchors the barge against the quay with a broken winch chain. Sorin prevents Voss from crossing to a smaller escape craft. Maelin holds the dawn rite over the deck while surrendered levies move prisoners behind the shield line. Lucan coordinates Vale's remaining Lancers and the Warrant's assault so that Voss cannot disengage long enough to recover.

Denied the Abbey blood reserve, Voss carries every wound from the sunless hall feint, the lower terrace, and the barge deck. He is destroyed before the lead craft reaches the outer channel.

Keln and much of the drowned force are broken in the fighting around the terraces. The Compact does not claim the Drowned Sluice. The Outer Chain and Sunken Turn remain uncontrolled, and drowned remnants withdraw into them. The canal has exacted the cost of being bypassed: the breakout reached the fortress battle, Vale's screen suffered heavily, and the final operation had to be divided around an exit the Warrant knowingly left open.

### The first night

Lucan does not wait for a ceremonial victory council.

Holt takes command of Vossgard overnight under the Ash Warrant. Drenn remains under parole as liaison to the grounded living garrison; her officers retain responsibility for the prisoner yards under Compact supervision. Ossa Ren and the Bell couriers transmit the stand-down and casualty routes. The Bell network passes to joint Compact and local control rather than becoming a trophy dismantled for parts.

Senior Reeve Maela Orr is instructed to convene a provisional Gloam March council at Garren with delegates from all three villages. The surviving granaries will supply the fortress and field hospitals under the recorded requisition system already in use. Families will receive lists of surrendered, wounded, and dead levy soldiers.

Voln remains in custody for a formal hearing. Her surrender is honored; her collaboration is not erased. Drenn's officers receive the same distinction.

Merrow maintains containment at the Iron Causeway. The heavy train never reaches Vossgard. The battalion tied there is unavailable for the final battle and for the first night of occupation. Terms are carried to Taal, but Lucan does not write the bridge as taken before it is.

At the Drowned Sluice, the Compact blocks immediate traffic and posts a watch. Tala writes that the canal is neither controlled nor safe. A later operation must clear, close, or rebuild it. The report prevents the battlefield destruction of Keln's breakout force from being mistaken for possession of the locks.

The Ashen Gate remains stable. It receives the wounded, protected prisoners, and civilians who choose evacuation without interrupting supply. Rusk can rotate part of the army home while leaving Holt, Dane, and a bounded force in the March.

The result is a military victory with the foundations of a durable one. Voss is destroyed. His organized field army is broken. Two exits are closed, and the third has spent its breakout force. The population is cooperative, the command network survives, the blood system is dismantled, and local government has somewhere to begin.

The victory is incomplete in visible ways. The Causeway remains contained. The Sluice remains uncontrolled. Morcant survives without a coherent rider command. The army is strained. The Compact has accepted hearings, repairs, patrols, and protection duties that cannot be discharged by leaving the next morning.

The victory rests on a battlefield where one open exit can be answered, a garrison can surrender, and a settlement can survive the night. Another route would have created a different battle and owed different debts.
