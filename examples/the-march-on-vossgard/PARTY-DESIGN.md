# The Ash Warrant

Marshal Aveline Rusk needs four people who can cross the gaps between army commands. They can move a company, condemn a bridge, receive a surrender, hunt a vampire officer, and make a promise the Compact must later honor. They cannot replace a battalion or be present at every crisis.

The Warrant's disagreements concern military means and obligations:

- whether to preserve a route or spend it for tempo;
- whether a mobile enemy is more dangerous than a prepared position;
- how much authority can be promised to civilians before the Compact knows it can remain;
- whether a living collaborator should be used, tried, paroled, or treated as another enemy commander; and
- when an incomplete encirclement is good enough to begin the final assault.

They agree that Voss must be removed. Their quarrels begin with what the Compact may spend, promise, and leave behind to do it.

## Fresh-play use

The four named members below form one demonstration, not the adventure's required cast. A fresh group may be three to five characters of any class, origin, or internal alignment. Across the group, establish four campaign functions: recognized authority or reputation, mobility across contested ground, a way to affect fortifications or massed troops, and someone prepared to make and police surrender or civilian commitments. One character may cover several functions, and none must resemble the named demonstration.

Rusk selects these four because they can survive beyond reliable command range and because their orders will be recognized by the Compact's recurring officers. The source does not require Lucan's command history, Tala's engineering judgment, Sorin's rivalry with Morcant, Maelin's legal vocation, their route, or their final settlement. When the authoritative adventure improves, revise this demonstration rather than bending the adventure to preserve it.

## The warrant

Marshal Aveline Rusk gives Lucan, Tala, Sorin, and Maelin a blackened brass field plate bearing four names and her campaign seal. The document behind it authorizes them to:

- redirect companies and specialist detachments;
- requisition matériel under recorded terms;
- receive battlefield surrenders;
- grant temporary parole subject to later hearing;
- negotiate local protection and supply agreements;
- order the destruction of military infrastructure when preservation is no longer feasible; and
- speak for the Marshal during operations beyond reliable signal range.

Soldiers call both the plate and the people carrying it **the Ash Warrant**. The name is not ceremonial. Anyone accepting the Warrant's terms expects the Compact to recognize the plate after the campaign. Duplicate terms travel back to Rusk's obligation ledger whenever a courier can return.

The warrant creates authority but not unlimited capacity. Every promise names the protected people or unit, the officer and resources that enforce it, and the review that ends or confirms it. A promise to protect hostages, hold a village, or conduct trials becomes a demand on actual troops, wagons, guards, and time. The Warrant cannot grant permanent sovereignty, final amnesty, or resources its bearers have not assigned.

## Captain Lucan Vey

Lucan is a former Grey Banner company commander and later campaign staff officer. He leads the Ash Warrant, translates broad war aims into executable orders, and decides when the Warrant must remain together rather than serve four separate emergencies.

Several years earlier, Lucan helped plan a rapid punitive expedition against a border prince. The expedition destroyed its target in three days and left no force capable of controlling the road, protecting surrendering soldiers, or stopping allied reprisals. The official report called it a decisive victory. Lucan does not.

### Useful habits

- Defines an operation by what must still be true after the attack, not only by the target destroyed.
- Makes allied commanders name their reserve, relief conditions, and acceptable losses.
- Recognizes when a tactical success has created an unsupported salient.
- Can hold a mixed command together while its specialists work on different parts of one objective.

### Pressure point

Lucan can mistake explicit control for prudence. He prefers plans with named forces and bounded consequences, and he may delay a necessary improvisation while asking for a reserve that does not exist.

He is also vulnerable to the opposite error at Vossgard. An early chance to reach Voss resembles the clean decapitation his former superiors always claimed to want. He must decide whether refusing that chance is discipline or fear of repeating an old victory.

### Course through the campaign

Lucan supports the Causeway and Villages at the opening, goes personally to the villages, and accepts the risk of leaving the Barrows weakly screened. He later insists that the first attack on Vossgard have a bounded objective and orders the pivot to the Red Abbey instead of spending the army in an incomplete assault.

## Tala Marrick

Tala is a Gatewright combat engineer. She has built crossings under fire, disarmed prepared roads, and condemned structures whose political owners insisted they could be saved. She represents Colonel Merrow's technical authority within the Warrant but is not subordinate to him while the warrant is active.

Tala believes armies reveal their real intentions through infrastructure. A force that preserves roads, locks, granaries, and hospitals is preparing to govern. A force that destroys everything inconvenient is preparing an alibi for leaving.

### Useful habits

- Separates what must be preserved from what merely appears valuable.
- Designs operations around the crews who must hold and use captured machinery after the Warrant moves on.
- Reads demolitions, loads, water movement, and defensive construction at encounter scale.
- Can turn battlefield measurements into orders that ordinary engineers can execute without her presence.

### Pressure point

Tala will spend soldiers and time to preserve a route because she can see its future uses more clearly than its immediate casualties. At the Iron Causeway, her preferred operation is the most complete and least rapid. At the Drowned Sluice, she dislikes leaving a working military canal to the enemy even when the campaign clock says to move.

She takes professional criticism personally when Lucan chooses containment or destruction over an engineering solution. Lucan must be able to reject her best technical plan without treating her expertise as irrelevant.

### Course through the campaign

Tala argues for adequate Causeway support at the Ashen Gate and accepts Lucan's decision not to accompany it. She remains dissatisfied while the heavy train is fixed behind a contained bridge. At the Black Bell and Red Abbey she has to win the campaign with captured signals, field works, and a ridge approach rather than the controlled siege breach she wanted.

## Sorin Halvek

Sorin is an outrider, vampire hunter, and former Dawn Lancer troop leader. A shadow wound ended his ability to remain permanently attached to a cavalry formation: prolonged exposure to a mass of living mounts causes the old injury to numb his hands and distort distance. He can still ride hard for an operation and is at his best moving between columns where no one else has current ground truth.

He has fought Morcant's night riders twice along the material border. He knows their doctrine and says so openly at the first council. He learned this doctrine in two prior border actions and states the limits of that experience openly.

### Useful habits

- Reads movement through remount use, forage, courier intervals, and the places a mobile force refuses to defend.
- Distinguishes a routed unit from one deliberately dispersing for later raids.
- Coordinates cavalry screens, pursuits, and moving ambushes.
- Tracks vampire troops without assuming that destroying a body has ended the formation around it.

### Pressure point

Sorin treats enemy mobility as the source of every later problem. He is often right and can still be strategically wrong. He will abandon a fixed objective to pursue a commander if he believes that commander will otherwise choose the next battlefield.

His reports are fast, useful, and occasionally too final. When visibility is poor, he may report a destroyed remount system or dead commander before every withdrawal lane has been checked.

### Course through the campaign

Sorin argues for supporting the Thorn Barrows at the opening and is overruled. Morcant's turn-two raid gives his concern immediate weight. During the barrow operation he pushes for a decisive strike on the remounts, then later corrects an overconfident first report when evidence shows that part of the enemy force escaped without its mounts.

## Advocate Maelin Rook

Maelin is a military advocate, dawn chaplain, and former prison inspector. She can conduct the Compact's sunlight rites, treat blood loss and enthrallment, record lawful surrender, and state terms in language that commanders on both sides can act upon.

She joined the campaign because living levies and hostages make ordinary distinctions between combat, captivity, and civil rule impossible to postpone until after victory. She does not believe good intentions survive without records, witnesses, and assigned guards.

### Useful habits

- Converts broad assurances into named protections, custodians, deadlines, and appeal procedures.
- Recognizes when surrendering soldiers remain under immediate threat from dead cadres or their own officers.
- Uses ritual authority publicly, making consecration and treatment part of military order rather than private magic.
- Can distinguish coercion, collaboration, and command responsibility without pretending that battlefield hearings are final trials.

### Pressure point

Maelin can commit the Compact beyond its capacity. A guarantee that should create an opening may instead tie down troops the campaign needs elsewhere. She also prefers a lawful living witness to a dead enemy officer, even when capture makes an operation harder.

Her conflict is not whether collaborators deserve consequences. It is whether summary punishment during an invasion destroys the surrender system needed to save other soldiers and hostages.

### Course through the campaign

Maelin writes the opening requisition and surrender terms, protects them from being diluted into propaganda, and carries the same commitments through the Villages, Black Bell, Red Abbey, and Vossgard. The final settlement depends on whether the Warrant has actually honored the obligations she accepted in the Compact's name.

## The arguments that shape the route

The route grows from four recurring arguments.

### Which opening axis is most urgent

- Tala wants the Causeway because the heavy train determines what the army can do at Vossgard.
- Maelin wants the Villages because removal and burning can become irreversible before the fortress is reached.
- Sorin wants the Barrows because an intact mobile force will punish every later redeployment.
- Lucan chooses adequate support for the Causeway and Villages, leaves the Barrows to a reconnaissance screen, and takes the Warrant to the Villages.

The choice is defensible and costly. Morcant uses the opportunity the Warrant knowingly left her.

### Whether to pursue Morcant

After the turn-two raid, Sorin wants to hunt Morcant until her command is destroyed. Tala argues that destroying the remount system is enough and that a long pursuit gives Voss time elsewhere. Maelin wants captured riders and handlers who can identify levy movements and receive terms.

Lucan sets the objective as reduction of the mobile formation, not Morcant's death. The Warrant can kill, capture, or drive her away as long as the riders lose the ability to operate as a coherent campaign force.

### Whether to seize or merely silence the Black Bell

Lucan initially favors a tower raid followed by immediate movement toward Vossgard. Tala argues that a captured bell can replace some of the command capacity the army lacks. Maelin wants the living couriers separated from the dead reserve and preserved as channels for surrender. Sorin wants the reserve drawn into a battle where the freed Lancers can break it.

The resulting plan combines all four priorities at higher risk: draw out Ossa Ren, seize the tower, and offer terms to living couriers while the reserve is divided.

### Whether the first Vossgard opening is enough

The captured Bell gives the Compact a central approach before full concentration. Lucan sees a real chance to reach the inner keep. Tala notes that the siege train remains behind the Causeway and that the approach cannot support a prolonged breach. Sorin points to the open ridge and canal exits. Maelin believes Drenn's living garrison can be separated but not while Voss can promise relief from the Red Abbey.

Lucan defines a limited investment: take and hold the outer Bell works, deliver surrender terms, protect accessible prisoners, and force Voss to commit troops. It does not attempt to clear the keep.

### Whether to leave Vossgard for the Red Abbey

Leaving an active fortress after reaching its wall feels like surrendering initiative. Lucan argues that the army is not withdrawing; Holt remains in the Bell works and fixes the garrison. Tala worries the fortress will repair the approach. Sorin believes the canal is the more urgent exit. Maelin insists that blood, prisoners, and the ridge road make the Abbey the one operation that changes both the final battle and Drenn's decision.

The Warrant turns toward the Abbey. It accepts that the return to Vossgard will occur after Voss reaches full concentration.

### What to protect during the final breakout

With the Bell and ridge exits closed, Voss prepares to leave through the Drowned Sluice. The final battle divides attention among the prisoner yards, the lower gate, and the pursuit into the keep.

The Warrant has enough allies to divide the final work only because its earlier promises and surrenders remain credible. Lucan, Tala, Sorin, and Maelin must delegate tasks each would prefer to control personally.

## Opening allocation

The checked-in playthrough will begin with the following orders:

- **Iron Causeway:** adequately supported by Colonel Merrow, a Gatewright assault group, and Grey Banner infantry. Their initial order is to fix the garrison, prevent demolition if feasible, and avoid committing the siege train before the galleries are controlled.
- **Tithe Villages:** adequately supported by Provost Dane, interpreters, wagons, and disciplined infantry. The Ash Warrant goes with this axis.
- **Thorn Barrows:** screened by Captain Vale and a reduced reconnaissance force. Vale is ordered to report and delay rather than accept a decisive engagement without support.
- **Ashen Gate:** stable, with a mobile reserve retained after the opening probe.
- **Campaign policy:** requisition is recorded; collective punishment is forbidden; battlefield surrender protects living troops from dead cadres and preserves them for later hearing; named hostage guarantees require a corresponding rescue or evacuation order.

The allocation is not optimal in hindsight. It is a serious attempt to preserve both the heavy offensive and the civilian base while accepting risk on the mobile flank.

## Planned campaign decisions

These decisions are firm enough to support a coherent journal but leave tactical outcomes available for the full playthrough.

### The Tithe Villages

Lucan fortifies the crossroads and sends the Warrant against Vosca's hostage convoy rather than distributing the column among all three settlements. Maelin publishes the Compact's terms; Lucan assigns named units to enforcement; Tala protects the principal granaries from demolition; Sorin follows the convoy rather than chasing scattered officials.

The intended result is cooperative population posture, stopped hostages, and a living surrender precedent. One granary report will later prove overstated and be corrected in the journal without erasing the original field report.

### The Thorn Barrows

Morcant's raid against the Causeway supply effort forces the Warrant east on turn two. Sorin uses village guides and Vale's screen to bring the Warrant against the remount barrows. The objective is to reduce the rider formation and free the Dawn Lancers, not necessarily to kill Morcant.

The operation should produce captured mounts or intact lateral lanes, but at a visible cost. Sorin's initial report will overstate how completely the riders were trapped; the later correction will preserve the distinction between a broken rider formation and a commander still at large.

### The Black Bell Redoubt

The Warrant uses mobility from the Barrows to reach the Redoubt before Ren can recall another garrison. It draws part of the reserve away, attacks the tower, and offers surrender to living couriers while destroying Vark and the dead signal cadre.

The intended result is a captured Bell, a broken field reserve, a held Bell road, and useful living order staff. The operation should strain the army rather than supply a costless command asset.

### First Vossgard investment

The Compact advances through the Bell road on turn four. The Warrant takes the outer Bell works, delivers terms to Drenn, and protects prisoners or surrendering levies exposed during Voss's counterattack. It sees all three known constraints directly:

- the causeway containment has left the heavy train behind;
- the Red Abbey still supplies blood and controls the ridge road; and
- the Drowned Sluice remains an open lower exit.

Lucan reads the visible roads and decides that a full assault would spend the army while leaving Voss two coherent withdrawals. Holt remains with the investment while the Ash Warrant turns toward the Abbey.

### The Red Abbey

The captured Bell and surrendered order staff allow the Warrant to reach the Abbey during a confused withdrawal sequence. It offers Voln lawful custody and uses the ridge to force Rane's knights into a battle outside the inner yards.

The operation must choose among prisoners, blood, and the ridge gate under time pressure. The intended strong direction is to deny the blood reserve, rescue enough prisoners to uphold Maelin's public promises, and close the ridge road. Broad terms for Voln remain a postwar obligation rather than a clean moral resolution.

### Final Vossgard assault

The Warrant returns on turn six. The Bell road and ridge are closed. The canal remains open, Keln's drowned force is intact, and Voss has prepared barges, bloodless vampire survivors, selected prisoners, and one focused breakout.

The Warrant enters through the held Bell works or Abbey ridge while Dawn Lancers and captured mounts screen toward the lower gate. Maelin reopens surrender with Drenn. Tala coordinates the approach without a heavy siege breach. Lucan assigns the strained army's limited simultaneous tasks. Sorin tracks the breakout rather than assuming the inner keep is the only decisive ground.

The final result should be a military or durable victory purchased through prior conduct, not a perfect encirclement. The open canal must matter before it is overcome.

## Planned five-session rhythm

### Session 1: The warrant and the villages

Open at the Ashen Gate under attack, issue allocation orders, establish campaign policy, and move immediately to the Tithe Villages. End with the hostage convoy stopped and the first public surrender accepted.

### Session 2: The mobile flank

Resolve Morcant's announced raid, cross the Thorn Barrows, reduce the remount system, and use the recovered mobility to begin the strike on the Black Bell. End with the reserve committing to battle or the Warrant reaching the tower.

### Session 3: The Bell road and first investment

Complete the Black Bell operation, advance to Vossgard, seize the outer Bell works, address Drenn, and confront the cost of attacking without the Abbey or Sluice controlled. End with the decision to hold the investment and turn toward the Red Abbey.

### Session 4: The Red Abbey

Use Bell traffic and prior surrender terms to isolate the Abbey. Resolve the ridge battle, prison yards, blood vault, and Voln's surrender. End with the fortress fully concentrated and scouts reporting the canal breakout preparations.

### Session 5: Vossgard

Return to the investment, separate the living garrison if the Warrant can, answer the canal breakout, pursue Voss through the known inner defenses, and issue immediate settlement orders before the army begins moving home.

## Journal behaviors to exercise in pass 5

The checked-in play state should include:

1. an opening visit to the Ashen Gate and explicit allocation consequences;
2. clues spotted and conclusions established through command actions rather than searches;
3. at least one encounter revisited, with Vossgard serving as the principal revisit;
4. a correction to an overstated battlefield report while retaining the original operation;
5. remote consequences from the contained Causeway and bypassed Sluice;
6. a mix of reduced, contained, and bypassed field positions;
7. a first terminal visit that does not end the adventure;
8. the living-garrison stand-down or refusal as an earned operational outcome;
9. Voss's breakout through the single known open exit; and
10. explicit settlement orders after the fortress battle.

## Resulting campaign identity

The Ash Warrant's playthrough is neither the fastest route nor the complete encirclement. It is an offensive that begins with civilian protection, answers a mobile threat, breaks enemy command, applies pressure to the fortress, and then chooses to remove one remaining strategic dependency before committing to the final battle.

Its strongest assets are legitimacy, mobility, surrender channels, and the denied blood reserve. Its central liability is a deliberately open canal. The final victory therefore depends on command under known pressure rather than on collecting the missing answer.
