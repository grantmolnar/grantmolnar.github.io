# The Ashen Gate

**Role:** necessary, start  
**Tags:** command, logistics, bridgehead

Rusk has one road home, three southbound demands, and enough strength to make only two real.

## Opening View

The council breaks when the hospital awning comes down. Orderlies drag the cots clear; a second carrion bolt hangs black in the Gate’s grey daylight. Three couriers reach Rusk together: charges at the Causeway, removal carts in the villages, horses being saddled among the barrows. Merrow has the heavy train, Dane the occupation companies, Vale the only fast reserve. One road leads home behind them. Rusk plants the Warrant’s blackened field plate on the campaign map. “Choose what reaches daylight first.”

## Encounter Material

## Situation

Rusk's campaign map is pinned to a hospital trestle because carrion-bolt fire has cleared the command mound. Merrow has written Gate strain and wagon intervals along one edge; Dane has marked prisoner and civilian space; Holt and Vale have set formation counters beside the only road. Every southbound commitment now competes with wounded soldiers, empty wagons, replacement horses, ammunition, and whatever obligations the Warrant brings back.

Three attacks arrive before the allocation is complete. A second carrion bolt drops the hospital awning. Ash-dead skirmishers stand in the drainage cuts. Riders show themselves on the road and turn away as soon as Vale's reserve moves. None can take the bridgehead while the pylons hold; each can spend the hour, company, or wagon room needed by an opening axis.

The first council is interrupted by three reports:

- demolition crews are strengthening the Iron Causeway;
- Voss's officials are gathering people and stores in the Tithe Villages; and
- night riders are assembling in the Thorn Barrows.

All three reports are true. The army lacks the strength to answer all three in force.

## Command

Rusk conducts the allocation with Holt's first Grey Banner battalions, Merrow's Gatewrights and siege-train head, Vale's Dawn Lancers, and Dane's civil-affairs officers, interpreters, and field police. The bridgehead garrison, hospital companies, wagon crews, and Gate artificers remain separate commitments; moving one south changes what the road can receive.

Voss initially commits only probing forces: ash-dead skirmishers, carrion bolts fired from beyond the earthworks, and a small rider detachment testing the road. These attacks are meant to expose the Compact's reserve and delay the first march, not to win a decisive battle.

## The Ash Warrant

Rusk turns the blackened field plate over. Five lines wait beneath every field act: the people or unit covered; the enforcing officer or formation; assigned matériel or custody; the duplicate record; and the ending condition and review. The plate lets its bearers redirect committed companies and specialists, receive surrender, grant temporary parole, record requisition, and make local protection or supply agreements when ordinary orders cannot arrive.

A field agreement begins at once but remains enforceable only through named troops, stores, transport, custody, or engineering capacity. Couriers return duplicate terms to Rusk's obligation ledger.

The Warrant cannot grant permanent sovereignty, erase command responsibility, promise absent support, turn receipt into delivery, or convert temporary custody into final judgment.

## Detached columns

The first allocation gives other officers bounded work while the Warrant moves elsewhere. An adequately supported column can reconnoiter, delay, preserve an objective, or establish containment when its order names the force and line that make containment real. It cannot reduce a position, capture its distinctive asset, complete a politically contested surrender, or produce operational discoveries offscreen. A weakly supported axis screens and reports until the Warrant returns or redirects additional force.

Report remote developments before they take effect whenever the Warrant can still answer them. Initial support changes what an allied command can attempt; it does not close the other roads to the Warrant.

## Ground and pressure

The Gate road, field hospital, supply park, prisoner enclosure, and command mound all lie within a few hundred yards. A localized attack or panic therefore creates choices between military priorities.

The Gate pylons require periodic stabilization. Surging too much matériel through at once can move an additional formation into the campaign, but leaves the passage strained until repaired. A strained Gate narrows the daylit apron and cannot absorb a large emergency evacuation without casualties and lost stores. An imperiled Gate can become a genuine assault objective for Voss rather than merely a target for raids.

## Campaign clock

The opening council is turn zero. After each major field operation, advance the march turn and announce its threshold reaction before asking for the next destination. On turn two, an unresolved Morcant conducts one visible raid. On turn four, Voss chooses one surviving outer command for his primary withdrawal, stripping, or counterstroke while the other positions visibly prepare. On turn six, every mobile surviving command receives the general concentration and breakout order. A contained force cannot execute that movement unless it first breaks containment, and no threshold silently resolves an encounter.

## Objective

Issue the first operational orders, defeat or ignore Voss's probe without losing tempo, and leave a coherent reserve at the bridgehead.

## Courses of action

### Concentrate on two axes

Support two initial positions adequately and leave the third to reconnaissance and delay. The first operations gain strength and credible containment; Voss also learns where the Compact is not advancing.

### Spread support across all three

Give every axis enough troops to move but none enough to solve its position alone. The broad front limits Voss's freedom and leaves the Warrant carrying more decisive work at every position.

### Hold a strong reserve

Send one reinforced axis forward, support a second weakly, and keep a formation at the Gate. The reserve protects the bridgehead against the turn-two raid and permits one rapid redeployment, at the price of a slower opening.

### Force a Gate surge

Order Merrow to push an additional battalion, engineering park, or reserve of consecrated ammunition through immediately. The asset arrives early; the Gate begins strained and demands a later repair.

## Results

### Strong result

The probe is repelled without delaying the march, the Gate remains stable, and two axes receive adequate support. The army begins ready with a usable reserve.

### Costly results

- The bridgehead holds, but the reserve is spent or a hospital section is lost. The army remains ready while the Gate can absorb fewer later consequences.
- A forced surge supplies all three axes, but the Gate becomes strained.
- Preserving every imported asset costs one march turn. Voss's turn-two reaction occurs before the first field position is fully resolved.

### Setback

A raid reaches the supply park or damages a pylon. The Gate remains open, but the army begins strained and Rusk must retain more troops at the bridgehead. The offensive still proceeds.

## What carries forward

- Which axes received adequate support.
- Whether the Compact retained a mobile reserve.
- Whether the Ashen Gate is stable, strained, or imperiled.
- Whether the army begins ready or strained.
- Whether the Warrant established clear surrender and requisition policies before entering inhabited territory.
- Which named obligations the Warrant has accepted and what forces or stores enforce them.
- What bounded orders the detached columns received and what they have reported.

## Returning

The Gate changes whenever the Warrant returns. Casualties, prisoners, civilians, and captured matériel accumulate. Rusk can reassign a freed formation, but she cannot create troops that were lost or detachments already committed to containment. A return also makes the cost of bypassed positions visible through raids, missing wagons, and exhausted escorts.
