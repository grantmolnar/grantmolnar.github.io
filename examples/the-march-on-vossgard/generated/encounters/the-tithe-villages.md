# The Tithe Villages

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** civil-affairs, logistics, occupation

Three bells divide fire, removal, and resistance while the Compact establishes the price of protection.

## Opening View

The bells of Garren, Orra, and Lume disagree. One calls fire, one removal, one armed assembly. Grain carts leave between bound householders and ash hounds while Senior Reeve Maela Orr holds three requisitions no Compact officer has signed. Deren Salk opens the seed store for weapons; Hessa Dorn bars Orra against both armies. At the crossroads, Vosca’s column divides. The first cart south carries grain, hostages, and the occupation’s reputation.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

Three council tables stand at the raised crossroads while carts move south. Garren's reeves count grain and ask which guards will remain. Orra's officers load military-age adults and selected households for Vossgard. Lume's volunteers pull tithe books and collaborator names before Tal Vosca's men can burn them. Voss's orders are already moving: remove people and stores, destroy what cannot travel, and avoid Compact regulars.

The councils disagree about resistance, neutrality, requisition, and punishment. They share four facts:

- Voss will punish them if the Compact leaves;
- an invading army can consume a harvest as thoroughly as a fire;
- families with members in the levy fear both armies; and
- several local officers have prospered under Voss and will fight to keep their place.

Allegiance follows roads, guards, receipts, and what happens to collaborators after the first victory. Compact protection must become more credible than Voss's revenge.

## Command and local powers

**Senior Reeve Maela Orr** speaks for Garren and coordinates the three village councils when they can agree. She will support the Compact if it posts troops, pays or records requisitions, and accepts responsibility for hostages taken toward Vossgard.

**Reeve Deren Salk** of Lume favors armed resistance immediately. His volunteers are useful but likely to punish collaborators and seize stores without discipline.

**Reeve Hessa Dorn** of Orra believes Voss will outlast the invasion. She prefers neutrality unless the Compact demonstrates that it can hold the roads.

**Blood Provost Tal Vosca** commands levy officers, household guards, ash hounds, and two companies of conscripts. His immediate orders are to remove hostages and grain, burn what remains, and avoid a decisive fight with Compact regulars.

Provost Liora Dane and Grey Banner infantry are present if the axis received adequate support.

## Ground

- Three villages connected by raised field roads and stone drainage dikes.
- Granaries separated from most homes but vulnerable to fire.
- A blood-tithe house with records, restraints, and transport carts.
- A central crossroads leading west to the causeway, east to the barrows, and south toward the abbey and sluice roads.
- Evacuation columns that mix officials, conscripts, hostages, livestock, and grain wagons.

No single strike can protect three villages, their granaries, and a moving hostage column.

## Objective

Prevent Voss from removing the population and useful stores while establishing a policy the villages believe the Compact will honor.

## Courses of action

### Distributed protection

Divide allied troops among the villages, establish signals, and strike the moving provost convoy. People and stores gain coverage; Vosca can defeat a thin deployment in detail if he concentrates.

### Seize the crossroads

Occupy the road junction and announce surrender and requisition terms. The crossroads blocks organized removal and creates a defensible center while outlying granaries and households remain exposed to fire.

### Decapitate the levy operation

Strike Vosca, his ash hounds, and the hostage convoy. Much of the living levy stands down without its command cadre; local reeves must then control reprisals and fires.

### Arm local resistance

Arm Salk's volunteers while Compact regulars advance elsewhere. The bargain preserves regular strength and may raise broad resistance; feud killings, theft, and irregular conduct then determine population posture.

### Bargain for neutrality and supply

Offer written guarantees, payment records, and limited local self-rule for food, guides, and refusal to serve Voss. Little fighting is required only if enough troops remain to enforce the bargain.

### Pass through

The army takes what it can carry and continues toward the second belt. This keeps the march moving. Voss removes people and stores behind it, and the Compact loses the strongest basis for a durable settlement.

## Voss's response

Vosca does not defend every village. He uses fire and hostage columns to create simultaneous emergencies, then withdraws toward the Red Abbey or Drowned Sluice. If the Warrant announces indiscriminate punishment for collaborators, Vosca spreads the words among the conscripts and gains a more cohesive retreat.

By turn four, removal and burning are visibly underway. If Voss chooses the villages for his primary turn-four reaction, one major hostage-and-grain column departs or one principal store burns unless the Compact answers it; the other settlements do not empty automatically. At turn six, any convoy still able to move joins the general concentration toward Vossgard or the Red Abbey. Reduction or containment determines how much can actually leave.

## Results

### Strong result

The hostage convoy is stopped, most grain survives, Compact requisition is disciplined, and the reeves publicly accept protection. The population becomes cooperative. Living levies begin surrendering elsewhere when shown the village terms.

### Costly results

- The people are protected, but the grain burns. Population posture improves while army condition worsens.
- The stores are captured quickly through coercive seizure. Army condition improves, but the population becomes opposed.
- Vosca is killed and the levy collapses, but local reprisals begin. The Warrant must spend authority and troops to stop them or accept a harsher political settlement.
- Evacuating threatened households saves lives and burdens the Gate and supply road.

### Reduced

Vosca's command is broken, the villages are no longer organized for Voss, and the principal hostages or stores are secure. Reduction leaves the population cooperative, watchful, or opposed according to Compact conduct.

### Contained

Compact troops hold the crossroads and prevent organized movement, but Vosca retains one or more villages. A battalion remains tied down, and local conduct continues to shape population posture.

### Bypassed

Voss gains hostages, grain, guides, and replacement levies. Later operations face better enemy reconnaissance and a larger living garrison at Vossgard. The Warrant can still rescue hostages and induce defections at the fortress, but it has lost the easiest opportunity to establish legitimacy.

## What carries forward

- **Cooperative population:** guides, food, casualty reports, and levy defections.
- **Saved granaries:** stabilize or improve army condition.
- **Village militia:** can guard roads if disciplined, or create political liabilities if not.
- **Hostage obligation:** records the specific people the Warrant has promised to recover.
- **Opposed population:** concealment and guides favor Voss, and occupation requires more troops.

## Returning

A later return shows whether the Compact's promises were kept. Villages can become a rest and resupply base, a field of burned stores and displaced families, or an insurgent rear area. The Warrant can need to suppress Compact looting, respond to a rider raid, or choose whether to send local forces toward Vossgard.
