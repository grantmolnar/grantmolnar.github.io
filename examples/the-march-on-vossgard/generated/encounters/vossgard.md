# Vossgard

**Role:** necessary, end  
**Tags:** finale, fortress, campaign

Every open road and unsupported promise takes its appointed place in Vossgard's final defense.

## Opening View

Vossgard displays the march in absences. A closed road leaves one gate undermanned; a surviving command sounds from another. Engines, guides, prisoners, blood carts, surrendered companies, and exhausted battalions take only the places earned for them. Castellan Drenn shifts the living garrison around those facts while three breakout standards wait below the inner keep. Somewhere beyond the wall, a gate opens—or fails to. The first assault order will decide what the Compact abandons to reach Voss.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

Vossgard is now a deployment board, not a riddle. Drenn's reinforcement marks, captured orders, and reports from the outer positions put the Bell gate, ridge postern, Underkeep Gate, garrison court, prisoner yards, and inner keep on one table. Merrow marks where engines can arrive; Dane marks surrendered troops, prisoners, and protected households; Holt and Vale mark the companies required to hold each open road.

The Compact already knows how to kill a vampire: sunlight mirrors, consecrated steel, stakes, fire, and soldiers drilled to use them. The difficulty is carrying those means to Voss while holding the walls, guarding prisoners, answering every force left in the rear, and closing the breakout road the Warrant left open.

The fortress takes the shape of the campaign that reached it. A preserved heavy route brings engines to the wall. Cooperative villages bring guides, food, and surrender channels. A bypassed command arrives as reinforcement or attacks the rear. Every closed exit consumes Compact troops; every open exit receives Voss's prisoners, blood, wagons, and surviving force.

## Command

Voss reads the same board from inside the keep. He commands the fortress and each surviving formation that can still answer through an open road. He prefers an organized breakout; if every military exit closes, he spends the living garrison and inner works to gain time for a sortie, last defense, or visible personal flight.

Castellan Lysa Drenn commands the living fortress garrison and household levies. Her loyalty belongs to the continuity of the Gloam March more than to Voss. A credible surrender record and visible separation of living levies from vampire cadres may bring her garrison to a stand-down.

Surviving encounter commanders appear according to campaign state:

- Arven Taal strengthens fortress engineering if he escaped the Causeway.
- Tal Vosca brings hostages and village levies if the villages were bypassed.
- Kasia Morcant provides mobile relief if the night riders survive.
- Halwen Rane and her knights defend the ridge and inner court if the Abbey remains active.
- Ossa Ren and the field reserve hold the bell gate if the Redoubt remains active.
- Ors Keln and the drowned force defend the lower gate if the Sluice remains active.

Return a commander only with the capability the Warrant allowed that command to preserve.

## The fortress on arrival

### Voss's concentration

The march-turn clock determines preparation:

- **Before turn four:** outer defenses are incomplete, many living troops remain distributed, and prisoners have not all been moved. The Compact arrives with less preparation of its own.
- **Turns four and five:** Vossgard has a coherent garrison, whatever stores or prisoners the announced turn-four reaction successfully moved, and at most the selected reinforcements that escaped their field positions. Other outer commands prepare, but none reaches the fortress merely because the clock advanced.
- **Turn six or later:** Voss has issued the general concentration and breakout order. Mobile bypassed forces can answer it; reduced forces cannot, and contained forces must break their named interdiction line before they can move.

### Army condition

- A **ready** army can conduct three major simultaneous tasks during the assault.
- A **strained** army can conduct two without direct intervention.
- A **spent** army can sustain one decisive main effort. Every other objective requires abandonment, local allies, or the Warrant’s direct intervention.

The tasks are:

1. breach and hold an approach;
2. screen bypassed forces and relief;
3. secure prisoner yards and prevent massacre or forced removal;
4. fix or separate the living garrison; and
5. pursue Voss into the inner keep.

### Military exits

- Red Abbey controlled or sufficiently contained: ridge road closed.
- Black Bell controlled or sufficiently contained: bell road closed.
- Drowned Sluice controlled or permanently blocked: canal exit closed.

With all three closed, Voss cannot organize a conventional breakout. With one open, he prepares a focused escape. With two or three open, he can feint at one route and move through another. Closed roads deny military movement, not every power Voss possesses. He may still abandon his army and attempt a visible escape by mist, climbing, or a dominated mount through the Compact's consecrated cordon. Such a flight becomes a pursuit through the line and carries no organized remnant, prisoners, blood, or stores.

## Choosing the final operation

### Rapid decapitation strike

Concentrate the army on the best available route and drive for the inner keep before Voss fully prepares. Early contact with him leaves prisoners, relief, and open exits with the least coverage.

### Full encirclement and assault

The Compact invests the fortress, assigns troops to all exits, and breaches through one or more controlled approaches. This converts prior reductions into a powerful final position but gives Voss time to prepare the inner defense and increases accumulated campaign cost.

### Living-garrison collapse

If the population is cooperative, surrender terms are credible, and **Enforceable terms can split the living levies from the dead** has been established, address Drenn and the levy companies before or during the assault. A stand-down removes living troops and may open a gate; Voss's dead and vampire cadres continue fighting.

### Forced sortie

If all exits are closed but the army is too spent for a full assault, the Compact threatens the blood stores, begins controlled bombardment, or demonstrates that no relief is coming. Voss may sortie to break the line. This brings him out of the keep at the cost of exposing the encircling troops to a concentrated attack.

### Limited siege and negotiated handover

Demand the surrender of the living garrison, prisoners, and fortress while offering Voss no safe conduct. The terms may isolate him and reduce casualties. The Ashen Gate and army condition put a hard cost on every hour of siege.

## The final operation

### Phase one: establish the breach

Select the ridge postern, bell gate, lower gate, a siege-train breach, or a costly improvised assault. Prior assets determine which routes exist and how many troops each consumes.

- The intact Iron Causeway and advanced siege train create a controlled wall breach.
- The Red Abbey creates a secure ridge approach.
- The Black Bell creates a central road approach and disrupts dead coordination.
- The Drowned Sluice creates a protected lower approach.
- A captured shadow-mount force can seize a gate before defenders fully redeploy.

### Phase two: answer the field consequences

Bypassed forces act openly. Night riders attack the rear, the Black Bell reserve advances, vampire knights counterattack, or causeway troops strike supply. The Compact must screen them, detach troops, or accept that they enter the fortress battle.

This phase collects the risks the Warrant knowingly purchased by bypassing field positions.

### Phase three: garrison court and prisoners

Drenn's living levies occupy the court while prisoners are gathered for movement or use as cover. Choose whether to assault through them, honor prior guarantees, attempt surrender under battlefield conditions, or assign another force to the yards.

Cooperative villagers, rescued prisoners, captured couriers, and prior surrender precedents turn a stand-down into an executable order addressed to actual companies, officers, and households.

### Phase four: inner keep

Voss retreats or counterattacks through the sunless hall, the officers' stair, and the coffin vault. He wins time by moving and recovering; the Warrant wins by keeping pressure on him.

If the blood reserve remains, Voss and surviving vampire troops recover between positions until access is physically denied. If it was destroyed, they fight on accumulated wounds. Consecrated assets provide the means; maintain pressure and close the escape.

### Phase five: settlement

Once Voss is destroyed, captured, or stopped during a personal flight, issue immediate orders:

- who commands the fortress overnight;
- how prisoners and surrendered troops are handled;
- whether local reeves form a provisional March council;
- whether the Compact occupies, protects, or evacuates the territory;
- what happens to collaborators promised hearings; and
- how much force remains while the rest returns through the Ashen Gate.

The Warrant's authority here is provisional. It can appoint overnight command, preserve records, assign custody, continue recorded protection and parole, and establish a temporary council from reeves, surviving civil clerks, road or canal officers, and surrendered commanders who have accepted review. It cannot settle permanent sovereignty or grant final amnesty; Rusk and the Compact's civil commission must review those questions at the next reliable contact.

Settlement begins before the blood dries. A spent army, opposed population, open roads, destroyed records, and intact enemy formations sharply limit which provisional orders can be enforced. An organized withdrawal must likewise name who protects or evacuates the population and how the fortress and crossing are denied to an immediate successor.

## Voss's behavior

Voss's decisions are governed by visible state.

- If two or more military exits remain open and the battle turns against him, he attempts an organized breakout with prisoners, blood, and the strongest surviving formation.
- If one exit remains open, he stages a major feint and commits his reserve to that route.
- If no exit remains open, he either launches a concentrated sortie, fights through the inner keep, or abandons the organized army for a visible personal flight through the cordon.
- If the living garrison remains loyal, he spends it to hold the court and walls.
- If the living garrison stands down, he relies on dead and vampire troops without gaining a replacement hidden force.
- If the blood reserve remains, he uses it to keep vampire units in action and sustain his own retreats.
- If the reserve is denied, he conserves vampire troops and seeks a shorter decisive engagement.

Show these intentions through deployments, signals, and orders. Once Voss commits a reserve, road, or prisoner column, that deployment fixes what the Compact can still intercept.

## Campaign outcomes

Use these outcomes to name the military and civil state left after the campaign.

### Decapitation

Voss is destroyed or captured, but major field forces, hostile population, or an unstable Gate prevent the Compact from holding the Gloam March. The Warrant can organize an evacuation or accept a dangerous occupation.

### Military victory

Voss is destroyed or captured, the organized field army is broken, the Ashen Gate survives, and the Compact controls the fortress long enough to choose an orderly withdrawal or occupation.

### Broken flight

Voss abandons his army and escapes alone or with a handful of attendants through the consecrated line. The invasion corridor, fortress, and organized field army are still broken, but the Warrant has failed its first war aim and leaves the Compact a continuing hunt, a political liability, and a ruler who may someday build another command.

### Durable victory

The military victory is joined to a cooperative or at least watchful population, functioning local administration, controlled roads, and enough army strength to protect the settlement. This need not mean direct Compact rule.

### Costly strategic failure

Voss escapes with a coherent force, or the Ashen Gate becomes unusable before the army and civilians can withdraw. The Warrant can still save thousands, destroy key enemy assets, or deny the immediate invasion corridor. Failure preserves the consequences of the campaign instead of resetting them.
