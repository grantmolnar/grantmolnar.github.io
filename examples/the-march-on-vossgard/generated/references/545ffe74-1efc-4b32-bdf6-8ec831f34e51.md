# The Ashen Gate

**Reference ID:** `545ffe74-1efc-4b32-bdf6-8ec831f34e51`  
**Kind:** Place  
**Aliases:** Ashen Gate, the Gate, the bridgehead  
**Tags:** place, bridgehead, daylight, logistics

## Summary

The iron-pinned daylight passage and single bridgehead road through which the Compact must move troops, supply, prisoners, civilians, casualties, and every promise it means to sustain.

## Reference Material

## Pylon light across the inner apron

The Ashen Gate stands upright in empty air, held open by iron pylons and repeated sunlight rites. Material grey daylight falls across its inner apron, weakening dead troops that enter the bar while forcing living formations to bunch beneath weapons firing through the passage. Voss cannot hold the apron or close the way while the pylons and rites endure, but the opening remains narrow enough to obstruct, bombard, and overload.

## One rutted lane, two directions

The Compact's bridgehead surrounds a single rutted traffic lane. Battalions, wagons, replacement horses, ammunition, surgeons, prisoners, protected civilians, messages, and wounded soldiers all compete for it. A field promise made deeper in the March eventually reaches this road as guards, transport, hospital space, paperwork, or a claimant who must be heard. Outbound ambition and inbound responsibility therefore consume the same capacity.

## Mark strain on the bridgehead board

The Gate defines the campaign's supply and evacuation constraint, not its present condition. Its sheet does not establish whether the pylons are stable, the hospitals intact, the lane clear, a reserve present, refugees admitted, a convoy moving, or the bridgehead threatened. Those facts belong to the campaign board and journal.
