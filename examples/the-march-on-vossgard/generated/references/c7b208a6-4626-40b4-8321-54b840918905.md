## Reference Material

## Garren, Orra, Lume on raised roads

Garren, Orra, and Lume occupy raised field roads and stone drainage dikes on the only broad ground in the Gloam March where mortal grain grows reliably without sorcery. Their granaries stand apart from most homes, a central crossroads joins the western, eastern, and southern campaign roads, and low canal country begins beyond their worked fields. The three settlements trade and suffer together without sharing one council or one political will.

## Grain, labor, levy, blood, household

The villages supply grain, labor, military service, and scheduled blood. Levy families, local officers, blood-tithe records, transport carts, labor rolls, drainage knowledge, and canal workers tie ordinary households to the March's military infrastructure. Hostage taking and requisition therefore reach beyond the person removed or the wagon emptied: they alter which council can act, which field can be worked, and which promise an army must later honor.

## Guards and receipts outlast the column

The villages provide a durable civil and logistical relationship, not a fixed allegiance. This record does not establish population posture, current guards, stores, fires, hostages, conscripts, collaboration, evacuation, requisitions, receipts, guides, labor records, or support for either army. Those facts depend on what the Warrant does and what the journal records.
