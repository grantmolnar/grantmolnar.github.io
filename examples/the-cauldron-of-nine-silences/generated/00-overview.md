## Synopsis

After Red Fen killed or scattered a third of the vale's field command, emergency work beneath Dunwarren's western gate opened the sealed chamber where King Branoc died. His oath-ring is the first true anchor recovered since his death—and the first chance to ask what he told Edric Taran before the gate closed. During the renewal feast, a crew must remove the Cauldron of Nine Silences or conduct Branoc's Full Asking before Lord Mael turns the households' memorial rolls into a Great Muster. Wax requires witnesses, kitchen bells open old roads, severed counsel guards the underkeep, and every escape ends in a dispute over who may keep what no one should own alone.

## Premise

Five weeks ago, Red Fen cost the vale a third of its field command. Masons underpinning Dunwarren's western gate against the next attack opened King Branoc's sealed command chamber. Efa Rill removed his oath-ring before Lord Mael closed the excavation. Branoc has never been summoned; the ring makes his first Full Asking possible. At dawn, the nobles renew the Gate Compact and House Taran's emergency powers in the name of his disputed command. At the fifth bell, Mael will chain the households' memorial rolls into a Great Muster and spend all nine silences for one military answer. Enter during the feast. Preserve ring and cauldron. Bring out the vessel or Branoc's whole witnessed answer before law hardens and the dead are diminished.

## Explanation

The cauldron's proper rite is the Full Asking. A true anchor, the dead person's whole name, an honestly stated first question, free witnesses, and a named living keeper invite one honored dead person to return in recognition, memory, judgment, affection, refusal, reply, and will. The dead may answer, refuse, accuse, forgive, or ask what became of the people their answer once concerned. Free departure settles the nine silences back into relation for another generation.

Branoc has never been summoned. His body and intimate regalia vanished when Dunwarren nearly fell, and every surviving account of his final order descends from Edric Taran's recital. Red Fen forced Mael to underpin the western gate; the work exposed Branoc's sealed chamber. Efa Rill removed the oath-ring before Mael closed the excavation, and Gwyne Marr now holds it. The coalition's first question is exact: *What command did you give Edric Taran at the western gate?* They have promised to preserve the answer even if it condemns their cause.

The coalition offered chapel authentication under three witnesses: Brother Caddoc, Lady Aderyn's founder authority, and an opposed household jurist. Mael answered the emergency by annexing each safeguard: sole custody of the anchor, detention of the excavation foreman, searches of the masons' homes, and a royal claim over the witness bench itself. He does not hide what he wants. He makes every new danger an argument that one more key, keeper, witness, or dead voice must pass beneath his hand. Gwyne withheld the ring and notified Mere House because removal requires a keeper and destination. Mother Eluned rejected an immediate royal Asking before three hearths could convene and sent the Green Larks to recover the vessel. Their claim is rival custody, not a second commission.

For the dawn renewal, each household brought a memorial roll naming its honored service. Mael has chained those rolls into one war register and fitted copper combs to pass hundreds of names through separately opened bands. The resulting counsel may be accurate, but it loses the love, fear, promise, and duty that gave each answer proportion. Twelve earlier needle trials injured twelve dead and left narrow answers in the House of Borrowed Voices; they spent no whole silence and never called Branoc. The Great Muster will consume complete human faculties, then present the answer to the same nobles asked to renew Mael's authority.

Sabotage may delay the fifth bell while leaving ring, future Askings, and custody unresolved. The bronze vessel and living-oak cradle must travel together. Every consecrated threshold records keeper, destination, and authority. The Barrow Stair records exact declarations; the Borrowed Voices turn truth without purpose into security; the vault makes each threatened faculty visible; and the Reed Weir separates vessel, anchor, testimony, and keeper so that no single pursuer can erase every gain.
