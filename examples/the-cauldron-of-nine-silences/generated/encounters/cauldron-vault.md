# The Cauldron Vault

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** heist, target, cauldron, full-asking, finale

The target is a rite, a six-mark load, and a constitutional crisis whose nine visible faculties can still be saved or spent.

## Opening View

The fifth bell has not sounded. The cauldron is ready anyway. Black water gathers every face at its rim; nine iron bands circle the bronze, four custody dogs bolt its living-oak cradle to the floor, and three witness benches oppose one another across the spring. Above them hang Mael’s copper combs and chained roll of names. One comb is already warm.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

The old drowned court lies beneath a low stone roof, though the spring still rises through pavement polished by generations of bare feet. The cauldron is black bronze and broad enough to hold every witness’s reflected face. Nine iron bands keep the faculties of a whole dead person in relation. Four separate custody dogs bolt the living-oak cradle to three floor channels. One system governs asking; the other governs carrying. Confusing them is how a frightened crew destroys what it meant to steal.

Mael keeps no permanent guard beside the water. He trusts the stair, borrowed council, band arrests, cradle dogs, and the difficulty of carrying away a sacred institution while claiming to protect it. Keys leave wax witnesses. Tools have named pegs. Every exit crosses the water's sightline. At the vault, infiltration becomes responsibility.

His trials used hair-fine name needles through three closed bands; they injured twelve dead without spending a whole silence, so all nine silences remain present for Branoc. The Great Muster waits around the cradle. Hundreds of honored names lie on the war roll; nine copper combs hang over the spring; the question board asks how the vale shall survive the northern host. The wording is prudent enough to hide the violence. No dead person will be present to ask what Mael means by *the vale*, whom he is willing to spend, or whether the knowledge was ever his to take.

## Chamber plan

The drowned court is an oval roughly thirty paces long. The spring rises beneath the cauldron and drains through three shallow channels.

**The witness steps** descend along the south wall. Three opposed benches face the cauldron: the claimant's bench, the keeper's bench, and the contrary bench. A person may sit wherever they choose; the old names describe functions, not factions. Thin grooves in each bench hold witness vellum. During a proper Asking, exact speech beads onto those leaves in black water and dries with the marks of the bands that were present.

**The war table** occupies the east wall. The war roll lies beneath a horn cover. Nine combs hang in order, each with a waxed maintenance duplicate. The question board can be lifted from two hooks, but changing the board does not silently change the rite: a living officiant must speak the question while the comb touches water. The board is an evidentiary and disciplinary device, not a magical command tablet.

**The keeper's tree** rises west of the cradle. It carries four bronze levers for the custody dogs, three route wheels, nine small arrest bells, and a single black handbrake. The mechanisms are old, comprehensible, and too interdependent to operate casually under pressure. The handbrake can hold the cradle on any channel, but it requires a living person to remain at the tree or a substantial wedge to replace them.

**The three routes** leave from the cradle channels. The north channel reaches the lifting shaft beneath the Rookwalk. The east channel climbs toward the service tunnel behind the Smoke Kitchens. The southwest channel descends to the Reed Weir. Stone lips prevent the cradle from changing channels until it returns to the central turntable. Choosing a route is therefore a commitment unless the crew can bring the target back under fire or divide the opposition long enough to turn it again.

**The spring cup** hangs above the water on a silver chain. A true anchor rests in the cup; it is never dropped into the cauldron itself. Immersion does not strengthen the rite. It gives the spring a physical claim over the object and makes later recovery depend on the named keeper. Caddoc knows this, Gwyne suspects it, and Mael may exploit ignorance by demanding that the ring be surrendered into the water.

## The nine bands

The bands are not nine equal charges. Each preserves one faculty by which an answer belongs to a person. During a Full Asking they remain closed and lend their silences together; free departure returns those silences. The Muster opens each band, combs many names through exposed water, and spends the faculty without admitting the persons who owned it.

Each band has three visible states.

1. **Closed.** The clasp is seated and the arrest bell is still. No silence is being spent.
2. **Raised to the arrest.** The key has opened the clasp and the band stands a finger's breadth from the bronze. Its bell sounds once. The chamber and borrowed voices react, but no silence is lost yet. A crew can reseat the band, jam it here, or negotiate while the cost remains prospective.
3. **Committed.** The matching copper comb passes through the water while names are spoken. The band exhales through its channel, the water darkens, and that silence is gone from the cauldron for a generation or longer. Closing the iron afterward repairs security, not sacred capacity.

The bands are arranged from the lip downward. Mael intends to spend them in an order that makes the remaining dead easier to use: Bar, Hearth, Hand, Measure, Road, Season, Face, Reply, and Rest. The old proper order is not an opening sequence at all. A Full Asking touches each closed band in turn and asks it to hold.

### The Band of the Face — recognition

A small hammered face appears only when water condenses on the iron. This band lets the dead recognize their own name, anchor, relationships, and the persons before them. Opened in the Muster, it produces facts whose speakers cannot be identified beyond broad office or craft.

Without the Face, Branoc may answer as “the king at the western wall” yet fail to recognize Edric's name, his own oath-ring, or why Gwyne's resemblance matters. The testimony can still contain true words, but opponents can plausibly argue that a royal residue rather than Branoc spoke.

### The Band of the Road — memory in sequence

Its inner surface is scored with one unbroken road that passes through nine gates. This band lets a dead person place events before and after one another, distinguish command from consequence, and connect an isolated phrase to the circumstances that made it meaningful.

Without the Road, Branoc remembers the western gate, Edric, riders, the river stair, and his own death, but offers them out of order. He may give every clause of the command while leaving witnesses to decide which clause governed which moment.

### The Band of the Measure — judgment

Nine uneven weights hang from its clasp and balance only when the band is seated. This band lets the dead evaluate reasons, distinguish a literal recital from an honest account, and say which duty had priority when duties conflicted.

Without the Measure, Branoc can repeat what he said but cannot explain whether barring the great leaves was subordinate to admitting the outer ward. Mael can claim the first clause; Gwyne can claim the last. The words survive while their governing judgment does not.

### The Band of the Hearth — affection

The iron is warm near names spoken with love and cold near titles spoken only for use. This band preserves the attachments that made counsel answerable to actual people: kin, soldiers, enemies under protection, households, and the unborn.

Without the Hearth, Branoc gives a tactically exact command stripped of the reason he valued the outer ward above the fortress. He may call casualties acceptable whom he once knew by name. The answer resembles Mael's preferred history even when the facts do not.

### The Band of the Hand — self-command

A closed bronze hand grips the clasp pin. This band lets the dead choose wording, resist prompts, correct a false summary, and remain the author of their own speech.

Without the Hand, Branoc tends to complete the grammar offered by the questioner. A leading follow-up can reshape his answer without making any sentence literally false. Witnesses may recognize the coercion, but the water-script records only the resulting words.

### The Band of the Season — temporal understanding

Its nine leaves show bud, flower, fruit, fall, and bare branch in a cycle that never quite repeats. This band lets the dead understand that their life is past, that the witnesses live later, and that an old order may not fit a changed world.

Without the Season, Branoc speaks as the western wall is failing. He may issue commands to present guards, mistake Mael for Edric, or treat the northern host as the riders already outside his gate. His military urgency becomes vivid and politically dangerous.

### The Band of the Bar — refusal

This is the plainest band. Its clasp is a single horizontal stroke. It preserves the dead person's ability to refuse an invitation, decline a question, stop an inquiry, or withhold counsel from a purpose they judge corrupt.

Without the Bar, consent becomes a formality. Branoc answers whatever is put before him and cannot withdraw an earlier willingness when he learns how the testimony will be used. Mael plans to open this band first because every later violation becomes easier once refusal is gone.

### The Band of the Reply — reciprocal speech

Two small mouths face one another across the clasp. This band lets the dead ask questions, challenge assumptions, request evidence, distinguish the question asked from the question that matters, and respond to what witnesses reveal.

Without the Reply, Branoc can answer the first question literally but cannot ask what became of the outer ward, correct a misleading digest, or explain what living arrangement would honor his command now. The inquiry becomes closer to an unusually rich record search than a meeting.

### The Band of Rest — voluntary departure

The lowest band bears a boat pulled onto reeds. This band lets the dead decide when the Asking is complete and depart without leaving fragments behind. It also returns the other eight silences to their places.

Without Rest, Branoc cannot end the audience. His voice remains in water, bells, and nearby sleep until the vessel is repaired, destroyed, or another custodian accepts the unfinished presence. Keeping him becomes a second theft even if the first question was just.

## Custody dogs and cradle movement

Four iron custody dogs bolt the oak cradle to the central turntable. They are not ritual bands and can be released without diminishing the Full Asking. Mael's public language deliberately calls both sets “clasps,” encouraging helpers and thieves to treat movement as sacrilege.

Each dog can be released in one of four ways: the keeper's lever, its square service key, removal of the oak shear pin beneath the runner, or enough force to tear the dog from the floor. The methods differ in consequence.

- **Keeper's levers** are quiet but require the three Barrow Stair declarations to be present in the speaking tubes. A provisional or contested declaration can work.
- **Service keys** are carried by Caddoc, Vane, and the vault steward during the feast. Aderyn's founder seal can authorize a fourth key from the chapel press.
- **Shear pins** can be removed from beneath the cradle. This is slow, wet work in confined space, but it leaves the ritual apparatus untouched.
- **Force** releases a dog immediately and sends the corresponding route wheel into motion. The borrowed voices receive a clear movement alarm, and the damaged dog cannot help brake the target later.

The full cradle weighs as much as a heavily laden farm wagon. Once on level stone, two determined people can start it and four can control it. On the east rise it needs the service capstan, a team, or leverage. On the north lift it needs the Rookwalk windlass and a counterweight. On the weir descent gravity becomes the threat; the keeper's tree, intact dogs used as drag shoes, or prepared ropes prevent the cauldron from outrunning its handlers.

The first hand's breadth of movement sends breath through all twelve copper channels into the House of Borrowed Voices. Cooling or crossing those lines changes the resulting orders, but nothing makes a moving cauldron wholly silent. The heist must survive response rather than negate every consequence.

At the chapel threshold and the inner Reed Weir grate, the cradle stops as though rooted. A living keeper must name themselves, the destination, and the authority under which the next Asking may occur. Joint custody is possible if every keeper names the same destination and accepts that no one of them may conduct the rite alone. A false declaration moves the target across one threshold, but the Band of the Hand rises to its arrest and will commit if the lie is not corrected before the next consecrated boundary.

## Conducting the Full Asking

A proper Full Asking can occur in the vault or wherever the intact cauldron, cradle, spring water, and nine bands are brought into settled custody. The vault is easiest because the witness steps, spring, and records are already present. It is hardest because Mael can bring soldiers and legal authority to the same room.

The procedure is exact but not fragile. The crew can learn it from Caddoc's rubric, reconstruct it from the chamber, or proceed imperfectly and accept visible defects.

1. **Still the channels.** Close the three route wheels and the twelve speaking cocks. The cauldron must hear one invitation rather than the operations of the fortress. A speaking line left open gives the borrowed voices portions of the rite and lets distant guards hear fragments.
2. **Seat all nine bands.** Each arrest bell is touched and answered by one breath of silence. An already committed band cannot be restored by this gesture; its missing answer is named aloud so the witnesses know the limitation.
3. **Name custody.** A living keeper states where the cauldron will remain after the dead departs and who may authorize another Asking. This prevents a successful rite from becoming an immediate scramble for possession.
4. **Place the anchor in the spring cup.** Branoc's oath-ring rests above the water, close enough for its reflection to enter. The ring is evidence and invitation, not fuel.
5. **Speak the whole name.** The caller uses Branoc's private oath-name from the ring and the royal chapel record, followed by the public name by which the witnesses know him. A title alone calls the legend; an intimate name alone calls a man without establishing public identity.
6. **State the first question exactly.** The agreed wording is spoken before invitation so the dead can decide whether to come for that purpose. Changing the first question afterward requires a fresh invitation.
7. **Invite consent.** The water shows no face. It first produces a voice asking who keeps the ring and who will preserve the answer. Branoc may consent, refuse, demand a different witness, or require the coalition to reaffirm that an unwelcome answer will be copied whole.
8. **Receive the presence.** If he consents, reflections gather until every witness sees Branoc from a different remembered age. His voice comes from the water and from no living throat. He possesses only the knowledge, uncertainty, loyalties, and errors of his completed life. He is not omniscient and does not know what happened after his death unless the witnesses tell him.
9. **Preserve speech.** Three unwaxed vellum leaves on the opposed benches receive the exact exchange in black water-script. The marks of the nine bands present appear at the foot. The witnesses may add signatures and objections but cannot change the wet text without visibly breaking its band marks.
10. **Permit departure.** Branoc says when he is finished. If the Band of Rest is intact, the reflections separate, the ring dries, and the nine silences return to equilibrium. The proper rite therefore uses accumulated power without consuming it.

The Full Asking does not force Branoc to answer more than the invitation promised. Once the first question is answered, reciprocal conversation remains possible while he consents and the witnesses maintain custody. Violence in the chamber does not automatically end the rite. Branoc may continue, refuse to speak over bloodshed, or depart. The GM should treat him as a person attending a dangerous political hearing, not as a spell effect waiting for interruption rules.

## Branoc's answer

The adventure has an underlying historical truth. The crew is not required to discover it before the Asking, and no clue should make the heist unnecessary by revealing Branoc's exact words.

Branoc ordered Edric Taran:

> Bar the great leaves against the riders. Keep the wicket and the river stair until the outer ward is within. If the wall cannot hold, leave Dunwarren and save the people. A fort is a vessel; the realm is those it shelters.

Edric obeyed the first sentence immediately. He closed the wicket and river stair before the outer ward was within, held the fortress, and later reported only the command to bar the gate. Branoc died before he could know which portions Edric obeyed. He can testify to his command and priorities, not to Edric's later account or motives.

The answer condemns the constitutional doctrine built from Edric's recital without producing a simple fairy-tale reversal. Branoc did order the great leaves barred. House Taran can argue that Edric exercised military judgment under collapse. The coalition can answer that he disobeyed explicit protections and then transformed a temporary tactical order into a hereditary right to sacrifice those outside the wall. The testimony creates a political struggle grounded in exact words rather than resolving governance by magic.

A whole Branoc asks questions in return:

- Were the names of the outer ward kept?
- Does House Taran still call the fortress the realm?
- What living people now face the northern host?
- Who will keep the cauldron when everyone in this room has a use for him?

His response to the current crisis depends on the answers. He will not endorse abandoning the vale's defense merely to purify its law. He favors evacuation routes, distributed stores, living scouts, and authority limited to the protection of named communities. This supports Aderyn's replacement compact without making an ancient king automatically competent to direct a changed war.

## Diminished Askings

If bands have been committed, Branoc can still be invited while enough relation remains, but the chamber announces what is missing. The GM should not conceal the defect and then invalidate player plans afterward.

- **One band missing:** Branoc is recognizably present but lacks the named faculty. His testimony is meaningful and visibly diminished.
- **Two bands missing:** Branoc appears intermittently. Exact fragments can be preserved, but every faction has legitimate grounds to dispute interpretation.
- **Three to five bands missing:** The rite produces a severed inquiry rather than a whole person. Facts may be accurate; consent, context, or authorship is too incomplete for the result to count as the Full Asking promised by the coalition.
- **Six or more bands missing:** Branoc cannot be distinguished from the royal and military fragments drawn by the Great Muster. The oath-ring may call his voice, but not Branoc as a moral agent.

The particular missing faculty matters more than the count. A one-band Asking without Refusal may be more ethically compromised than a two-band Asking without Road and Season. The thresholds indicate political and ritual stability, not a numeric spell system.

## The Great Muster and substitution

The Great Muster requires four components: a band raised past its arrest, its matching comb, spoken names from the war roll, and the broad question voiced by an officiant. Removing any one component delays that portion of the rite. A mismatched component creates a visible failure rather than secretly redirecting sacred power.

**The war roll** supplies names and memorial claims. A substituted roll can survive visual inspection if its cords, seals, and handwriting are copied. Names cut too shallow for the comb remain on parchment but do not enter the water. The band stays at its arrest while Mael and Caddoc diagnose the failure. If they force the comb through blank water, the silence begins to leak without producing counsel.

**The copper combs** carry scratched names into the spring. Resin in the teeth, a maintenance duplicate without consecrated wear, or a comb exchanged for the wrong band causes the water to reject the names. This is an effective delay and an excellent way to expose that the rite is mechanical coercion rather than inevitable ancient necessity. It is not a harmless prank: leaving an opened band arrested too long eventually leaks its silence.

**The question board** disciplines witnesses and officiants. Replacing it with a narrower question does not compel Mael to speak that wording, but it forces him either to read the changed question before the room, improvise a different one without the prepared record, or reveal that the public board was never controlling the use. A substitution can therefore create a political interruption even when it cannot magically rewrite intent.

**The band keys** can be filed one tooth short or waxed over an inserted sliver. The clasp then rises to the arrest and jams before commitment. The sabotage survives ordinary seal inspection and becomes evident only when the key turns. A prepared crew can use that pause to extract the target, invoke respite, confront Mael, or complete the Full Asking.

A crew seeking only delay can disable several components and leave. Mael can replace parchment and tools within days or weeks if he retains the cauldron. A crew seeking a false appearance of success can let the first apparatus fail noisily while moving the cradle through another route. The room supports substitution as heist craft without pretending that tricking one object permanently solves the ethical conflict.

## The one-night respite

The oldest Gate Compact contains a narrow emergency provision called the **Night of Hearing**. When the sacred instrument itself is disputed, three authorities may halt every use until the next dawn: a chapel witness, the founder's seal, and a military surety who accepts responsibility for the living defense during the pause.

Brother Caddoc can serve as chapel witness. Lady Aderyn can produce the founder's seal. Captain Vane or Marshal Hew can serve as military surety; Gwyne cannot, because she commands no present defense. The provision transfers the keys to joint custody, seats the bands, and forbids removal from the drowned court during the night unless all three agree. Permanent ownership and Mael's office remain for later judgment. The provision buys enough lawful time for Branoc's Asking and for the nobles to hear the result before renewing the Compact.

Mael omitted the provision from the public renewal copies but did not destroy it. He will call it obsolete and dangerous. Vane's guards, however, are sworn to the older compact as well as to Mael. If the three authorities invoke it before a band is committed, ordinary household guards hold the chamber rather than attack either side. Mael must bargain, expose his willingness to violate the foundational law, or rely on personal loyalists.

## Bargaining in the vault

Mael bargains only when the crew can deny him both Muster and orderly custody. He answers indictment by demanding an alternative command structure with names, keys, wagons, expiry, and a defense order executable before dawn. If the structure is credible, he does not repent; he tests every clause for a seat, countersignature, emergency override, or custody share that keeps him inside it.

A credible replacement defense covenant has four parts:

1. Aderyn or another accepted noble coordinates levies only until a council can meet after Branoc's testimony.
2. Hew receives immediate access to living scouts, current maps, engineers, stores, and ferrymen rather than contextless dead counsel.
3. Vane retains authority to protect the feast, roads, and households through dawn without enforcing a disputed renewal oath.
4. The cauldron enters named joint custody under Caddoc and at least one non-Taran keeper; no coercive Muster occurs before a public decision.

Mael may accept the Full Asking first and a living war council afterward. He demands to sit on the contrary bench, places one Taran countersignature on movement of the cauldron, asks Branoc one non-leading follow-up about current defense, or requires that the vessel remain in the vale. These are not signs of self-knowledge. They are his attempt to survive defeat as an indispensable clause. Gwyne can accept limited terms if the first question and whole answer remain intact. Aderyn prefers an expiring seat without unilateral override. Hew accepts if current intelligence is already assembled. Vane accepts if the crew has not made civil order impossible through indiscriminate violence.

A **bounded Muster** remains possible but costly. Mael may propose opening one or two bands for a tightly named military problem. Every participant can see exactly what Branoc will later lack. Opening Road may yield old routes while leaving Branoc unable to order his own memories. Opening Season may produce comparisons across winters while making Branoc confuse past and present. Opening Measure may yield tactical ratios while destroying his ability to explain priorities. No band is a morally neutral “military charge”; consent does not make the loss neutral.

## Principal resolutions

### Intact extraction

Release the four custody dogs, name a keeper or joint keepers, choose a route, and move before Mael or the Green Larks control the same channel. All nine bands remain seated. The crew preserves the later Full Asking but carries the political burden of custody into the escape. At each consecrated threshold, anyone claiming ownership must speak plainly enough to be recorded.

Extraction is strongest when the crew has already arranged destination, witnesses, and morning defense. Without those, Aderyn and Vane may oppose foreign removal even while agreeing that Mael's Muster must stop. Gwyne supports flight to a neutral chapel. Caddoc will accompany the cauldron if no qualified keeper remains. Tomas Wren helps move the load only until the Keeping threshold requires him to name his patron.

### Full Asking in the vault

Seat the bands, close the channels, assemble willing and politically credible witnesses, and invite Branoc before the fifth bell. This is the fastest route to the answer and the weakest route to unchallenged custody. Once the water-script exists, Mael can no longer prevent testimony merely by retaining the object. He can still contest the witnesses, interpretation, and authority to postpone the dawn oath.

The strongest witness set includes Gwyne, Aderyn, Caddoc, and one skeptical military voice such as Vane or Hew. A partisan audience still receives a real answer, but public acceptance becomes harder. If Branoc refuses, the cauldron remains intact and the refusal itself is preserved. The crew must then decide whether to steal the instrument, ask what condition he requires, or stop Mael without receiving the hoped-for testimony.

### Lawful witnessed seizure

Invoke the Night of Hearing with Caddoc, Aderyn's seal, and Vane or Hew as surety. Joint custody holds the cauldron in the vault until dawn. Bring the three authorities together, stop Mael before a comb commits a band, and hold the chamber against loyalists or Green Larks who reject the respite.

### Temporary sabotage

Jam keys at the arrest, replace combs, remove the war roll, foul the speaking cocks, or seize the question board. Sabotage can save every silence tonight if Mael cannot improvise before dawn. The cauldron remains in his reach, and replacement tools begin the next race. Record delay as a real success with an unresolved custody problem.

### Substitution and apparent failure

The crew may arrange for the Great Muster to begin and visibly fail while the cauldron is already rolling toward another route. A shallow-cut war roll, jammed first key, or false maintenance comb buys a sharp interval because Mael initially believes the defect is local. The House of Borrowed Voices then receives conflicting objectives: protect the ceremony, seize the intruders, and stop the moving vessel. This resolution rewards preparation but leaves evidence of tampering and may let Mael blame Caddoc or household staff.

### Negotiated custody

Offer Mael the replacement defense covenant, whole Branoc testimony, and a prohibition on foreign transfer. The cauldron enters joint custody, the Great Muster ends, and the Gate Compact renewal is postponed. Mael signs only after every office, key, expiry, and override is read aloud. He remains where the covenant explicitly places him and nowhere else; whether that narrow seat is tolerable becomes the coalition's continuing dispute.

### Partial Muster

Permit one or more bands to be committed in exchange for immediate military counsel, access, or safe passage. Record the exact faculty lost and the names used. The later Asking is diminished in a known way. Caddoc insists that the cost be stated publicly. Hew insists that the question be narrow enough to produce actionable information. Mael presses for the Bar first; any character who understands the bands knows why.

### Destruction

The cauldron can be destroyed by cracking the bronze at the spring seam while cutting at least three bands, or by dropping the unbraked cradle down the weir shaft at low tide. Merely denting the vessel or cutting one band produces damage, not final destruction.

Destroying the cauldron releases brief unanchored voices through every bell, basin, kettle, and sleeping mouth in Dunwarren. They speak names and fragments, never coherent testimony. This instrument can coerce no dead person again; it can conduct no future Full Asking. Branoc's ring becomes historical evidence. Destruction is a defensible catastrophe when every custodian intends abuse or capture is certain.

## Restoration after loss

Iron can be reforged in weeks. A committed silence cannot. Each faculty returns only through a long public practice that repairs the relation Mael consumed.

- Face returns when erased names are restored to graves and household records.
- Road returns when whole lives are told rather than useful episodes excerpted.
- Measure returns through judgments that preserve reasons as well as verdicts.
- Hearth returns when descendants fulfill neglected obligations to those the dead loved.
- Hand returns when coerced testimony is repudiated and unabridged words are preserved.
- Season returns through anniversaries kept across generations without pretending the past is present.
- Bar returns when refusals by the powerless are honored despite inconvenience.
- Reply returns when the living answer questions the dead left them in deeds rather than ceremonies.
- Rest returns when disputed remains, memories, and offices are allowed to cease being instruments.

One community can begin at once; a fully committed band ordinarily needs a generation. Make restoration a campaign obligation, never a repair spell.

## The fourth and fifth bells

At the fourth bell Mael descends with Marshal Hew, Brother Caddoc, two living guards, and the war roll unless pressure elsewhere changes the party. Caddoc checks the spring cup and bands. Hew verifies the military names. Mael opens the custody-key box and offers one last chance for private compliance. If Vane has been alarmed, he arrives at the outer drowned court rather than beside Mael; this gives him distance from the first disputed order.

The Green Larks reach the chamber by whichever route the party leaves easiest. Tomas Wren offers tools and bodies for movement. He prefers an intact extraction and refuses to conduct the Asking. At the Keeping threshold he must finally name his patron or relinquish the cradle; that confrontation belongs to the extraction session, but the vault makes avoidance impossible.

At the fifth bell Mael turns the key of the Band of the Bar. The band rises to its arrest and rings once. Caddoc must then present the first comb and speak the first names. This creates a final reversible interval. The silence is not lost merely because the bell sounded. It is lost when the comb touches the water under spoken names. The distinction gives every prepared route—force, persuasion, substitution, respite, extraction, or immediate Asking—one last concrete action before irreversible harm.

## Separated objectives

After the Full Asking, four objectives can travel together or split: cauldron and cradle preserve future Askings; Branoc's ring preserves the anchor; black-water leaves preserve testimony; the recorded keeper preserves authority. Recovering one does not erase the others.

The three route wheels make the alternatives explicit. North sends the cradle to the Rookwalk windlass and public roof. East sends it to the kitchen service rise and staff yard. Southwest descends to the Reed Weir and private tide road. Each bears the complete target if its machinery remains intact. Southwest is fastest during low water, east is easiest to disguise, and north is easiest to witness.
