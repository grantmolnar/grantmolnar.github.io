# The White Hart Court

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** heist, social-infiltration, gate-compact, security, lord-mael

Mael turns hospitality, memorial rolls, and Red Fen's dead into a public argument for inherited emergency power.

## Opening View

“Wax admits the paper,” calls the first herald. “A witness admits the person.” Masked households pass beneath the white antlers while clerks bind their memorial rolls into one iron register. Musicians play over the chain’s scrape. Captain Vane’s guards carry shields and rope knives, but no bows face the sacred hill. At the fire, Mael rehearses Branoc’s command before Branoc has spoken.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

The White Hart Court is gate, feast yard, and throne room with the roof taken off. Red rushes hide the mud. Musicians play above the portcullis. The central fire is large enough to feed the court and bright enough to lend every mask the same honest expression. Mael has made abundance part of his constitutional argument: Dunwarren feeds the vale; therefore Dunwarren must command it.

Every courtesy leans toward tomorrow’s oath. The oldest recital says Branoc ordered Edric Taran to close the western gate, abandon the outer ward, and preserve the stronghold at any cost. House Taran has drawn roads, grain, levies, and noble wards from that sentence whenever danger is declared. Red Fen makes the danger real: a third of Hew's field command is dead or scattered, and the northern road depends on scouts and two uncertain bridges. Mael means the old emergency to explain the new obedience.

The court is open by categories, not by trust. Wax admits a document; a living witness admits a person. Servants pass by tally, performers by search, lesser guests under a greater household. Difficult arrivals are not dragged away. They are seated near the gate, given wine, and placed where Captain Rhod Vane can count every door they look at.

## Cast

- **Lord Mael Taran** stands between the central fire and the chained memorial rolls, correcting wagon counts while the herald speaks. Challenge the Muster and he records the objection, then asks who commands the wagons, keeps the western gate, holds the ring, and answers for the next dead patrol.
- **Captain Rhod Vane** keeps the living-guard keys at the gate table and gives orders by door, object, and bell. Show him counsel stripped of its conditions, and he asks which order remains lawful tonight.
- **Lady Aderyn Taran** wears no mask. She names every servant who approaches, measures proposals against morning, and leaves for the founder's seal after Mael's declaration unless the crew forces an earlier choice.
- **Marshal Hew Pale** keeps the war roll chained to his wrist. He answers claims with road days, grain figures, uncertain bridges, and scouts returned; ask whether the dead have advised him, and he looks first at the living shortages.
- **Nia Bell**, junior herald, knows invitations are being reused and altered. Procedure is the only protection her office gives her, and she has seen three masons' names added to the quiet arrest list.

## Ways through

### Pass as invited guests

A genuine invitation and plausible name clear the wax. A living witness clears the person. Recruit Aderyn, press a minor noble who owes Gwyne, replace Nia, join a noisy household, or make the heralds admit arrivals faster than they can verify them.

A failed witness seats the crew at a gate table under observation. The feast, kitchens, musicians' stair, petition bench, and holding rooms remain reachable, but Captain Vane watches every later deception.

### Enter as staff or suppliers

Kitchen carts circle behind the musicians' gallery. Their loads are opened but not individually searched. Workers are counted by colored tally cords. A correct cord gets a person into the court; a missing person on the kitchen roll creates an investigation at the second bell.

### Climb from the outer wall

The heralds never look up during an arrival. The musicians' gallery joins the Rookwalk above the portcullis. Reaching it requires the old chain bridge, a climb from the rain spouts, or access through the performers' stair.

## The second-bell declaration

At the second bell Mael raises no skull and promises no miracle. Each household has brought a memorial roll for the renewal's customary recitation of service. His clerks fasten those separate rolls into one chained war register: hundreds of honored captains, scouts, engineers, ferrymen, and physicians, presented by the same households that will receive the answer. Mael announces that at the fifth bell the cauldron will gather their counsel into one response to the question, “How shall the vale survive the northern host?” At dawn, after that response is read, the nobles will renew the Gate Compact.

Mael names every living shortage correctly and gives the number of widows after Red Fen. He calls the dead's cost a final service implied by honor. He never names the right to refuse, and he says nothing of the reasons his twelve trials left behind.

Interrupt, expose evidence, demand the war roll, or make Mael define the rite before the households. He answers narrowly without lying: the dead “are not compelled to speak falsely.” He will not say they are free to remain silent.

## Opportunities

- The compact copies quote Branoc only through Edric Taran's later testimony. No surviving line bears Branoc's hand or seal.
- Three excavation laborers are held in rooms off the gatehouse. Their presence can confirm that the western chamber was not a plague crypt, though none saw Efa take the ring.
- Captain Vane carries the living-guard keys. He never carries keys to the Cauldron Vault; he considers that separation essential.
- Marshal Hew leaves the war roll with a clerk during the first dance after Mael's speech. Altering it can spoil tonight's Muster but cannot permanently stop Mael from reconstructing the names.
- Lady Aderyn leaves for her solar after the speech and returns before the fourth bell. She can become ally, adversary, witness, or lawful claimant.
- The Green Larks use the feast as camouflage. Tomas Wren has arranged for a masked dancer to collapse at the third bell, pulling the court physician and two guards away.

## Pressure responses

The court answers suspicion socially before it answers violently.

**Noted.** A herald remembers the crew's faces, cover, carried box, or claimed household.

**Questioned.** Nia or Vane asks for a second witness, inspects an object, or separates one crew member from the others.

**Contained.** The crew is seated, escorted, or given a courteous guard. Movement remains possible through favors, confusion, a new identity, or a public petition.

**Exposed.** The gates close and the living guard searches for a specific description. The feast continues because Mael refuses to let thieves dictate the night's meaning.

## What changes here

Aderyn can witness an identity and later release the founder's seal. Evidence of counsel stripped from purpose can detach Vane's living guard from Mael's ritual command. Displaying the oath-ring forces Mael to claim custody or emergency privilege in public. Proving the Compact rests on Edric's testimony gives hesitant nobles grounds to delay the oath for a Full Asking.

## Recovery articles

Captain Vane's standing articles for sacred household objects hang behind the gate sergeant's chair. They forbid arrows, fire, hooked blades, or mounted pursuit wherever cauldron water might be spilled. Bearers are to be taken alive when possible; guards seize wheels, ropes, doors, and the named keeper rather than striking the vessel. Mael can countermand the articles, but doing so before witnesses exposes that he values immediate control above the institution he claims to protect.

The renewal oath carries its own restraint. One authenticated black-water leaf, supported by two named witnesses, stays the oath until noon and summons opposed witnesses when it contradicts the Compact's recital. It decides no succession, guilt, or custody; it stops the households from swearing a known abridgment in Branoc's name.
