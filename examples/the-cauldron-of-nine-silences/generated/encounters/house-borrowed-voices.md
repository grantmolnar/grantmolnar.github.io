# The House of Borrowed Voices

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** heist, borrowed-voices, ethical-complication, security, honored-dead

Twelve accurate answers without remembered purposes predict the heist and command living guards through visible machinery.

## Opening View

“Close the gate,” says a dead woman from an empty chair. “Leave one road for the wounded,” answers another. A black cauldron marker crosses the relief map without a living hand. Warm water trembles beneath twelve copper masks; colored command lines can be uncoupled and crossed at the switchboard below. Both voices are accurate. Somewhere overhead, the keep begins obeying them.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

The House once held living witnesses while a Full Asking took place below. Mael has made it a room where the dead advise without being present. Twelve empty oak chairs face a relief map of Dunwarren and the vale. A copper mask hangs from every chair; warm spring water waits beneath it; pipes arrive from the stair, chapel, kitchens, roofs, gate, weir, and the cauldron’s pressure channels.

The masks do not contain twelve dead people. Each retains one narrow answer taken through a name needle: a crossing depth, a granary measure, a road interval, a gate doctrine. The answer can recognize its conditions and speak in the dead person’s voice. It cannot remember the sister, child, oath, terror, mercy, or obligation that once told that person when the answer should not be used.

The trials stole threads through three closed bands without spending a whole silence. Hearth, Hand, and Reply are strained; all nine faculties remain available for Branoc. The council's facts are often exact. A room that remembers no purpose cannot reconcile twelve exact answers about what deserves to survive.

## The command table

Five movable markers stand on the relief map: **the vessel**, **the anchor**, **the war roll**, **the intruder**, and **the witnesses**. Pressure from the fort's tubes shifts these markers mechanically. A runner on the cauldron moves the vessel. Opening the oath-ring box near a consecrated tube draws the anchor marker. A disturbed gate, roof chain, kitchen count, or tide grate moves the intruder marker toward the relevant route.

Move the markers by hand, wedge their tracks, substitute weighted counters, or create simultaneous signals elsewhere. The voices reason from the table before them; they see no more of the keep than its pipes report.

A brass switchboard beneath the western edge connects twelve command throats to six living posts: White Hart Gate, East Passage, Rook Gallery, Chapel Watch, Drowned Court, and Reed Weir. Each line bears one of four shutters:

- **White:** verify, count, question, or escort.
- **Red:** close, seize, hold, or compel.
- **Blue:** alter water, fire, bridge, bell, or machinery.
- **Black:** wait unseen and take the predicted route at the right moment.

Dunwarren's guards are trained to act immediately when two voices agree or when one order carries Mael's black override. A single unconfirmed voice produces local verification. Contradictory orders make the post hold position and seek Captain Vane's judgment. This prevents the damaged council from casually killing household members, but it gives a clever crew a powerful objective: make the voices disagree faster than Vane can reconcile them.

The switchboard collars are designed to be changed during maintenance. Crossing two lines sends a correct order to the wrong post. Rotating a color shutter changes the posture of the receiving guard without changing the words. Opening every throat at once produces a recognizable emergency and raises heat sharply, but the resulting commands are often mutually impossible.

## How the council predicts a heist

The House works through accumulation.

**One sign**—a missed kitchen tally, a moved roof chain, an incomplete stair declaration—wakes the two or three voices whose domains fit. They recommend verification or a local closure.

**Two aligned signs** let the council infer a route. A heavy-cart discrepancy plus a disturbed east passage sends Mairwen and Madoc toward the kitchens. A moved windlass plus a false lantern wakes Iwan and Rhian toward the Rookwalk.

**The first movement of the cauldron** wakes all twelve. If the table still presents one coherent route and objective, several voices converge on the Reed Weir and chapel thresholds. If the crew has created simultaneous evidence, moved the witness or war-roll markers, or crossed command lines, the room instead sends a storm of incompatible instructions.

**Branoc's oath-ring** creates an authority fault. Several voices recognize the private royal oath-name or the rank of the anchor and advise protection, witnessed inquiry, or delay. Others see only a strategic object that must be seized. Displaying the ring is therefore not a master key. It creates a valuable pause at the price of revealing exactly what the crew carries.

## The twelve borrowed answers

### 1. Captain Alwen Reed — the Low Water Mask

Alwen knows channel depths, tidal delays, hull loads, and every crossing that remains usable after a sluice or bridge fails. Her retained answer sends a moving cauldron toward the Reed Weir and orders the moorings cut behind the last defensible boat.

What is missing is that Alwen never meant the last **military** boat to be the last boat. During the Flood Retreat she kept one ferry running for households abandoned by the levy. Her descendants still operate skiffs below Dunwarren, but their rescue verse has lost its final line and the family is increasingly remembered as having disobeyed a clean withdrawal order.

Her reason survives in the chapel ferry litany and in an old knot-board at the Smoke Kitchens: *No boat is last while someone waits on the bank.* Restoring that sentence makes Alwen retract any order that strands living people and can keep the Reed Weir post open long enough for evacuation or pursuit alike.

### 2. Queen Mairwen Hearth-Giver — the Granary Mask

Mairwen knows consumption rates, spoiled stores, household reserves, and how long a fortified population can live after every road closes. Her answer orders the lower granary sealed from the town, heavy carts searched, and kitchen workers confined before supplies disappear.

She has lost the winter for which the numbers mattered. Mairwen broke her own royal seal and fed the outer wards before the garrison. The present midsummer feast still repeats her grain blessing, but the children's names have vanished from the household copy.

The uncensored winter account rests in the Widow's Solar, and Nessa Pike knows the kitchen form of the blessing: *A store that outlives its people is a tomb.* Reconnecting the answer to that reason makes Mairwen distinguish theft for private gain from movement intended to keep the community alive.

### 3. Tegid Far-Step — the Long Road Mask

Tegid knows retreat roads, marching times, pursuit distances, and where a smaller force can disappear from a larger one. His answer identifies the Rookwalk descent as the crew's fastest unencumbered escape and recommends sealing it while sacrificing whoever remains behind.

The retained counsel has forgotten why Tegid became famous. He chose the longer western road because the wounded company could travel it. Later chronicles reduced his decision to an elegant feint; the names of the wounded have faded from their memorial board.

The chapel veterans' roll preserves the missing company, and a faded stair graffito gives Tegid's own qualification: *The road is not safe if only the swift survive.* Restored context makes him reject plans that depend upon abandoning an identified companion, prisoner, witness, or bearer.

### 4. Abbess Morwen of Nine Lamps — the Closed Door Mask

Morwen knows quarantine intervals, water separation, burial order, and how quickly contamination moves through a crowded household. Her answer closes the east passage, isolates the chapel, and recommends flooding any route that may carry corruption upward.

She no longer remembers that she crossed her own cordon every night to tend the infected novices. Mael has retained the boundary and discarded the obligation that justified it. At the abbey, novices now dream of nine lamps outside a barred door and cannot name the woman carrying them.

Caddoc's old rubric and the infirmary register preserve the names. The needed reason is: *A boundary keeps faith only if someone crosses it to tend the sick.* Restored context can turn Morwen's closure into an escorted passage, quarantine witness, or demand that guards accompany rather than abandon those below.

### 5. Iwan Stonehand — the Broken Bridge Mask

Iwan knows load, leverage, stone fatigue, counterweights, and the exact point at which a bridge, stair, or lifting frame should be sacrificed. His answer can drop the Rookwalk windlass, jam the Barrow maintenance ramp, or identify which vault runner will fail under uneven weight.

He has lost the reason he designed sacrificial joints. Iwan always spent stone to spare the houses beneath it. The present engineering roll praises his ruthless collapses while omitting the evacuation marks built into every design.

His mason's mark is visible on the Rookwalk counterweight and on the western gate excavation plan. The qualifying sentence is: *Stone is spent for lives, not lives for stone.* Restoring it makes Iwan warn before a collapse and choose the failure that leaves the broadest living escape.

### 6. Rhian Gorse-Eye — the Lantern Mask

Rhian knows sightlines, signal ranges, weather visibility, and how a false light betrays movement on a wall. Her answer orders unverified lanterns extinguished, roof figures held against the sky, and the gallery cleared until every signal has a named sender.

She has forgotten that her famous beacon chain was built to guide scattered households home through smoke, not merely to direct soldiers. The old roof keepers still use her three-lantern pattern, but no longer know that the lowest lamp meant civilians were on the road.

The Rookwalk signal verse and a White Hart tapestry preserve the reason: *A lamp is first a promise that someone will be found.* Restored context lets Rhian accept a signal that guides witnesses, refugees, or bearers out, even while she continues to expose an anonymous infiltrator.

### 7. Prince Owain Ash-Spear — the Open Ground Mask

Owain knows cavalry ground, crowd clearance, charge lanes, and how quickly a court or market can become a killing trap. His answer orders the White Hart Court emptied, the east yard trampled flat, and mounted retainers held where they can strike either gate.

The answer omits why Owain once refused the obvious charge. The field was planted with winter barley, and destroying it would have won an afternoon at the cost of the following spring. His descendants praise the delay as cunning but have forgotten the farmers whose harvest governed it.

The harvest tithe in the Smoke Kitchens and Aderyn's campaign book preserve his sentence: *Ground feeds a host before it carries one.* Restored context makes Owain protect food, workers, and evacuation space before optimizing a charge.

### 8. Nesta Vale-Hand — the Three Breaths Mask

Nesta knows field triage, blood loss, carrying capacity, and which wounded person must be moved first when time and hands are scarce. Mael's retained answer has been summarized as “save command first.” It orders healers seized, injured servants separated from decision-makers, and bearers redirected toward officers.

Nesta did not rank lives by title. She first saved the person most likely to keep three others breathing: a surgeon, bearer, parent, or guide. Her infirmary school still uses the phrase “three breaths,” but recent copies attribute it to command continuity.

The chapel infirmary ledger and Nessa Pike's household practice preserve the full rule: *The first life is the one that keeps three others breathing.* Restored context can make Nesta prioritize a wounded witness, custodian, or ordinary worker over Mael or a noble claimant.

### 9. Seneschal Madoc Three Keys — the Locked House Mask

Madoc knows internal doors, duplicated keys, stores, servant circulation, and how long a household remains defensible after its public gates fail. His answer locks the kitchen passage, solar stair, and chapel door in an order that herds intruders toward the underkeep.

He has lost the servants' stair he always left unsealed. Madoc believed a defended house still owed its people a way out. Modern plans mark that stair as a construction error, and House Taran's training version praises complete closure.

An old key board behind Nessa's flour bins and the founder's correction leaf in the solar preserve his warning: *A key that closes every door serves the fire.* Restored context makes Madoc leave one escape route open and identify it aloud, though he may choose a route inconvenient to the crew.

### 10. Elidyr Snow-Warden — the Waiting Pass Mask

Elidyr knows northern tracks, ambush positions, snow movement, and why pursuit often fails at a narrow exit. His answer tells the guard not to chase a moving target through Dunwarren. It tells them to wait at the Reed Weir or the Rookwalk throat and take the burdened crew when speed is gone.

The answer has forgotten the shepherd hamlets beneath his most famous ambush. Elidyr abandoned the ideal avalanche because victory would have buried them with the enemy. His memorial now celebrates patient destruction without naming what patience spared.

The northern relief map in the solar and the shepherds' midsummer petition in White Hart Court preserve his rule: *No victory is clean when it falls downhill.* Restored context prevents him from ordering flood, collapse, or fire that predictably strikes uninvolved households below.

### 11. Seren Bell-Bearer — the Sealed Message Mask

Seren knows wax, witness chains, false dispatches, cadence, and how to distinguish an urgent lie from a genuine command. Her answer orders messengers detained, chapel tubes silenced, invitation witnesses separated, and every unverified statement held until authority is clear.

She has lost the safe-conduct under which she carried enemy terms through a mob. Seren believed a false message could be seized without making every messenger fair prey. The current herald manual retains her verification tests and omits the protection that made them usable.

The White Hart witness roll and chapel safe-conduct bell preserve her sentence: *A lie may be seized; the mouth under safe-conduct may not.* Restored context makes Seren protect declared witnesses and messengers even while she challenges their documents.

### 12. Bledri Rowan-Fire — the First Roof Mask

Bledri knows smoke movement, firebreaks, roof pitch, wind, and which building must be sacrificed to keep flame from crossing a settlement. His answer can close the Smoke Kitchens, open the roof vents, or recommend burning a service wing to deny an intruder cover.

He has forgotten that the first roof he burned was his own hall, after every servant and dependent had left it. Later accounts made the act a model of noble decisiveness and quietly shifted the threatened sacrifice onto poorer houses.

The charred lintel above the kitchen smoke door and the compensation account in the solar preserve his qualification: *The first roof offered must be mine.* Restored context prevents Bledri from choosing a sacrifice whose cost falls only on people without authority in the room.

## Recovered reasons and release

Speaking a personal name near a mask creates only a hesitation. To reconnect an answer to a reason, the crew needs three elements:

1. the dead person's whole name;
2. a credible record, object, or living tradition preserving the missing reason;
3. the mask's return cock turned inward toward the cauldron channel.

When all three are present, the retained answer can speak one qualified sentence in its owner's fuller cadence. It may retract an order, amend it, protect someone the stripped tactic ignored, or choose to fall silent. Opening the return cock afterward ends the retained answer and sends its breath back toward the cauldron.

This proves neither complete restoration nor replenished silence. Caddoc claims only that the answer is no longer held apart and used against its governing purpose. False or sentimental reasons fail; the mask repeats its stripped counsel with mounting impatience.

A crew does not need to restore all twelve voices. One or two carefully chosen reasons can alter the specific guard response that blocks the chosen route. Restoring several creates witnesses to the Great Muster's moral cost and may persuade Caddoc, Vane, or Aderyn. It also consumes time and makes a covert crew speak names into an active command system.

## Noncombat approaches

**Cross quietly.** Cool selected bowls, wedge the intruder marker, and move between the chairs while the relevant voices sleep. Warm water returns through the floor, so silence is temporary.

**Create a false objective.** Move the war-roll or witness marker toward another region. The voices may protect the wrong object while the crew approaches the cauldron.

**Create contradictory routes.** Ring a kitchen service bell, move the roof windlass, and disturb the weir float within the same interval. Voices issue incompatible orders; living posts hold and ask Vane to decide.

**Cross the command lines.** Swap collars so a correct Reed Weir ambush reaches White Hart Gate, or turn a red seizure order into white verification. Careless changes remain obvious to the next maintenance inspection.

**Recover a reason.** Use evidence gathered elsewhere to make one voice qualify or retract the order most dangerous to the crew's route.

**Invoke royal authority.** Present Branoc's ring. This divides the council between protection, seizure, and witnessed inquiry. The pause is real, but every active tube can report that the anchor exists.

**Release the answers.** Open return cocks after restoring context, or open all cocks and let the answers collapse into overlapping fragments. The first is slow and morally legible. The second disables much of the room but destroys evidence and may cause several masks to issue final uncontrolled commands.

**Use the room as evidence.** Bring Caddoc, Aderyn, Vane, household witnesses, or even Mael into a demonstration. Asking each mask whom its advice was meant to protect reveals the same absence. This can transform a hidden heist into a contested seizure or public refusal before the Compact.

## Council states

**Listening.** Warm bowls breathe; only directly relevant masks answer. No living guard has a coherent description of the crew.

**Focused.** Two or more voices agree on one route and objective. Matching commands reach the relevant post. Show the shutters moving before guards finish repositioning.

**Divided.** Different objectives or routes produce incompatible commands. Posts hold, verify, or seek Vane. This creates delay rather than total paralysis.

**Royal fault.** Branoc's anchor is recognized. Protection, seizure, and inquiry orders collide. Caddoc may descend; Mael accelerates if the report reaches him.

**Full alarm.** The cauldron moves with an unconfused table, or Mael applies his override. All twelve speak. The likely exits receive black waiting orders, the chapel receives red seizure, and the kitchens receive white verification. The crew can still escape by changing the table, severing lines, winning a living ally, or moving faster than the council can revise its model.

**Released.** Enough return cocks are open that the room can no longer produce two agreeing commands. Individual factual fragments may still speak, but the predictive guard network has ceased to function.

## Immediate play

Run the House as an adversarial planning scene in real time. Show which masks wake, which marker moves, what order is spoken, which shutter turns, and which tube carries it. The crew may hide from the model, corrupt its inputs, divide its priorities, restore a missing reason, or make the room testify against Mael.

The advisers remain among Dunwarren's most competent minds. What they cannot choose is which competence deserves obedience when its remembered purpose is gone.

## Recovery orders

A red strip beneath the six post shutters carries Vane's standing recovery order: **hold the route, take the bearers living, preserve the water, and do not strike the cradle**. The borrowed voices translate that order into barriers, rope seizures, wheel chocks, and attempts to separate keeper, anchor, and testimony. They do not ordinarily order indiscriminate missile fire or a rush against the moving vessel.

Mael's brass override can turn a single voice into sufficient authority for harsher action, but the physical command line still reveals where the order came from. Crossing red seizure with white verification makes guards demand names and witnesses instead of charging. Crossing it with blue machinery sends them to brakes, grates, capstans, and boats. A crew that understands the network can shape the pursuit's objective after concealment fails.
