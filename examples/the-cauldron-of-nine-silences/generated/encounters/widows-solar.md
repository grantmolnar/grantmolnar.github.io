# The Widow's Solar

**Role:** optional  
**Tags:** heist, optional, politics, gate-compact, founders-seal

Aderyn holds founder authority and a replacement defense, but will not trade one ungoverned morning for another.

## Opening View

One candle serves two unfinished plans: Dunwarren’s western gate before doctrine sealed it, and a replacement covenant with three blank signatures. Lady Aderyn folds mourning linen over the founder’s seal. Gwyne’s nine letters lie unanswered beside the excavation packet. Aderyn studies the old road, not the visitors, when she asks who will govern it if the inherited answer fails.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

Aderyn’s solar overlooks the feast and refuses its light. The room can be reached from the mourning gallery or by a rain balcony from the chapel roof. A ring of household keys, the founder’s seal, and the dawn renewal text occupy three corners of the table. Aderyn sits at the fourth, close enough to interrupt the next oath and unwilling to pretend that means she owns it.

Aderyn does not know Branoc’s command. She knows every surviving recital passes through Edric Taran after Branoc and the outer-ward witnesses were dead, displaced, or voiceless. The record deserves doubt; the northern road still needs orders in the morning.

Two plans share her table and resist one another. Gwyne’s papers make the case for hearing Branoc. Aderyn’s draft binds three households to roads, grain, scouts, and watch duty if Mael’s authority is stayed. She will not help the crew win the first plan by leaving the vale without the second.

## Aderyn's position

Aderyn gave Gwyne information, withheld the founder's seal until she sees the ring, and promised to delay the oath for credible testimony. Mael's refusal made action likely. It did not win her consent to foreign ownership, destruction, or a private Asking whose answer can be edited before the households hear it.

If present, Aderyn asks four questions in order:

1. Is the ring authentic enough that Caddoc will risk the rite?
2. Who will witness Branoc, and who chooses the first question?
3. Where will the cauldron rest after the Asking?
4. What keeps the north road and granaries governed if Branoc's answer breaks the Compact?

She listens for whether the crew treats the answer as evidence or a weapon already aimed. “We do not know what he will say” earns more trust than any promise of Edric's guilt.

## Principal contents

**The founder's seal.** A heavy silver matrix bearing the nine-house mark beneath the later Taran stag. It opens the Barrow Stair, the inner Reed Weir grate, and the cauldron's lawful custody clasps. Used publicly, it lets Aderyn claim that removal is seizure under the older compact rather than ordinary theft.

**The first Gate Compact.** Branoc's supposed command appears only in a recital beginning, “Edric Taran testified before the surviving households.” Branoc's own seal is absent. Later renewals silently treat the recital as direct royal command.

**The dawn renewal text.** Each noble is to swear to preserve the vale “as Branoc commanded at the western gate” and grant Mael emergency powers until three consecutive harvests pass without declared threat. Several marginal notes show that hesitant nobles will accept a delay if Aderyn invokes a succession question before the oath begins.

**The excavation packet.** Plans, mason lists, and two contradictory inventories establish that the chamber was sealed from the inside after the gate fell. One inventory records a gold object on the skeleton's right hand; the later official copy omits it. Neither proves who killed Branoc or what he ordered.

**Gwyne's letters.** The letters grow more exact rather than more frantic. They request access to the cauldron, propose witnesses, define tests for a true anchor, and promise to publish Branoc's answer in full. Aderyn's unsent response agrees to those conditions but refuses to let the cauldron leave the vale without neutral custody.

**The replacement compact.** Three households have tentatively pledged riders, grain guards, and bridge crews if emergency authority is distributed for thirty days. The arrangement is incomplete. It is enough to answer Captain Vane's immediate concern, not enough to settle succession.

**The underkeep plans.** They show the Barrow Stair, House of Borrowed Voices, drowned court, lifting shaft, and Reed Weir. Aderyn has marked which old mechanisms answer the founder's seal.

## Ways through the scene

**Recruit Aderyn.** Show the ring, the chapel authentication, or evidence of Mael's cost to the dead. Promise witnessed testimony and a named custodian. Aderyn gives the seal, plans, and a household witness; she also insists on accompanying or sending Sella Penn to the Asking.

**Bargain without trust.** Offer to deliver the cauldron to a neutral abbey, let Aderyn hold the ring until the vault is reached, or secure Captain Vane's support for the replacement compact. She may release one asset while withholding the rest.

**Steal from her.** The balcony, servant passage, and roof window permit theft. Taking the seal without the compact produces access but not public authority. Aderyn becomes an intelligent pursuer who tries to recover the seal without helping Mael destroy the cauldron.

**Expose her.** Mael can be told that Aderyn corresponded with Gwyne. He confines her and hardens the solar, but the resulting family dispute may delay the compact and draw guards away from the chapel.

**Leave a message.** If the clock or route makes a meeting impossible, the crew can leave proof and terms. Aderyn acts at the fourth bell according to what they offer: delay the oath, send the seal, withdraw her household witness, or denounce both crews.

## Political outcomes

Aderyn can transform the meaning of success.

- With her seal and witnesses, extraction can become temporary lawful custody under the older nine-house compact.
- With her replacement plan, Captain Vane can oppose Mael without believing he has abandoned the vale.
- With the original compact, Branoc's answer can be presented as correction of a contested recital rather than an attack on every oath ever sworn.
- Without agreement on custody and publication, Aderyn may prefer an immediate Full Asking in the vault to removal by unknown thieves.

The Solar buys what flight alone cannot: witnesses, replacement government, and a claim that survives the waterline.

## The oath cannot outrun testimony

Aderyn's legal packet contains an unused contradiction clause from the founding oath. A new Gate Compact cannot be sworn over authenticated royal testimony that bears directly on its recital. One black-water leaf and two witnesses delay the oath until noon; all three leaves and the founder's seal require a public hearing before a replacement text can be certified.

The clause deposes no lord, returns no land, and grants Gwyne no cauldron. It preserves one morning in which the nobles hear Branoc before binding themselves in his name. Aderyn will expose her support for that much and no less.
