# The Rookwalk

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** heist, roof-route, surveillance, extraction, rival-crew

Broken chains and message rooks offer speed, surveillance, and a spectacular extraction route that cannot remain secret.

## Opening View

The brass-ringed rook screams at the lantern, not at you. Rain runs along two load chains above the broken bridge, and each gust knocks the old lifting hook inside its tower. Court, chapel, kitchens, and western gate lie open to this height. One shutter governs the birds. The windlass beside it was built for cargo no rook could carry.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

The Rookwalk once carried messages and light loads from the outer rook tower to the gallery above the gate. Smugglers cost it its boards; the two load chains remain, black with rain and fixed deep in both towers. Beyond the gallery, one broken line of slate joins the chapel cote, Aderyn’s balcony, six kitchen chimneys, and the disused siege lift above the underkeep.

No one owns the whole view. Gate guards leave the roof to the rook keeper; musicians leave it to the watch; the watch trusts anyone moving with household confidence. Rain, smoke, bells, and feast lanterns alternate blindness with moments when one body stands visible to half the fort.

## Roof elements

**The chain crossing.** A plank, crawl sling, or careful traverse crosses the gap. A fall sends someone onto the sloped gatehouse roof with noise, injury, and witnesses.

**The ringed rooks.** Dunwarren's birds carry short messages between watch posts. They cry at unfamiliar lantern colors, not unfamiliar faces. Food, darkness, a correct lantern shutter, or releasing all the birds can defeat the warning. Killing them brings the rook keeper and creates a very specific crime scene.

**The musicians' gallery.** The gallery is crowded during the first two bells and nearly empty during Mael's speech. Its back stair reaches the White Hart Court and the passage above the kitchens.

**The rain chain.** A broad copper chain drains the chapel roof into a cistern beside the Barrow Stair. It can serve as a descent line. Removing or diverting it floods the chapel aisle and exposes the older stone hatch beneath the font.

**The lifting tower.** An oak windlass once raised funeral stones from the underkeep. The cable has been cut, but the drum, brake, and roof aperture remain. Its beam carries the same nine-band maker's mark as the cauldron cradle. Re-rigged, it can lift the cradle to the roof. The first inch of movement sends breath through twelve copper speaking tubes below, waking every borrowed voice and giving the fort twelve different predictions of the theft.

## Cast

- **Ivo Rook**, keeper of the message birds, is proud of his animals and indifferent to court politics. He can be bribed with coin, enlisted through concern for the birds, or impersonated poorly because few people know his face.
- **Sella Penn**, harpist and covert correspondent for Lady Aderyn, uses the gallery to exchange messages through the rooks. She can provide a solar route and knows Aderyn expects Gwyne to act tonight.
- **Kerr and Lowen**, two young watchmen, share the chapel roof circuit. They are more worried about being blamed for wet bowstrings than about legendary thieves.

## Heist uses

Choose what the Rookwalk is buying: entry, lateral movement, surveillance, extraction, or escape. Using it for one purpose changes the others.

- Using it quietly preserves surprise but limits the crew to what they can carry by hand.
- Rebuilding the windlass creates a strong extraction route and a conspicuous fixed position.
- Releasing the rooks creates confusion across every watch post and destroys the crew's ability to intercept messages.
- Cutting the rain chain opens the chapel hatch but floods evidence and alerts Brother Caddoc.
- Crossing toward the solar gives access to Lady Aderyn's letters and seal, but places the crew above her private guard.
- Lowering a cord through the bell cote provides a route for the oath-ring or ritual papers even when the cradle must leave elsewhere.

## The rival crew

The Green Larks reach the musicians' gallery after their dancer falls at the third bell. They have no roof specialist. Control the route and Tomas bargains; damage it and he turns to the kitchens; leave gear and the Larks use it under whatever blame follows. He wants the cauldron intact for Mother Eluned and regards Branoc's question as urgent evidence obtained through a dangerous royal precedent.

## The old siege lift

The lifting tower's north windlass still aligns with the vault's north route channel. A counterweight equal to six burden marks raises the complete cradle to the roof; less requires a hauling team, and more sends the load upward hard enough to damage the stop beam. The outer siege crane can then lower it beyond the inner wall onto the tide road.

When the weir grounds, this is the fastest alternative and the least private. Rooks scatter, the roof bell sounds, and White Hart Court watches the cauldron cross the skyline. Aderyn's seal, Caddoc, Branoc's leaves, or a disciplined declaration can make the spectacle a witnessed seizure. Otherwise every household sees a theft.
