# The Reed Weir

**Role:** necessary, end  
**Tags:** heist, escape, tide, custody, aftermath

The tide road forces vessel, anchor, testimony, people, and custody into boats too small to carry every claim together.

## Opening View

The punt is waiting. Tomas Wren is standing in it. Behind him, a green-lashed skiff noses through the reeds while black water drains from the inner grate and the runner road rises stone by stone. The burden marks allow six shares for the cradle and three for everything else. Tomas has not drawn a weapon. The tide has begun taking the road back.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

The Reed Weir is older than its use as an escape. When the Nine Hearths passed the cauldron from one community to another, its oak runners descended this braked stone throat to the mere. Wall rings, burden marks, keeper cuts, tide notches, and grooves polished by the cradle still describe that work more clearly than any surviving charter.

Low water gives the road forty minutes. Gwyne’s black punt waits beyond the inner grate, but Tomas Wren stands in it. A second Green Lark skiff lies behind the reeds with tow lines and a folding crossbeam. Tomas has not murdered the boatman or sunk the promised craft. He has paid Sib’s nephew, put him safely ashore, and occupied the one place from which every successful theft must become a custody negotiation.

The fort arrives in pieces. Borrowed voices predict unless divided. Vane descends under articles protecting bearers and sacred water. Mael may choose ring or witness leaves over vessel; Hew may offer military necessity without ritual ownership. Ask four things: can the load reach water, who is keeper, which people and records travel, and what remains when claims outweigh boats.

## The carrying chamber

**The southwest brake.** A long oak lever controls the cradle's descent from the vault. With the brake whole, two people regulate the loaded runners while the rest guide. If the associated custody dog was torn from the floor or the brake line cut, the cradle arrives hard. Hauling lines, wedged beams, or several bodies can still stop it. Failure costs position, time, a tool, or injury before it threatens the target itself.

**The tide shelf.** A dry ledge runs above high water. The complete cradle can wait here out of sight from the outer bank, but not from anyone descending the passage. Hiding it through a missed tide preserves the artifact while conceding the prepared escape and giving pursuers time to establish claims around it.

**The keeper's stone.** Three shallow handprints face the grate: current keeper, receiving keeper, and witness. The old circuit expected custody to be transferred here. Mael's iron plate covers the latter two prints and recognizes only his emergency custodian. The founder's seal removes the plate; tools can pry it loose; a complete Barrow declaration can make it withdraw from within.

**The winding socket.** The grate rises quietly with the outer tide key, inner winding bar, or founder's seal placed in the square rose. Force bends the teeth and leaves the route open behind the crew. Cutting the chain drops the grate rather than raising it and can trap the cauldron inside.

**The tide notches.** The upper notch marks the first water that will carry a loaded boat without pinning it against stone. The lower notch marks the last water deep enough to float the cauldron safely.

**The reed road.** Outside, a raised footpath follows the channel for half a mile before splitting toward the Crooked Magpie, Mere House, and the north road. People who cannot ride in the loaded punt can move along this path, tow from shore, hide among reeds, or make for another extraction point. A split party is vulnerable but not abandoned.

## The tide in four states

Advance one state when the crew spends substantial time, loses control of the load, performs the Full Asking after low water opens, or accepts a consequence explicitly costing tide.

### Pinned water

Before the upper notch, the outgoing current remains too high. An empty boat can approach; a loaded punt is pressed sideways against the wall. The crew can prepare the cradle on the shelf, open the grate, place tow lines, negotiate custody, or send evidence ahead. Early departure requires shore lines and skilled handling. Failure pins the boat, breaks a pole, or exposes the outer party rather than capsizing the cauldron without warning.

### The black lane

The middle twenty minutes are the clean window. The current carries the loaded punt through the reeds while remaining deep enough for the six-mark cradle. A prepared boat, intact brake, and open grate make movement reliable. Pursuit becomes a contest over ropes, keeper, anchor, and witnesses rather than repeated checks to see whether the boat simply moves.

### Sucking water

During the last ten minutes mud ridges appear. Safe capacity falls by one burden mark, poles replace oars, and every halt risks grounding. The crew may transfer people to the Lark skiff, send them to the reed road, discard a bulky tool, or accept grounding at a chosen bank. A grounded boat can be reached by foot pursuit but does not damage the cauldron by itself.

### The bare road

After the lower notch, the punt cannot float the intact cradle. The old runner grooves continue through exposed mud. Twelve steady people, two draft animals, a functioning capstan line, or an ingenious equivalent can drag it to the first deep pool. Alternatively the crew can hide the target, reverse toward another exit, negotiate witnessed seizure, or wait roughly two hours for flood. Missing the window changes posture while leaving several outcomes available.

## The target as a load

Use **burden marks** only for this carrying problem.

- Cauldron and complete cradle occupy **six marks**.
- The black punt carries **nine marks** safely during the black lane and **eight** during sucking water.
- Two competent boat hands are required, one mark each.
- The named keeper occupies the remaining ordinary mark.
- An incapacitated person, bulky tool chest, cooper's handcart, or large evidence coffer occupies one mark.
- Branoc's ring, three rolled witness leaves, ordinary gear, and a compact rite kit travel on a person without another mark.

The black punt therefore carries target, two boat hands, and one keeper. It cannot also carry the whole crew, every witness, wounded allies, prisoners, tools, and rival claimants. Additional people use the reed road, Green Lark skiff, or another exit.

The Lark skiff carries four people and cannot bear the cradle alone. Lashed beside the punt, it adds hands, stability, and witness capacity without changing which craft carries the cauldron. Cooperation solves a practical problem while making Tomas's custody demand unavoidable.

The marks are disclosed earlier through kitchen chalk, Rookwalk ledgers, and the punt's draft marks. Clever crews can prepare another boat, recruit shore haulers, reduce the party at the target, or choose another exit.

## The final custodial threshold

The cauldron crosses the grate only after a keeper states their whole name, destination, and authority for the next Asking. A complete Barrow declaration carries forward. The keeper need not repeat it unless custody changes.

Possession does not replace the recorded keeper. Tomas may seize a tow line, Vane surround the boat, or Mael recover keys without gaining authority for a Full Asking. A thief who named a false destination still owes correction before the bands answer again.

At the keeper's stone, current keeper names the receiver, receiver accepts destination and authority, and a witness touches the third print. Nine soft contractions confirm a coherent transfer. A contested transfer leaves the vessel movable but ritually closed. A coerced transfer proves possession, not authority.

Thus the crew may lose the boat and preserve custody, lose custody and save testimony, or surrender the vessel to Vane under terms that bar Mael's immediate use.

## The Nine Hearth claim

At the keeper's stone, Tomas can produce the old carrying calendar: Mere, Ash, Rowan, Gorse, Reed, Stone, Bell, Road, and Lamp, each receiving the cauldron for one winter and surrendering it onward. War, inheritance, and a keeper's own house required three disinterested hearths.

The calendar records fifty-three years of Taran emergency custody and no final release. Mere, Rowan, and Bell retain complete keeper lines; Ash and Reed bring broken seals; the remaining hearths survive through memorial societies.

**Mother Eluned of the Mere House** sent the Green Larks to carry the vessel intact to Mere House under temporary Trust custody before any ruler uses it again. She will not let an urgent royal question erase communal keeping, but her claim is not a verdict: three hearths are not nine, her seniority is disputed, and the old road never anticipated anchor, Muster, and dawn oath arriving together.

## Tomas Wren's offer

Tomas shows a green cord tied in nine unequal loops, a Mere House carrying token, and a letter naming Eluned's claim. He offers the second skiff, four skilled hands, a clean route through the reeds, false pursuit signs toward the north road, and silence about the crew.

His price is a keeper declaration naming Mere House and the surviving Trust, with no Full Asking until three disinterested hearths convene. If Branoc already spoke, Tomas demands all three witness leaves travel separately and unabridged to Mere House rather than directly to Gwyne.

Four kinds of leverage can move him.

**The exact question.** It asks what Branoc commanded, not who should rule.

**The dawn clause.** Waiting for three hearths may make the answer legally useless for another generation.

**Shared custody.** Tomas can accept one current keeper, one Mere House receiver, and a three-hearth limit on every question after Branoc's first Asking.

**Branoc's consent.** If Branoc explicitly consented to publication, Tomas will not claim he protects the dead by suppressing a willing witness. He still insists the vessel go to communal custody.

If no bargain is reached, Tomas may cooperate until the party clears Dunwarren and contest custody on the reed road. He prefers a live dispute over captured or damaged sacred power. Murder of a Lark, sale to a private patron, or announced use as spoils makes him uncompromising.

## Green Lark movement

At the third bell the Larks trigger the dancer's collapse and enter by the easiest route the crew leaves open. At the first neglected asset, they take one useful thing—hooks, tide key, service key, tally cord, or unattended boat—and use it. When the cauldron moves, Mabli Quill follows the transport line while Tomas moves to the receiving end. If the crew controls both ends, the Larks shadow rather than appear through a sealed wall.

At the custodial threshold Tomas must name Eluned and the Trust. Rebuffed, the Larks seize logistics: tow lines, skiff, capstan, winding bar, an unattended ring, or the public claim itself. Their intervention creates a rival solution and a new bargain, not compulsory combat.

## Pursuit as four objectives

Track four separable objectives.

**Vessel:** cauldron and cradle preserve future Askings.

**Anchor:** Branoc's oath-ring preserves the chance to call him again and authenticates the present inquiry.

**Testimony:** three black-water leaves preserve exact exchange and may stay the dawn oath even if the vessel is recaptured.

**Keeper:** recorded custodian carries authority to transfer, correct, or permit the next use.

Ask where each objective is and who carries it. Keeping everything together simplifies coordination but exposes all stakes to one recovery. Splitting them protects partial success while creating several vulnerable routes.

- **Vane** seeks vessel and keeper. His written order is: **hold the route, take the bearers living, preserve the water, and do not strike the cradle**. If shown exact testimony or replacement defense, he may secure rather than return the cauldron.
- **Mael** seeks the vessel before Branoc speaks. After testimony exists, he prioritizes ring and leaves because bronze cannot restore the old recital's credibility.
- **Hew** seeks a defensible morning. He may permit testimony to leave if a living council replaces the Muster.
- **Green Larks** seek vessel and custody declaration. They have little reason to steal the ring after Branoc speaks except to prevent a second immediate royal question.
- **Gwyne's coalition** seeks ring and testimony first if the cauldron is lost.

At each pursuit beat, choose one objective a pursuer can reach and one fitting means: close a grate, seize a rope, challenge a keeper, isolate a witness, offer terms, block a road, or force public identification. Do not repeatedly ask whether generic guards catch the whole party.

## Captain Vane's bounded recovery

Vane's guards use barriers before weapons. At the weir they can chock runner grooves, brace the grate, seize shore lines, occupy the reed road, or shield the keeper. They will not ordinarily shoot into the loaded punt, ignite reeds, or strike the cauldron with hooked poles. A character who knows the articles can force guards to choose between standing law and Mael's override before witnesses.

Vane can accept Night of Hearing; temporary Mere House custody inside the vale; testimony leaving while he secures the vessel; or Aderyn's replacement defense compact. He cannot accept an unnamed destination, sale to a foreign buyer, casual destruction, or departure leaving no northern command. Solving the morning-after problem is stronger leverage than exposing Mael's hypocrisy.

## Other exits

### East through the Smoke Kitchens

The east channel reaches the service rise. Four capstan hands lift the cradle to kitchen level; a reinforced cooper's cart carries it through staff yard and postern. This is slow and easiest to disguise at low Heat. Workers become witnesses, helpers, potential hostages, and people endangered by recovery. Nessa can turn the yard into ordinary legitimacy and refuses passage if the crew harmed staff or leaves the vale undefended.

### North through the Rookwalk

The north channel reaches the lifting tower. Correct counterweight raises the cradle; the siege crane lowers it beyond the wall. The court, roofs, and road see the movement. The route favors witnessed seizure, ruins secrecy, and remains strongest after the tide is gone. Founder seal, Caddoc, testimony, or a nine-hearth token can make it a public claim rather than naked theft.

### Upward through chapel and court

The crew can abandon escape and carry the dispute into chapel or court. This requires Caddoc, Aderyn, Vane or Hew, founder authority, and credible testimony. It produces Night of Hearing, public Asking, or witnessed seizure. It is strongest after Branoc speaks and weakest if the crew offers no replacement defense.

### Back into the underkeep

Retreat is valid. Tide shelf, chapel crypt, counterweight gallery, and disconnected command posts can hold the target temporarily. Retreat buys a bargain, second route, or Asking while allowing factions to converge and Mael to replace tools.

## A compact extraction procedure

1. State which bands remain, whether Branoc spoke, and who is keeper.
2. Place vessel, anchor, testimony, and keeper.
3. Choose route and transport.
4. Name the first pursuer and objective using Heat, command-network state, and faction interest.
5. Present one concrete obstacle: brake, grate, load, rope, road, witness, authority, or bargain.
6. On success move to the next stage. On failure concede position, time, exposure, injury, an objective, or a custody term. Do not reset the same obstacle.
7. At the final threshold settle destination and authority.

A normal extraction takes two or three beats after the vault. Add one for split objectives, damaged routes, or several factions at the same stage.

## Failure that preserves consequence

**Cauldron recaptured, testimony escapes.** One authenticated witness leaf and two living witnesses stay renewal until noon. Mael has physical custody but cannot renew the old recital without openly suppressing hearing.

**Cauldron escapes, ring seized.** Muster is prevented and future Askings remain possible, but Branoc cannot be called again until the anchor is recovered. Existing leaves remain valid.

**Ring escapes, cauldron recaptured.** Branoc's first opportunity survives as leverage. Mael may accelerate another rite, fortify custody, or offer a bounded Asking.

**Named keeper captured.** Carriers can continue, but future Asking remains closed until custody transfers or corrects. Vane may hold the keeper as guarantor rather than ordinary prisoner.

**Boat grounds.** The target remains intact on the old carrying road. Tow, hide, bargain, split objectives, call the Larks, or reverse routes. Grounding ends secrecy, not agency.

**One band is committed.** Record the faculty lost. Continue a diminished Asking, preserve the remaining eight, and begin restoration. Delay does not itself spend bands; commitment still requires comb, water, and spoken names.

**Crew surrenders under terms.** Surrender can establish Night of Hearing, Vane's neutral security, Aderyn's compact, Mere House custody, or public noon hearing. It is total defeat only if terms restore unrestricted Muster and erase every objective.

## If Branoc has spoken

A complete Full Asking produces three black-water leaves. Ordinarily one goes to chapel witness, one to founder or opposed household bench, and one to keeper. Each preserves the entire exchange, including Branoc's questions, conditions, uncertainty, or criticism.

One authenticated leaf with two witnesses stays the dawn renewal until noon. Three leaves and Aderyn's seal require a public hearing before a replacement Compact. No leaf automatically deposes Mael or awards land. Political force comes from preventing nobles from swearing a known simplification as Branoc's command.

Branoc confirms that he ordered the great leaves barred against riders while commanding Edric to keep wicket and river stair open for the outer ward and abandon the fort if necessary to save the people. Edric preserved the first order and violated or buried the governing protections. Mael's doctrine loses its claim to be Branoc's whole command while present defense remains necessary.

If the answer occurred without willing or credible witnesses, the leaves remain ritually exact but politically vulnerable. Extraction may prioritize a living witness as strongly as bronze.

## Immediate aftermath before dawn

### Full testimony reaches the court

The old Compact cannot renew unchanged. Aderyn proposes a one-season defense covenant: Vane commands household guard, Hew chairs a living intelligence and engineering council, levies require named valley consent, wards remain with households, and cauldron custody is heard separately at noon. Mael may accept bounded command, oppose testimony, or force a naked choice between himself and the defense he claimed only he could provide.

### Testimony escapes while the vessel remains

The oath is stayed. Mael still holds the cauldron, but each attempted use occurs under scrutiny and Vane's articles. The coalition shifts to protecting witnesses, proving excavation chain, and forcing a noon hearing.

### Vessel escapes before Branoc speaks

Muster is prevented and first Asking remains available if ring and custody survive. Nobles may nevertheless renew the Compact at dawn because no contrary testimony was heard. Custodians must ask immediately at Mere House, return under safe conduct, or accept that preserving the institution let old law harden temporarily.

### Nine Hearth Trust receives the vessel

Eluned seats the bands and recognizes no permanent owner. If Tomas accepted Branoc's urgent first Asking, the Trust sends a keeper and witness to noon hearing. If not, Eluned convenes hearths and may delay for days while the Compact renews. The artifact is safer; the political opportunity may be lost.

### Vane secures the vessel

Vane closes both Mael and thieves out of immediate use, honors witness leaves, demands living defense, and places guards under Caddoc's supervision. This becomes constitutional middle ground or military seizure depending on whether Aderyn and households constrain him.

### Mael regains unrestricted custody

If no bands were spent and ring or testimony escaped, Mael recovered the object but not certainty. He can bargain, accelerate, or override Caddoc and Vane. If every objective is recovered, excavation evidence, Trust claim, and internal opposition still prevent the world from returning unchanged.

## Campaign-scale aftermath

**Who keeps a communal sacred instrument?** The Trust has oldest claim, Taran has continuous custody and infrastructure, Caddoc represents rite, Aderyn founder authority, and outer-ward coalition excluded people. Stable settlement likely divides keeping, witnessing, and authority to ask.

**What follows Branoc's command?** Edric's conduct can be judged separately from Mael's office. Land restoration, memorial correction, compensation, succession, and blame require living evidence. Branoc does not govern the present from the cauldron.

**How is the vale defended?** Hew's living council, Vane's command, valley scouts, engineers, granary keepers, and limited levies can replace the Muster. Failure to build that arrangement strengthens Mael's argument even when his rite was wrong.

**What is owed to the twelve borrowed dead?** Each answer can be recontextualized and released. Families may demand records, apology, and restoration. Caddoc's complicity is not erased by one brave act.

**Can the nine hearths be renewed?** Dead communities, disputed seals, and altered settlements make simple restoration impossible. New hearths may be admitted, old offices ended, or a different trust written. The heist can begin a campaign about building an institution worthy of the cauldron rather than finding one true owner.

## Closing the end encounter

Record five facts:

1. Which silences remain intact?
2. Did Branoc speak, and where are the leaves?
3. Who is keeper, what destination was named, and under what authority may the next Asking occur?
4. Where are vessel, ring, testimony, and keeper at dawn?
5. What living defense replaces, limits, or fails to replace Muster and Compact?

Those answers define success better than a single boat does. Measure the heist by what truth, consent, defense, and sacred power survive—and by whom they leave answering for them at dawn.
