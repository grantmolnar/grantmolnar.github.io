# The Crooked Magpie

**Role:** necessary, start  
**Tags:** heist, planning, safehouse, branoc, rival-crew

A ruined tide-road tavern offers Branoc's ring, one exact question, three approaches, and a custody dispute already waiting at the exit.

## Opening View

The rain reaches the ring before Gwyne Marr does. One drop strikes the lead box between three cups, an unanswered letter, and Sib Crowle’s open tide book. Across the mere, fresh scaffolding braces Dunwarren’s western gate while feast lamps climb the hill toward dawn. Gwyne watches both preparations, then asks which danger the crew means to prevent.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

The Crooked Magpie did not close all at once. First Lord Mael diverted the tide road. Then the carriers stopped, the floor warped toward the mere, and Sib Crowle began serving only people who preferred a ruined house to a witnessed meeting. Rain works at the shutters. Across the flats, Dunwarren shines with midsummer lamps while seven valleys send masks, musicians, wine, and sworn representatives uphill to renew the Gate Compact at dawn.

Five weeks ago, the levy screen broke at Red Fen and Dunwarren lost a third of its field command. Mael ordered the western gate underpinned before the northern host could test it again. Three weeks ago, that work settled through a chamber absent from every surviving plan. The masons found a crowned skeleton beneath the inward-fallen roof, a blade wound under its ribs. Mael’s officers called the bones plague dead and closed the work before noon. **Efa Rill** had already taken one object from the skeleton’s hand: a gold oath-ring, worn thin at the inner edge.

The ring rests on the Magpie’s table in a lead box. Its private oath-name, old wax flaw, bodily wear, and gate mortar establish one thing: the cauldron can invite Branoc as a person. They say nothing about what he commanded. Gwyne makes that boundary the price of touching it.

## The commission

Gwyne speaks for a narrow, mutually suspicious coalition: descendants of households left outside the western gate, two jurists who distrust the Compact's recital, an abbot who once kept the cauldron's rites, and Lady Aderyn Taran, whose support remains deniable even within the coalition. Payment is old silver, clean passage from the vale, and patrons who will deny the meeting.

The commission has two named successes. **Bring out the cauldron intact, with its oak cradle and nine iron bands, before the fifth bell.** If removal fails, conduct Branoc's Full Asking inside Dunwarren before the Great Muster. Sabotage may save tonight and still leave Branoc unheard, the instrument damaged, and Mael in possession.

The coalition's agreed first question is written on a single strip of vellum:

> What command did you give Edric Taran at the western gate?

The question assumes neither an open gate nor Edric's guilt nor Branoc's murder. Gwyne has accused House Taran for twenty years. She will not purchase vindication by leading the first answer that can test her charge.

## Why tonight

Put three deadlines on the planning table.

1. **The anchor is new.** Branoc has never been summoned. His body and intimate regalia were believed destroyed until the gate chamber opened.
2. **The law is about to harden.** At dawn, the assembled nobles renew the Gate Compact by swearing to preserve the vale “as Branoc commanded at the western gate.” If they hear contrary testimony first, many cannot swear. If they swear first, undoing the new obligations may require rebellion rather than refusal.
3. **The cauldron is about to be spent.** Mael will use the fifth bell to begin a Great Muster. Instead of inviting one dead person through a true anchor, his rite burns the nine silences accumulated in the cauldron's bands and tears military counsel from hundreds of honored dead named on the war roll. Brother Caddoc believes nothing sufficient for a Full Asking will remain afterward.

The coalition offered chapel authentication under Caddoc, Aderyn's founder authority, and an opposed household jurist. Mael demanded sole emergency custody, declared the cauldron a military instrument, moved it into the drowned court, imprisoned Efa's foreman, and searched the masons' houses. Gwyne withdrew the ring from an inquiry Mael alone could close. The heist begins where that bounded lawful offer ended.

## What the crew knows

- Dunwarren's public court, kitchens, roofs, chapel, and underkeep were built in different centuries and do not share one security system.
- The feast creates three plausible approaches: enter among masked guests, enter with the kitchen train, or cross the roofs from the old rook tower.
- The cauldron is black bronze, broad enough to bathe a body, and fixed to an oak carrying cradle by nine iron bands. The bands store the accumulated silences; damaging them may make the Full Asking partial or impossible.
- The proper rite returns one honored dead person in voice, memory, judgment, and will. It does not compel an answer.
- Mael has tested his corrupted method on twelve honored dead with hair-fine needles through still-closed bands. Fragments of their counsel now speak in the House of Borrowed Voices below the chapel; the trials strained the instrument without yet spending a whole silence.
- The Reed Weir opens to the tidal mere at low water. Gwyne has arranged a flat-bottomed boat, but nobody yet knows how to open the inner grate.
- Mael knows a small royal object was removed from the gate chamber because its impression remained in the dead hand's mortar dust. He does not know who holds it, and he may not know it is Branoc's oath-ring.
- Each household delegation has brought a memorial roll for the renewal feast. Mael's clerks are chaining those rolls into the Great Muster's war register, which is why hundreds of named dead and the nobles asked to receive their counsel are present on the same night.

## Cast

- **Gwyne Marr** keeps the question strip beside the lead box. She marks every claim as *establishes*, *suggests*, or *does not prove*, and stops the table whenever anyone treats the ring, Edric's recital, or her own ancestry as Branoc's answer.
- **Efa Rill** places her bandaged wrist beside the ring and reconstructs the collapse with cups and the lead box. She will enter Dunwarren only if the crew puts her name beside the act instead of using her as an invisible source.
- **Tomas Wren**, rain-dark coat still wet, takes an unoffered chair and turns every threat into a transport problem. The Green Larks enter at the third bell whether the crew accepts cooperation or merely leaves them a road.
- **Sib Crowle**, the Magpie’s former keeper, measures courage in tides, invoices, and how far a handcart must be pushed under fire. She will not enter Dunwarren. She will provide tide tables, staff gossip, storage, and a false cooper’s mark, then tell the crew which favor has become a debt.

## The planning table

Put four lists before the players: **approach**, **cover**, **target handling**, and **testimony**. Each commitment buys an advantage and names a risk; leave the rest open for adaptation.

### Approaches

**Masked guests.** Gwyne can provide one genuine invitation and enough household knowledge to improvise the rest. The seal passes a quick inspection, but Dunwarren verifies important guests through a living witness. The crew must recruit, deceive, replace, or avoid that witness.

**Kitchen train.** Sib can place the crew on a late ale or charcoal cart. The kitchens are hot, crowded, and permeable. They also count every worker at the second bell and close the east passage whenever the serving bells are wrong.

**Rook tower.** The ruined tower stands outside the inner wall and is reachable from the tide road. A chain walk once connected it to the keep roof. The chains remain, but their boards were removed after a smuggler's hanging.

Reward coordination across doors. The crew need not enter together.

### Covers and assets

Gwyne and Sib can assemble three strong assets before the first bell. Each additional asset costs time, creates a witness, or advances the rival crew.

- A genuine invitation naming a dead knight whose face is not widely known.
- Two servant tabards and a tally of the current kitchen shifts.
- A coil of tarred rope, roof hooks, and a narrow plank.
- A cooper's handcart reinforced to carry an unusually heavy cask.
- An old copy of the Full Asking rubric, missing the custodial clause and final witness response.
- A shallow impression of the founder's seal, too blurred for close ceremonial use.
- A sleeping draught strong enough for one cup, not a whole table.
- A tide key that fits the outer Reed Weir lock but not the inner grate.
- Efa Rill as a living witness to the gate chamber, at the cost of placing her inside Mael's reach.

### Target and testimony questions

- How many of the nine silences remain whole after Mael's experiments?
- Can the oak cradle be dismantled or substituted without dispersing the stored power?
- What causes the twelve borrowed voices to warn the fort when the cauldron moves?
- Can Brother Caddoc perform a Full Asking away from Dunwarren?
- What makes Branoc's oath-ring an accepted anchor rather than merely old gold?
- Who must witness Branoc for the testimony to matter before the Gate Compact is sworn?
- Does the coalition intend to publish Branoc's whole answer even if it condemns its own cause?

## The bell clock

Before the first bell, the crew can gather assets, recruit one inside contact, or test one approach. At the first bell the White Hart gates open and the kitchen count begins. At the second, Mael announces the Great Muster and presents the Gate Compact for dawn renewal. At the third, the Green Larks enter behind a planned court distraction. At the fourth, Mael descends with the war roll and begins opening the nine band-locks. The low-tide extraction window follows. At the fifth bell, the Great Muster begins unless prevented. Dawn brings the renewal oath.

End planning when the crew commits to at least one route into Dunwarren and names the bearer of the oath-ring. A return before the first bell is free; after the feast begins, each return costs a pressure step and risks Sib, Efa, or Gwyne's coalition.

## The nine hearth marks

Sib's tide book predates the Magpie. Its inside board bears nine hearths joined by a carrying road, the mark once burned onto carts that moved the cauldron from settlement to settlement. Gwyne recognizes the sign. Before House Taran claimed continuous emergency custody, nine communities kept the vessel in rotation. Three still maintain complete keeper lines; two others survive through disputed seals and descendant households. The old trust is therefore neither dead nor able to speak with one uncontested voice.

After Branoc speaks, Gwyne proposes Mere House under Caddoc, one outer-ward keeper, and one representative acceptable to Aderyn. She copied that proposal to Mother Eluned because no threshold accepts *away* as a destination. Eluned refused a royal Asking before a three-hearth council and sent Tomas Wren to recover the vessel first. The Green Larks carry a keeper's knot, not a buyer's purse, and Gwyne's notice gave their rival claim a road into the heist.
