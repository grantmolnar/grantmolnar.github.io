# The Smoke Kitchens

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** heist, service-route, kitchens, inside-contact, great-muster

Soot, tallies, serving bells, and overworked staff govern the keep more precisely than its nobles suspect.

## Opening View

Three hundred plates. Nine copper combs warming by the furnace. Two colors of pipe case where only one should be. Nessa Pike catches the wrong knot on a charcoal carrier’s tabard and closes the tally board with her burned hand. An ash cart waits beside the third-bell door. The kitchens do not stop for strangers; they may, however, put them to work.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

Dunwarren’s kitchens are older than the court they feed. Hearths press against a pre-conquest wall; ovens, cisterns, sculleries, stores, and sleeping shelves fill the remaining space. Too many bodies can hide a stranger. Nessa's count can expose one, because every body, bowl, key, and ladle belongs somewhere.

Three bells govern the work. The first sends bread and meat upward. The second returns platters and releases half the scullions. The third clears the east passage for ash carts before the final service. For seven minutes, no living guard stands between that passage and the obsolete road below the chapel. The opening is not secret. It survives because the people who know it are usually too busy carrying other people’s feast.

## Cast

- **Nessa Pike**, mistress of kitchens, is small, burned along one forearm, and impossible to hurry. She despises Lord Mael's steward for counting workers who sleep in her kitchens as if they were spoons. She will conceal competent help and expose anyone who endangers her staff.
- **Bran Tallow**, cellarer, sells access in small increments: a cord, a key impression, a quiet stair, a missing cask. He is already taking money from the Green Larks and will serve both crews until forced to choose.
- **Melly Reed**, ash girl, knows the undercroft by smell and echo. Her family keeps the memorial song of Captain Alwen Reed. Since Mael tested the borrowed-counsel rite on Alwen, the verse naming the ferrymen he saved has vanished from every written copy, and Melly dreams of a man who remembers the river but not the people in it.
- **Brother Caddoc**, keeper of the Last Word, comes for heated spring water before every bell. He uses it to warm the copper throats in the House of Borrowed Voices. He carries the chapel key openly because nobody in the kitchens wants his work.

## Operating the room

Make every intrusion take a job. Trenchers move toward court. Spits keep a character near Nessa. Casks reach the cellar ramp. Ash opens the east passage and leaves soot for later witnesses.

### The tally

Workers wear braided cords: red for hearths, gray for stores, yellow for service, and black for ash. Nessa's slate records names only for permanent staff; temporary workers are counted by cord and task. A forged cord survives a glance. A crew member who does no plausible work is challenged by another worker before a guard notices.

### The serving bells

The bells are rung from a rope beside the pastry hearth. Nessa controls the first two. Bran Tallow is supposed to ring the third when the steward's board returns. Ringing early clears the east passage but disrupts the feast, brings the steward down, and gives the Green Larks their planned entry signal. Delaying it leaves the route guarded but strands two ash carts in the kitchen, creating concealment and resentment.

### The ash passage

Beyond the east passage, a brick chute drops to an old cart way beneath the chapel. It is steep, greasy, and too narrow for the cauldron's cradle. It is an excellent route for people, tools, the oath-ring, or a dismantled hoist. At the bottom, one branch reaches the Barrow Stair and another drains toward the Reed Weir through a locked iron grate.

## Discoveries and leverage

- A kitchen order requests twelve covered bowls of heated spring water for the House of Borrowed Voices. Each bowl is labeled with a dead captain's name and a question Mael's clerks have forbidden the servants to read.
- Bran's payment token from Tomas Wren proves the Green Larks intend to use the third bell.
- Melly can identify three families whose memorial texts changed after Mael's trials. Their losses are not forgotten facts in living minds; the dead themselves have ceased to carry the reasons behind what they knew, and the surviving records are beginning to follow them.
- The cooper's ramp and flour rollers can move the oak cradle once it reaches this level. Doing so wrecks the last service and makes secrecy impossible.
- The inner Reed Weir grate uses a kitchen spit as its removable winding bar. Nessa keeps it above the cold hearth because the steward repeatedly loses purpose-made tools.
- Scraps from the war roll arrive as kindling. They list captains, engineers, scouts, and healers, confirming that the Great Muster seeks many narrow answers rather than one summoned council.

## Failure and escalation

A mistake brings labor before steel. Nessa demands an explanation and a useful hand; Bran prices the information; the steward detains someone for Vane. Violence, fire, poison, or a clear attack on Mael brings armed guards at once.

Earn Nessa's protection and she can hide one person, delay a count, choose a bell, or place the reinforced cart. She will not carry a sacred burden to settle a noble quarrel. Show her what the Muster consumes, or what Branoc's answer changes for people who still eat tomorrow.

## The east service rise

The ash passage broadens below the ovens into an old service rise worked by a capstan in the cooper's room. Its platform was built for winter barrels and accepts the cauldron's oak runners with two fingers to spare. Four steady hands on the capstan replace most of a carrying team, but the platform stops at kitchen level. From there the target must pass as a broad cooper's load through the staff yard and east postern.

A chalk table beside the capstan gives the cauldron and cradle six burden marks before bearers, evidence, or tools. The figures match the Rookwalk windlass and Reed Weir punt. Nessa can clear the yard with a false grease fire or surround the load with household witnesses. She will stop the lift before she abandons an injured worker.
