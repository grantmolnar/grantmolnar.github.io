# The Barrow Stair

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** heist, threshold, oath-security, custody, underkeep

Three stone mouths record whom the crew calls, what it will ask, and who answers for the cauldron afterward.

## Opening View

“Whom do you call whole?” asks the first stone mouth. The chapel light narrows behind you. “What must be asked first?” says the second. “Who answers after the dead depart?” asks the third. Black wax softens behind their teeth; witness basins wait at every landing. The stair does not record intention. It is already taking down your words.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

The Barrow Stair is the old rite turned into architecture. Three broad landings descend through the burial mound, each with a stone mouth, a witness basin, and a black-wax register behind a funerary panel. One tube carries every declaration upward to the chapel. Another carries it downward to the House of Borrowed Voices. The stair makes entry possible by making intention public.

The stair reads declarations, not hearts. It asks whether an anchor relates to one dead person, whether the first question has exact words, and whether a living keeper names destination and future authority. A careful liar may pass where an honest but shapeless appeal fails. The keepers preferred recorded words, witnesses, and correction to a priest's claim of secret intention.

Mael has fitted an iron warrant tongue over each mouth. His counterseal can substitute a war roll for one person, a strategic subject for one question, and permanent Taran custody for a named keeper. The older questions remain underneath. Restoring them is mechanically simple and politically unmistakable.

## The Landing of Name

The first mouth asks:

> Whom do you call whole?

For a proper Full Asking, the speaker gives the dead person's whole ceremonial name and presents a true anchor over the witness basin. The basin does not authenticate historical claims by itself. It reacts only when the object and name belong together strongly enough for the rite to distinguish a person from a title, legend, or namesake. Branoc's oath-ring warms the basin and makes the wax beneath it take the ring's lifetime flaw. No earlier relic available at Dunwarren could have done this.

The landing admits several imperfect responses:

- **True name and true anchor.** The mouth repeats the name once to the chapel and opens without alarm. The wax preserves the anchor's impression but does not seize the object.
- **Memorial authority.** A keeper may use an abbey roll, funerary token, or family witness to identify a dead person for ordinary rites. That authority opens the underkeep; a whole Full Asking still requires a true anchor.
- **Emergency warrant.** Mael lays the war roll across the basin, stamps the warrant tongue, and names “the honored defenders of the vale.” The old mouth continues trying to ask for one person beneath the iron cover. Anyone listening closely hears the two procedures disagree.
- **A false pairing.** A true old object spoken under the wrong name leaves no impression. The mouth repeats both name and object description through the chapel tube and remains closed.
- **No declaration.** The ash-cart bypass joins below this landing. The roof shaft also skips it. The omission is entered as a blank in the lower registers, which matters only if the crew later attempts a Full Asking or a claim of lawful seizure.

The name landing's hidden service panel contains the warrant tongue's hinge pin. Removing the pin lets the crew restore the old procedure quietly; reversing the tongue makes Mael's own war roll fail when he descends. Smashing the mouth opens the passage permanently, but every name spoken near the stair thereafter pours into the House of Borrowed Voices as undifferentiated sound.

## The Landing of Asking

The second mouth asks:

> What will you ask before you ask anything else?

The landing requires one complete first question. It judges no fairness or wisdom. Soft black wax takes the exact words and the chapel rail repeats them. If invited below, the dead hears that same question and may answer, refuse, correct its assumptions, or insist on another matter.

Gwyne's agreed wording passes cleanly:

> What command did you give Edric Taran at the western gate?

A speaker who says only “We seek the truth” or “We will ask about the gate” is asked again. After the third evasion, the mouth raises its voice until the chapel can hear. A speaker may deliberately register a different question, but the alteration becomes discoverable evidence and may divide the coalition. A question that names facts Branoc could not have witnessed still opens the landing; the failure appears later when Branoc refuses or cannot answer.

The roof lifting shaft opens beside this landing. A crew entering from above can reach the lower stair without answering the first mouth, but it must either cross the Asking landing, crawl through the counterweight gallery, or descend the exposed cradle shaft directly into the drowned court. The landing's wax strip can be removed, replaced, or turned over. Doing so hides the question from later readers but leaves a clean rectangular absence that Brother Caddoc recognizes immediately.

Breaking this mouth has a different consequence from bypassing it. The question already registered reverberates continuously through the chapel and campaign room whenever the cauldron moves. That can create politically useful witnesses, but it makes a covert extraction nearly impossible.

## The Landing of Keeping

The third mouth asks:

> Who answers for the cauldron when the dead have gone?

The response names three things: a living keeper, the place where the cauldron will rest, and the authority under which another Asking may occur. The declaration is provisional while the crew descends. It becomes operative only when the cauldron itself crosses a consecrated threshold. This allows petitioners to inspect the underkeep without automatically taking custody, but it prevents the target from leaving under an anonymous hand.

Possible declarations include:

- **Single custody.** “Gwyne Marr; the Abbey of Nine Silences; under the older compact and the witness of the abandoned households.” The stair records the claim. Whether it proves politically durable depends on the seal, witnesses, and events above.
- **Joint custody.** Two or more keepers may name one destination and one authority. The stair binds their declarations together. At extraction, the cauldron will stop if they give incompatible instructions or one openly repudiates the arrangement.
- **Neutral custody.** The founder's seal and a willing keeper can name the chapel, a neutral abbey, or an assembled household council pending inquiry. Captain Vane regards this as the least destabilizing lawful seizure.
- **House Taran custody.** Mael names himself or his office, Dunwarren, and the Gate Compact. The old mouth accepts the form even while the founder's correction clause remains hidden beneath the warrant tongue.
- **False custody.** The stair cannot know that a speaker intends to sell the cauldron elsewhere. It issues a hollow iron ring rather than a solid oak token when the spoken destination and the bearer’s later action diverge. At the first target threshold, the lie opens one band enough to leak part of its silence.
- **Contested custody.** If rival claimants name different destinations or authorities, the landing opens a side recess called the waiting grave. It does not imprison them. It repeats both claims to the chapel and asks, “Who will answer to whom?” A shared witness set, a temporary neutral destination, or one claimant's withdrawal resolves the contest. Force bypasses the recess but carries the conflict to the cauldron itself.

A crew that bypasses the Keeping landing may still reach the vault. It simply postpones the declaration until the target is moving and the tide or pursuit makes deliberation expensive.

## Intersections and bypasses

Three old service systems cut across the formal descent.

**The ash-cart run** leaves the Smoke Kitchens, slips through a low fire door, and joins between Name and Asking. A person can pass easily; the intact cradle cannot. The run is valuable for tools, witnesses, and divided crews rather than final transport.

**The roof lifting shaft** descends from the Rookwalk windlass beside Asking and continues to the drowned court. It bypasses Name and can bypass the speaking landings entirely if the crew accepts an exposed vertical descent. Raising the cauldron later requires the counterweights to remain intact.

**The counterweight gallery** runs behind all three stone mouths. Its inspection crawl lets a small person remove warrant tongues, wedge registers, cross speaking tubes, or lift one mouth by hand. Opening the full gallery drops a broad maintenance ramp over the stairs. That ramp can carry the cradle toward the chapel, but the falling weights disable the roof windlass and ring every funeral bell above.

Water from the chapel font can be diverted into the witness basins. Cold water softens the wax and makes the mouths whisper rather than project for several minutes. Flooding does not erase a declaration already carried to the chapel tube, and warm spring water from below restores the voices quickly.

## Stair states

Track the stair by posture, not a single open-or-closed state.

**Quiet.** Complete declarations or careful bypasses leave no coordinated alarm. The chapel and borrowed voices possess a record but no reason to treat it as hostile.

**Noted.** An incomplete name, evasive question, missing register, or reversed warrant tongue gives Caddoc or the lower council something specific to investigate. Heat rises only when a living witness connects the anomaly to the crew.

**Contested.** Conflicting custody, a displayed royal anchor, or open challenge to Mael's warrant sends both versions of the claim upward and downward. The House of Borrowed Voices begins issuing divided advice. This is dangerous, but it can also prevent the living guard from receiving one clear order.

**Broken.** Destroyed mouths, dropped counterweights, or a forced maintenance ramp open the route to heavy transport. They also disable one later advantage: break Name and the voices hear every name; break Asking and the registered question repeats whenever the target moves; break Keeping and the cauldron will demand live, witnessed custody at each threshold rather than accept the stair's token; drop the ramp and the roof extraction is lost.

## Immediate play

Offer several workable relations to the stair.

- A crew carrying the ring can register Branoc honestly, preserve the agreed question, and name a provisional neutral keeper. This makes later testimony and seizure easier but announces the heist's true stakes to anyone controlling the tubes.
- A social crew can use Aderyn's seal and Caddoc's witness to contest Mael's emergency warrant openly.
- A covert crew can bypass Name, register a harmless inspection question, and postpone custody, accepting that Branoc's Asking will need to be regularized below.
- A technical crew can reverse the warrant tongues, cross the chapel and campaign tubes, or prepare the maintenance ramp for extraction.
- A divided crew can send one group through the ash run, one down the formal stair, and one through the roof shaft, creating enough incompatible evidence to confuse the borrowed council.
- A desperate crew can break the landings and convert the underkeep from a ritual problem into a loud transport problem without making the adventure automatically fail.

Its pressure lies in the verbs the crew chooses: state, record, postpone, falsify, or publicly contest what it intends for the dead and the instrument that calls them.

## Possession after Keeping

The black-wax correction leaves distinguish possession from custody. A porter, thief, guard, or victor may physically hold the cradle without becoming the keeper named at the Landing of Keeping. The declaration remains attached to the cauldron until the keeper freely corrects it, dies, yields before witnesses, or is proven to have made a materially false claim.

Taking rope or boat grants no ritual authority. A false destination is not harmless either: when action contradicts the record, the bands close to future Asking until witnesses amend it. The discrepancy blocks use; it does not compel the keeper.
