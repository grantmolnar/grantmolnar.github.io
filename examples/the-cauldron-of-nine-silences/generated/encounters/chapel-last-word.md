# The Chapel of the Last Word

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** heist, ritual, records, full-asking, underkeep-route

Caddoc's chapel can authenticate Branoc, expose the wounded rite, and open the underkeep—if the crew can answer for custody.

## Opening View

“Last,” says the darkness beneath the font. Brother Caddoc sets down the copper throat he has been warming and covers the chapel key with his palm. Nine black candles remain unlit. Twelve names on the memorial wall still keep their golden deeds, though ferrymen, children, rivals, and promises have been scraped away. Below, the hidden voice waits for another word.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

The Chapel of the Last Word stands where household Dunwarren ends and the older underkeep begins. Nine communities built it when no lord kept the cauldron beyond one winter. Benches crowd a low room around an unraised altar. A whisper at the water reaches every witness; no keeper occupies the space between a living question and the dead.

Each black candle marks a completed communal silence: one long watch in which the hearths asked nothing of the dead and spoke every remembered name before dawn. Those silences gathered in the nine iron bands below. Together they can restore one person’s faculties without spending them. Mael’s Great Muster would separate the bands and turn that patient relation into fuel.

The injury is already legible. Twelve memorials retain their deeds and lose their reasons. Captain Alwen Reed still held the crossing for three hours, but the wall no longer names her sister’s children in the last boat. Queen Mairwen still opened the granary, but the winter children have vanished from the sentence. Caddoc has copied the stones, locked the copies away, and watched the same absences appear in ink.

## Caddoc at the candles

**Brother Caddoc** has copied the disappearing names onto fresh sheets and set them beneath the black candles. Mael calls him a household chaplain—an instrument needs maintenance, not conscience—but Caddoc corrects every ritual verb: *invite*, never *raise*; *depart*, never *dismiss*. He helped with the first trials to save the northern bridges and continued after seeing the cost because an untrained operator might ruin the cauldron at once. He names that defense and does not ask absolution for it.

Mael's hair-fine **trial needles** entered inspection holes beneath the closed Hearth, Hand, and Reply clasps. Each carried one memorial name and one narrow question, drawing a useful thread into a copper mask before the nine silences could gather around a person. Three bands are strained and twelve dead diminished; no whole silence was spent. The Great Muster opens whole bands, combs hundreds of names through water, and consumes complete faculties.

Caddoc knows Branoc has never been summoned. He also knows the newly found ring would change what is possible. He has not seen it. If shown the oath-name, the interior flaw, and the black mortar, he can authenticate it provisionally against the chapel's royal wax casts. He will then face a choice he has postponed: obey Mael's emergency warrant, or preserve the rite he was sworn to keep.

Caddoc begins cautious rather than hostile. He asks where the ring came from, who will witness Branoc, what exact question will be asked, and who will keep the cauldron afterward. Threats can force access but not competent ritual help. Evidence that Gwyne intends to accept an unwelcome answer earns more from him than outrage on behalf of the dead.

## The Full Asking

The complete rubric is distributed across the room because no single keeper was meant to control it.

1. **Anchor.** A personal object must have been carried, worn, made, or freely given by the dead person and must still bear their identity. Public regalia and copied seals are insufficient by themselves.
2. **Name.** The dead person's whole living name and one name by which they accepted honor are spoken over the water. Branoc's private oath-name is engraved inside the recovered ring.
3. **Question.** The first question is stated before the invitation. It must identify what the living genuinely seek to know. Clever ambiguity weakens the rite rather than trapping the dead.
4. **Silence.** The witnesses keep silence while the nine bands answer in sequence. Each witness must be free to leave; coerced silence produces only an echo.
5. **Consent.** The dead arrives as a person, not a record. They may answer, refuse, challenge the premise, or demand that another question be asked first.
6. **Custody.** When the Asking ends, a living keeper names where the cauldron will rest and under what authority it may next be used.

A Full Asking gives no corpse back. Water holds a presence as complete as memory permits: voice at the surface, face in depth, gestures without visible hands. The dead remembers through death, understands ordinary speech, and recognizes known people and objects. The rite ends when the dead withdraws or the ninth candle fails.

All nine silences make that fullness possible. A damaged band leaves literal, brief fragments stripped of judgment—the condition Mael exploits. Breaking the instrument stops the Muster by spending the very capacity the crew came to preserve.

## Mael's corrupted rite

The Great Muster substitutes a **war roll** for a true anchor and one broad military question for an invitation to a person. Copper combs scrape names from the roll into the water while the bands are opened separately. Each opened band forces a narrow answer from many dead at once. The answers can be accurate and still be ruinous because the dead lose the attachment that gave each answer proportion.

Caddoc's test records show the pattern:

- A ferryman remembers exactly where the channel is shallow but no longer remembers that refugees were aboard his boat.
- A queen remembers the granary inventory but not the children for whom she broke the seal.
- A captain remembers which gate can be held longest but not whom the gate was meant to save.

The stripped reasons also begin disappearing from memorial text, family dreams, and later rites. The dead are not annihilated, but they are diminished into usable expertise. Caddoc believes a complete Great Muster will damage hundreds and consume every silence sufficient for Branoc's Full Asking.

## Principal elements

**The Nine-Silence Register.** A narrow book records when each communal watch was completed, which households participated, and how strongly each iron band presently answers. All nine remain intact, though three show strain from Mael's trials.

**The petition of inquiry.** Gwyne, two jurists, and Abbot Teilo offered to present any authentic Branoc anchor in the chapel under Caddoc, Lady Aderyn's founder authority, and an opposed household witness. Mael's written refusal rejects that three-witness inspection, demands that the anchor enter his sole emergency keeping, and postpones any hearing until after the Gate Compact is renewed.

**The royal wax casts.** Six old impressions preserve the flaw and inner lettering of Branoc's oath-ring. They can authenticate Gwyne's ring without relying on her testimony alone.

**The black font.** The font conceals the upper latch of the Barrow Stair. It opens with the founder's seal, Caddoc's keeper phrase, or enough water to float the internal catch.

**The witness rail.** A copper tube runs from the vault threshold to the chapel rail. In proper use, witnesses hear the dead through it. Mael has reversed the tube so the House of Borrowed Voices can relay alarms upward.

**The preparation chest.** It contains nine band keys, witness veils, the copper name combs used in the corrupted rite, and blank custody rings. Removing the combs can delay the Great Muster. Removing or changing the keys can force Mael to open bands destructively.

## Ways to use the chapel

- Recruit Caddoc to perform Branoc's Full Asking elsewhere.
- Authenticate the oath-ring before risking public exposure.
- Take the complete ritual text and custody clause.
- Open the Barrow Stair quietly through the font.
- Reverse, block, or listen through the witness tube.
- Steal the band keys or copper combs to delay the Great Muster.
- Carry the petition, test records, or changing memorial copies into the White Hart Court.
- Invite Aderyn, Vane, or hesitant nobles here for a controlled confrontation.

## Failure and escalation

If discovered, Caddoc first attempts to contain the matter as a ritual dispute. He may lock the chapel, summon Aderyn, or demand that Captain Vane witness the ring before Mael sees it. If the crew damages memorials, threatens witnesses, or handles the ring carelessly, he turns against them. If Mael learns the ring is authentic, he orders the chapel sealed and advances the Great Muster to the earliest moment the bands can be opened.

Use the chapel as route, archive, recruitment scene, or temporary tribunal. Branoc's answer lies below; here the crew learns the grammar and names the cost.

## Custody before House Taran

Behind the lowest memorial board, Caddoc keeps the cauldron's carrying calendar. Nine hearth marks repeat in a circuit: Mere, Ash, Rowan, Gorse, Reed, Stone, Bell, Road, and Lamp. No keeper held the vessel for more than one winter, and questions concerning war, inheritance, or a keeper's own house required three disinterested hearths to witness the first wording. House Taran's emergency writ interrupted the circuit fifty-three years ago. The record contains renewals of the emergency but no release by the surviving hearths.

The same chest defines black-water testimony. A complete Asking produces three exact leaves marked by the bands present and the dead person's free departure. One leaf with two named witnesses stays an oath founded on a contrary recital. Three establish a durable record; living judgment still decides consequence.
