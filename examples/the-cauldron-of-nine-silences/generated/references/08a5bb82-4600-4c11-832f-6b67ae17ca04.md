## Summary

Outer-ward advocate and coalition broker who frames the heist, protects the exact question to Branoc, and refuses to let ancestry, possession, or desired outcomes outrun the surviving evidence.

## Reference Material

## What the record can bear

Gwyne Marr's mother's line descends from an outer-ward household stripped of land after the western gate closed. She has challenged House Taran's recital for twenty years, but refuses to use ancestry as a substitute for proof. She knows household rolls, oath forms, service roads, and the limits of each surviving source. The coalition's first question therefore remains exact and non-leading: *What command did you give Edric Taran at the western gate?*

## The coalition and the exact question

Gwyne speaks for descendants of the excluded households, two jurists, an abbot formerly connected to the cauldron's rites, and Lady Aderyn's deniable support. They seek to preserve the cauldron, conduct Branoc's first Full Asking, and preserve the whole answer even if it defeats their charge. Their earlier offer was a chapel inspection under Brother Caddoc, Aderyn's founder authority, and an opposed household jurist; Gwyne withdrew the anchor when Mael required sole emergency custody.

## What she cannot prove

Gwyne can commission agents, provide payment, records, routes, witnesses, and a publication commitment. Efa Rill's account, the oath-name, wax flaw, wear, and gate mortar let her establish an evidentiary chain for the ring without proving Branoc's command. Gwyne commands no present defense and cannot serve as military surety, settle the Nine Hearth Trust's claim, or turn escape into lawful custody without a named keeper and destination. Her present possession, allies, and negotiated terms remain matters for play.
