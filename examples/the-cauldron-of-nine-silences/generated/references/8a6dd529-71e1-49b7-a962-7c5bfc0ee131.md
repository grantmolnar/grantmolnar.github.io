# Captain Rhod Vane

## Summary

Captain of Dunwarren's living guard, whose bounded orders, sacred-object recovery articles, and concern for a defensible morning can separate civil protection from Mael's disputed ritual command.

## Reference Material

## Orders bounded by bell and place

Captain Rhod Vane commands Dunwarren's living guard through explicit orders bounded by place, object, bell, and lawful purpose. He carries the living-guard keys and deliberately carries no key to the Cauldron Vault. When borrowed voices disagree, household posts hold position and seek his judgment rather than treating every dead answer as command.

## Preserve the water

Vane's standing articles for sacred household objects forbid arrows, fire, hooked blades, and mounted pursuit wherever cauldron water might be spilled. Guards are to hold routes, preserve the water, take bearers living, and seize wheels, ropes, doors, or the named keeper before striking the vessel. Mael can countermand those articles, but a public countermand exposes the distance between preserving the institution and recovering it at any cost.

## What he can secure

Vane can secure doors and roads, withdraw or redirect patrols, preserve witnesses, recognize a bounded replacement defense, and serve as military surety for a Night of Hearing. He does not authenticate anchors, keep the rite, supply founder authority, own the cauldron, or settle constitutional title. His present allegiance, guard posture, available keys, and willingness to oppose a particular order remain matters for play.
