# Party Design: The Unsworn Hand

The Unsworn Hand is not a company. Gwyne Marr assembles five people because every institution with a claim on the Cauldron of Nine Silences has already found a reason not to act. Rhoswen Pell gives the crew its name as a dry statement of law: they have accepted a commission, but none has sworn the Gate Compact, entered Taran service, or recognized a permanent keeper. Below Dunwarren, the phrase acquires weight. A hand may carry, question, restrain, release, record, or strike. Touching the vessel proves only that someone can bear it; the heist asks what could make that bearing just.

The characters are system-neutral and deliberately incomplete. Each brings a practical competence, a credible relationship, a kind of authority, and a temptation to mistake one part of the problem for the whole. No one is the appointed leader or conscience. Their disagreements should alter routes, witnesses, timing, and custody rather than remain speeches between encounters.

## Rhoswen Pell

**Function:** Former clerk of the Gate Compact, household genealogist, and social infiltrator.

**What she brings:** Rhoswen recognizes oath forms, witness chains, household colors, precedence disputes, emergency clauses, and the small procedural courtesies by which a guarded court decides someone belongs. She carries a compact renewal from twelve years ago with her own marginal notes, three blank witness slips, sealing wax, a mourning veil, and the coalition's exact question written in her hand.

**Prior connection:** Rhoswen copied the last renewal of the Gate Compact. She noticed that the recital attributed only one sentence to Branoc while later clauses treated Edric Taran's interpretation as though it were the king's full command. Her superior ordered the distinction removed from the clean copy. Rhoswen obeyed, then kept the rejected draft. She lost her clerkship the following winter after asking why the outer-ward household rolls had been excluded from the oath bench.

**Opening position:** Branoc must be asked before dawn, and every word of the answer must be preserved before opposed witnesses. Rhoswen wants the cauldron removed because testimony obtained under Mael's custody can be abridged, challenged, or followed immediately by the Great Muster.

**Private fear:** She fears that procedure is the only form of courage she possesses. She can always name another witness, seal, hearing, or review that ought to precede action. She also fears learning that the official recital is substantially true and that her life's grievance rests on an omission less dramatic than treason.

**Red line:** No one may improve Branoc's answer, conceal an unwelcome clause, or ask a leading first question.

**Temptation:** Rhoswen may convert the heist into a courtroom before the crew controls the room. The founder's seal, Caddoc's rite, and one sympathetic officer can make a public hearing look safer than it is.

**How she argues:** She separates testimony from consequence. Branoc may establish what he commanded; he cannot decide present succession, compensation, or military authority. When another character says the truth will settle the matter, she asks which matter and under whose jurisdiction.

**Table voice:** Dry, exact, and patient until someone paraphrases a document she has read. Under pressure she quotes the dangerous clause rather than naming the principle behind it. “That is not what the Compact says. It is what Edric's recital invites you to forget it says.”

**Growth demanded:** Rhoswen must use law as a tool of action rather than a reason to postpone it. Her best ending accepts that the first lawful step may be an unlawful seizure whose legitimacy depends on exact testimony, divided custody, and a credible morning-after settlement.

## Brother Aneirin Cade

**Function:** Dismissed funerary cantor, ritual reader, and advocate for the consent of the honored dead.

**What he brings:** Aneirin can authenticate personal anchors, distinguish memorial names from intimate names, read the nine band registers, conduct most of the Full Asking under Caddoc's authority, and recognize when a ritual preserves a person or merely extracts information. He carries a clapperless handbell, black thread, clean spring salt, a small book of departure responses, and a letter from Brother Caddoc refusing Mael's military amendments.

**Prior connection:** Aneirin trained in the Chapel of the Last Word. He left after arguing that the emergency custody clause did not authorize name needles, even when the bands remained closed. Caddoc agreed privately and refused publicly to protect the chapel. Aneirin has not decided whether that was cowardice or prudent stewardship.

**Opening position:** The Great Muster must be stopped, the nine silences preserved, and Branoc invited as a person rather than deployed as evidence. Aneirin accepts theft because Mael has made consent impossible inside ordinary custody, but he does not accept that the coalition's need gives it unlimited access to the dead king.

**Private fear:** He fears that his reverence for consent is easiest when the dead cannot contradict him. Branoc may refuse, demand a different witness, or rebuke the coalition. Aneirin also fears discovering that he still wants Caddoc's approval more than he wants the rite protected.

**Red line:** No band may be committed, no question may be substituted after consent, and no presence may be held after voluntary departure.

**Temptation:** Aneirin may preserve ritual purity while the political opportunity dies. He can always argue that one more authority, witness, or custodial correction is required before asking.

**How he argues:** He asks what the dead person has been told, what they consented to, and how they may stop. He is willing to accept risk to the living but not to hide that risk inside sacred language.

**Table voice:** Formal in ritual, plain elsewhere. He dislikes euphemism. “You are not opening the Bar. You are removing his ability to refuse us.” Anger makes him quieter and more grammatical.

**Growth demanded:** Aneirin must distinguish consent from institutional permission. The proper rite requires Branoc's willingness, but reaching a place where that willingness can matter may require disobedience, divided custody, and imperfect witnesses.

## Huw Rill

**Function:** Mason, heavy rigger, excavation witness by family connection, and practical target handler.

**What he brings:** Huw reads settlement, hidden chambers, old carrying roads, counterweights, runner grooves, wall repairs, and the difference between an object secured against theft and one secured against its own weight. He carries wedges, a short maul, two lifting slings, chalk, an iron dog key blank, and a carpenter's rule marked with the burden lengths of local carts.

**Prior connection:** Huw is Efa Rill's elder brother. He worked the western-gate stabilization but was above ground when Efa entered the exposed chamber. He knows which stones moved, which foreman vanished into Mael's custody, and how quickly the official repair concealed the opening. He did not know Efa had taken Branoc's ring until Gwyne opened the lead box.

**Opening position:** The ring and cauldron must leave Dunwarren before Mael can seize one and spend the other. Huw distrusts plans that depend on a lord, dead king, or legal hearing behaving well after the crew reveals itself.

**Private fear:** He fears that the heist will make Efa the visible criminal while jurists and nobles keep clean hands. He is prepared to destroy the excavation records, ring, or cauldron rather than let her be tortured for a chain of proof. That protective instinct can erase the very evidence she risked herself to preserve.

**Red line:** No worker, servant, rigger, or mason may be left carrying the apparent blame for a noble operation. The crew must name who ordered each public act.

**Temptation:** Huw may choose a technically certain destruction over a politically uncertain rescue. Every band, chain, grate, and wall presents him with a way to make the problem impossible for everyone.

**How he argues:** He asks what weighs how much, who has to stand under it, and who will be named when it falls. He distrusts plans that use “we” for the decision and someone else's name for the consequence.

**Table voice:** Concrete, impatient, and occasionally funny in the manner of someone who assumes absurdity is a structural defect. “The cauldron is six marks. Your principles may ride in the second boat.”

**Growth demanded:** Huw must accept that preservation can be more exacting than destruction. His best work is not breaking the target or hiding Efa's act, but making a route on which the vessel, witnesses, workers, and blame all travel visibly.

## Gareth Bryn

**Function:** Former Taran levy quartermaster, field-security reader, and advocate for a living defense after the Compact fails.

**What he brings:** Gareth knows Dunwarren's guard habits, supply assumptions, post orders, barrier doctrine, muster rolls, military bluff, and the practical difference between Captain Vane's standing articles and Mael's black override. He carries an old levy badge, a quartermaster's tally knife, northern road reports, and the names of three valley captains who would answer Vane but not Mael.

**Prior connection:** Gareth served under Marshal Hew Pale during a winter incursion and under Vane during a grain riot. He respects both men. He left service after Mael used ward hostages to compel a levy extension. Gareth believes the northern danger is real, the Great Muster is an atrocity, and the coalition has not yet taken seriously what happens if Mael loses emergency authority before another command structure exists.

**Opening position:** Stop the Great Muster and preserve Branoc's testimony, but do not leave the vale undefended at dawn. Gareth supports Aderyn's replacement covenant: Vane holds the household guard, Hew chairs a living intelligence council, valley levies require named consent, and cauldron custody is decided separately.

**Private fear:** He fears that Mael is right about necessity even when wrong about everything else. If the crew succeeds and the northern road breaks, every principled distinction may look like vanity beside the dead.

**Red line:** The heist may not cripple living defenses, burn stores, poison kitchens, collapse occupied routes, or leave every officer without a command they can lawfully obey.

**Temptation:** Gareth may bargain away the cauldron's future or Branoc's full answer for a bounded military use that appears survivable. He is especially vulnerable to Hew's argument that one silence spent now may save hundreds of living voices.

**How he argues:** He converts political claims into orders that must be issued tomorrow morning. If the Compact is stayed, who opens the granaries? Who commands the north road? Who can dismiss the levy? His questions are valuable and can become a demand that justice wait for perfect administration.

**Table voice:** Measured, courteous, and openly conditional. “I will help end this command. I will not pretend the road stops needing one.” Under stress he begins issuing orders before noticing he has been granted no authority.

**Growth demanded:** Gareth must learn that replacement authority can be provisional, divided, and reviewable without becoming unreal. His best ending secures Vane and Hew for a one-season living defense while refusing every attempt to turn that necessity back into hereditary emergency power.

## Sian Mere

**Function:** Tide runner, former Green Lark, surviving Nine Hearth descendant, and contested custodian.

**What she brings:** Sian knows the tide road, reed lanes, flat-bottomed boats, covert signals, Lark habits, carrying knots, old hearth marks, and the places where a sacred custody claim becomes an excuse for control. She carries a nine-loop green cord with one loop cut, an outer-weir key, a narrow reed knife, and a Mere House carrying token she has never returned.

**Prior connection:** Sian ran with Tomas Wren for three years. She left after Mother Eluned delayed an urgent Asking until three hearths could convene; the dying fisher whose mother was sought did not live to hear the answer. Sian still believes the cauldron belongs in communal circulation rather than royal custody. She no longer believes senior keepers should confuse deliberation with moral innocence.

**Opening position:** Branoc's first Asking may proceed because the anchor, question, and dawn deadline are exceptional, but the vessel must leave royal custody afterward. Sian will carry it toward Mere House only under divided keys, a public record of the completed Asking, and a limit on any second question.

**Private fear:** She fears that she left the Green Larks because she wanted authority rather than speed. The Barrow Stair may accept her as keeper. Tomas may accept her compromise. The ease with which others begin asking her to decide what happens next is precisely the danger she claims to oppose.

**Red line:** The cauldron may not become House Taran property, coalition spoils, a private antiquity, or a machine whose questions are set by one lineage.

**Temptation:** Sian may convert temporary physical control into a permanent communal claim before the surviving hearths, Caddoc, Aderyn, or the affected households can answer.

**How she argues:** She asks who carried the vessel before the current claimant, who may carry it next, and whether custody has a road out of itself. She distrusts every phrase that turns a route into an owner.

**Table voice:** Spare, amused, and edged by old familiarity when speaking to Tomas. “You did not bring a claim. You brought a second boat. Let us be honest about which one we need first.”

**Growth demanded:** Sian must accept recorded temporary custody without pretending it resolves ownership. Her best ending names herself keeper long enough to move the cauldron, then divides the next decision among Mere House, chapel, founder, and affected households.

## Crew fault lines

The heist should make these disagreements operational rather than decorative.

- **Ask now or move first:** Rhoswen and Aneirin favor a witnessed Asking once the complete rite is available. Huw and Sian prefer securing a route before revealing the ring. Gareth's answer depends on whether a replacement defense has been assembled.
- **Lawful respite or covert removal:** Rhoswen sees the Night of Hearing as a durable opening. Huw sees it as time for Mael to isolate witnesses. Sian accepts it only if custody changes immediately.
- **Who keeps the vessel:** Sian has the strongest living relation to the old trust; Aneirin has ritual competence; Rhoswen has public-record credibility; none has a complete claim.
- **What Mael is owed:** Gareth believes Mael is owed a real answer about northern defense, not obedience. Huw believes Mael used necessity to choose every convenient victim. Rhoswen insists that military need and historical testimony be adjudicated separately.
- **What Branoc can settle:** Rhoswen and Aneirin sharply limit the dead king's jurisdiction. Gareth is tempted to ask him about present defense. Sian fears a royal answer will dominate communal custody. Huw wants the one promised question and no dynastic séance.

## Demonstration route

The canonical playthrough divides the Unsworn Hand at entry and recombines it before the underkeep.

- Rhoswen and Gareth enter through the White Hart Court using a genuine invitation and Aderyn's covert witness.
- Aneirin, Huw, and Sian enter through the Smoke Kitchens with tabards and tally colors.
- Huw and Sian prepare the Rookwalk windlass as a false heavy-extraction route and emergency fallback.
- The whole crew reaches the Chapel of the Last Word, then divides briefly so Rhoswen and Gareth can secure Aderyn's seal and replacement covenant in the Widow's Solar.
- The crew declares Branoc, the exact question, and Sian's temporary divided custody on the Barrow Stair.
- In the House of Borrowed Voices, they restore Captain Alwen Reed's missing reason and cross command lines to preserve the weir route while sending pursuit toward the Rookwalk.
- In the vault, they conduct Branoc's first Full Asking before opposed witnesses, preserve all three leaves, release the custody dogs without opening a band, and still remove the cauldron because the Great Muster remains possible after testimony.
- At the Reed Weir, they bargain with Tomas for the second skiff, accept temporary Mere House custody under divided keys, ground the punt in the late tide, recover by the old runner road, and separate vessel, ring, testimony, and public witnesses so no single pursuer can reverse every success.

The route is an example, not an intended solution. It demonstrates that the adventure can sustain social entry, service entry, vertical preparation, ritual procedure, political coalition-building, security manipulation, immediate testimony, intact extraction, rival negotiation, and a consequential partial failure without requiring one character to dominate every stage.
