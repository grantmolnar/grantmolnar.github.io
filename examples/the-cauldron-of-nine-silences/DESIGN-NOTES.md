# Design Notes: The Cauldron of Nine Silences

## Format commitment

This is a short heist, not a compressed dungeon crawl. Ten encounters are enough if each encounter changes access, cover, timing, testimony, target handling, custody, or aftermath. The adventure should produce planning, adaptation, and extraction rather than a linear sequence of guarded rooms.

## The legendary register

The target draws on British and Welsh legendary cauldrons associated with the dead while remaining original and system-neutral. Its proper function is not resurrection and not ordinary corpse interrogation. The Full Asking permits one honored dead person to return in voice, memory, judgment, and will through a true personal anchor and a witnessed invitation.

The cauldron's power accumulates through nine communal silences kept across generations. The nine iron bands hold those silences in relation. They are not nine interchangeable charges. Together they allow a dead person to be present as a person rather than reduced to literal answers.

## Causal spine

The adventure depends on eight propositions that must remain distinct and causally joined.

1. **Branoc has never been summoned.** His body and intimate regalia were lost when Dunwarren nearly fell. No prior rite could identify him as a person rather than a royal legend.
2. **The present military danger opened the past.** Red Fen broke the levy screen five weeks ago. Emergency underpinning of the western gate then exposed Branoc's sealed command chamber three weeks before play.
3. **The anchor is newly available.** Efa Rill removed Branoc's oath-ring before Mael closed the excavation. The ring's oath-name, flaw, bodily wear, and provenance make the first Full Asking possible.
4. **The coalition attempted a bounded lawful hearing.** Gwyne offered chapel authentication under Caddoc, founder authority, and an opposed household witness. Mael demanded sole emergency custody of the anchor and searched the excavation workers before any hearing.
5. **The question has present force.** The Gate Compact and House Taran's emergency authority rest on Edric Taran's claim that Branoc ordered the western gate shut.
6. **The law is about to harden.** At dawn, the nobles will renew the compact. Testimony before the oath can prevent or delay it; testimony afterward confronts newly sworn obligations.
7. **The two deadlines are one ceremony.** The households brought memorial rolls for the renewal's recitation of service. Mael has chained those rolls into the Great Muster's war register and will present its answer to the same nobles before asking them to swear.
8. **The instrument is about to be spent.** At the fifth bell the Great Muster will burn the nine silences. Branoc's first Full Asking will then be incomplete or impossible for generations.

No clue or scene should imply that Branoc has answered before. Mael's twelve prior experiments involved other honored dead and used hair-fine trial needles through inspection holes in still-closed bands. They strained Hearth, Hand, and Reply and produced the House of Borrowed Voices without committing a whole communal silence.

## Why theft rather than sabotage

The adventure should make intact theft rational without making sabotage foolish.

- Destroying the cauldron prevents both coercive extraction and every future Full Asking.
- Damaging or removing bands diminishes Branoc's possible recognition, memory, judgment, affection, self-command, temporal understanding, refusal, reciprocal speech, or voluntary departure.
- Stealing the war roll, combs, or keys can delay tonight's Muster but leaves the target and future rebuilding capacity in Mael's hands.
- Exposure can halt Mael if the court accepts the evidence, but does not by itself secure the cauldron or ring.
- Intact theft or witnessed seizure prevents the Muster, preserves Branoc's first Asking, and forces an explicit custody decision.
- Immediate Full Asking in the vault is a valid alternative when extraction becomes impossible.

The players may still choose sabotage or destruction. Those choices should be costly resolutions, not errors the adventure refuses to recognize.

## Heist principles

1. **Security is divided.** The public court verifies social belonging, kitchens verify labor, roofs expose bodies to witnesses, chapel rites verify anchors and questions, the stair verifies name and custody, and the borrowed voices predict aims. No master key defeats every layer.
2. **Planning buys position, not certainty.** Assets and covers should make opening moves strong while leaving later discoveries meaningful.
3. **Failure changes posture.** Suspicion creates witnesses, escorts, closed routes, accelerated schedules, and rival opportunities before it creates a general fight.
4. **The target is an operation.** Reaching the cauldron is not equivalent to stealing it. Bands, cradle, speaking channels, custody, tide, witnesses, the ring, and the chosen resolution must be handled.
5. **The owner has reasons.** Mael is correct that the military emergency is real and that old expertise can save living people. His wrong is treating the dead as stores of useful facts and constitutional history as a tool to be renewed before it can be examined.
6. **Inquiry is not guaranteed clean vindication.** Gwyne does not know Branoc’s answer. The fixed truth condemns Edric’s omissions while confirming that Branoc did order the great leaves barred under real military pressure. Gwyne’s credibility depends on preserving the exact mixed answer rather than turning it into a simpler accusation.
7. **Rivals are mobile consequences.** The Green Larks should exploit neglected routes and pressure, not appear only as a final combat encounter.

## Topology

The Crooked Magpie is the sole start. The White Hart Court, Smoke Kitchens, and Rookwalk form the initial approach triangle. Each can reveal and support the others, allowing split entry and recovery from a failed cover.

The Chapel of the Last Word is the principal ritual and evidentiary hinge. The optional Widow's Solar provides authority, excavation evidence, the original compact, and a political replacement plan but is not required for structural progress.

The Barrow Stair, House of Borrowed Voices, and Cauldron Vault form the underkeep chain, with cross-support from the roof, kitchens, chapel, and solar. The Reed Weir is the prepared end encounter, supported by multiple internal routes and its own tide and custody procedure.

Route edges represent actionable knowledge and access, not literal one-way corridors. Several clues establish physical routes, but others establish the social, ritual, or legal means to use a known route safely.

## Target ontology

The following are maintained unless current evidence exposes a contradiction:

- A Full Asking invites one honored dead person through a true personal anchor.
- The dead returns in voice, memory, judgment, and will, not as a reconstructed corpse.
- The dead may answer, refuse, challenge the question, accuse, forgive, or ask questions in return.
- The full rite requires anchor, whole name, honest first question, willing witnesses, consent, and named custody afterward.
- The nine accumulated silences preserve recognition, ordered memory, judgment, affection, self-command, temporal understanding, refusal, reciprocal speech, and voluntary departure. Damage produces a named diminishment rather than a generic loss.
- Mael's Great Muster extracts narrow answers from many honored dead without inviting them as persons.
- The extracted facts remain accurate while the dead lose the reasons and attachments that governed their use.
- The cauldron and nine-banded oak cradle are one operating object; four separate custody dogs govern movement without requiring a ritual band to open.
- Movement wakes the twelve borrowed voices through copper channels; they alert living guards through prediction rather than direct supernatural pursuit.
- Consecrated thresholds require a living custodian to name themselves, a destination, and the authority for future use.
- Branoc has never been summoned.

## The salient question

The coalition's agreed first question is:

> What command did you give Edric Taran at the western gate?

The question is intentionally narrower than “Who betrayed you?” or “Was House Taran founded on a lie?” It asks for an event Branoc personally witnessed and avoids presupposing guilt. The subsequent political consequences arise from the answer rather than being encoded into the question.

The underlying answer is now fixed, but it is not available through ordinary clues. Branoc ordered Edric to bar the great leaves against the riders, keep the wicket and river stair open until the outer ward was within, and abandon Dunwarren if necessary to save the people. Edric obeyed the first clause, violated the protections and fallback, and later preserved only the gate-closing command in his recital.

This truth does not make the inquiry a disguised confirmation exercise. Gwyne and the coalition do not know it, and the answer remains politically mixed: Branoc did order the great leaves barred, Edric faced a real military collapse, and Branoc died before learning what Edric actually did. The Full Asking establishes exact command and governing priority without magically adjudicating every later motive, legal consequence, or current defense problem.

## Faction positions

**Lord Mael Taran:** Preserve the vale, complete the Great Muster, renew the Gate Compact, and retain authority over the cauldron. He may accept a bounded alternative if it produces credible defense and does not transfer the artifact to a foreign patron.

**Gwyne Marr:** Preserve the cauldron, ask Branoc the agreed question, and publish the answer without abridgment. Her family history gives her motive but not certainty.

**Lady Aderyn Taran:** Obtain truthful testimony without leaving the vale ungoverned. She will support witnessed Asking and neutral custody if a replacement defense arrangement survives the night.

**Captain Rhod Vane:** Preserve living soldiers and civil order. He can turn against Mael if the borrowed counsel is shown to be contextless, operationally dangerous, or politically designed rather than merely old and sacred.

**Brother Caddoc:** Preserve the Full Asking, authenticate the ring, and stop further diminishment of the dead. He is complicit in the trials, frightened of untrained handling, and potentially decisive.

**Marshal Hew Pale:** Obtain military counsel before the northern threat arrives. He can be persuaded by evidence that stripped answers make bad strategy, but not by ritual purity alone.

**The Green Larks:** Return the cauldron intact to Mother Eluned and the surviving Nine Hearth Trust. Gwyne notified Mere House because the thresholds require a credible keeper and destination; Eluned rejected an immediate royal Asking before three hearths could convene and dispatched Tomas independently. Tomas cooperates on movement and preservation but treats the completed Asking as both urgent evidence and a dangerous royal precedent. His claim is serious, incomplete, and negotiable through the exact question, dawn deadline, Branoc’s consent, and shared custody.

**The borrowed voices:** Not whole persons and not a unified faction. Each is a severed answer with an expertise, a missing reason, and a possible route toward contradiction, recognition, or release.

## Record and contradiction decisions

The Barrow Stair is a record-and-accountability system, not a universal lie detector. It can relate a true anchor to a name, require complete declarations, preserve exact words, and expose later contradiction. It cannot know private intention or decide which claimant deserves authority. This keeps deception possible while making names, questions, and custody consequential.

Mael's warrant tongues are physical and constitutional alterations laid over the older mouths. They explain how he can use a person-specific funerary institution for a mass military category without implying that the old rite naturally accepted the Great Muster.

The House of Borrowed Voices predicts through visible inputs: a relief map, five objective markers, route sensors, and twelve command lines to living posts. It does not see everything supernaturally. Players can therefore understand and manipulate the model rather than guess at an invisible GM response.

The twelve voices remain retained answers, not trapped whole souls. Trial needles stole narrow threads while the bands remained closed; this distinguishes three strained bands from a committed silence. A recovered name and reason can let an answer qualify or retract itself and return toward silence, but this does not constitute a Full Asking, prove complete restoration of the dead, or replenish spent cauldron power.

The voices' weakness is not factual incompetence. Their counsel is often correct within its domain. Their weakness is that stripped purposes cannot rank grain against people, stone against houses, speed against the wounded, or victory against those beneath it. Contradiction therefore arises from real competing goods rather than arbitrary magical confusion.

## Extraction decisions

The final act tracks four separable objectives: vessel, anchor, testimony, and keeper. A pursuer can recover one without automatically erasing the others. This prevents a single failed boat or chase action from nullifying the investigation, rite, and custody work that preceded it.

The Green Larks' patron is Mother Eluned of the Mere House, acting for the surviving Nine Hearth Trust. The Trust predates House Taran's fifty-three years of renewed emergency custody, but only three complete keeper lines remain. Its claim is historically serious without becoming a mechanically correct answer to permanent ownership. Eluned's refusal to conduct an immediate royal Asking protects the cauldron from political instrumentalization while risking renewal of the lie the crew hopes to test.

Physical possession and ritual custody remain distinct. A captor can hold vessel, keys, or boat while the prior keeper remains recorded. A false declaration can move the cauldron but closes future Asking until corrected. Vane's recovery is bounded by sacred-object articles: preserve water, take bearers alive, and control routes rather than strike the vessel.

Three heavy exits remain viable. The Reed Weir trades tide and split loads for privacy; the kitchen rise trades speed for disguise and staff witnesses; the Rookwalk trades secrecy for visible proof. Chapel and court provide a political resolution when witnessed seizure or Night of Hearing becomes more valuable than escape.

One authenticated witness leaf and two living witnesses can stay the dawn oath until noon even if Mael recaptures the cauldron. This is a delay and hearing right, not magical adjudication.

## Demonstration decisions

The Unsworn Hand demonstrates the adventure through split social and service entry, roof preparation used as a false objective rather than the final route, complete ritual authentication, optional founder politics, a provisional divided keeper declaration, recovered context for two borrowed answers, Branoc's first Full Asking, intact target movement, rival-custody bargaining, and a late-tide grounding recovered through the old runner road.

The optional Widow's Solar is deliberately costly. Its founder seal and replacement defense covenant make the public outcome durable, but the delay pushes extraction from the middle black lane into sucking water. The encounter offers leverage at a real cost rather than a free completion bonus.

The canonical ending separates the four extraction objectives. Vessel and ring reach Mere House; exact testimony travels in three directions; Sian remains temporary keeper under divided limits; and Vane and Hew operate a one-season living defense. Mael remains lord but cannot renew hereditary emergency authority through Branoc's abridged recital.

## Voice decisions

The final pass treats the adventure as one argument about whether memory may be possessed. Water, names, burdens, and thresholds now recur as material signatures rather than decorative motifs. Questions always imply an asker, witness, refusal, record, and keeper; custody always implies a destination, term, and road out of itself.

Principal speakers were differentiated by what they measure. Gwyne measures proof; Efa sequence and stone; Mael necessity and inherited service; Aderyn the morning after; Vane lawful orders; Hew seasons and road capacity; Caddoc ritual verbs; Tomas transport and bargaining; Branoc the difference between sheltering people and preserving walls. The borrowed voices remain accurate but lose proportion because each lacks the reason that once limited its tactic.


## Second-look coherence decisions

The coherence pass joined four previously adjacent facts into one chronology.

- Red Fen caused the emergency gate works that exposed Branoc's chamber; the discovery no longer arrives through unexplained subsidence beside an unrelated military crisis.
- The Great Muster and Gate Compact renewal are one public operation. Household memorial rolls supplied for the traditional recitation become Mael's war register, and the extracted answer is to be presented before the same households swear at dawn.
- Gwyne's coalition offered a concrete neutral inspection rather than asking Mael to trust an unseen ring. Mael's demand for sole custody, coupled with arrests and searches, makes withholding the anchor and turning to theft a defensible last resort.
- Gwyne notified Mere House because extraction required a keeper and destination. Mother Eluned's refusal and independent Green Lark recovery attempt therefore arise from the coalition's custody problem instead of from rival omniscience.

The pass preserves Mael's legitimate military case. Red Fen killed or scattered a third of Hew's field command, the northern road still requires living orders at dawn, and the Great Muster could produce tactically useful facts. The abuse lies in severing counsel from purpose and using that answer to renew authority before the underlying constitutional testimony can be heard.

## Construction boundary

All ten authored regions, forty-one revelations, one hundred fifty clues, operating aids, named crew, full playthrough, canonical journal, final consistency audit, and voice audit are complete. The second-look density pass preserved all one hundred thirty-two original clues and added eighteen independent non-route discoveries. Identifiers, destination unlocks, route topology, journal history, and demonstrated outcomes remain fixed.

## Second-look Voice I decisions

The source-level pass trusts Dunwarren's materials and procedures to carry more of the adventure's argument. Wax and witnesses distinguish document from person; bells and tallies expose institutional labor; black water records exact speech; speaking tubes show how stripped counsel becomes command; burden marks make custody physical. Explanatory paragraphs after those operations were shortened or removed.

The revised source uses direct GM imperatives where table procedure matters and sharper working vocabularies for the principal cast. It preserves the ten player-facing openings, all one hundred fifty clues, all forty-one revelations, the thirty-six route edges, exact edge connectivity four, and the byte-identical 149-event journal. Voice II completes the cross-file reconciliation.


## Second-look Voice II decisions

The final pass keeps the authoritative source nearly fixed. Nine editorial intrusions were tightened: planning closes on a declared route and ring bearer; failed court witness places the crew under Vane's eye without pausing to reassure the GM; the Rookwalk fall states its consequence directly; Aderyn's value is named positively; memorial authority and bounded Muster costs are stated without a hidden-correct-answer aside; and the public lifting route and partial-testimony outcome stop grading themselves.

Current operating aids use encounter terminology and completed tense. The playthrough preserves its chronology, decisions, and ending. No lead, revelation, route, opening, identifier, journal event, or demonstrated outcome changed during the final prose reconciliation.
