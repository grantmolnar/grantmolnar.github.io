# Full Playthrough: The Unsworn Hand

This playthrough follows one road through *The Cauldron of Nine Silences*. The crew chooses well, spends too long making the morning survivable, and grounds the vessel because time will not forgive the quality of their intentions. They enter by court and kitchen, prepare a roof escape they never use, invite Branoc for the first time, carry the cauldron intact, bargain with rival keepers, and preserve a living defense without letting the old Compact borrow the dead king’s authority again.

The route spans three table sessions. The canonical `play-state.example.json` preserves the same decisions as a 149-event append-only schema-version-6 journal; the prose below interprets those events but does not replace them.

The Unsworn Hand consists of **Rhoswen Pell**, **Brother Aneirin Cade**, **Huw Rill**, **Gareth Bryn**, and **Sian Mere**.

## Starting commitments

At the Crooked Magpie, the Unsworn Hand chooses three assets:

1. the genuine invitation naming the dead knight Sir Celyn Vaud;
2. two kitchen tabards with current tally colors;
3. roof hooks, tarred rope, and the narrow plank.

They decline the reinforced handcart. Huw judges that a false cooper's cart will not pass the underkeep and that the oak cradle already has runners. The decision leaves them dependent on old lifting gear or outside boat hands for the final load.

Brother Aneirin carries Branoc's oath-ring in a waxed packet sewn beneath his left sleeve. Rhoswen carries the exact first question. Sian carries the outer-weir key. Gareth memorizes Aderyn's replacement defense proposal from Gwyne's notes. Huw marks the burden of each character's gear and makes everyone surrender anything they cannot climb with.

The crew agrees on four constraints:

- Branoc's first question will not be altered.
- No band will be committed to the Great Muster.
- Any testimony will be copied whole before interpretation.
- If the cauldron moves, its keeper, destination, and authority will be stated aloud rather than improvised after capture.

They do not agree who should be keeper. Sian refuses to accept the role before seeing whether the Nine Hearth marks remain valid. Aneirin refuses because ritual competence is not custody. Rhoswen says the question can wait until the Barrow Stair forces it.

## Session 1 — Masks, ash, and the old chapel

### The Crooked Magpie

Gwyne begins with the road by which the ring reached the table. Red Fen forced Mael to underpin the western gate; the repair opened Branoc's chamber; Efa took the ring before the excavation was sealed. She then separates what the object proves from what it permits. The workmanship, mortar, private oath-name, bodily wear, and excavation chain make Branoc a plausible identity. None of those facts proves what he commanded. Aneirin confirms that the ring is a true anchor because its intimate name and long bodily use can call a person rather than a public legend.

Rhoswen reads the coalition's question aloud. Gareth objects that Branoc may answer the historical issue without solving the northern emergency. Gwyne agrees. She is not asking the dead king to govern the present. Gareth adds the first concrete term to the crew's plan: before they expose Mael, they will seek a living command arrangement for the next morning.

Sian recognizes the nine-hearth mark inside Sib Crowle's tide book and the cut of Tomas Wren's green cord. Gwyne admits that she notified Mere House because the cauldron cannot cross a sacred threshold toward an unnamed destination. Mother Eluned rejected an immediate royal Asking before three hearths could convene and sent Tomas anyway. Sian tells the others that the Green Larks are not ordinary thieves. They will prefer a live custody dispute over a broken vessel, but they will take control of transport if the crew offers no destination beyond “away.”

The entry plan divides the crew. Rhoswen and Gareth will enter the White Hart Court. Gareth will wear Sir Celyn's mask and mantle; Rhoswen will pose as his mourning niece and witness clerk. Aneirin, Huw, and Sian will enter with the late charcoal train through the Smoke Kitchens. The two groups will converge in the chapel after the second bell. Huw and Sian will then prepare the Rookwalk windlass as an alternate extraction route and a false objective for the borrowed voices.

### The White Hart Court

The public court is crowded enough for disguise and orderly enough to punish a bad story. Sir Celyn's invitation is genuine, but the knight died abroad eleven years ago. The gate clerk asks for the living household witness named on the seal.

Rhoswen does not fabricate one. She asks the clerk to summon Lady Aderyn's chamber witness and cites the Compact provision allowing a mourning household to enter under founder sponsorship. The clerk interprets the request as aristocratic confidence and sends for **Maraith Fen**, Aderyn's senior attendant.

Maraith recognizes Rhoswen and understands the danger immediately. She does not affirm that Gareth is Sir Celyn. She says, truthfully, “Lady Aderyn admits this mourning household under her witness.” The distinction passes the gate and creates a debt. Rhoswen and Gareth now know Aderyn is willing to spend visible authority before she has heard Branoc.

Inside, Mael rehearses the dawn recital before household benches. He quotes Branoc's supposed command as a single sentence: bar the western gate and preserve Dunwarren. Rhoswen notices that the herald's copy omits the river-stair notation preserved in her rejected draft. Gareth studies Vane's court guards. Their barriers face outward toward panic, not inward toward the nobles. Vane expects disorder and has ordered restraint.

At the second bell, Mael has each household's memorial roll fastened into a single chained register. Names brought for the renewal's recitation of service become the Great Muster's war roll before their own descendants. He frames the cauldron as accumulated ancestral service owed to the living. Marshal Hew does not applaud. Gareth approaches him under cover of presenting Sir Celyn's old levy respects. He asks whether the northern road can be held by living scouts, engineers, and valley captains if the Muster is delayed. Hew says it can be held for a season, perhaps two, but only if someone gives Vane authority to issue orders at dawn. He does not promise to oppose Mael. He does provide three names and a grain figure that later make Aderyn's replacement covenant credible.

The court scene produces useful access but also reveals the first cost of public infiltration: Mael's herald remembers Rhoswen's face. The crew can no longer expect its testimony to appear anonymously.

### The Smoke Kitchens

Aneirin, Huw, and Sian enter beneath wet charcoal sacks. Nessa Pike checks the tally colors, notices that one tabard is tied in last month's pattern, and closes the counting board before the junior steward sees it. She knows Sib's mark and asks no broad questions.

The kitchens show the Great Muster as logistics rather than doctrine. Nine name combs are warming beside the scullery furnace. Copper pipes arrive in mismatched cases. A war-roll clerk demands salt, lampblack, and hundreds of memorial tags. Nessa explains that the apparatus was assembled from chapel, armory, laundry, and siege stores because no proper ritual workshop would build it whole.

Huw identifies three delay points: exchange two comb sleeves, wet the lampblack, or send the wrong pipe case below. The crew does not sabotage yet. Aneirin warns that a malformed apparatus could commit a band unpredictably rather than simply fail. They instead switch two empty packing labels so Mael's attendants will discover a mismatch during setup and lose several minutes verifying every component.

Sian finds the knot-board preserving Captain Alwen Reed's missing ferry sentence: **No boat is last while someone waits on the bank.** She copies it onto the back of a tally chip. Nessa tells her the Reed Weir post has orders to close behind the last military movement. The old sentence may force the borrowed command to recognize civilian or witness passage.

At the third serving bell, the east passage empties exactly as promised. The kitchen group reaches the old ash corridor. Before descending, Huw and Sian climb an abandoned service ladder to the lower end of the Rookwalk.

### The Rookwalk

Rain has made the roof chains slick and the old lifting tower loud. Huw uses the narrow plank to bridge the missing boards and reaches the windlass. The cradle's old lifting hook still fits the roof shaft, but moving the cauldron this way would expose it above the whole inner court.

They do not prepare a full extraction. Instead, Huw reeves the tarred rope through the windlass, sets the hook where one hard pull will drop it into the shaft, and leaves a chalk burden mark visible from the House of Borrowed Voices' relief map. Sian alters the rook-feeding shutter so the birds will rise from the north roof at the fourth bell.

The preparation creates three future options:

- a genuine heavy lift if the weir fails;
- a visible claim that could turn covert theft into witnessed seizure;
- a false objective that may draw predictive security away from the Reed Weir.

Sian sees a Green Lark signal on the outer tower: two quick hooded flashes, then one long. Tomas has entered earlier than Gwyne expected because the crew took a fourth preparatory action after the first bell. The Larks are now inside Dunwarren.

### The Chapel of the Last Word

The two groups converge in the chapel while the feast above reaches its loudest course. Brother Caddoc initially refuses to open the underkeep. He has already lost control of the cauldron once and believes another unauthorised transfer may destroy the chapel's remaining claim.

Aneirin gives him the ring without giving him the question. Caddoc authenticates the private oath-name, wax flaw, mortar, and wear. He says aloud what no one in the coalition has yet heard from a serving keeper: **Branoc has never been summoned.** The chapel has no record of a valid anchor, attempted invitation, refusal, or incomplete presence.

Rhoswen then shows the exact question. Caddoc reads it twice and returns it unchanged. He explains the complete Full Asking, including the custodial clause Gwyne's copy lacked. The dead person must be told who keeps the anchor, who preserves the answer, and by what authority the vessel remains after departure.

The crew learns the nine bands as faculties rather than charges. Aneirin is particularly disturbed by the Band of the Reply. Without it, Branoc could answer but could not ask what became of the people for whom he issued the command. Gareth understands why Mael's extracted military counsel is dangerous even when tactically correct.

Caddoc also produces the rejected lawful petition. Gwyne offered to present the ring in the chapel under Caddoc, Aderyn's founder authority, and an opposed household jurist. Mael did not deny that a Full Asking might be proper. He rejected those witnesses, declared the cauldron a military instrument, and demanded that the ring first enter his sole custody while officers searched the excavation workers. The crew now has documentary proof that theft follows a concrete failed lawful offer.

When the chapel tubes report confused orders from below, Caddoc realizes the Green Larks or the crew have already disturbed the command network. He agrees to open the Barrow Stair on two conditions: no band is committed, and one witness leaf returns to the chapel if Branoc speaks.

### The Widow's Solar

Rhoswen and Gareth spend their remaining margin on the optional political encounter. Huw calls it the first bad decision of the heist: every minute spent improving the aftermath narrows the extraction tide.

Aderyn admits Rhoswen and Gareth through a linen press behind the mourning gallery. She has the founder's seal, the sealed excavation packet, the earliest surviving Compact, and a draft replacement defense covenant. The old Compact does not grant House Taran unrestricted hereditary emergency power. It grants temporary custody of roads and stores while a named danger persists and requires renewed household witness.

Aderyn will not denounce Mael before hearing Branoc. She will authorize a Night of Hearing and a fourth custody key. Gareth tests her replacement covenant against Hew's figures. They revise it together:

- Vane commands the household guard until the noon hearing.
- Hew chairs a living council of scouts, engineers, granary keepers, and valley captains.
- Levies continue only under named valley consent.
- Noble wards return to their households.
- Cauldron custody is separated from military command.
- The covenant expires after one season unless renewed publicly.

Aderyn presses the founder's seal into a blank custody tongue and agrees to descend as a witness if called. Rhoswen leaves one black witness slip in the solar so Aderyn's involvement cannot later be reduced to rumor.

The first session ends with the whole crew reunited in the chapel. The fourth bell is approaching. They have a true anchor, exact question, complete rite, founder authority, replacement defense, prepared roof diversion, one restored reason, and no agreed keeper.

## Session 2 — The three declarations and the borrowed council

### The Barrow Stair

The Landing of Name accepts Branoc's intimate oath-name, public name, ring, and chapel authentication. The emergency warrant tongue tries to substitute the war roll for one dead person. Aneirin uses Caddoc's key to retract the tongue rather than breaking it.

The Landing of Asking repeats the exact question in Rhoswen's voice. The stair does not judge the question. It records it. Gareth starts to add a second question about the northern road, stops, and lets the strip pass unaltered.

The Landing of Keeping forces the unresolved issue. The crew cannot carry the cauldron across a consecrated threshold while promising to decide custody later.

Sian proposes the declaration:

> Sian Mere keeps the vessel until dawn, carrying it to Mere House under the witness of Rhoswen Pell and Brother Caddoc, by the surviving hearth line, the founder's temporary seal, and the consent of the dead person asked. No second question proceeds before three hearths and the chapel answer.

Rhoswen objects that Branoc has not yet consented. Sian changes the clause from “by the consent” to “subject to the consent.” Aneirin adds that if Branoc refuses the Asking, his refusal must travel with the same force as an answer. Gareth adds that the vessel remains available for a noon public custody hearing rather than vanishing into Mere House.

The final declaration is provisional, divided, and formally complete. The stair records Sian as keeper, Mere House as destination, and surviving hearth line plus founder seal as present authority. It does not declare that the Nine Hearth Trust owns the cauldron. The distinction becomes decisive at the Reed Weir.

### The House of Borrowed Voices

The House is already active. Huw's roof mark, the switched kitchen cases, and the movement beneath the chapel have produced three objective markers. The masks disagree about whether the crew seeks the Rookwalk, the Great Muster apparatus, or the cauldron itself. Living posts are holding for Vane.

The crew identifies the Low Water Mask as Captain Alwen Reed. Sian speaks Alwen's whole name and the missing ferry sentence from the kitchen knot-board. Huw turns the return cock inward. The mask pauses, repeats its earlier order to close the weir after the last defensible boat, and then corrects itself:

> No boat is last while someone waits on the bank. Hold the inner road for named bearers and witnesses.

The corrected answer goes to the Reed Weir post. It does not order the guards to aid theft. It prevents them from treating the first military withdrawal as the final legitimate passage.

Rhoswen then restores Seren Bell-Bearer's safe-conduct principle using the White Hart witness roll and Caddoc's chapel bell: **A lie may be seized; the mouth under safe-conduct may not.** Seren's mask begins protecting declared witnesses while continuing to challenge false documents.

Huw and Gareth physically cross two command collars. The Rookwalk receives a seizure order intended for the cauldron route. The Reed Weir receives a verification order intended for the witness route. Sian pulls the prepared roof rope from below, waking the rooks and dropping the lifting hook into the shaft. Three masks agree that the heavy target is moving north. Two others insist that the anchor is below the drowned court. The six posts hold for Vane.

The crew could release more retained answers. Aneirin wants to. Huw points to the fourth-bell sand. They settle for recording all twelve names and carrying the two restored reasons forward. The remaining ten answers are still injured and still in service when the crew leaves.

A red recovery strip reveals Vane's standing order: hold the route, take bearers living, preserve the water, do not strike the cradle. Gareth recognizes that Vane has already bounded Mael's response. They decide that if Vane reaches the vault, they will present the founder covenant and ask him to witness rather than fight.

The second session ends as the crew opens the drowned-court door. Above them the fourth bell sounds. The first mismatch in Mael's apparatus has delayed the war-roll procession, but not for long.

## Session 3 — The king's answer and the carrying road

### The Cauldron Vault

The cauldron stands full of dark spring water. All nine bands remain closed. Hearth, Hand, and Reply show hairline strain from the name needles, but none has been committed. The four custody dogs still hold the oak cradle.

The crew has time either to begin moving the vessel or to conduct the Full Asking before Mael arrives. Huw argues for releasing the dogs and putting the cradle on its runners first. Rhoswen argues that movement wakes every remaining voice and may force the Asking under pursuit. Aneirin says the ring, witnesses, and nine intact silences may never again be present together. Sian, now recorded keeper, decides to ask first and move immediately afterward.

Caddoc opens the chapel spring line. Aderyn arrives with the founder's fourth key. Vane enters moments later with six guards, following contradictory orders from the House. He does not arrest the crew because Rhoswen displays Aderyn's seal, Gareth recites the replacement covenant, and Caddoc invokes the Night of Hearing clause. Vane orders his guards to hold every door and admits that he has no lawful article authorizing the Great Muster over Caddoc's refusal and founder objection.

Mael and Marshal Hew reach the outer passage while the rite begins. Mael demands the ring. Aderyn answers that the founder's seal has placed the vessel under one-night joint custody. Hew reads Gareth's living-defense figures and remains outside the water line as an opposed military witness.

The rite proceeds.

Aneirin names the nine faculties. Rhoswen states the first question exactly. Sian names herself keeper, Mere House as temporary destination, Caddoc and Rhoswen as witnesses, and the noon hearing as the limit of her authority. Branoc's ring rests above the spring cup.

The water first asks who will preserve an answer that displeases them. Rhoswen places her rejected Compact draft beside the cup and promises three whole copies. Sian promises that Mere House will receive the answer rather than a digest. Aderyn promises that House Taran's founder authority will authenticate the leaves even if they condemn Edric. Mael refuses to promise publication. Vane promises that no guard under his command will seize a declared witness leaf before noon.

The water then asks who commands the northern road when the old oath is stayed. Gareth gives the replacement covenant in concrete terms. Hew corrects two grain figures and adds the eastern scouts. Branoc's reflection appears only after living people have answered the present problem without asking him to rule it.

Rhoswen asks:

> What command did you give Edric Taran at the western gate?

Branoc answers:

> Bar the great leaves against the riders. Keep the wicket and the river stair until the outer ward is within. If the wall cannot hold, leave Dunwarren and save the people. A fort is a vessel; the realm is those it shelters.

He explains that the clauses were one command, not competing orders. The great leaves were to stop mounted attackers while smaller passages admitted people. Dunwarren's preservation was subordinate to its purpose. He knew Edric repeated the first clause. He died before knowing which later clauses Edric obeyed, concealed, or could no longer carry out.

Mael asks whether Branoc would risk the vale now by denying counsel from its dead defenders. Branoc refuses to answer because that was not the promised first inquiry and because no dead king can inspect the present road from memory. He asks Hew whether living scouts have been sent. Hew answers yes. Branoc tells him to believe them before ancestors who cannot see the weather.

Gareth asks no second question.

Branoc then asks what became of the outer ward. Rhoswen gives the dispossession record without claiming every later grievance as Edric's direct act. Sian names three surviving hearth communities. Huw names Efa and the masons who opened the chamber. Branoc asks that his answer not be used to make their descendants instruments of a new royal claim.

He departs voluntarily. The ring dries. All nine silences return to equilibrium. Three black-water leaves form, each containing the complete exchange, including Mael's challenge, Hew's figures, Branoc's uncertainty, and his conditions.

The testimony stays the dawn oath, but it does not make the cauldron safe. Mael can still attempt the Great Muster once the Night of Hearing expires or if he discredits the joint custody. The crew proceeds with removal.

Caddoc, Vane, Aderyn, and the vault steward each control one custody key. The steward refuses. Huw uses the founder-seal key in the fourth dog and Vane orders the steward's key taken under the Night of Hearing. No ritual band is opened. The oak cradle settles onto its old runners.

Huw and two guards begin the load. Vane does not help the crew escape, but he refuses Mael's order to strike the cradle. The conflict shifts from hidden infiltration to contested custody.

### The Reed Weir

The corrected Low Water Mask has kept the inner runner road open for named bearers and witnesses. The Rookwalk diversion has drawn half the recovery force north. The crew reaches the weir during the final ten minutes of the black lane rather than the middle twenty. Their delay in the Widow's Solar now matters.

Tomas Wren waits with Mabli Quill, three other Green Larks, and the second skiff. He recognizes Sian's recorded keeper phrase through the threshold echo and does not attempt to seize the cradle. He presents Mother Eluned's terms: Mere House destination, no further royal Asking, and all witness leaves carried to the Trust.

Sian refuses the last demand. Branoc consented to public preservation, and one leaf must remain with the chapel while another reaches the opposed household bench. She offers instead:

- vessel and ring to Mere House until the noon hearing;
- Sian as current keeper, Caddoc as ritual key, and an Aderyn-appointed household witness as the other two custodial voices;
- no second question until three hearths convene;
- the first Asking recognized as complete and not subject to retroactive veto;
- one exact leaf to Mere House, one to the chapel, and one to the founder bench.

Tomas says Eluned will call it theft wrapped in procedure. Sian answers that Eluned may say so at noon while the cauldron remains intact. Tomas accepts because the alternative is Mael's custody and because Branoc explicitly asked that the answer not become a new royal instrument.

The load is divided. The cauldron and cradle take six burden marks. Sian, Huw, and Mabli take the remaining three in the punt. Sian carries the ring. Aneirin and Caddoc take the second skiff with the Mere House witness leaf and rite kit. Rhoswen, Gareth, and Aderyn remain on the inner road with the founder leaf, intending to return through the chapel and court. Vane's guards hold between them and Mael's household men.

The punt enters sucking water too late. Its right runner grounds on the old stone road. The tide has made the decision.

Huw orders the punt lightened but refuses to separate the cauldron from the cradle. Aneirin transfers to the reed bank. Mabli takes a tow line into the skiff. Sian names the correction aloud: the route has changed from water carriage to the old runner road, but Mere House and noon hearing remain the destination and limit. The threshold accepts the correction.

Green Lark hands expose the buried road stones. Huw wedges the grounded runner, Tomas's skiff takes strain, and the punt crosses the last shelf as the black lane closes. The recovery costs time, leaves visible tracks, and makes the Nine Hearth Trust indispensable. All nine bands remain seated; Branoc's testimony survives.

Mael arrives at the inner grate and changes priorities. Since Branoc has spoken, he orders the ring and witness leaves seized. Vane refuses the order as contrary to the Night of Hearing and his standing article protecting living bearers and the cradle. Hew supports Vane because the replacement defense is already operating. Mael still commands loyal household retainers, but attacking the boats would require openly breaking founder custody, funerary law, and his own captain's written recovery limits before opposed witnesses.

Rhoswen gives Vane the founder leaf long enough to authenticate it, then carries it upward with Aderyn. Gareth remains with Vane to issue the first northern-road orders under the temporary covenant. The crew separates, and the testimony enters the political arena before Mael can describe the night as simple theft.

The punt and skiff reach the reeds. Sian, Huw, Aneirin, Caddoc, Tomas, and Mabli carry the cauldron toward Mere House. Rhoswen and Aderyn return to the White Hart Court with one leaf. The chapel retains the second through Caddoc's sealed copy. Mere House receives the third with the vessel.

## Demonstrated end state

### Vessel

The Cauldron of Nine Silences leaves Dunwarren intact in its oak cradle. All nine bands remain whole. Hearth, Hand, and Reply remain strained by Mael's earlier name-needle trials, but no communal silence has been spent. The vessel enters temporary Mere House custody under Sian, Caddoc, and a founder-appointed witness pending the noon hearing.

### Anchor

Branoc's oath-ring travels with Sian and the vessel. Its chain of discovery and authentication is preserved by Efa's testimony, the chapel register, Aderyn's excavation packet, and the complete witness leaves.

### Testimony

Branoc's full exchange survives in three black-water leaves:

- one travels with the cauldron to Mere House;
- one is sealed in the Chapel of the Last Word;
- one reaches the White Hart Court under Aderyn and Rhoswen.

The dawn Gate Compact cannot renew unchanged. The nobles stay the oath until a noon hearing.

### Keeper

Sian remains the recorded temporary keeper. Her declaration does not establish permanent Trust ownership. It establishes a destination, authority, witnesses, limits, and a correction path. The noon hearing must decide future custody without pretending that physical possession has already answered the question.

### Living defense

Vane commands the household guard under a one-season provisional covenant. Hew chairs a living council using scouts, engineers, granary keepers, and valley captains. The first northern reports are imperfect but current. No counsel is extracted from the honored dead.

### House Taran

Mael remains lord of Dunwarren and retains substantial support. He loses the claim that the Gate Compact reproduces Branoc's whole command. His future authority must rest on present consent, military competence, or force rather than inherited emergency recital. Aderyn's public role prevents him from reducing the heist to foreign theft.

### The outer ward

Branoc's answer does not automatically restore land, assign every later crime to Edric, or establish a new heir. It does require correction of the memorial record and a living inquiry into the closing of the wicket and river stair. Efa and the excavation workers enter founder protection as witnesses rather than anonymous criminals.

### The borrowed voices

Captain Alwen Reed's retained answer is released from active command after her reason is restored. Seren Bell-Bearer's answer is qualified and no longer permits declared witnesses to be treated as unprotected couriers. Ten other retained answers remain in the House. Their names and missing reasons are now copied into chapel and founder records, creating a future obligation rather than a falsely complete rescue.

### The Nine Hearth Trust

The Trust recovers practical custody but not an uncontested monopoly. Tomas's assistance is publicly recorded. Eluned must answer why three-hearth deliberation should govern future use and how emergency questions can be heard without turning urgency into permanent exemption. Sian must answer the corresponding question: when does temporary keeping end?

## What the playthrough demonstrates

- The heist has a reason to prefer theft over sabotage without making sabotage irrational.
- Branoc's newly recovered anchor, dawn oath, and fifth-bell Muster form one coherent deadline.
- The exact historical question matters, but the dead king does not govern the present.
- Social, service, roof, ritual, political, underkeep, vault, and extraction play all contribute distinct leverage.
- The optional Widow's Solar is valuable but costs enough time to produce a real tide consequence.
- The borrowed voices can be opposed through recovered reasons and contradictory priorities rather than combat or universal shutdown.
- Full success does not require every NPC to join the crew or every institution to become good.
- Failure at the weir changes who is necessary, who gains leverage, and how visible the escape becomes without erasing prior achievements.
- Vessel, anchor, testimony, keeper, and living defense remain separable outcomes.
- The final settlement is provisional, divided, and administratively inconvenient. That inconvenience is evidence that no one actor has converted emergency possession into unquestioned rule.
## Final image

Dawn reaches the mere before it reaches Dunwarren. In the reeds, the cauldron rests on runner stones with all nine bands dark and seated. Sian’s hand remains on the keeper’s ring, not because the vessel is hers, but because someone must still answer for the next mile. Behind her, three boats and three witness leaves take different roads. Above them, the western gate stands open for repairs, and for the first time its scaffolding looks less like a bandage than an admission that stone can be corrected without pretending it was never broken.
