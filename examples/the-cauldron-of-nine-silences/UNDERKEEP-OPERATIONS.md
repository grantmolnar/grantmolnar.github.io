# Underkeep Operations: The Cauldron of Nine Silences

## Purpose

This document is a compact table aid for the Barrow Stair and House of Borrowed Voices. The authoritative prose remains in `adventure.json`; this aid collects the declarations, bypasses, command network, twelve retained answers, and likely responses in one place.

The underkeep should not feel like a succession of password doors. It is an old funerary government that Mael has overlaid with emergency machinery. Players can comply with it, contest it, falsify it, bypass it, or turn its procedures into public evidence.

## Barrow Stair quick reference

| Landing | Old question | What it actually tests | Quiet ways through | Consequence of force |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Name | Whom do you call whole? | Whether a name and anchor or memorial authority identify one dead person | Branoc's ring, memorial roll, family witness, reversed warrant tongue, ash or roof bypass | Every nearby name reaches the borrowed council without distinction |
| Asking | What will you ask before anything else? | Whether one exact first question is placed on record | Speak the question, replace or turn the wax strip, counterweight crawl, direct roof descent | The registered question repeats whenever the cauldron moves |
| Keeping | Who answers for the cauldron when the dead have gone? | Whether a keeper, destination, and future authority are stated | Single, joint, or neutral custody; founder's seal; postpone by bypass | Each threshold demands live witnessed custody rather than accepting a token |

### What the stair can know

The stair can compare a name to an anchor, determine whether a declaration has the required parts, and preserve exact words. It cannot read private intention or decide which political authority is morally legitimate.

A formally complete lie may therefore pass. The protection lies in the register, witnesses, and the cauldron's later response when a declared custodian acts against the stated destination or authority.

### Name responses

- **True name and anchor:** quiet opening; the witness wax takes the anchor's identifying flaw.
- **Memorial authority only:** passage to the underkeep, but no complete Full Asking until a true anchor is supplied.
- **Mael's emergency warrant:** the iron warrant tongue suppresses the demand for one person and accepts the war roll as a military category.
- **False pairing:** no impression; the name and object description are repeated to the chapel.
- **Bypass:** a blank remains in the register and must be regularized later if the crew wants a proper Asking or lawful seizure.

### Asking responses

- **Exact question:** recorded and repeated once to the chapel witness rail.
- **Evasion:** the mouth asks again; after three evasions it projects the exchange loudly.
- **Altered question:** accepted if complete, but the alteration is preserved and may divide the coalition.
- **Impossible question:** accepted; Branoc may later refuse or explain that he cannot answer.
- **Removed wax:** hides the words but leaves an absence Caddoc recognizes.

### Keeping responses

- **Single custody:** one keeper, destination, and authority receive a solid oak token.
- **Joint custody:** accepted if all claimants give one destination and authority; disagreement later stops the target.
- **Neutral custody:** founder's seal plus a willing keeper can place the cauldron with the chapel, a neutral abbey, or a household council pending inquiry.
- **False custody:** a hollow iron ring is issued; acting against the declaration at a threshold leaks part of one silence.
- **Contested custody:** the waiting grave repeats both claims and asks who will answer to whom. Shared witnesses, temporary neutrality, or withdrawal resolves it.
- **Bypass:** custody is postponed until the cauldron is moving.

## Barrow bypass map

**Ash-cart run:** joins between Name and Asking. It moves people, tools, and small evidence, not the intact cradle.

**Roof lifting shaft:** joins beside Asking and continues to the drowned court. It can bypass all mouths, but the descent is exposed and later extraction depends on preserving the counterweights.

**Counterweight gallery:** runs behind all three mouths. It allows register tampering, tube crossing, warrant-tongue reversal, and a broad maintenance ramp. Dropping the ramp opens heavy transport to the chapel but disables the roof windlass and rings the funeral bells.

**Chapel-font water:** makes the mouths whisper and softens register wax briefly. Words already carried through the witness tube remain.

## Stair posture

| State | Trigger | Immediate effect | Opportunity created |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quiet | Complete declarations or careful bypass | Record exists without hostile interpretation | Covert passage and later legal use |
| Noted | Missing register, incomplete claim, reversed warrant tongue | Caddoc or the lower council investigates one anomaly | A specific but containable problem |
| Contested | Rival custody, displayed ring, open challenge to Mael | Both versions travel upward and downward | Borrowed voices divide; witnesses may become useful |
| Broken | Destroyed mouths, dropped ramp, forced counterweights | Heavy route opens; one later advantage is lost | Fast extraction at the cost of secrecy and procedure |

## House of Borrowed Voices quick reference

The House receives signals from the Barrow Stair, chapel witness rail, roof windlass, kitchen bell frame, gatehouse treadle, Reed Weir float, and the cauldron runners. Its relief table tracks five objectives:

- vessel;
- anchor;
- war roll;
- intruder;
- witnesses.

The room reasons from those markers. It does not see the fort directly. False signals, moved markers, simultaneous routes, and crossed command lines can corrupt an otherwise accurate prediction.

## Living command network

Twelve mask throats terminate in a switchboard serving six posts:

1. White Hart Gate;
2. East Passage;
3. Rook Gallery;
4. Chapel Watch;
5. Drowned Court;
6. Reed Weir.

The four command shutters are:

| Shutter | Guard posture |
|---|---|
| White | Verify, count, question, escort |
| Red | Close, seize, hold, compel |
| Blue | Alter water, fire, bridge, bell, or machinery |
| Black | Wait unseen at the predicted route |

One unconfirmed voice prompts local verification. Two agreeing voices prompt action. Contradictory voices make the post hold and seek Captain Vane. Mael's black override lets one voice act as though two agreed.

Maintenance collars can send a correct order to the wrong post. Rotating a shutter can change seizure into verification or an open pursuit into an ambush posture.

## Recovered-reason procedure

A personal name alone causes hesitation. A retained answer is meaningfully recontextualized when the crew supplies:

1. the dead person's whole name;
2. a credible record, object, or living tradition preserving the missing reason;
3. the mask's return cock turned inward toward the cauldron channel.

The answer then speaks one fuller qualification. It may retract an order, protect someone its tactic ignored, or choose silence. Opening the return cock ends the retained answer as an active command source.

The procedure does not prove that the dead person is completely restored or that spent cauldron power returns. It ends the separation and use of that particular answer. A vague moral sentiment or invented family story does not work.

## Common crew approaches

**Quiet crossing:** cool the relevant bowls and wedge the intruder marker before passing among the chairs.

**False objective:** shift vessel, anchor, war roll, or witness markers so the council protects the wrong thing.

**Contradictory routes:** disturb kitchen, roof, and weir signals together. The council produces mutually impossible commands and the posts wait for Vane.

**Crossed lines:** reroute a Reed Weir ambush to White Hart Gate or turn a red closure into white verification.

**Recovered reason:** recontextualize the one voice most dangerous to the chosen exit.

**Royal fault:** display Branoc's ring. The masks divide between protection, seizure, and witnessed inquiry; the pause reveals the anchor.

**Public demonstration:** ask several masks whom their advice was intended to protect in front of Caddoc, Vane, Aderyn, or household witnesses.

**Release:** restore and return selected answers, or open all return cocks at once. The first is slow and legible. The second destroys much of the command network and much of the evidence while risking final uncontrolled orders.

## Sample alarm sequences

### Kitchen extraction

1. Heavy-cart discrepancy moves the intruder marker to East Passage.
2. Mairwen orders a red cart search; Madoc orders kitchen and chapel doors locked.
3. The crew moves the witness marker toward White Hart and rings the roof windlass.
4. Seren orders witness verification while Rhian orders roof exposure.
5. East Passage receives incompatible red and white instructions and waits for Vane.

### Roof extraction

1. The windlass moves and wakes Iwan, Rhian, and Tegid.
2. Iwan orders the counterweight dropped; Rhian orders roof lights extinguished; Tegid orders the lower landing held.
3. Restoring Iwan's reason forces a warning and preserves the load-bearing route.
4. Crossing Rhian's line sends the lantern order to White Hart Gate.
5. The roof crew gains one interval before the council revises its model.

### Reed Weir extraction

1. Cauldron movement wakes all voices; the table predicts the prepared tide route.
2. Alwen and Elidyr agree on a black waiting order at the weir.
3. Restoring Alwen's ferry vow removes one of the agreeing voices.
4. Moving the witness marker toward the chapel wakes Seren and Morwen, who demand witnessed custody and quarantine rather than ambush.
5. Reed Weir holds for Vane instead of acting immediately.

## GM principles

- Speak the voices' answers clearly enough that players can reason about them.
- Show which marker moved, shutter turned, and tube carried the order.
- Let accurate counsel remain dangerous; do not make every stripped answer obviously foolish.
- Let one recovered reason materially change the relevant situation.
- Contradiction buys delay and changed posture, not permanent paralysis.
- A broken system remains playable. Force should exchange procedural leverage for noise, damage, and public consequence rather than end the heist.
- The moral fact is not that expertise is wicked. It is that expertise severed from purpose cannot decide what deserves to survive.
