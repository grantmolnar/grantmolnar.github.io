# The Bell Chapel

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** location, occult, mechanism

A rehearsal already speaking in borrowed voices reveals body, mouth, and reach; strip, cord, and deposition can answer the peal at different costs.

## Opening View

The first word spoken in the chapel returns in a glass voice. No bell moves. Ropes narrow the nave; petition cards command from the floor; fresh wax binds a Vale ribbon into a mouth-shaped knot. A severed strand crosses the chalk road from museum to belfry to winter garden. Above, the practice clapper slips. Every rope answers.

## Encounter Material

Edrin chose the chapel because its bell once called attendants to the west infirmary. The rehearsal has been left speaking. Four workings cross the nave: a mirrored frame over interchangeable petition cards; a mouth knot tied around a Vale hair ribbon; a relay board with six pin-shaped recesses; and a chalk route joining the Grand Belfry, East Gate, River Ward, and Vale Manor. Five intact pins appear on the plan. Cracked wax marks the spent museum trigger. The three Grand Belfry recesses read **witness**, **mouth**, and **crown**; the lesser towers read **relay**. Relay pins repeat the crown note. They cannot make the body or take a voice.

The rehearsal still answers. Exchange the petition cards and hand-sized glass attendants recite incompatible civic virtues. Put a hand inside the mouth knot and the nearest figure borrows the last sentence spoken in the room. Strike the relay board and the chapel bell answers without moving; one listener feels a sharp urge to obey the clearest imperative just heard. The figures fail to recognize Edrin, Olyra’s doors, or the same childhood memory twice. These tests separate language, voice, range, and instability. Let investigators stop after one proof, press for more certainty, or dismantle the workshop. Each additional test risks another line, pin, or spoken command.

The room punishes careless position. Waxed ropes tighten under crossed weight. A live pin snaps toward nearby bell metal. A cut tie drops the practice clapper through the nave. The wrong line sends a pulse that steals one complete sentence from the nearest speaker and gives it to the figures until the next bell note. Colored sleeves, tie-offs, and Edrin’s safe order reward a careful search. Tavia’s shorthand in the margin records the museum cut. Bell training, patient trials, or two workers approaching from opposite sides permit controlled dismantling. A rush still yields evidence, but may leave a pin live, damage one counterkey part, or preserve the investigators’ last imperative in the rehearsal.

The **counterkey** answers the rite in three pieces. The notched tuning strip identifies a live pin and lets a gloved hand lift it without carrying the vibration into the bell. The braided reversal cord changes a command knot into a confined or answering knot. The pitch table aligns those forms with the three long notes and the necessary lines of Olyra’s deposition. Strip alone: safe pin removal. Cord plus table: retuning. All three, Olyra’s text, and a willing breath-giver: bounded answering. A damaged set narrows the work; each surviving part keeps its listed use.

Every answer changes what the city will hear. Silence the witness bell and no body forms, but the procession loses its opening cadence. Lift the mouth pin after the first note and the glass body remains voiceless. Lift or mute the crown and the manor remains dangerous while the city escapes command. Retune the outer knot and confine the third note to everyone in the winter garden. Turn it outward into an answering form and a willing speaker lends one breath per answer, risking muteness until dawn. Remove the vow-bed and the speaking body collapses while the heartglass survives. Break the heartglass and every manifestation ends with Olyra’s last material relic.
