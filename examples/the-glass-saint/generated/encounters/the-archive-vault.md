# The Archive Vault

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** location, records, investigation

Four record packets prove substitution, suppressed history, modern concealment, and ritual contact—but custody determines what each proof can do.

## Opening View

Three books lie open to the same accession. Heartglass. Substitute. Missing leaf. Registrar Senna Drail checks the pale torn edge of the newest gap, then covers an unsigned deposition with one hand. The vault door stands open only as far as its chain permits. Beside her keys, the custody log waits for its first name.

## Encounter Material

Senna has chained the vault door with junior copyists Eren Sol and Mave Kitt inside. Before she opens another drawer, she makes the visitors name a recipient and write the first custody entry. She will certify exact copies that leave through a lawful hand or through investigators willing to defend her office; originals stay under her keys. Dorr may close the public stair. The conservation stair still reaches the vault, but using it costs Senna’s signature and may cost her position.

Four packets compete for the copyists’ hands. The **relic packet** joins accession numbers, conservation photographs, donor letters, and a private receipt proving Alis substituted the painted copy and kept the heartglass at Vale Manor. The **Olyra packet** joins her unsigned deposition, council wax, patient indexes, and sermons that turn refusal into obedience. The **modern suppression packet** contains the six-week-old conservation finding and the trustee minute calling it “credible and materially significant” while postponing publication. The **ritual-contact packet** holds Tavia’s slips, Edrin’s consultation requests, Alis’s warning that a Vale voice may become common property, and the chapel-to-garden bell diagram.

Each packet opens different action. Relic records plus the gallery trail support recovery of the vow-bed. Modern suppression plus an independent witness exposes Dorr’s present obstruction. Olyra’s papers plus the infirmary’s material record authenticate the history. Ritual contact plus an answer-pin supports emergency inspection at the belfry or manor. One certified packet can leave quickly. Two require divided labor and two custodians. A complete set before sunset demands an open vault, outside copyists, or rough copies Dorr can challenge.

Custody gives evidence its reach. A watch seal carries immediate legal force but may lock the packet inside a trustee dispute. Senna’s seal carries museum authentication and endangers her office. The House makes copies public and vulnerable to charges of partisanship. The infirmary lends independent history; Caldra will not turn a working hospice into the city’s warehouse. Distributed copies survive seizure only if signatures preserve their chain. Often the strongest case places different packets in different hands.

If Dorr takes the vault, he seals drawers, removes Senna from duty, and sends one copy to the Trustees’ Chamber. That act creates delay and a named obstruction. It leaves family copies, accession gaps, Tavia’s cut leaf, and every certified packet untouched. Stolen papers retain information but acquire a custody objection. When Senna refuses a dangerous request, she names the evidence or recipient that would let her sign. Reward a chosen packet and a defended custodian; the vault cannot yield everything before sunset.

**If skipped or reached late.** Senna protects the originals and logs every order. She chooses no packet for absent investigators. A suspension closes the public stair and enters Dorr’s signature in the book; the conservation stair, a lawful recipient, or divided trustees can reopen access. Tavia’s cut leaf proves one suppressed passage, never the full relic, Olyra, suppression, or ritual chain. Delay narrows authentication without deleting the archive.
