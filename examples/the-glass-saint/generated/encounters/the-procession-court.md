# The Procession Court

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** location, social, public

A collapsing civic rehearsal turns Tavia’s transfer into partial public testimony and forces an early choice between pursuit, protection, and publication.

## Opening View

“Stop opening empty carts,” Sergeant Vela Rusk shouts. The petition wagon has sagged under a weight it no longer carries, and a grey museum cloak is trapped beneath its wheel. Lantern children are already being formed into ranks. A copyist runs with a loose page; a trustee’s deputy follows. At the far gate, the Vale carriage turns once in the sun and disappears into traffic.

## Encounter Material

The procession’s fixed order makes every break visible. Six watch outriders clear the route. Twenty-four children carry glass lanterns under schoolmistress Anet Vey. Four museum porters bear the silver canopy. A petition wagon and three House copyists follow. West Door families carry white ribbons for the Ash Fever dead; Dorr and the civic officers close the line. The devotional image used outdoors is separate from the shattered case, so Dorr can truthfully say the ceremony can proceed. Sergeant Vela Rusk’s detail is searching carts without knowing what the stolen silver looks like.

Tavia crossed the court in a grey museum cloak with the vow-bed under the petition wagon. Lantern bearer Pellen Vey saw the slats bow. Driver Hara Bex felt the weight and later found fresh Vale wax on a replacement strap. Copyists Iven Quill and Sera Mott saw Edrin’s carriage pause and heard Tavia say, “The city gets the words before you get the bell.” When Officer Den Rusk stopped the wagon, she abandoned the cloak, passed the wrapped silver into the carriage, kept a packet of copied records, and fled toward the House of Petitions. Each witness owns one piece. Assemble the handoff; no statement supplies it whole.

Choose what the court preserves. Pursuit may catch or shadow Tavia before the House. Counter-signed statements give Renn the Vale destination and protect witnesses from later claims of crowd invention. A precise description of the vow-bed releases innocent carts and turns panic into a theft search. Crowd control keeps children, wheels, and glass lanterns from becoming injuries. Public accusation makes suppression harder. The West Door families may remain in the line, withdraw, or carry their ribbons with the evidence; the ceremony changes around that choice.

Public pressure follows acts. Name the silver and carriage before witnesses, and rumor becomes an allegation with owners. Put authenticated pages into the crowd, and Dorr loses the comfort of “vandalism.” Win petitioner or West Door commitments, and the accusation acquires a body. Each gain has a cost: Dorr may advance the procession, trustees may order seizure, and frightened families may leave. Quiet protection can preserve a witness who matters more at the manor than a shouted claim does here.

Tavia’s route fails forward. Intercepted, she admits cutting the bed free, refuses trustee return, and asks for a public custodian; she knows nothing of the vocal cost. Shadowed, she reveals the House’s entrances and allies. Lost, she distributes the cut archive leaf and becomes harder to detain privately. Violence lets her escape, but Pellen, Hara, and at least one copyist remain. Meanwhile the procession continues to take position. Delay changes people, orders, and routes rather than subtracting invisible minutes.

**If skipped or reached late.** Tavia reaches the House with the cut leaf. Edrin’s carriage continues to the manor. The procession advances. Pellen stays with the lantern children, Hara returns to the wagon yard, Iven and Sera go to the House, and Den reports to Renn. Their partial accounts remain available. A late investigation follows people and changed ranks, not a preserved chase.
