# The Shattered Gallery

**Role:** necessary, start  
**Tags:** location, investigation, authority

The broken case yields three evidence chains while museum, watch, and trustee authority compete to seal them first.

## Opening View

One last sliver drops from the saint’s case and rings against the floor. Lio Marr bleeds beside the plinth while Ossa Pell chalks the curved spray. The painted figure still hangs in place; beneath it, a clean silver-bed rectangle remains. Provost Dorr calls for the procession doors. Iria Vale spreads the witness charter across his seal before anyone can close the room.

## Encounter Material

The rehearsal leaves docent Lio Marr bleeding, a crowd against the doors, and three offices reaching for the wreckage. Iria Vale lays down the emergency witness charter. It permits independent inspection of museum rooms, consensual staff interviews, counter-signed copies, and a written entry whenever evidence moves. It grants no search of private Vale property, compelled testimony, or ownership. Captain Ors Renn can seal the gallery and detain a named suspect. Floor warden Ossa Pell controls keys and handling. Provost Helian Dorr, procession marshal and senior trustee, wants a vandalism report and open doors before sunset. Registrar Senna Drail has taken the archive keys below. Tavia Sorn is absent from roll call.

The painted bone copy remains strapped to cracked plaster. The clean rectangle beneath it held the engraved silver vow-bed. A hidden answer-pin caught the rehearsal note and threw the case glass outward. One mounting screw was loosened in advance; the second was cut during the panic. Packing felt bears Vale carriage wax. Tavia’s numbered narrow saw is gone. Lio saw a grey museum cloak pass through the service door while the room watched the glass. Ossa can trace every key, though she saw no hand lift the silver.

Sort the scene into three evidence chains. The answer-pin and bell dust establish mechanism. The saw, cut mounts, and cloak route establish removal. The felt, wax, and accession number establish provenance and destination. Leave a chain under watch seals, bag it through Ossa with counter-signatures, or take a sample and exact rubbing while the original stays. Watch custody best supports a warrant. Senna’s certification best supports authenticity. Distributed rubbings and statements survive suppression but invite Dorr to call them incomplete. If he gains the originals, preserve what has already left the room: Lio’s statement, investigators’ notes, and every signed copy. The next problem becomes access, not forgotten discovery.

Several jobs can proceed at once. Treat Lio and gain a calm witness. Reconstruct the service-door sight line before boots destroy it. Give Renn a usable description of the stolen silver. Keep Dorr from reopening the gallery before Ossa closes the log. A poor search still finds the outward spray and exact absence. A lost argument produces Dorr’s written order and the objects he chose to seal. Rough handling contaminates one chain, not the whole case. The evidence points toward the court witnesses, the accession record, the chapel’s pin work, the west infirmary named on the backing, and the Vale carriage route. A rush to the manor remains possible; it arrives with less law and fewer witnesses.

**If skipped or left early.** Ossa numbers the evidence, Renn seals the room, and Dorr places unclaimed originals under trustee seal. Lio receives treatment and remains available. The outward rupture, silver-shaped absence, and staff roll stay observable. Tavia reaches the Procession Court, and every copy or note already made continues to circulate. Later entry may require Senna, Renn, or a written challenge to Dorr. The gallery never resets.
