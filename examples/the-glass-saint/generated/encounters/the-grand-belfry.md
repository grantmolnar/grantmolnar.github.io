# The Grand Belfry

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** location, action, public

Every rope is a public choice: the divided ringers can silence, confine, warn, or carry the rite, and bronze remembers what they do.

## Opening View

Six ropes descend through the crew, three answer-pins humming among them. Jori Kest holds Dorr’s sealed order. Mela Fen strikes the locked wheel-loft door once from above. “First lift,” calls Rian Voss. “Full peal,” answers Karel Venn. The counterweight rises through their disagreement, hauling every command toward bronze. Nobody has released a rope; nobody has agreed what the city is about to hear.

## Encounter Material

Name every hand on a rope before the first lift. Jori Kest directs the ringing room and will not gamble a ringer on an unexplained device. Rian Voss treats Dorr’s sealed order as the crew’s only shield. Mela Fen found Karel Venn in the wheel loft and has locked herself on the crown deck with the tuning ledger. Pavo Tern and Lis Aven hold tie-offs and carry messages; either may run when fear outruns discipline. Karel, one of the Returned Names, is trying to preserve the full peal because rumor convinced him delay would bury the record again. Edrin never sent him.

The crew is split across the tower: ropes and brakes below; live pins and moving counterweights in the loft; Mela, the ledger, and the bell mouths above. Once a bell moves, its rope lane can lift, drag, or strike anyone who crosses it. A loose counterweight can take the stairs. Bare metal against a humming pin carries its imperative into the next bell. Stop machinery, secure tie-offs, and control the crew before treating the tower as a puzzle.

The three live pins remain **witness**, **mouth**, and **crown**. The first raises the reflected body at Vale Manor; the second opens the nearest Vale as speaker; the crown launches the command to any connected relay. East Gate and River Ward can repeat only that third note. Removing the crown protects the city while leaving body and voice danger at the manor; removing all three stops the rite here, though Dorr can move ceremonial music to the lesser towers.

Put the case on the crew floor. The chapel strip identifies the linked pins; the pitch table and Mela’s ledger match stages to bells; Tavia can prove sabotage. A warrant or signed hold gives legal protection, while named petitioners or infirmary witnesses give public protection. One technical proof plus either form of cover is enough for controlled work. Without it, Rian calls the watch, a junior may run, and Dorr can call the intervention a second attack.

Choose what the bells will be allowed to do. **Strip all stage pins:** stop all three bells, secure the loft, and use the strip; every stage fails, the full peal is lost, and the crew is publicly implicated. **Lift the crown:** one controlled ascent prevents city-wide command while leaving body and voice danger at the manor. **Mute or bind one bell:** fast without the counterkey, dangerous under motion, and visible as sabotage; that stage remains silent until repaired. **Retune the sequence:** reversal cord, pitch table, Mela’s ledger, Kest or equivalent skill, and two cooperative ringers confine the crown note to the winter garden while preserving the first two. **Sound the warning:** two ringers and an intact warning bell draw watch, petitioners, and witnesses toward the manor; concealment ends, panic may begin. **Preserve the peal:** an informed choice for bounded answering or accepted risk.

A sounded note cannot be unsounded; every later note remains contestable. Before the witness note, every operation stands. After it, mouth, crown, retuning, and warning remain. After the mouth note, the Vale voice is already gone, but crown removal still protects the city and retuning still confines the command. Once the crown starts, braking the bell, breaking the pin, or severing the rope may reduce the relays; no action recalls a sounded command. Record lifted pins, cut ropes, convinced ringers, injuries, warnings, and orders shown. Dorr can send officers and new paper. He cannot instantly restore bronze, trust, or rigging.

**If skipped entirely.** Kest follows Dorr’s peal order because no proof or protection reaches him. Rian readies the crown rope, Mela stays locked above, Karel works to preserve the full peal, and Pavo or Lis carries any order that actually arrives. When manor and crew are ready, intact stage pins sound in order. No warning sounds by default. Later testimony, pin placement, and consequences remain. Skipping the tower accelerates the rite; it erases nothing.
