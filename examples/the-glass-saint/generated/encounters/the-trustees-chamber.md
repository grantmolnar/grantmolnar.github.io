# The Trustees’ Chamber

**Role:** optional  
**Tags:** location, social, authority

A divided hearing converts distinct kinds of proof into warrants, delay, public record, or a signed refusal that can travel farther.

## Opening View

Each burst of applause from the court shortens the trustees’ silence. Iria Vale’s empty chair faces the museum seal. Beside Provost Dorr, a clerk has begun two incompatible orders: hold the crown bell for a quarter hour; seize the House of Petitions’ ledgers. Witnesses are called in the order Dorr chooses. The ink remains wet enough for either order to become the city’s answer.

## Encounter Material

Four trustees have different breaking points. Helian Dorr controls the agenda and procession seal; he trades minutes for order and resists publication he cannot stage-manage. Collections keeper Irena Moss accepts split custody and a manor recovery warrant when the relic is endangered, but keeps originals away from crowds. Halwen Gorse speaks for guilds and merchants; danger to spectators buys a short delay. Maelin Tress speaks for the west wards and breaks with Dorr once Olyra’s deposition has independent authentication. Iria’s empty chair asks whether she will sit as curator, speak as a Vale, or surrender the museum seal. Captain Renn attends only to request an order.

Build the hearing from four kinds of proof. **Theft** needs the missing vow-bed plus a credible Vale transfer or Tavia’s admission. **Imminent danger** needs chapel notes, a live pin, or a demonstration that the peal can carry compulsion. **Independent authentication** comes from Senna’s chain, infirmary material, or authenticated House copies under a named custodian. **Public witness** requires two counter-signed court statements, a committed petitioner, or someone ready to speak in the chamber. Quality and custody matter. Four versions of one museum claim still count once.

Proof becomes authority in combinations. Theft plus a Vale destination yields a narrow recovery warrant. Danger plus authentication yields belfry inspection and a quarter-hour hold. Theft plus danger yields emergency manor entry and watch support, but no automatic publication. Authentication plus public witness enters the deposition and trustee response into the same-day council record. Any three combine warrant, hold, and record. All four divide the board and place Dorr’s obstruction beside the evidence. Once written, these outputs persist.

A refusal also travels. A denied warrant bears the rejected proof in Dorr’s hand. Renn can preserve the gallery and shadow the carriage without exceeding it. A lost cancellation demand may still win a hold, belfry inspection, or permission for the West Door families to withdraw. Covert records may fail formal authentication and still move Maelin or Corven. A confrontation may close the vault and open the chamber windows to the crowd.

Dorr did not build the rite. His fault is choosing controlled disclosure over the people who pay for delay. He can accept split custody, a timed public reading, a guarded search, or a warning pattern that preserves order without preserving silence. He rejects invented occult motives. Expose him, bargain with him, bypass him, or make him sign the refusal. A forum secured here matters to Edrin because it already holds records, witnesses, and custody; another private review does not.

**If skipped or reached late.** Dorr acts on reports that reach him. Without contrary evidence he orders containment, preserves the peal, and calls the rupture theft and vandalism. He cannot invent the archive or Tavia’s location. The other trustees keep their divisions. Every order, abstention, and limited inspection remains usable later. Once copies, witnesses, or a warning peal spread, even Dorr’s preferred hearing begins from public facts.
