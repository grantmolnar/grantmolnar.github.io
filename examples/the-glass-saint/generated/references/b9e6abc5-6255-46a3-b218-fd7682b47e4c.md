## Summary

The principal antagonist: a meticulous Vale historian who exposes a true civic concealment by building a rite that can steal one living voice and compel the city through its bells.

## Reference Material

## Grievance and manner

Edrin Vale is Alis Vale’s grandson. He spent years authenticating Olyra’s suppressed history and documenting the trustees’ renewed silence. He prefers exact argument, exhibits his evidence, accepts personal cost, and refuses another private promise after the procession. His grievance is sound; his chosen remedy remains coercive.

## The undertaking

Edrin receives the stolen petition-bed from Tavia, joins it to Olyra’s heartglass and old shrine panes at Vale Manor, and connects the apparatus to answer-pins and the sunset peal. He begins on the speaker mark intending to lose his own voice. He knows the first note forms a reflected body, the second takes the nearest living Vale voice inside the circle, and the third can carry one command through fitted bells until dawn.

## Authority, posture, and limits

Edrin controls his participation, household access available to him, the apparatus he assembled, and whether he opens or closes its knots. He has no authority to take another voice, compel listeners, assign public custody, or make a composite manifestation settle Olyra’s meaning. He pauses for a hearing that already has public anchors, records, witnesses, and credible custody. Two independent anchors plus named custody can move him toward witnessed terms. Private seizure, destroyed copies, or unannounced assault harden him, though he takes no hostages and attacks no surrendered witness.
