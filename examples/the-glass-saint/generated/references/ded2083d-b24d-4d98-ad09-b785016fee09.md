## Summary

An assistant conservator and culpable accomplice who steals the vow-bed to force publication, then may turn against the rite when she learns that its second note takes a living voice.

## Reference Material

## Work and culpability

Tavia Sorn is an assistant conservator whose family petitions and infirmary record were among the material withheld by the museum and trustees. She helped plan the rupture, set the museum answer-pin, removed the silver vow-bed under cover of the burst case, passed it to Edrin in the procession court, and carried a cut archive leaf toward the House of Petitions. Lio’s injury, sabotage, theft, and evidence removal remain hers.

## What she wants

Tavia wants the buried record outside closed Vale and trustee custody before sunset. She joined Edrin expecting a reflected witness, not an involuntary speaker. Proof of the vocal cost can divide her from him because she never accepted that price. She expects arrest after cooperation and does not treat confession as absolution.

## States, authority, and limits

In defiance, Tavia admits the theft to Nessa, refuses private trustee custody, and withholds the manor service route while testing whether investigators mean to bury the pages. Proof of voice loss plus credible outside custody moves her toward cooperation: she gives the transfer statement, chapel deliveries, service route, and public rejection of the mouth stage. Captain Renn’s public seal can carry her testimony into lawful custody. A closed transfer ordered by Dorr ends cooperation; threatened private seizure or summary violence sends her toward the infirmary. Tavia may confess, testify, distribute her copy, and reveal routes she used. She cannot speak for the Sorn families, authorize Edrin’s rite, or place current petitions into it for absent petitioners.
