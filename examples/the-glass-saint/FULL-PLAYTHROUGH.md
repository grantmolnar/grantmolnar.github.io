# Full Playthrough: The Counterseal Witnesses

This is one subordinate demonstration through *The Glass Saint*: three sessions, seven visits, thirty-seven clues spotted, five explicit misses, fifteen consequences, all sixteen revelations established, and 116 append-only journal events. It is not a canonical solution and does not define what fresh investigators know, choose, or achieve.

The Counterseal Witnesses pursue a failed-warrant public route through seven encounters and skip the West Infirmary and Bell Chapel. Those omissions remain real throughout play. The other thirty-two authored clues are unseen alternatives, not thirty-two additional failures.

## Session One: What the Glass Threw Out

### The Shattered Gallery

Iria Vale opens the emergency charter while Dorr argues that the burst reliquary is vandalism and the procession can continue. Mira refuses the word “relic” until the scene establishes what is missing. Pell braces the remaining mount. Ossa and Mira separate the scene into mechanism, theft, and destination packets.

The painted copy remains. The silver vow-bed does not.

Aven takes a counter-signed accession rubbing before the original shard goes under Renn’s seal. Lysa records Lio’s service-door view after Pell clears the dangerous glass. Tavia’s numbered saw is absent, and a strip of felt bears fresh Vale wax.

The Counterseal Witnesses already have a direct route to the Manor. They do not take it. That is a choice, not a lock.

### The Procession Court

The court is not one crowd. Pellen saw the weight. Hara felt the wagon change and found Vale wax. Iven and Sera heard Tavia argue with Edrin. The statements overlap without becoming identical.

The Counterseal Witnesses let Tavia reach the House rather than trading every witness for a chase. Iven and Sera carry word that the investigators have evidence of a possible living cost. Lysa asks the West Door families only to preserve what they saw. They withdraw their children from the front rank but promise neither testimony nor custody.

Dorr’s summons identifies the Trustees’ Chamber. Tavia’s token identifies the House. Her shelf slip confirms the Archive lead.

### The Archive Vault

Senna Drail offers four packets and no magical extension of time. Aven prioritizes Olyra’s deposition and the modern concealment minute. Eren and Mave make two certified sets while Senna preserves every original.

The deposition says Olyra opened the sealed infirmary against council order. The recent minute calls the evidence credible and materially significant, then delays publication until after the procession. Alis Vale’s transfer letter warns that the shrine glass can make a family voice common property.

Dorr’s suspension order reaches the public stair before Aven can open the ritual diagram packet. The witnesses leave with strong documentary and vocal-risk evidence but without the Chapel’s working details.

One certified set goes under Renn’s seal. One remains available for public reading. Senna logs the closure instead of becoming an off-screen conspirator for either side.

The first session ends with truth, destination, and danger established—but no warrant and no control of the bells.

## Session Two: A Warrant Refused

### The Trustees’ Chamber

Dorr does not deny the substitution, theft, or concealment. He denies that an old warning proves immediate private-property danger. He refuses a Manor warrant and a full procession hold.

The refusal is not a dead end. It is signed.

Renn obtains a narrow Belfry safety inspection. Dorr preserves the full peal and orders petition copies held rather than destroyed. Iria acknowledges the false display and family custody in public, but does not claim the right to surrender the heartglass or excuse Edrin.

The Counterseal Witnesses leave with less authority than they wanted and more public evidence of obstruction than they had before entering.

### The House of Petitions

Tavia reads Alis’s warning. She had accepted sabotage, theft, and a forced hearing; she had not accepted the taking of a living voice. She confesses her acts and rejects Edrin’s mouth stage without asking anyone to call her innocent.

Nessa Quill distributes certified copies through three named keepers. The Quiet Keepers retain one sealed packet and decline the march. The Returned Names want the Manor opened immediately. The Open Hands begin a reading. The West Door Circle sends word toward the families but does not promise their participation.

“Open the doors” is the most repeated current petition. No one carries that packet into the apparatus. Public language and ritual input remain separate material facts.

The House gives Aven and Lysa a public-custody pledge and a warning pattern. It does not give them authentication they failed to obtain or the counterkey they never recovered.

### The Grand Belfry

The tower makes the missing Chapel visit impossible to ignore. The Counterseal Witnesses have no notched tuning strip. Pell cannot safely lift the live crown pin and says so.

Mela’s ledger and the separate pin resonances identify witness, mouth, and crown bells. The narrow inspection order, Tavia’s confession, and the evidence in the tower are enough for Kest to cooperate. Rian will not remove a pin. He will accept a brake-post bind that remains visible and inspectable.

Kest sounds the warning peal. Then Pell, Kest, and Rian bind the crown rope before it begins moving. The witness and mouth bells remain operable. No controlled retuning is possible. No bounded answering form is available.

Dorr hears the warning only after the city does.

The second session ends with a public approach forming at Vale Manor and the crown stage physically blocked, while the body and voice stages remain live.

## Session Three: The Saint Who Could Not Answer

### Vale Manor

The Counterseal Witnesses reach the west lawn behind petitioners reading the deposition. Their second public anchor is the certified copy chain already outside Vale and museum custody. Before negotiating, they name interim custody: Renn for the heartglass under seal, the House for the vow-bed under inventory, Senna for archive originals, distributed keepers for copies, and Leva’s household inventory for the shrine panes pending a hearing.

Tavia rejects the mouth stage in public. Iria acknowledges the Vale concealment. Kest certifies the crown bind and warns that the witness and mouth notes remain live.

Those facts move Edrin from Declaration to Witnessed Terms. He opens the garden doors and agrees that no current petition packet will be placed on the silver. He asks to let the first note demonstrate what the city nearly suppressed.

Aven records the terms, and the four Witnesses permit a witness-only formation.

The glass body recites “open the doors,” “wait for lawful order,” and pleas unrelated to either sentence. Shown a copy of the fever doors, it repeats a civic slogan. It answers no question and recognizes no person.

The witnesses record what happened without calling the figure Olyra.

Before the second note, Edrin steps off the speaker mark and opens the mouth knot. Iria remains outside the circle. No voice is taken. The bound crown bell prevents the third note from launching. Peren lowers and seals the heartglass; the body collapses without broken panes or injured servants.

Edrin and Tavia submit to Renn’s custody. Their cooperation matters and does not erase their acts.

## Demonstrated aftermath

### Manifestation, voice, and command

A witness-only body formed before named observers. It displayed unstable petition language and no stable identity. No living voice was taken. No crown command launched. Neither relay tower sounded.

### Custody

Renn seals the acoustically active but unused heartglass pending a public hearing. The House of Petitions inventories the vow-bed. Senna retains every archive original. Certified copies remain distributed. Leva shutters the shrine panes under a household inventory rather than allowing any one institution to absorb the entire apparatus.

This is interim custody, not a final property judgment.

### Publication

Olyra’s deposition and the trustee concealment minute receive a witnessed public reading. Certified copies exist beyond Vale, museum, and trustee control. The Counterseal Witnesses never visited the West Infirmary, so no family testimony or physical hospice authentication is added to the public claim.

### Accountability

Edrin answers for conspiracy, receiving stolen civic property, and attempted compulsion. Tavia answers for sabotage, Lio’s injury, theft, and evidence removal; her confession and prevention of the mouth stage are mitigation, not absolution. Dorr and the participating trustees face inquiry for concealment, obstruction, and the preserved peal order rather than occult conspiracy. Iria’s professional and political responsibility remains part of the hearing.

### Public memory

The obedience cult fractures. Some citizens treat the deposition as decisive, some treat the witness body as proof of sanctity, and some regard the unstable figure as evidence that no institution should ring the relic again. The city now knows Olyra opened the west doors. It has not agreed what that act requires of it.

## What the route did not prove

The Counterseal Witnesses did not visit the Infirmary. They did not earn Caldra’s testimony, family custody, shrine-frame access, or an independent breath-giver.

They did not visit the Chapel. They did not obtain the tuning strip, reversal cord, pitch table, controlled pin removal, retuning, or bounded answering.

They did not obtain a Manor warrant. Their public approach and later custody must be defended after the fact.

These omissions are part of the demonstrated result. They are not silently repaired because the adventure source contains the missing options.
