# Ritual and Bell Operating Sheet

Use this sheet once the investigation reaches the Bell Chapel, the Grand Belfry, or any event that changes the procession peal. It records physical facts. Do not reduce the rite to a countdown or a generic ritual-progress score.

## The six answer-pins

All six pins were cast from metal taken from the old west-infirmary bell. Their common pitch makes the network possible.

| Pin | Present location | Function | What happens if removed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Museum trigger | Cracked and spent after the gallery rupture | Resonated with the noon rehearsal and broke the case outward | Nothing further; it remains evidence of planned sabotage |
| Witness pin | Grand Belfry west tenor | First long note forms the reflected petition body at Vale Manor | The body cannot form from the procession peal |
| Mouth pin | Grand Belfry silver bell | Second long note opens the nearest Vale as the body’s speaker | The body may persist, but it cannot take a Vale voice through the bell sequence |
| Crown pin | Grand Belfry crown bell | Third long note launches the command into the relay network | No city-wide command can leave the winter garden |
| East Gate relay | East Gate procession tower | Repeats a launched crown command through the eastern wards | The east does not receive the command; it cannot begin a stage |
| River Ward relay | River Ward procession tower | Repeats a launched crown command along the river wards | The river wards do not receive the command; it cannot begin a stage |

The lesser towers are relays, not backups for the full rite. Dorr may move ceremonial ringing to them after interference at the Grand Belfry, but they cannot form the saint or take a voice without the three grand-tower stages.

## The three-part counterkey

The counterkey answers the rite in three pieces. They may be separated, copied imperfectly, damaged, or carried by different people.

1. **Notched tuning strip:** identifies witness, mouth, crown, and relay pitches; permits safe lifting of a live pin.
2. **Braided reversal cord:** reproduces the outer knot needed to confine or reverse the command sequence.
3. **Pitch table and deposition cues:** aligns the knot with the three long notes and identifies the words required for an answering form.

| Parts available | Operations supported |
|---|---|
| Tuning strip only | Identify pins; safely remove one or more pins |
| Reversal cord only | Copy the physical knot, but not reliably align it with a bell stage |
| Pitch table only | Identify stage timing and deposition cues; no safe physical alteration |
| Cord + pitch table | Retune with an expert bell crew, at risk of misidentifying a live pin |
| Tuning strip + either other part | Safe partial interference, but not a complete answering form |
| All three parts | Safe removal, confinement, and preparation of the bounded answering form |
| All three + Olyra’s deposition + willing breath-giver | Bounded answers rather than a broadcast command |

A damaged set narrows the work; each surviving part retains its listed use.

## Tower levels and hazards

- **Ringing room:** six rope lanes, brake posts, Dorr’s sealed order, public warning chart. A moving rope can lift or drag anyone standing in its lane.
- **Wheel loft:** three stage pins, wheels, counterweights, maintenance tie-offs. A loose weight can break the stair and isolate the crown deck.
- **Crown deck:** bell mouths, Mela, tuning ledger, view of the court and relay towers. A sounding bell makes speech difficult and bare-metal contact with a live pin dangerous.

Use hazards to make control, positioning, crew cooperation, and timing matter. Do not make a successful technical plan fail because of an unrelated random mishap.

## Event-based pressure sequence

Advance only when the named fictional act occurs.

| Event | Trigger | Visible change | What can alter it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Museum rupture | Fixed opening event | Case bursts outward; vow-bed enters Edrin’s route | Cannot be undone; can be publicly defined or misreported |
| Containment order | A public accusation names theft, or investigators request formal authority | Dorr issues preservation orders, controls official messaging, and assigns runners | Trustee division, signed denial, public copies, or Renn’s independent evidence log |
| Network wakes | Edrin has assembled heartglass and vow-bed **and** Dorr authorizes the final bell test | Stage pins hum; relay towers answer faintly; chapel figures turn toward the manor | Delay the bell test, interrupt the manor assembly, remove a stage pin, or retune the network |
| Procession takes position | Any hold ends, fails, or is bypassed | Ranks form; ringers take ropes; Dorr can no longer conceal the dispute as a museum-only matter | Renewed authority, public disorder, a warning pattern, or physical bell control |
| Witness note | Grand Belfry west tenor sounds through its pin | Petition reflections form a body in the winter garden | Remove or mute witness pin beforehand; after it sounds, address later stages |
| Mouth note | Edrin closes the inner knot and the silver bell sounds through its pin | Nearest Vale loses their natural voice | Break the inner knot, remove mouth pin, move Vales, or dismantle the body before the note |
| Crown note | Crown bell sounds through an intact launch pin | Command enters any intact relay tower | Lift, mute, brake, break, or retune the crown stage; after launch, limit remaining relays |

Searching a room, taking a rest, or table debate does not by itself advance the sequence. NPCs act when their information and material position permit an action.

## Information channels and bounded responses

### Edrin Vale

Edrin learns through Vale household runners, Hara Bex or another carriage witness, a petitioner messenger, the private manor signal in the bell log, or a public warning peal.

| What Edrin learns | Bounded response |
|---|---|
| Authenticated copies and named witnesses are already public | Sends draft surrender terms and pauses before the first note for a witnessed reply |
| Dorr promises another private review | Treats it as repetition and continues assembly |
| Tavia publicly cooperates after learning the vocal cost | Offers a direct meeting and allows her testimony to be read before the rite |
| Tavia is privately seized or disappears into unacknowledged custody | Sends his duplicate dossier to the House and accelerates assembly; does not take hostages |
| Grand Belfry is muted or pins removed | Adapts to a local winter-garden manifestation; cannot rebuild city-wide reach before sunset |
| Warning pattern summons the city | Orders the household out, displays the records openly, and refuses any private settlement |
| Watch approaches under a narrow warrant | Admits theft and presents the apparatus; bars unrelated seizure unless forced |

Edrin does not know about covert archive access, chapel discoveries, or belfry alterations until a named person or public signal carries the information.

### Provost Helian Dorr

Dorr learns through watch reports, trustee clerks, museum staff, the belfry signal book, and visible crowd movement.

| What Dorr learns | Bounded response |
|---|---|
| Rupture remains unsupported rumor | Preserves the procession and frames the event as vandalism |
| Theft is proven but ritual danger is not | Authorizes property recovery, not procession cancellation |
| Ritual danger is independently authenticated | Grants or accepts a bounded belfry inspection or short hold |
| Copies and witnesses are already public | Stops trying to restore secrecy; contests interpretation and custody instead |
| Investigators seize the belfry without technical or public support | Sends Renn or a watch detail to secure the crew and mechanism |
| Jori verifies the pins and a public constituency stands behind him | Avoids ordering the crew to sound a device they have certified as dangerous; seeks a substitute ceremony |
| Warning peal sounds | Redirects the procession and tries to prevent a crush at Vale Manor rather than pretending nothing is wrong |
| Signed denial or obstruction becomes public | Defends the decision procedurally; cannot erase the document or witnesses |

Dorr is obstructive, not omniscient or suicidal. Once danger is undeniable, he protects civic order by changing tactics rather than ordering a proven compulsion to proceed unchanged.
