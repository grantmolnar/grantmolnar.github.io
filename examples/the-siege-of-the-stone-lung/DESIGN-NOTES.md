# Design Notes: The Siege of the Stone Lung

**Terminology note:** Early construction sections preserve the project’s historical `encounter` and `party` vocabulary. Current operating material uses **encounter**, **commission**, **company**, and the named **Basalt Hand**. The live source contains forty-six clues.

## Design objective

The investigation had to arise from the defense itself. Every important discovery therefore comes through holding a wall, preserving food, settling a ration crisis, clearing a cistern, or entering an enemy sap.

Each encounter was required to do three things:

1. a live defensive problem that matters even if the party learns nothing;
2. evidence that changes how the city can be defended; and
3. a contribution to the final defense of the Stone Lung.

The party never leaves the siege to find the plot. The work of defending Kest Mourne is what makes the truth visible.

## Concepts considered

### The Night-Sea harbor

A fortified island city on a subterranean sea would face blockade, amphibious assault, and attacks through drowned tunnels. This offered strong geography but risked turning the adventure into a sequence of docks, boats, sluices, and flood controls. It also placed too much explanatory weight on an unfamiliar underground tide system.

### The city in the colossus

A settlement built inside the fossilized ribs and marrow chambers of an immense dead creature offered memorable spaces and unusual siege routes. Its imagery was strong, but the skeleton threatened to become the adventure's main subject. The siege would have been subordinate to discovering what the colossus was and how its remains worked.

### The Stone Lung

A shared ventilation engine binds air, food, water, industry, refugees, and military engineering into one material system. It gives the Host an intelligible objective without vindicating the invasion, and it lets earlier work arrive in the finale as crews, tools, measurements, routes, and authority.

The Stone Lung became the adventure.

## Premise

Kest Mourne occupies a shelf of basalt above a deep rift. Its oldest chambers surround the Stone Lung, a pre-imperial engine that expands and contracts with geothermal pressure. Each stroke draws fresh air through porous strata and pushes stale air into abandoned reaches. Six public lanterns in the Lantern Court burn in time with its cycle. Citizens speak of the Lung as civic machinery, weather, and heartbeat in equal measure.

The Host of the Lower Road has besieged Kest Mourne for nineteen days. It holds the caravan tunnels, has cut the western aqueduct, and is advancing three countermines toward the inner wards. The outer gate remains standing, but its right tower has split. Food stores are adequate for weeks only if the Pale Gardens survive. Crowded refuge galleries are consuming air faster than the old calculations permit.

The adventurers begin in the Lantern Court when two events arrive together: the Shattered Gate requests every remaining reserve, and one of the six public lanterns gutters blue. The First Warden gives the party authority to move among sectors, requisition limited resources, and report directly to command. She cannot give them enough soldiers to solve every crisis by force.

## Hidden truth

The Host is not primarily trying to occupy Kest Mourne. Its strategic objective is the Stone Lung.

The lower-road settlement of Lorn once shared the Lung's southern branch. Eighteen years ago, a fast-growing mold blight entered Kest Mourne through that branch and killed hundreds in the low wards. The magistrates ordered a temporary seal. Their engineers warned that Lorn would lose most of its fresh-air exchange and specified a later purge, living filters, and controlled reopening. The quarantine stopped the blight. After the contaminated strain died out, the magistrates abandoned the restoration work, classified the closure, falsified the public diagrams, and described Lorn's subsequent decline as exhausted caverns and poor maintenance.

Lorn survived on auxiliary chimneys while it contracted, sickened, and lost political power. Six weeks before the adventure, its last reliable auxiliary shaft collapsed. Venn sent Kest Mourne an original Lorn conduit plate and demanded a joint inspection. The council dismissed it under the authority of its own false map and sealed the demand beside the old minutes. The siege began after that refusal.

Conditions have changed since the seal was placed. A pocket of methane-rich black breath has accumulated behind it. The Stone Lung now compensates for the obstruction by drawing harder through the remaining branches. Kest Mourne's current stale-air episodes, dying gardens, and cistern contamination are early signs that the engine is approaching destructive imbalance.

Marshal Sera Venn commands the Host. She intends to capture the Lung and force a controlled reopening of the southern branch. She has accepted the starvation and bombardment of Kest Mourne as the cost of saving Lorn. She will negotiate only from a position that guarantees access to the engine.

Chief siege engineer Korr Bas no longer believes controlled access is possible. The Host's wartime articles give him technical command over calibration and firing procedure once a resonance crew reaches its assigned work. He has used that narrow authority to conceal a larger yield, issue a valid-looking countermand beyond Venn's strategic limits, and build Heartstrike in the old lower road. The next full assault fixes Kest Mourne's defenders while his sappers place the charge against the southern seal. Its fracture flash will ignite the black breath and carry the explosion through both cities' ventilation galleries. Bas knows the risk and considers mutual destruction preferable to Lorn's extinction.

Several elders in Kest Mourne know part of this history. None understands the current pressure state. The First Warden inherited the false diagrams and knows only that Venn's pre-siege demand cited a branch the city records deny.

## What defense means

Kest Mourne is guilty, and the Host is still an invading army.

The Host is committing an act of war. Its assaults kill people who had no part in the old decision. Its blockade threatens children, refugees, and laborers before it threatens the officials who kept the secret. Bas's plan would kill both populations. The city must be defended against immediate military defeat and catastrophic sabotage.

At the same time, restoring the old seal and driving the Host away leaves Lorn to suffocate. A complete victory requires the party to hold Kest Mourne, stop the resonance charge, and force or construct a settlement in which the Lung serves both communities again.

Partial victories remain possible:

- repel the Host and reseal the southern branch;
- destroy the Lung to deny it to both sides and evacuate Kest Mourne;
- reopen the branch without adequately venting the black breath;
- expose the truth but lose the city before a remedy is enacted; or
- secure a truce that buys time without correcting the engine.

These outcomes follow from decisions, damaged sectors, and missing resources. There is no morality score behind them.

## Initial siege model (superseded)

The first design used four global siege states. Route testing later replaced this with the contested/held/breached sector ledger, which preserved the useful escalation without making route length more important than decisions.

### Encirclement

The city still has coherent command. The Gate holds. The gardens can recover from local damage. The refugees are frightened but governable. Enemy saps have not reached the old city.

### Breach

A section of the outer wall or gate becomes untenable. The Host gains a foothold or forces a costly withdrawal. Refuge routes crowd. Foundry production shifts from repair to emergency demolition.

### Choke

The Lung's imbalance becomes public. Blue flame, headaches, animal distress, and fungal die-off spread through several wards. The council can no longer describe each failure as local sabotage.

### Heartstrike

Bas arms the resonance charge and the Host begins its full assault. The Stone Lung enters an unstable cycle. The finale is now active whether or not the party has established every revelation.

The state model was intended to change pressure and available help without erasing clues. Its concrete consequences survived in the final sector ledger.

## Cast positions

The final cast positions are recorded below.

### First Warden Ilyra Dain

Ilyra commands Kest Mourne's defense from false infrastructure records. She will spend lives to hold the city and refuses gestures that merely look resolute. Evidence can change her orders. Public accusation without a defensible course of action cannot.

### Speaker Edda Marr

Marr leads the civil council and is the granddaughter of one of the magistrates who ordered the southern seal. She knows that a secret work was performed during the mold blight and has protected the sealed council minutes. She believes disclosure during the siege would fracture the city and vindicate the Host. She is wrong about concealment and right that timing matters.

### Ventwright Tamar Ohn

Ohn maintains the accessible parts of the Stone Lung. She understands its present imbalance but has never seen the original southern-branch diagrams. Her first remedy is to preserve Kest Mourne by permanently choking the sealed branch. She can be persuaded toward a shared solution if the party brings reliable measurements and a way to vent the black breath.

### Gate Captain Brann Sile

Sile holds the Shattered Gate. He values clear orders, reserves, and defensible ground. He will cooperate with investigation that solves an immediate military problem. He has no patience for removing engineers or troops from the wall on speculative grounds.

### Marshal Sera Venn

Venn commands the Host of the Lower Road. She is neither secretly friendly nor committed to extermination. Her objective is enforceable access to the Stone Lung after Kest Mourne rejected her conduit plate and demand for joint inspection. She honors local truces, exchanges prisoners, and continues the siege while negotiations proceed. Proof of Bas's concealed yield can split her command, but only if the party can also show a credible path to reopening the southern branch and a form of access Kest Mourne cannot revoke after the emergency.

### Engineer Korr Bas

Bas designed the Host's resonance weapons and the final charge. He survived Lorn's worst air failures and has concluded that Kest Mourne will never surrender its monopoly voluntarily. He is lucid about the risk of ignition. His leverage comes from technical command, isolated firing communications, and crews who can plausibly mistake his countermand for lawful necessity. Appeals to uncertainty do not move him; an executable retuning sequence, Venn's authenticated revocation, and a safe route for the crews to obey it might.

## Encounter registry and rhythm

### 1. The Lantern Court

**Mode:** command, testimony, and allocation.

The Court is the entry encounter and the adventure's command hub. Six tall lanterns correspond to the six principal sectors. Messengers, casualty lists, ration demands, captured signals, and contradictory technical reports arrive faster than the staff can reconcile them.

A local, foreign, or mixed company receives the six-lantern commission here, chooses its first commitment, and sees that every defensive resource has an opportunity cost. The writ permits bounded requisitions, emergency reallocations, evacuation, exchange, and temporary parley; it does not authorize surrender, permanent transfer of the Lung, or technical orders beyond what competent crews can execute. Later evidence can return here to change citywide orders, expose the old seal, authorize evacuation, or establish a negotiating position.

**Finale contribution:** command authority, reserves, evacuation routes, and the ability to make a settlement binding on the city.

### 2. The Shattered Gate

**Mode:** direct defense and battlefield intelligence.

The right tower has cracked, a murder gallery is cut off, and the Host is testing the breach with disciplined assaults rather than spending troops recklessly. Prisoners and captured signal slates show that some attacks are timed to subterranean hammer strokes.

The Gate must be held or deliberately shortened. Success preserves soldiers and prevents the Host from reaching the inner ring. Failure changes later encounters by bringing enemy skirmishers and refugees into the same passages.

**Finale contribution:** time. A stable gate delays the Heartstrike assault and keeps enemy reserves away from the Lung.

### 3. The Cinder Foundry

**Mode:** engineering, labor, and controlled danger.

The foundry casts braces, repairs pumps, and builds counterweights for the walls. It also receives fragments of the Host's resonance shells. Reconstructing their frequency reveals that they were designed against deep masonry, not the outer fortifications.

The foundry is overworked and politically divided between military production and repairs to civilian systems. A furnace accident or labor refusal can destroy the city's ability to build the apparatus needed for the finale.

**Finale contribution:** tuned braces, cutting tools, charge-disarming knowledge, and crews capable of working beside the Lung.

### 4. The Pale Gardens

**Mode:** ecology, food security, and distributed observation.

Terraced fungi feed most of the city. Several beds have turned translucent, while cave insects cluster near sealed vents. Enemy infiltrators have introduced a bitter mold, but not every failure is sabotage. The gardens are also registering the Stone Lung's altered airflow before instruments do.

The party can save harvests, distinguish poisoning from suffocation, and organize growers into a living map of air movement. Burning contaminated beds may be necessary; burning all suspect beds creates a famine the Host can exploit.

**Finale contribution:** biological filters, spore tracers, and ward-by-ward readings of whether a remedy is improving the air.

### 5. The Refuge Galleries

**Mode:** civil order, memory, and contested legitimacy.

Families from the outer wards crowd processional galleries beneath the council quarter. Rations, sleeping places, and rumors are becoming political weapons. Among the refugees are retired workers from the mold-blight emergency and descendants of families transferred from the sealed southern wards.

The galleries contain a memorial wall whose names do not match the official casualty register. Following the discrepancy leads to witnesses, private letters, and the sealed council record. The party must obtain truth without allowing accusation or suppression to turn the refuge into a riot.

**Finale contribution:** witnesses, civic legitimacy, labor, and an old maintenance passage from the memorial crypts toward the Lung.

### 6. The Cisterns of Black Breath

**Mode:** exploration, utility failure, and environmental hazard.

The lower cisterns have developed an oily skin and pockets of dead air. Water crews blame enemy poison. The deeper cause is pressure drawing gas through cracks around the southern seal. Old inspection marks reveal that the public conduit map was deliberately redrafted.

Clearing the cisterns keeps the city supplied and allows direct measurement of the black breath. Reckless fire, magic, or demolition can ignite a local pocket and foreshadow the finale's danger.

**Finale contribution:** gas measurements, safe venting routes, breathing equipment, and access to the Lung's lower valves.

### 7. The Countermine

**Mode:** infiltration, counter-sapping, and contact across enemy lines.

Defenders digging toward the Host break into an older lower road omitted from current maps. The passage contains enemy sappers, resonance cabling, and a route used by Venn's negotiators. It can become a battlefield, a covert approach to the charge, or the site of a local truce.

The party can learn that Venn and Bas no longer share the same end state. Captured orders establish the timing of Heartstrike. A sortie can destroy part of the charge, but unsupported demolition may bring the roof down onto the Lung.

**Finale contribution:** access to the enemy charge, contact with Venn, and the possibility of turning or isolating enemy sappers.

### 8. The Stone Lung

**Mode:** simultaneous technical, military, and political crisis.

The Lung occupies a cathedral-sized pressure chamber. Stone vanes flex around a geothermal throat. The southern seal lies behind an old service wall, now wired to Bas's charge. Three practical approaches enter from the Foundry galleries, the Refuge memorial passage, and the Countermine.

The finale begins when the Heartstrike state is reached or when the party deliberately enters to act first. Prior investigation determines available crews, measurements, authority, access, and time. No clue is a binary key.

**Finale function:** decide whether Kest Mourne and Lorn can both remain inhabited, and whether the military siege ends by repulse, truce, collapse, or mutual ruin.

Edges:

  B-C, C-D, D-E, E-F, F-G, G-B

The graph has fifteen distinct undirected connections and exact global edge connectivity three. Its smallest cuts isolate one of the degree-three sector encounters or the terminal. The ring means a party can move from one crisis to the next without repeatedly returning to the Court. The hub means command reports can introduce any neglected sector before the route narrows excessively.

The three approaches to the Stone Lung correspond to three kinds of leverage:

- **Foundry:** engineering and disarmament;
- **Refuge Galleries:** history, legitimacy, and the old civic route; and
- **Countermine:** enemy plans, negotiation, and direct access to the charge.

## Revelation architecture

The planned source will contain eleven necessary revelations, each supported by at least three clues from at least three source encounters.

### Encounter revelations

1. The Shattered Gate needs independent intervention.
2. The Cinder Foundry can identify and counter the resonance weapons.
3. The Pale Gardens defend the city as both food store and living airflow instrument.
4. The Refuge Galleries preserve witnesses to the old seal.
5. The Cisterns of Black Breath expose the altered conduit system.
6. The Countermine reaches the enemy's true line of effort.
7. The Stone Lung is the objective of the siege and the site of the final charge.

The final wording makes each revelation a conclusion the party can act on, not a place name.

### Conceptual revelations

8. **The siege is shaping access to the Stone Lung.** The outer attacks are diversions, tests, and time-buying operations for the lower-road plan.
9. **Kest Mourne sealed Lorn's air branch.** The act stopped a real blight and was then deliberately concealed.
10. **The enemy command is divided.** Venn wants enforceable access; Bas intends catastrophic forced opening.
11. **The Stone Lung can be retuned.** A shared-air solution requires pressure relief, black-breath venting, and controlled reopening in a specific sequence.

The optional return-to-Court revelation was retained because field evidence changes reserve movements, evacuation authority, disclosure, and parley terms.

## Clue independence principles

Clue redundancy must cross modes of play. Three documents saying the same thing are not independent in the useful sense.

The conclusion that the siege targets the Lung might arise from:

- attack timing at the Shattered Gate;
- shell-frequency reconstruction in the Foundry; and
- cabling and orders in the Countermine.

The truth of the southern seal might arise from:

- biological changes in the Pale Gardens;
- testimony and sealed minutes in the Refuge Galleries; and
- redrafted maps and physical masonry in the Black Cisterns.

The divided enemy command might arise from:

- prisoner testimony at the Gate;
- conflicting technical tolerances on foundry fragments; and
- direct orders or parley in the Countermine.

The retuning method might arise from:

- foundry models and surviving engine components;
- garden spore tracers and living airflow observations; and
- cistern pressure measurements and old valve marks.

The final clue list should avoid making every encounter support every central revelation. Each sector needs a distinct informational identity.

## Finale design

The Stone Lung should run on a visible three-stroke cycle once Heartstrike begins.

### First stroke: compression

The chamber seals. Pressure rises. Bas's charge arms. The Host begins its full assault above. The party must assign crews, secure approaches, and decide whether to contact Venn.

### Second stroke: draw

The Lung pulls air through every open branch. Black breath enters cracks around the southern seal. Measurements, garden tracers, and cistern work determine where it moves. A mistaken opening can poison occupied wards.

### Third stroke: release

The engine vents through the available branches. The charge detonates unless disabled, delayed, or taken over. The party enacts its chosen remedy while the city's military defense reaches its limit.

The cycle can repeat with worsening conditions if the party buys time but does not resolve the system.

### Complete remedies

#### Shared retuning

Vent the black breath into abandoned high galleries, brace the Lung, open the southern branch in stages, and synchronize Lorn's sappers with Kest Mourne's ventwrights. This is the most demanding remedy. It preserves both settlements and makes a truce materially enforceable.

#### Defensive reseal

Disarm the charge, collapse the lower road, and permanently choke the southern branch. This saves Kest Mourne and likely breaks the Host's assault. It leaves Lorn's underlying crisis unresolved and may turn the present siege into a longer war.

#### Sacrificial venting

Rupture an upper chimney and abandon the wards above it. This prevents ignition and restores air to Lorn, but the Stone Lung loses the pressure needed to sustain Kest Mourne at its present size. The city survives only through evacuation and contraction.

Other improvised remedies should be adjudicated from the same physical facts. Silencing the Lung or destroying the charge is not by itself a complete solution.

## Tone and voice

The siege should be serious without becoming uniformly grim.

- Describe pressure through failing objects, crowded spaces, casualty work, ration measures, and competing orders.
- Keep the attackers disciplined and intelligible. Do not make them an undifferentiated horde.
- Keep Kest Mourne worth saving. Its people are not reducible to the council's old crime.
- Let competent NPCs disagree because they are responsible for different losses.
- Give defensive success visible consequences: a gate still standing, clean water reaching a refuge, a harvest saved, a prisoner exchange honored, or a full breath returning to a crowded gallery.
- Do not make revelation of the old crime erase the immediate obligation to stop Bas.

## Risks for the next session

The authoritative source had to solve several design problems in play rather than bury them in explanation:

1. The Lantern Court cannot become a six-clue vending machine. Its reports must emerge through active command decisions and changing siege states.
2. The sector ring must support plausible lateral clues. Neighboring encounters should connect through personnel, infrastructure, or immediate consequences, not arbitrary directions.
3. Thirty-three required clues may be too many if each receives long prose. The source should keep clues compact and put encounter depth in encounter content.
4. The old civic crime must be discoverable without making every defender complicit.
5. The best remedy must be difficult because it requires coordinated resources, not because the party must collect all eleven revelations.
6. Venn's willingness to negotiate must not remove the need to hold the city. She requires credible leverage and continues military operations until she has it.

These governed the authoring pass.

### Encounter-writing standard

Each encounter now contains:

- an immediate defensive problem with consequences if neglected;
- at least one competent local authority with a defensible position;
- evidence obtained through the work of defending the city;
- a specific contribution to the Stone Lung finale; and
- failure consequences that worsen the siege without closing the adventure.

The source does not assign a universal siege clock. Escalation appears as lost reserves, buried tools, burned food beds, displaced witnesses, contaminated water, completed charge work, and reduced time during Heartstrike. The route test confirmed that these changes remain clear without another subsystem.

## Session-three route and table revision

Four priority-driven routes were tested through the authored clue graph:

1. **Wall first:** Lantern Court — Shattered Gate — Cinder Foundry — Stone Lung.
2. **Civilians first:** Lantern Court — Pale Gardens — Refuge Galleries — Stone Lung.
3. **Utilities first:** Lantern Court — Black Cisterns — Countermine — Stone Lung.
4. **Counterattack first:** Lantern Court — Shattered Gate — Countermine — Stone Lung.

Each route reaches the terminal through leverage appropriate to its encounters. The Foundry route reaches the crown and resonance controls. The Refuge route reaches the southern valves and civic platform. The Countermine route reaches Heartstrike and enemy command. The routes therefore do not collapse into three interchangeable entrances.

### Early terminal arrival

The route test confirmed that early arrival at the Stone Lung is common rather than exceptional. This is desirable: the terminal is a contested place, not a sealed final cutscene. The encounter now states explicitly that a party may reconnoiter, secure one station, disrupt peripheral preparations, open contact, identify missing assets, and withdraw. The second-look repair makes the boundary explicit: arrival and hidden reconnaissance do not start Heartstrike; detected seizure of the charge gallery, main cable, or southern valves can.

Preparation remains consequential. A party without pressure readings can still move a valve, but it risks carrying black breath into occupied wards. A party without foundry crews can still attack the charge, but it cannot perform clean detuning under pressure. A party without civic authority can still save lives, but it cannot make a durable joint-control agreement by declaration. Missing assets create cost and simultaneous failure, not binary locks.

### Siege escalation

The earlier draft relied on consequences distributed among the encounters but did not give the GM a concise procedure for bringing them into play. A universal clock was considered and rejected. Route length should not matter more than the quality of the party's decisions, and careful work inside a defensive crisis should not consume arbitrary turns.

The Lantern Court now defines a sector ledger:

- **contested:** the live crisis remains unresolved;
- **held:** the sector contributes its full or diminished finale asset; and
- **breached:** the encounter's `If neglected` consequence takes effect.

After a return to command, or after two consecutive field sectors, the GM reports one benefit from a held sector and one worsening elsewhere. Early reconnaissance does not begin Heartstrike. Detected seizure of the charge gallery, main firing cable, or southern valves does; so does delay after Bas's plan is exposed or the completion of his assault route through three breached sectors. It never begins before the party has established an approach and never resolves offscreen.

### Clue revision

Three clue descriptions were tightened without changing identifiers or graph semantics:

- the Refuge air tally now describes stale air below documented capacity rather than implausibly high per-person consumption;
- the Court's hammer triangulation now locates the enemy deep inside the wall line rather than using ambiguous vertical language; and
- the Gardens' foundry clue now shows soot pulled backward on the Lung's draw, making the infrastructure connection visible.

### Table-facing result

The adventure now supports four distinct opening priorities, lateral recovery after an incomplete route, reversible early contact with the finale, and visible siege escalation tied to actual sector outcomes. No tested route grants the shared-retuning ending by itself. The best outcome still requires several categories of leverage, but no specific encounter checklist is mandatory.

## Session-four runtime and playthrough revision

The named-company journal covers runtime behavior the route tests cannot: the Basalt Hand reaches the Lung after the Gate and Foundry, secures the crown, cuts one relay, records what is still missing, and withdraws. The second visit is a changed encounter, not a repeated scene.

### Fixed showcase route

```text
Court — Gate — Foundry — Lung — Gardens — Refuge — Cisterns — Countermine — Court — Lung
```

The route visits every encounter, revisits the command hub after evidence changes its authority, and revisits the terminal after the Hand has assembled the missing categories of leverage. The route is not presented as optimal. It follows the Basalt Hand's priorities: preserve military time, understand the weapon, test early access, measure the environmental danger, establish the city's act, split Bas from Venn, and only then attempt shared retuning.

### Runtime behaviors exercised

The 85-event journal records:

- ten visits, including two visits each to the Lantern Court and Stone Lung;
- every revelation established and every encounter available;
- thirty-two spotted clues, leaving fourteen authored alternatives unseen;
- held and costly-held sector consequences;
- a later Gate consequence that records an orderly fall after the sector bought the required time;
- a command return that changes evacuation, reserve, disclosure, and parley orders; and
- one append-only correction.

The correction follows the early Lung reconnaissance. A field report initially states that Heartstrike was disarmed. Hadrik clarifies that the Hand cut only the crown relay; the lower charge and manual trigger remain. The journal keeps the incorrect consequence in raw history, voids its operation, and records the narrower active consequence. The correction is plausible table behavior and exercises schema-version-3 semantics directly.

### Why no archived duplicate journal was added

The active example journal is sufficient to test the Run, Journal, Reports, and archive surfaces. A second archived copy would repeat the same playthrough without adding a contrasting route or state. Archive creation, comparison, restoration, refusal, and deletion were dogfooded against a temporary copy of the finished example instead. The repository therefore keeps one canonical Basalt Hand journal and no redundant archive copy.

### Product findings

The adventure fit the existing data model without backend changes. Encounter revisits, consequences, notes, explicit revelation establishment, generated summaries, and append-only correction all express the siege cleanly.

Two limits remain visible but do not justify feature work in this example:

1. **Sector state is prose, not a first-class field.** The contested, held, and breached ledger belongs to the adventure's procedure and is recorded through consequences. A dedicated generic status model would risk making the backend inherit one adventure's siege vocabulary.
2. **The Run workspace does not compare repeated visits side by side.** The timeline and per-visit notes preserve the information, but a GM running a return-heavy adventure may need to scroll between visits. This is a presentation pressure, not a domain gap.

No core functionality was changed during the four-session build.


## Voice pass

The voice pass left the graph, revelations, clue support, route, journal operations, and outcomes unchanged. It concentrated on the reader-facing prose. Revisions removed explanatory conclusions after scenes that already carried them, reduced repeated “not X but Y” constructions, replaced abstract design language with physical orders and consequences, and tightened the full playthrough around what the Basalt Hand sees and decides. The generated packet was rebuilt from the revised source.

## Second-look coherence pass

The coherence pass preserved the eight encounters, twelve revelations, thirty-six clues, clue identifiers, wheel-and-terminal topology, exact edge connectivity three, and the byte-identical 85-event Basalt Hand journal. It repaired nine source-level seams.
