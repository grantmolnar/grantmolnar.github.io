# The Refuge Galleries

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** civilians, history, social

Eleven missing ration sacks, a threatened witness, and forty-three omitted names turn bad air into public judgment.

## Opening View

The ration cart arrives eleven sacks short, and the Refuge Galleries move as one body. Families surge toward the council stair with yellow lamps, sleeping children, and memorial tablets absent from the public wall. A relief officer grips Deren Vask's wrist. A healer adds another collapse beneath the Lung's draw column. Above them, Edda Marr's clerk carries an order to close the crypt. The missing food is blamed on the Gate; the bad air on a branch the maps deny. Someone stumbles, and the whole accusation leans downhill.

## Encounter Material

## The crowd beneath the council

Eleven ration sacks are missing when the lower-gallery lamps turn yellow. Families sleep beneath civic reliefs while chalk marks divide air, water, and floor space. Conversations shorten. Children wake gasping when the Stone Lung begins its draw.

Deren Vask, a former vent mason, has accused the council of sealing Lorn's air during the mold blight. Relief officers move to arrest him for incitement as the short ration cart reaches the crowd. A quartermaster diverted the food to the Gate; the families see one policy in the missing sacks and the threatened witness.

Arrest restores silence by driving the old workers into hiding. A rush on the council stair may uncover records and destroy the command needed to use them. Protected testimony, a public accounting, or a hearing held while safer passages open can keep truth and order in the same room.

## Deren Vask

Deren laid masonry during the blight emergency. His crew was told the southern works were temporary quarantine. Afterward, supervisors reassigned Lorn's workers and ordered Kest Mourne's masons never to speak of the branch. Deren knows the service marks and the memorial stair. He does not know what now waits behind the seal.

For eighteen years he has heard his memory called age, drink, grief, or treason. Accept every claim without testing it and he becomes reckless. Test him in public, and he becomes exact.

## The memorial wall

The public memorial names the citizens killed by mold. Private family tablets name forty-three more workers, recorded only as transferred or lost on the lower road. Their families placed the tablets around an older civic service door and arranged the first signs as a ventwright mark. Grief preserved a route the records erased. Behind the memorial crypt, a narrow stair descends to the Stone Lung's southern controls.

Speaker Edda Marr arrives when command hears of the gathering. Her response depends on prior dealings: she may bring the sealed minutes and Venn's rejected conduit plate, or order the crypt shut. The minutes record a real lethal blight, a deliberate temporary seal, the expected loss of Lorn's air, the planned purge and filter works, and the later decision to keep the emergency measure after the contaminated strain died. Venn's ultimatum records the collapse of Lorn's last reliable chimney and demands inspectors from both cities at the southern controls.

## Water and gardens

Refuge healers have kept better records than the council. Fainting follows the Lung's draw, not meals. The worst cases cluster around water from the lower cistern line. Their ledgers point toward the Pale Gardens and Black Cisterns. The stewards who kept those ledgers can become the city's evacuation corps.

## If neglected

The ration dispute becomes a crush on the council stair. Deren vanishes into custody. Edda moves the minutes. Families scatter into disused passages with their lamps and records. The memorial stair remains discoverable, but no organized civilian force waits to empty the lower wards.
