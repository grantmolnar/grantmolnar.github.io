# The Stone Lung

**Role:** necessary, end  
**Tags:** finale, engineering, siege

Heartstrike turns the breathing engine into a battle for three approaches, five threatened sectors, and one military interval in which Kest Mourne and Lorn may both survive.

## Opening View

The war reaches the Stone Lung before anyone does. Dust pours from the Foundry gantry; memorial ribbons snap toward Lorn's sealed branch; the Countermine tubes carry Bas's order to arm Heartstrike. Above, the Gate drum calls Brann's last reserve while Host boots answer from the lower road. Every approach delivers the soldiers, crews, witnesses, and wounds the siege left there. The first counterweight strikes its stop, and a firing signal begins moving down the ceramic ribs.

## Encounter Material

## The chamber

Each stroke makes the pressure vault declare itself: loose cloth points toward the southern seal, counterweights climb into darkness, and porous vanes close around the geothermal throat. Behind a later service wall, Heartstrike ribs and cables web the southern seal; whether they are silent, charging, or under fire depends on when the company arrives. The chamber answers every intervention with pressure, heat, and movement; no one can treat it as a room between battles.

Three approaches enter the chamber.

- **The Foundry gantry** reaches the crown, counterweights, and charge harmonics. It favors bracing, disarmament, and retuning, but the Host can shell its upper supports from the Gate works.
- **The memorial stair** reaches the old civic platform and the southern branch's manual controls. It favors witnesses, evacuation, and public authority, but any mixed crew descending it is exposed to both cities' suspicion.
- **The Countermine gallery** reaches the charge and the Host assault force. It favors sabotage, parley, and direct confrontation, and it is the road Bas can reinforce fastest.

An approach is a military road, not merely an entrance. Whoever controls it can move orders, wounded, tools, and armed bodies into the Lung. Allies and enemies arrive by the roads the siege kept open, lost, or exposed.

## Arriving before Heartstrike

An early visit finds Bas still deploying the weapon. Reconnaissance can measure the Lung, reveal the southern controls, signal Venn, or cut a peripheral relay so that its silence resembles a fault. It also gives the enemy information. A missing patrol brings Host scouts. A moved valve warns Bas that the old controls have been found. A captured tube lets either command imitate the other's order.

Entering the defended charge gallery, severing the main firing cable, broadcasting a seizure, or moving the southern valves turns reconnaissance into battle. Bas then arms early and orders the Host to take whichever approach is least protected.

Before the first stroke, name the military deficit rather than an empty station: the Gate reserve committed elsewhere, a crew without an escort, civilians still in a gas path, Venn beyond signal range, or Bas holding the manual trigger. The deficit tells the GM where the enemy can force the next decision.

## Heartstrike under fire

Begin when Bas arms the charge or an intervention forces his hand. The Lung still moves on three strokes, but each stroke is also an exchange of orders and violence. Announce Bas's initiative, Venn's answer if she can give one, and the sector about to fail before the company acts. Then let the battlefield answer what they do.

### Compression: commit the reserves

The stone vanes close. Pressure rises behind the southern seal. Heartstrike charges its ceramic ribs. Bas sends assault troops through the Countermine while the Host launches its heaviest attack at the Gate. Brann must choose whether his last reserve holds the breach, escorts a technical crew, or descends to contest the charge gallery. A reserve cannot do all three.

### Draw: break the command chain

The Lung pulls through every open branch. Black breath enters cracks around the seal and moves toward occupied wards. Bas orders the southern valves pinned and the firing relays guarded. Venn may revoke him if proof and a protected signal reach her; otherwise her army continues pressing the Gate while Bas claims the chamber in Lorn's name. This stroke is won by severing, deceiving, or replacing a command chain while the gas path is turned.

### Release: survive the decision

The Lung vents through the branches still open. Heartstrike fires unless disabled, delayed, deceived, or seized. Bas reaches for the manual trigger if his crews abandon him. At the same moment the Host tries to convert any weakness at the Gate, Foundry, or Countermine into possession of the city. The chosen remedy must survive both the pressure release and an enemy attempt to take it.

The Lung may take another breath only if the defenders still hold enough ground to permit one. Each repetition costs a named sector, formation, or route: the Gate tower, the Foundry gantry, the Countermine mouth, a refuge stair, or the escort around the southern controls. Do not spend an abstract grace cycle. Show which soldiers withdraw, which road closes, and which civilians lose protection.

## Battlefronts and commands

The chamber concentrates the siege; it does not suspend it. Track the fronts that can still affect the Lung.

- **The Gate:** Brann's line buys minutes and controls the upper city road. If it falls, Host troops can descend behind the Foundry crew and Venn gains leverage at every negotiation.
- **The Foundry gantry:** Mera's workers and Tamar Ohn can brace or retune the crown, but only while an escort holds the shelling galleries and counterweight stairs.
- **The southern corridor:** Deren Vask, refuge crews, and witnesses can clear the gas path and operate the old valves. Their work decides who is warned and whether reopening is seen rather than merely promised.
- **The Countermine:** Bas's sappers, defectors, and any force sent to stop them fight over the charge, relay drums, and lower-road exit.
- **The command link:** Ilyra, Venn, Brann, and captured signal equipment determine whether troops receive one order, two contradictory orders, or none.

Ask who commands each surviving force, what road carries that command, and what enemy unit can interrupt it. Technical competence without a held road arrives too late. Military control without the right crew preserves only the weapon or the blockade.

## Korr Bas

Bas stands in the charge gallery or speaks through its tubes. He knows the ignition risk and accepts it because he believes Kest Mourne will keep Lorn dying for as long as caution remains an available excuse. Appeals to patience confirm him. A visible reopening sequence attacks the premise beneath Heartstrike; a promise made while the southern seal stays closed does not.

Venn's authenticated revocation can split his crews only when they have a protected route to obey it. If soldiers lower their weapons, Bas calls them deserters and tries to fire manually. If Kest Mourne seizes him without opening the branch, other Host officers may continue the assault under a cleaner command.

## Sera Venn

Venn continues the attack until Lorn has enforceable access to the Lung. Proof of Bas's plan stops the detonation while the claim that brought her army remains. She can order a ceasefire corridor, withdraw units from the Gate, or send trusted soldiers to hold Bas's crews apart, but each concession risks her authority while Lorn still waits for air.

She accepts a temporary truce only when reopening has begun or the company offers leverage that survives the emergency: public admission, custody divided across control keys and machinery, mixed crews physically occupying the controls, or records neither city can quietly erase. Ilyra can bind that compact for the present battle. Permanent control, reparations, prisoners, and withdrawal remain unsettled.

## Complete outcomes

### Shared retuning under truce

Venn opens a ceasefire corridor. Brann yields room for a mixed escort. Foundry and Lorn crews brace the crown, vent black breath into abandoned high galleries, fit living filters, and open the southern branch by stages while both armies watch the readings. The operation succeeds only if the truce survives Bas, a frightened defender, and the pressure release. Both settlements keep their air; neither army can claim sole possession of the controls when the stroke ends.

### Defensive reseal

Kest Mourne's forces take the charge gallery, disarm Heartstrike, collapse the lower road, and choke the southern branch permanently. The Gate can then be held or relieved. Lorn's air continues to fail. This is a military victory for Kest Mourne and the beginning of a larger war, not a repaired machine.

### Sacrificial venting

A fighting withdrawal clears an upper sector while engineers rupture its chimney and open the southern branch through the falling pressure. Lorn regains exchange. Kest Mourne loses homes, fortifications, and the population the damaged Lung can no longer sustain. The sector survives only if soldiers hold its evacuation road instead of the wall they were ordered to defend.

Other outcomes answer to the same battlefield. Destroying Heartstrike prevents one catastrophe. Killing Bas may break or harden the Host command. Silencing the Lung buys hours in which armies can still take ground. None of these acts settles who controls the next breath.

## Resolution

End when the pressure crisis and the battle reach a position no commander can immediately overturn. Name the held and lost sectors, the units that withdrew or surrendered, the road by which civilians escaped, the fate of Bas, and the order Venn gives her army.

The aftermath should look like a siege after a decision: six lanterns burning evenly with a seventh lit for Lorn while mixed sentries face outward; an upper ward dark and empty behind a new defensive line; Host prisoners carrying filters through the opened branch; or the southern controls buried while both armies prepare for another campaign.

At settlement, Ilyra reads the operational compact over the open command tubes. Edda releases duplicate minutes to the sector runners. The false southern map comes off the Court floor, a corrected plate is mounted where six commands can see it, and the southern keys pass to mixed crews who cannot turn them alone. That is Kest Mourne's admission: records, roads, and machinery no longer permit the city to restore the lie quietly. Later law can judge the blockade and the attack. The finale first decides who is alive, who holds the roads, and whether either city can still breathe.
