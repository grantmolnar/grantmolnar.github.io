## Summary

Processional galleries converted to civic refuge, joining relief records, the memorial wall, family tablets, an old service door, and the memorial stair to the Stone Lung's southern controls.

## Reference Material

## Processional stone under ordinary need

The Refuge Galleries were built for civic processions. Their reliefs, broad stairs, lower halls, service doors, and water connections make them a natural shelter when the upper city fails. Relief stewards keep air, water, ration, illness, and movement records close to the people those numbers describe.

## The memorial preserves a road

The public wall names citizens killed during the mold blight. Family tablets around an older service door preserve additional workers and arrange the first signs as a ventwright mark. Behind the memorial crypt, a narrow stair descends toward the Stone Lung's southern controls. Grief retained geography that official maps removed.

## Shelter does not settle testimony

The Galleries can gather witnesses, records, evacuation labor, and a road below. They do not decide who has arrived, whose testimony holds, whether records are released, which families move, or whether the memorial stair remains usable. Crowd pressure, rations, air, custody, routes, and civic standing change with the emergency.
