# Full Playthrough: The Ashlar Company

This playthrough follows the Ashlar Company through *When the Swine Kneel*. It is not an optimal tour of the adventure, does not collect every clue, and does not constrain fresh play. The company moves according to immediate danger, professional judgment, interpersonal disagreement, and the civic consequences of delay. The corresponding event journal is `play-state.example.json`; the generated runtime view is `generated/05-play-summary.md`.

## The company

- **Tessa Rane**, former ward sergeant, handles authority, interviews, and chains of custody.
- **Nell Harth**, drover and animal healer, reads the pigs as livestock before treating their behavior as evidence.
- **Orris Cale**, instrument maker and former civic-acoustics apprentice, designs the tests and interprets the buried machinery.
- **Sera Dain**, veteran sapper, manages unstable structures, evacuation distances, and failure plans.

Their route is:

```text
Hall of Petitions
  -> Southgate Stockyards
  -> Rillcross Farm Belt
  -> College of Civic Measure
  -> Hall of Petitions
  -> Chapel of the First Survey
  -> Nine-Mile Pump House
  -> Deep Bell
```

In shorthand: `Hall -> Southgate -> Rillcross -> College -> Hall -> Chapel -> Pump House -> Deep Bell`.

The order is not imposed by the graph. It emerges from the company’s priorities: save the Southgate herd before evidence is destroyed; learn the rural pattern before asking the College to explain it; return to the Hall only after law can accomplish something concrete; recover the old doctrine before confronting the official who changed the pumps; and enter the Deep Bell only after the company can control both the sounding network and the waterworks.

## Session One: A case before it becomes a scandal

### The Hall of Condemnations

Alda Mere expects to hire the Ashlar Company for a narrow inspection. Mara immediately asks for the proposed commission in writing and discovers that it allows observation and limited access but does not authorize the company to stop a cull, seize records, or interfere with water service. She does not reject the commission. She writes the missing powers in the margin and makes Alda refuse each one on the record.

Nell leaves the advocates to argue and speaks with the Rillcross petitioners. She separates the witnesses, asks each one to describe the pigs in order, and notices that all six farms report the same sequence: water refusal, rooting at buried metal, turning toward Veyr, and kneeling. The farmers disagree about causes. They do not disagree about events.

Orris examines the bronze pin while Sera walks the evidence courtyard. The pin carries an obsolete civic mark and trembles too faintly to hear. Sera feels a periodic movement through the paving, but not through the walls or the air. When the sixth bell sounds, the gilt in the courtyard stops digging, turns toward the inner city, and kneels. The pin moves under Orris’s fingers.

The company accepts the commission with three immediate concessions: custody of the pin, a two-hour stay on slaughter at Southgate, and preservation of the Rillcross petition bundle. Mara does not claim that Alda has solved the jurisdictional problem. She has only prevented the evidence from disappearing before the company reaches it.

The company recognizes three available directions. Southgate is the urgent crisis because the cull begins there. Rillcross offers the broadest pattern. The College has the instruments to distinguish resonance from disease, imitation, or possession. They choose Southgate first because lives and evidence are about to be lost.

### Southgate Stockyards

The eastern pens are already compressing against a cracked retaining wall. The nearest trough is fed by a channel the pigs will not approach, and the cull crew is arming outside the gate. Mara places the yard under the temporary commission and assumes personal responsibility for delaying the condemnation. Pell agrees only because the written stay still has minutes to run.

Nell and Ina Rill reorganize the herd around an older dry passage. Sera braces the retaining wall and coordinates the removal of injured workers. Orris sets the Hall’s bronze pin against a disused weighing frame. The pigs stop at an invisible boundary several breaths before either piece of metal begins to vibrate. The reaction precedes the measurable pulse.

The company does not yet state that the pigs are warning anyone. Nell insists that anticipation is not interpretation. The animals may be responding to something the company has not identified.

Beneath the eastern scale, Sera finds the socket from which a bronze marker was recently removed. Inspector Rusk Tal admits that Jalen Orr ordered him to take it. Mara offers Tal protection rather than threatening him in public. He identifies the mark as belonging to the dissolved First Survey and says similar instruments were removed near the surveyors’ roadside chapel.

The company also compares Pell’s condemnation order with Alda’s commission. The order claims authority the Hall never granted. Mara preserves the discrepancy but cannot yet turn it into a durable stay. One defective order will not restrain the Compact, the Office of Waters, and the farm quarantine together.

At the sixth bell, thousands of pigs kneel in silence. The buried beat rises through the rails and paving. The company keeps the cull crew outside, saves the eastern herd, and moves Tal into Alda’s protection. The stay expires the following morning. Their success has bought a night, not resolved the crisis.

Nell argues for Rillcross next. Orris wants the College, but she points out that a laboratory can only test the questions brought to it. The farms have six separate manifestations and one complete cycle before night ends. Orris concedes.

## Session Two: The farms teach the question

### Rillcross Farm Belt

The company divides among the six holdings and agrees to meet at Harl Rill’s kitchen table before the next resonance cycle. Nell works with handlers and ditch crews. Mara collects quarantine notices and correspondence. Orris times animal behavior against water movement and buried vibration. Sera inspects the places where pigs have rooted up bronze fittings.

A fresh subsidence opens beneath a child near one of the outer fields. Sera and Nell bring the child out without breaking the bronze diaphragm exposed below. Orris identifies it as a pressure vent connected to a larger buried system. The opening descends toward Veyr, but the earth is still shedding and the pressure changes with every pulse. The route to the central works is now known. The company explicitly refuses to take it.

This is the first major decision not to follow an available encounter. The Six-Line Bell can be reached, but the company lacks the operating doctrine, has no control over the pumps, and cannot yet explain the six lines. Early access is not readiness.

The farm families organize a synchronized watch. Across one cycle, six herds kneel at different moments, refuse different channels, and turn as the disturbance passes from line to line. Combined with the Southgate observation, the map settles the matter for Nell: the pigs sense the danger before the pulse arrives and distinguish failures the city’s surviving instruments no longer record. The company establishes that the pigs are reading the system rather than causing it.

Mara finds a copy of a warning sent to the Office of Waters and marked received before the behavior reached Southgate. It is evidence that Dast’s office knew something was wrong, but not yet proof that Dast personally chose the overdrive. She refuses to make the larger accusation from a received letter alone.

The company does not follow the Nine-Mile maintenance wagons. Doing so would abandon the farms before the timed observation and destroy the six-herd map. This is the first clue they knowingly leave behind.

They leave Rillcross with the pressure vent marked and lightly braced, and with Ina coordinating a permanent observation relay. Orris now has a field trace worth testing. The company turns back toward Veyr and the College.

### The College of Civic Measure

The lower acoustics laboratory is being closed when the company arrives. Archivists are loading First Survey volumes onto a cart addressed to Corven Dast. Mara does not seize the cart. She makes the water-office proctor read his authority before witnesses while Archivist Vos searches the charter for a stay provision.

Orris gives Lecturer Quill the Rillcross timings and the retained bronze pin. Nell supervises a borrowed sow so the experiment does not become cruelty disguised as science. The apparatus reproduces the buried frequency. The sow turns, kneels, and refuses a water basin only at that frequency; masking it ends the response without otherwise calming or distressing her.

The company now has an independent laboratory confirmation of the warning behavior, but that revelation has already been established from field evidence. The additional clue strengthens the public case rather than creating a new conclusion.

The pulse trace matches the phases published for the Nine-Mile station, but its amplitude requires a much higher load. A dated impound order shows that Dast suppressed the College’s first dangerous measurement before authorizing a larger water quota the next morning. Rector Sen will sign the trace and preserve the instruments, but she will not yet accuse Dast without an operating directive bearing his hand.

Quill models three possible responses: unload the hydraulic system, counter-ring the sounding network, or vent the strain through one line. Orris understands that more than one remedy may exist, but he does not yet treat the model as sufficient operating doctrine. The College can show mathematical possibility; it cannot show what centuries of neglected mechanisms will survive.

The company deliberately skips a second amplitude map of Southgate. They have already visited the yard and have a limited calibration window. Orris chooses to spend it on the pump correlation and response curves instead.

Vos finds the charter provision. Taken together, the defective Southgate condemnation, the Rillcross quarantine appeal, and the College’s right to preserve civic evidence give Alda enough law to act. Mara proposes returning to the Hall. Nell initially objects: every hour spent in Veyr leaves the farms under threat. Mara promises that every order will name the affected farms and sites rather than hiding them inside a general emergency. Nell agrees.

### Return to the Hall

The return is a real cost. It consumes most of the afternoon and places the company’s evidence in official hands before they know how far the concealment reaches. It also turns their discoveries into usable civic capacity.

Alda widens the commission in public. She stays the Southgate cull, suspends the Rillcross quarantine, protects the College and First Survey records, authorizes emergency entry into waterworks, and grants evacuation authority for named sites. Mara insists on named sites because broad emergency language is how rural losses disappear into administrative totals.

Orris accepts the delay because the final operation will need trained workers, citywide flow changes, and public warnings. Four specialists can reach the machinery. They cannot safely move a city around it without authority and allies.

The company now chooses the Chapel before Nine-Mile. They know the pump house is driving the danger, but confronting Dast without the old operating doctrine would leave them dependent on his account of the system. The Chapel may also explain what “venting one line” has meant before.

## Session Three: What the city buried

### The Chapel of the First Survey

The surveyor families are sealing the crypt against both rising water and an approaching Water Office seizure detail. Anja Veil refuses entry until the company accepts terms of custody, attribution, and respect for the dead. Mara agrees in writing. Sera recognizes that the founder’s tomb is the fastest route to the archive and the route most likely to destroy it. The company chooses the slower memorial sequence.

Sera braces the flooding aisle while Orris and Tomas Veil tune the memorial rods. The bronze plates release fragments of recorded instruction. The recovered folio maps the six Rillcross lines. An auxiliary plan shows how Nine-Mile can drive the older resonator beyond its drought setting. A register encodes the surveyors’ stair to the tuning crown. A disordered bronze tablet preserves three emergency remedies.

The College’s response curves and the tablet agree. Shutdown, retuning, and controlled collapse are all real. They differ chiefly in who bears the immediate danger and loss.

The records also reveal the Tanneries decision. During an earlier failure, the Surveyors vented a collapse beneath one district to save central Veyr. Sixty-three people died. The council dissolved the office, condemned the choice publicly, and kept the machinery in service. Nell reads the history as a warning against treating an outer tract as empty merely because its residents are absent from the chamber where the choice is made.

Sergeant Korr arrives with orders to seize bronze instruments and burn contaminated papers. Mara reads Alda’s stay. When Korr hesitates, she shows him the Tanneries tablet and asks whether he is willing to destroy the only doctrine that can keep another district from being chosen in secret. Korr withdraws rather than pretend his order is harmless.

The company leaves the Southgate wall plan in place. Removing it would endanger the aisle, and they already possess living observations and stockyard evidence. This is another clue left uncollected because its information is redundant and its extraction has a cost.

Anja agrees to accompany the company to the tuning crown. She does not grant the city ownership of the archive. She requires that the Surveyors’ record and the names of the Tanneries dead return to public proceedings after the crisis.

### Nine-Mile Pump House

The company arrives openly under the widened commission. Captain Thane recognizes the order but refuses to surrender a running industrial station without a safe transfer of command. Mara accepts the condition. She has not come to win a jurisdictional gesture while a cavitating pump tears itself apart.

Sera and Chief Engineer Salk stabilize the failing line. Nell frees Tamar Vey from the instrument room. Orris ignores the new gauge faces and reads the old mechanical controls beneath them. During the surge, the station team proves that the station can reduce load and alter phase without an uncontrolled shutdown.

Mara confronts Dast with three documents gathered at three encounters: Harl’s received warning, the College impound order, and Dast’s signed overdrive directive. The sequence is unambiguous. Dast knew the danger, suppressed the evidence, and increased the load.

Dast does not deny the chronology. He argues that the reservoirs cannot maintain hospitals, fire cisterns, tenements, and workshops through a sudden shutdown. His argument is materially correct. It does not justify imprisoning Tamar, falsifying the books, or leaving the farms unwarned.

The company removes him from command. Sera opposes removing him from the operation. Dast knows undocumented bypasses whose absence could kill the workers below. The compromise is explicit: Tamar and Salk hold operational authority; Captain Thane guards Dast; Dast may advise but cannot command guards, pumps, records, or prisoners.

This settles the second major open decision. Dast neither escapes through institutional protection nor receives a redemptive restoration. He remains useful, constrained, culpable, and present.

The company discovers the service conduit, the signed directive, the chapel seizure warrant, and the independent controls for load, phase, and venting. They do not search for the seized College recorder or the Rillcross pressure ledger. The College archive survived, and the farm relay is live. Those searches would add corroboration but not change the operation now before them.

Tamar and Salk balance Nine-Mile below full drought load. The station can execute either staged shutdown or a coordinated counter-phase. The company descends before the next evening cycle.

## Session Four: Five lines answer

### The Six-Line Bell

The company uses all three approaches rather than choosing one route for everyone.

Orris, Sera, and Anja descend the surveyors’ stair to the tuning crown. Nell and Ina enter through the Rillcross pressure vent with reports from the six herds. Mara joins Tamar, Salk, and the guarded Dast through the pump conduit. Quill remains above with the calibrated College instruments. Alda coordinates evacuations and ration priorities from the Hall.

The first change below twists the stair and floods the pump conduit. At the same moment Ina's runner reports the third-line herd leaving water and pressing west. Sera braces the crown but suffers a crushed hand. Dast identifies an undocumented bypass. Tamar drains the conduit without returning authority to him. Quill's needle confirms that the animals reported a moved pulse rather than a general worsening.

Before committing a remedy, the company argues.

Sera favors shutdown and bracing. It is the most structurally conservative choice, and she distrusts a retuning plan that depends on damaged rods, delayed relays, and live animal readings. Orris argues that the College curves, Anja’s sequence, and the current field relay make retuning possible. Nell rejects controlled collapse outright because every proposed target is inhabited and because the city has already shown what happens when it describes a community as a venting tract. Mara rejects a full shutdown once Alda confirms that even prioritized reserves will not protect both hospitals and fire cisterns through the ensuing unrest.

They do not choose a perfect remedy. They choose a bounded retuning.

The plan is to preserve five sounding lines, isolate the failing Southgate line, evacuate the eastern pens and warehouses, and accept reduced water service and local subsidence. No district will be secretly selected for destruction. The company is willing to lose infrastructure that has been publicly cleared; it is not willing to designate an inhabited tract as the hidden cost of saving central Veyr.

When Tamar changes the pump phase, the pigs no longer kneel together. The Southgate herd rises early and turns west while five Rillcross herds remain fixed. Nell identifies the Third Urban corridor as the break. Quill confirms that the divergence follows the intervention rather than the hour. Alda orders the named Southgate blocks cleared before the paving opens.

Orris and Anja reset five tone rods. Tamar and Salk rephase the pumps. Dast holds the manual bypass against the surge under guard. Mara keeps the chamber crews and surface relays under one command and refuses Dast’s request to widen the collapse zone when the Southgate rod slips. Sera crosses the throat bridge and waits for the counter-beat.

The Southgate rod tears free before the next farm report. Sera holds the release until Ina's lantern confirms that the eastern pens are empty. She strikes the counter-beat. Five corridors answer in sequence: kneeling, then water, then ordinary movement. The sixth goes silent. The buried river rises once, strikes the old foundations, and settles into the surviving network.

Above, the evacuated eastern warehouses and part of the weighing yard subside. No occupied district or farm tract is deliberately sacrificed, and no one dies in the collapse. Veyr retains reduced water service. The Deep Bell survives with one line permanently dead.

The pigs do not celebrate. The Southgate herd rises, drinks, and returns to ordinary agitation. Across Rillcross, the animals cease their common alignment as the surviving lines settle.

## Aftermath

The company carries copies of the operating orders, College traces, farm warnings, and Survey doctrine back to the Hall. Mara refuses a private settlement. Nell requires the farm witnesses to stand in the same chamber as the civic officers. Anja requires the Tanneries dead and the dissolved Surveyors’ Office to be named in the record.

Dast remains under guard pending a public hearing. His service during the retuning is entered into the record beside his concealment, falsification, and unlawful confinement. Tamar and Salk retain temporary operational command of Nine-Mile. The station is rebuilt under independent audit and public ration priorities.

The Southgate eastern pens remain closed over the dead sounding line. The surviving herd returns to its owners. The six Rillcross families become recognized custodians of the field pins and required observers for future changes to the network. The First Survey archive remains with the chapel families rather than passing automatically into city custody.

The Ashlar Company succeeds because it refuses both simple exoneration and simple destruction. It uses Dast’s knowledge without giving him command, uses civic authority without allowing the farms to disappear inside it, and accepts a bounded material loss rather than pretending that any remedy is free.

## Decisions fixed by this playthrough

1. **Chapel before Pump House.** The company learns the history and moral content of the remedies before hearing Dast’s practical defense.
2. **Dast remains an adviser under guard.** Tamar and Salk command; Dast provides undocumented technical knowledge without regaining authority.
3. **Five-line retuning.** The company isolates the failing Southgate line, accepts reduced service and evacuated structural damage, and refuses a secret controlled collapse.
4. **Thirteen clues remain unseen.** The company does not collect the five original alternatives—the Southgate amplitude map, Nine-Mile wagon trail, Southgate sounding plan, seized College recorder, or Rillcross pressure ledger—or any of the eight clue-density additions.

## What the journal demonstrates

The checked-in journal uses the runtime surfaces as intended:

- A clue can be spotted without immediately establishing its revelation.
- Conclusions can cite evidence gathered at several earlier visits.
- An optional revelation can justify a meaningful revisit.
- An unlocked terminal encounter can remain deliberately unvisited until the company is ready.
- Missed clues do not break progress because the authored graph contains redundant support.
- Visit notes preserve the company’s reasoning at the time.
- Encounter consequences record durable changes without rewriting the authored adventure.
- A full playthrough can remain separate from the reusable source and can later be archived, restored, or deleted through the journal lifecycle commands.
