## Summary

Veyr's First Deputy Clerk of Petitions, who turns witnessed facts into bounded public orders and widens emergency authority only when the record names its reasons, costs, and limits.

## Reference Material

## A commission written for one cycle

Alda Mere serves in the Hall of Petitions, where a complaint becomes civic fact only when witnesses, custody, and reasons can survive copying into the public record. She can commission a bonded local, foreign, or mixed company when ordinary offices are implicated in the same dispute. Method and accountable sponsorship matter more to her than prestige.

Her ordinary inspection writ permits witness interviews, inspection of affected livestock and public records, entry to nonrestricted civic works, preservation of listed evidence, requests for watch assistance, and short administrative stays. It does not itself interrupt water service, transfer property, authorize arrest or accusation, or place trained crews under unqualified outside command.

## Authority grows through named evidence

Alda can widen a commission when the docket supports each added power. A preserved chain of custody may justify longer stays, compulsory preservation or testimony, protection for named witnesses, temporary ration or evacuation priorities, or removal of a named official from custody of the case. She insists that a widened order identify the fact, the threatened harm, the person responsible for carrying it out, and the point at which the order expires.

## Public law cannot perform an engineering act

A Hall seal can protect messengers, records, observers, and crews; it cannot certify a measurement, make a dangerous shutdown safe, settle permanent ownership, or decide guilt without later process. The commission's bearer, recognition, exercised scope, stays, orders, custody decisions, and legal effect remain part of the play state actually reached.
