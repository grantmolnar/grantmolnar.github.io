# The Office of Waters

## Summary

Veyr's water authority, responsible for pumps, cisterns, mains, drought allocation, technical records, and the guarded operating chain through which a civic water decision becomes work.

## Reference Material

## Water is administered as one finite service

The Office of Waters operates Veyr's pumps, cisterns, intakes, mains, lower galleries, and drought schedules. It treats the system as a connected allocation problem: more service to one ward may mean less water for a hospital, fire main, tenement, market, or work crew elsewhere. Masters, surveyors, engineers, guards, inspectors, record clerks, and operators hold different parts of that responsibility.

The office inherited modern hydraulic works and partial diagrams from the dissolved First Survey. It retained auxiliary load controls and service knowledge but not the complete six-line doctrine, historical account, or independent surface map. Its ordinary powers over maintenance, safe access, operating records, trained crews, and water allocation do not erase the need for public authority when an intervention evacuates, rations, seizes, confines, or deliberately sacrifices an inhabited place.

## Command is divided even inside one office

A master may set policy, a surveyor certify a reading, an engineer reject an impossible sequence, a guard recognize custody, and an operator turn a control. Treating those roles as one transferable key hides both competence and accountability. The office can remain technically necessary even when particular orders or records are unlawful.

An office chart cannot make today's order executable. Officers, quotas, guards, records, custody, confinement, station control, service priorities, and settlement require named people and acts.
