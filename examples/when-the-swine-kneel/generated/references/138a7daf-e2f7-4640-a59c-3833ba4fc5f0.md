## Reference Material

## A warning instrument beneath inhabited ground

The Six-Line Bell, traditionally called the Deep Bell, is a bronze resonator wider than the Hall of Petitions and suspended above the black course of the buried river. Six sounding galleries leave its chamber, pass beneath Veyr's edge, and continue through Southgate and Rillcross. Counterweights, sluice gates, pressure vanes, diaphragms, a tuning crown, and hydraulic doors let the system distribute strain as well as announce it.

The Bell has no will and offers no terms. Its tones follow load, phase, moving water, structural contact, and changes made elsewhere in the network. Pigs reveal those ground-borne effects more clearly than the surviving central instruments: kneeling marks the central phase, water refusal follows pressure through a channel, and rooting exposes a sounding pin ringing out of time.

## Three approaches reach different controls

The Rillcross pressure vent reaches an outer diaphragm and can remain connected to farm reports. The surveyors' stair reaches the tuning crown and its six-line sequence. The Nine-Mile conduit reaches hydraulic load, phase, and pressure doors. Each approach can be entered without the knowledge, allies, or surface communication that would make it safe.

The sheet names the chamber and three approaches; it opens none. Air, water, damage, reports, tools, crews, keys, readings, and remedy follow the route and play state. The stable encounter identifier remains `the-deep-bell`.
