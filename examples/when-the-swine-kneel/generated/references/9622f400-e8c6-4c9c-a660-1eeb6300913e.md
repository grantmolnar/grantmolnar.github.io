## A modern station built around an older branch

The Nine-Mile Pump House straddles the buried river southeast of Veyr. Modern piston halls, intake works, galleries, sumps, service bridges, gauges, speaking pipes, and crew spaces surround an older auxiliary intake and pressure branch. The station feeds wards, cisterns, fire mains, markets, and lower districts whose needs become immediate when drought service is reduced.

Nine-Mile can alter three related but distinct quantities: hydraulic load, phase, and pressure. A staged reduction is therefore different from an uncontrolled stop. Pump order, valve timing, intake stability, downstream service, trained hands, and communication with the surface determine whether an intervention buys time, floods the lower hall, bursts the intake, or transfers strain into another line.

## Controls do not choose their own public cost

The older branch connects the station to the Deep Bell and provides one route into its chamber. Partial plans and undocumented bypasses can expose the relation without supplying the complete six-line map or safe counter-ring sequence. Mechanical traces can outlast falsified gauge faces, but a reading still requires competent interpretation and a lawful or emergency decision about what service will be lost.

A station diagram cannot say what a machine can bear now. Load, phase, pressure, gauges, pumps, crews, injuries, keys, orders, rationing, and conduit access belong to the live station.
