# The Hall of Condemnations

## Summary

Veyr's public petition house, where spoken complaints, copied testimony, named custody, and signed reasons give a disputed fact enough civic life to support bounded provisional orders.

## Reference Material

## A grievance must survive copying

The Hall of Condemnations occupies the ground floor of Veyr's old council house. Complaints are spoken before clerks and witnesses, copied into a docket, and either admitted to the civic record or allowed to die as rumor. Its hearing chamber, signing table, evidence courtyard, seal, and copying desks make authority visible: a witness can hear what was claimed, a clerk can name who took custody, and an official must place a name beneath an order.

The Hall is built for disputes among offices as well as private grievances. It can commission bonded investigators, preserve listed evidence, stay an administrative act for a short period, protect named witnesses, and widen provisional authority when later facts justify each added power. Those acts remain separable. Admitting a fact does not certify a measurement, transfer permanent property, decide guilt, or make a hazardous engineering sequence executable.

## Paper crosses the city through named hands

A Hall order matters because another office can recognize its seal, read its reasons, and identify the person charged with carrying it out. The same procedure exposes gaps: unsigned condemnation, indefinite seizure, and authority that cannot name its endpoint become part of the record rather than disappearing inside institutional custom.

The Hall does not carry its own case. The open docket, commission bearer, sealed evidence, temporary orders, and later findings belong to the encounter and journal.
