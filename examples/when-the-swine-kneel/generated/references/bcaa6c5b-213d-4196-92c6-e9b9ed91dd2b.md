## Water counted as public survival

Corven Dast rose through the Office of Waters by keeping pumps, wards, cisterns, fire mains, and lower crews functioning through scarcity. He treats the system as one table of finite service rather than a collection of independent machines. When another authority demands a reduction, he asks which hospital, tenement, market, fire main, or district will lose water and whose name will stand beneath that choice.

Three days before the Hall hearing, Dast ordered Nine-Mile to run uninterrupted at high load through the Convocation of Grain. ## Knowledge enough to conceal, not enough to repair

Restricted plans and the seized measurements told Dast that Nine-Mile was striking older civic works beyond tolerance. They did not give him the First Survey's six-line doctrine or a complete safe remedy. He can recognize a reading that survives scrutiny and will not dispute the same reading twice; instead, he shifts the argument to allocation, signatures, and immediate service loss.
