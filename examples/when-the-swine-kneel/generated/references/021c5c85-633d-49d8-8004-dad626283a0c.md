## Reference Material

## A recorder beneath institutional rank

Selya Quill lectures in acoustics at the College of Civic Measure. Her rank is low, but her apparatus recorded the dangerous buried pulse after Nine-Mile's first uninterrupted evening. She correlates pendulums, smoked glass, bronze pins, pump phases, and animal response through repeatable tests rather than intuition.

Quill's central claim is narrow: the pigs are sensitive indicators of a ground-borne disturbance, not its cause and not messengers carrying an intention. A controlled crossing, reliable Southgate observation, or sounding pin can align animal response with the laboratory trace. Her instruments can later distinguish a genuine reduction or retuning from silence caused by a damaged gauge, broken line, or destroyed witness.

## A measurement needs custody and certification

Quill can design and explain a test, preserve calibration, and name what a trace does and does not establish. She cannot alone bind the College, protect an archive, command a pump crew, or authorize a civic remedy. Her apparatus, trace, access, witnesses, institutional backing, route, custody, and participation in later readings remain live.
