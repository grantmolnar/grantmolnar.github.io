# The First Survey

## Summary

Veyr's dissolved surveyors' office, which mapped the six sounding lines, maintained the Deep Bell, named surface corridors, and was abolished after a life-saving collapse killed sixty-three people.

## Reference Material

## The office joined ground, water, and habitation

The First Survey mapped Veyr's foundations, buried river, sounding lines, pressure works, and the inhabited places above them. Its surveyors installed bronze pins at surface stations, maintained the Deep Bell's tuning system, recorded routes through the hollow, and named each line by the district, road, yard, or farm passage that would bear its consequences.

During the Night of Broken Foundations, surveyors vented a collapse beneath the Tanneries to save central Veyr. Sixty-three people died. The council condemned the judgment, dissolved the office, and quietly divided its useful remains. The Office of Waters kept auxiliary controls and partial service diagrams. The College retained censored catalogues, maps, and measurement traditions. Surveyor families kept memorial records, route keys, and the six-line doctrine at their chapel.

## Dissolution divided knowledge without ending responsibility

No successor possesses the whole operating system. Modern hydraulic competence cannot replace the surface map or historical sequence; chapel doctrine cannot allocate water or operate a pump station; College measurement cannot authorize a sacrifice. The office's history explains why Veyr must reunite knowledge without pretending that old authority survives intact.

The dissolved office leaves methods, not command. Surviving records, pins, plates, keys, copies, permissions, interpretations, and custody must be established in play.
