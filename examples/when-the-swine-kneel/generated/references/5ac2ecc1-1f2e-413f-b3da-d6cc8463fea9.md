## Summary

A roadside chapel and flooded crypt where the dissolved Surveyors' families keep their dead, the six-line operating doctrine, and a record of the sacrifice Veyr preferred to remember incompletely.

## Reference Material

## The archive is also a burial place

The Chapel of the First Survey stands on a low rise beyond Veyr's old boundary road. Its plain nave serves families descended from the dissolved Surveyors' Office. Beneath it, six tomb aisles correspond to the six sounding lines. Surveyors were buried with instruments and records they had been ordered to surrender, so opening the archive also means entering family custody and handling human remains.

Bronze rods in the memorial floor can be tuned against a current pulse. Correct tones open archive niches and wake recorded statements stored in resonant plates. The surviving doctrine names lines by the inhabited surface passages above them and preserves the sequence for resting, retuning, or deliberately venting one. It complements the Office of Waters' hydraulic controls and partial service diagrams; neither body of knowledge can safely operate the system alone.

## Memory is part of the safety procedure

The chapel preserves the Night of Broken Foundations, when surveyors vented a collapse beneath the Tanneries to save central Veyr and sixty-three people died. The city condemned the judgment, dissolved the office, and retained the machinery. The families insist that later users name custody, copies, attribution, the dead, and the surface cost of any comparable act.

The crypt keeps doctrine, not present permission. Water, damage, keys, warrants, consent, records, copies, custody, and any opened route are decided at the table.
