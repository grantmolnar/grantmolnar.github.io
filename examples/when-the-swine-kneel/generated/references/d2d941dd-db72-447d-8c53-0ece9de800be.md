## Reference Material

## The measurement Dast could not absorb

Tamar Vey is an under-surveyor in the Office of Waters, trained to compare pump phases, pressure changes, buried works, and surface effects. After Nine-Mile's first uninterrupted evening cycle, she matched the College's dangerous trace and the farm reports to the station load. She urged a staged reduction with immediate rationing and directed later readers toward the restricted plans and First Survey material.

Dast's office possessed fragments rather than the whole doctrine. Tamar knows the modern pump phases, the auxiliary branch, the separate controls for load, phase, and pressure, and the sequence by which a reduction reaches the buried system. She does not know every six-line relation without College, chapel, farm, and chamber evidence.

## A surveyor can operate without allocating the city

Tamar can test a reading, name a hydraulic consequence, coordinate with trained operators, and work the pressure doors. She cannot by technical expertise alone decide which district is rationed, authorize a deliberate collapse beneath inhabited ground, or transfer public custody. Her confinement, rescue, authority recognized, measurements in hand, route, cooperation, control of doors, and willingness to execute a particular remedy remain unresolved until play establishes them.
