## Reference Material

## The cost of an institutional signature

Olyra Sen is rector of the College of Civic Measure, which certifies Veyr's weights, foundations, bells, bridges, and public instruments. The College's authority depends on visible disinterest even though powerful offices fund much of its work. Sen fears the loss of charter, staff, instruments, and public trust; she will not falsify a conclusion, but she prefers conclusions the institution can still defend after the crisis.

A result becomes certifiable when the procedure is repeatable, calibration is preserved, witnesses remain named, and custody can be explained. Sen can convene a faculty hearing, commit College instruments and witnesses, and sign a finding that contradicts an office on which the College depends. Private work by one lecturer may preserve knowledge without giving it institutional force.

## Certification does not choose the city

Sen can decide what the College will test, certify, preserve, and state publicly. She cannot allocate water, order a cull, command guards, transfer chapel custody, or choose a corridor for collapse. Her present commitment, access to instruments, custody terms, certification, witnesses, route, and willingness to stand behind a later remedy remain live.
