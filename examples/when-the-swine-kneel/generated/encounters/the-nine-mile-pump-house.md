# The Nine-Mile Pump House

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** rural, industrial, confrontation

A falsified control hall keeps Veyr watered while its master decides whose danger counts as an acceptable reading.

## Opening View

A pump cavitates hard enough to shake rust from the roof. Workers drag a burned engineer from a falsified gauge while Corven Dast’s uninterrupted-load order lies open at the control rail. Oren Salk orders one line unloaded before the intake fails; guards wait for lawful command. From the locked instrument room, Tamar Vey answers the machinery with six measured blows. Captain Thane opens the company’s writ as the river lifts beneath the floor.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

Nine-Mile is under uninterrupted high load. One pump cavitates, workers sleep beside machines, guards hold the bridge, and Tamar answers from a locked room. After weeks of intermittent pumping, Dast's order has run three days with less than one ordinary day in the reservoirs and the Convocation crowding Veyr.

The first full cycle produced farm warnings, the College trace, and injuries below. Tamar urged staged reduction and rationing; Dast impounded the trace and raised the quota. Paint blisters in the halls, damped gauges read ordinary, and the station must keep water moving while its instruments, crew, and command are disputed.

Dast is present unless earlier events call him to the council or remove him from free command. When an instrument reading survives scrutiny, he marks it on the station board and demands a named service cut beside it. He does not dispute the same reading twice; he changes the dispute from whether danger exists to whose hand will sign the ration order. He will not sign first. The first emergency load may have been defensible. Concealment, increased load, unlawful confinement, and silence toward the farms followed measurable warning.

## Cast

- **Corven Dast**, Master of Waters, is controlled and formidable. He will accept disgrace if Veyr survives and assign mortal risk to people never asked to bear it.
- **Tamar Vey**, the missing under-surveyor, is locked in an instrument room after trying to trip the emergency gates. She holds the clearest recent measurements and no patience for Dast's paternalism.
- **Chief Engineer Oren Salk** keeps the station running because an uncontrolled stop could burst the intake and kill his crew. With recognized authority and protection, he supports staged reduction.
- **Captain Maris Thane** commands the water guard. Her loyalty is to preventing sabotage, not to illegal confinement or falsified records.

## What Dast knows

Restricted plans identify Nine-Mile as an auxiliary branch of an older resonant system and show separate controls for load, phase, and pressure. They omit the six-line network, the safe counter-ring sequence, and the Tanneries history. Tamar and the College trace proved that the present load was striking something larger. Dast ordered field pins removed before farmers and College staff could map that system. Removing passive markers hid the figure; it relieved no pressure.

Dast can operate Nine-Mile, identify undocumented bypasses, reduce or shift load, and reach the hydraulic conduit. At a control change, he names the key before he names the person who should turn it. If Tamar stands beside him, he gives her a reading without acknowledging why she was locked away. He cannot safely retune the Deep Bell alone.

## Immediate pressures

One pump cavitates. Each resonance cycle brings the intake closer to rupture. A sudden shutdown may flood the lower station; continued service drives the Bell toward failure. Dast intends to hold the schedule through the Convocation and reduce it quietly afterward. Tamar's figures place the next full-load alignment first.

The Hall's initial writ does not command a running water station. Open entry, worker channels, preserved records, a widened order, exposure of unlawful confinement, or sabotage each create a different chain of custody. A lawful transfer still requires Thane to recognize the order and Salk or another competent engineer to accept an executable sequence. Public accusation lets Dast call the company reckless before people who know what dry wards mean. Covert action may preserve evidence while leaving the crew unprepared for the next load change.

## Running the encounter

Alternate argument with industrial failure. Altered gauges hang beside older mechanical traces that resist falsification. Workers know when readings changed and fear prosecution for saying so. Tamar may be freed by law, stealth, persuasion, or force. Pistons, hot galleries, flooded sumps, and pressure doors provide danger without requiring a fight.

A surge should interrupt the dispute. One team must unload a line, vent a chamber, or keep a failing pump turning while another reaches the manual gate. Success buys hours and demonstrates that load can change without abandoning Veyr. Failure injures workers, raises the subterranean pulse, and makes Dast's claim to sole command easier to defend.

Dast hears evidence as a proposed service order. He writes the reading on the slate, then asks which ward loses water, which cistern empties, and whose hand signs below his. If no one signs, he cites the blank line as proof that only he is willing to govern. If someone does, he follows an executable reduction exactly while recording each cost against that name. He supplies no next concealed fact unless a gauge, worker, or seized book forces it into the room.

## Developments

Command can remain with Dast only by narrowing it in front of witnesses: Thane holds custody keys, Tamar holds the unaltered readings, Salk executes station orders, and Dast names bypasses under countersignature. Removing him transfers those four functions separately rather than pretending one replacement inherits his whole office.

Staged reduction slows the crisis and begins rationing; it does not restore the Bell. Destroyed pumps flood the station and remove a crucial control surface. A competent team left behind can hold a reduced schedule or prepare shutdown while the company descends, subject to injury, lost contact, or renewed orders. Nine-Mile keeps the first emergency decision and the later cover-up separate: practical necessity can explain load, not falsification and confinement.
