# The Hall of Condemnations

**Role:** necessary, start  
**Tags:** urban, civic, social, entry

A condemnation docket asks whether frightened animals, farm testimony, and buried bronze deserve an official life.

## Opening View

The veterinary condemnation order has reached the signing table. Harl Rill sets two dead piglets beside a rooted bronze pin; Veyra Doss names the cull’s cost; Jalen Orr claims the pin as Water Office property. In the courtyard, a gilt tears at the paving above the same buried line. Alda Mere turns a blank one-cycle commission toward the assembled witnesses. The clerk with the order waits for a name beneath the finding while bronze scrapes stone outside.

## Situation

Two dead piglets and a rooted bronze pin reach the signing table before the condemnation order. Harl's farmers can still be heard together; drovers demand another gate; the Compact demands a cull before rumor ruins the Convocation; Waters calls the pattern drought, crowding, and imitation. In the courtyard, a gilt tears at paving above the same buried line.

Nine-Mile entered uninterrupted Convocation service three days ago. Farm families have witnessed two evening cycles; the first city herd reaches the same phase at Southgate today. Veterinary condemnation, provincial quarantine, and Water Office seizures take effect at different hours the following morning. The next full-load sixth-bell cycle follows that evening. Once the orders scatter animals, witnesses, and records among separate offices, no docket will contain the whole case.

First Deputy Clerk Alda Mere needs observers outside Waters, the Slaughterers' Compact, the provincial reeve, and the College. She may commission a local, foreign, or mixed company able to post a civic bond or name a recognized sponsor. Prior service helps; the bond and a witnessed method matter more.

Alda's first writ lasts one resonance cycle. It permits inspection of affected livestock and public records, entry to nonrestricted civic works, witness interviews, preservation of listed evidence, requests for watch assistance, and short administrative stays. It does not interrupt water service, transfer property, authorize arrest or public accusation, or place another office's trained crews under unqualified command.

## Cast

- **Alda Mere**, First Deputy Clerk of Petitions, is exact, exhausted, and willing to widen a commission when the record can bear the reasons.
- **Harl Rill**, a respected breeder, lost two litters after his sows refused one ditch. He is angry and careful; he never claims more than he saw.
- **Veyra Doss**, counsel for the Slaughterers' Compact, fears market panic more than the animals. She presses for a swift cull paid for by the farms.
- **Jalen Orr**, deputy warden of the Office of Waters, speaks for Corven Dast. He minimizes the reports, reaches for the bronze pin, and records every question that interests the company.

## Immediate pressures

The hearing is one insult from a brawl. Doss has arranged for the condemnation order to be signed at dusk. Orr wants the pin classified as stolen civic property. Harl and the other farmers will leave if treated as fools, taking testimony and cooperation with them. In the evidence courtyard, a gilt has begun scraping at the paving directly beneath the council house.

Every alignment spends trust. Supporting the farmers opens Rillcross and hardens Waters. Accepting an official escort grants access while letting Orr choose what is shown. A general emergency stays more orders and also summons panic, supervision, and officials eager to inherit command.

## Running the encounter

The hearing should produce a record, not one persuasion check. Witnesses can be separated; dates and accounts compared; the pin and gilt examined; the condemnation order traced; the writ negotiated word by word; Orr's claims entered under his own name. The farmers disagree about cause and timing. They agree on the sequence: water refusal, rooting at buried metal, turning toward Veyr, and kneeling.

At the sixth bell, the gilt stops digging, faces the inner city, and lowers itself onto the stones. The pin trembles on the evidence table. Few people feel the pulse. Everyone sees the animal. Cause, jurisdiction, and remedy remain disputed; the docket can no longer honestly record that nothing happened.

## Developments

A hearing kept intact gives Alda a durable basis for later orders. Evidence gathered elsewhere may support stays on culls, quarantines, and executive seizures; compulsory preservation and testimony; protection for named witnesses; suspension of a named official from custody of the case; and temporary ration or evacuation priorities through the council's emergency minute. These powers remain provisional. They do not decide guilt, transfer permanent ownership, choose a district for collapse, or let untrained investigators dictate what machinery can safely do.

Disorder brings a hostile overseer and a narrower writ. A pin carried away by Orr reappears among seized materials at Nine-Mile; a pin kept under the Hall seal can be matched against marks elsewhere.

On departure, place three urgent entries beside the commission: Southgate's public crisis, the College's threatened instruments, and Rillcross's wider pattern. None is the prescribed first route.

The two-day clock does not require one company to perform every later task. Once a method, responsible witnesses, tools, and authority are named, yard crews, farm observers, College staff, chapel custodians, or pump workers may continue bounded work. Record who holds each task and what can sever it. Delegation creates vulnerable parallel action, not automatic success beyond the table.
