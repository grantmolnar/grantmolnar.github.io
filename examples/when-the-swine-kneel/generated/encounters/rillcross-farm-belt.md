# Rillcross Farm Belt

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** rural, livestock, pattern

Six farms turn separate animal warnings, water refusals, and rooted pins into one buried map.

## Opening View

Quarantine boards rise at six farm lanes while a child remains somewhere below Seva Hane’s collapsed orchard. One family hides its herd, another offers theirs for confiscation, and the pigs at the refused ditch nose dry buckets beside running water. Ina Rill’s flags mark separate kneelings, rootings, and panics, but no holding can see the whole pattern. The ground shudders. Farmers who would not share a boundary rope begin passing it hand to hand toward the hole.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

Quarantine boards rise at six lanes while six households report warnings that do not behave like one disease. One herd rejects a running ditch but drinks from a well; another roots at bronze; another turns toward Veyr; another lies down along subsiding ground. Ina's flags place the reports on six lines converging on the city, Southgate on the third. No holding sees the whole pattern, and a child is missing beside the clearest break.

The first coordinated warning followed Nine-Mile's uninterrupted schedule. Buyers have stopped coming. The provincial reeve has drafted quarantine and confiscation orders. Drought makes every ditch and field indispensable; even a temporary closure threatens the autumn planting. Neighbors argue over hiding the animals, driving them into Veyr as protest, or submitting before the watch arrives.

## Cast

- **Harl Rill** is a careful witness and reluctant spokesman. He wants the farms preserved but accepts hard measures once their necessity is shown.
- **Ina Rill** moves between holdings, knows the pigs individually, and notices a changed pattern before anyone else.
- **Reeve Calven Brost** holds lawful quarantine authority. Dast has not taken him into confidence. Evidence can move him; fear of carrying disease into Veyr can move him faster.
- **Seva Hane**, an elderly farmer whose land holds the clearest pin, remembers a forbidden children's rhyme about "the bell below the boar."

## Immediate pressures

A useful reading requires several herds tracked through late afternoon and fixed at the sixth bell. Brost intends to post quarantine first. One refused ditch is the only practical water source for a crowded holding. A shallow collapse has opened beside Seva's orchard, and a child who followed a loose pig is missing below the lip.

Standing places and trusted witnesses matter. The rescue can expose an old sounding chamber. Delaying confiscation may stake Alda's writ on a pattern not yet proven. Mapping the arcs requires families who blame one another for official attention to share boundaries, clocks, and signals.

## Running the encounter

Treat Rillcross as three linked sites within a compact landscape: the refused ditch, Seva's survey field, and the fresh subsidence. Movement among them shows the warning changing with distance and water flow. The pigs do not point toward Veyr at every moment. Separate herds answer separate lines; one erupts whenever distant machinery changes load.

Survey the arcs, excavate a pin, compare waters, question ditch crews, follow a loose boar to a hidden vent, or enter the collapse. Below waits no dungeon: loose soil, stale air, a child in darkness, and a bronze diaphragm shuddering under pressure. Damaging it quiets one local sign and drives another herd into panic. The animals carry no contagion or message. They register strain.

At the sixth bell, the farms answer in sequence. One herd kneels; another refuses its trough; a third tears open the earth above a pin. From a ridge or on a shared map, the movements draw six lines inward through the city edge.

## Developments

A method, assigned line, agreed signal, and protection from immediate seizure turn Rillcross into a live relay. Ina and the farm observers can continue reporting while the company visits the College, chapel, Hall, or Nine-Mile. Confiscation, panic, broken roads, or a worsening line can still silence them.

Dispersed or seized herds end the live readings and harden the families' anger. A severe failure widens the subsidence and consumes part of a holding before Veyr admits danger. What survives this encounter is practical rather than merely explanatory: a six-line map, the third line through Southgate, named surface observers, and an outer pressure vent descending toward the Bell.
