# Southgate Stockyards

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** urban, livestock, crisis

Thousands of thirsty pigs hold one buried line while cullers, drovers, and a failing wall spend the same minutes.

## Opening View

The eastern wall bows under thousands of thirsty pigs while handlers clear an injured drover from the rails. The herd refuses one invisible line through pen, trough, and paving; outside, the cull crew passes out poleaxes. Ina Rill spills flour across the boundary and calls for the old dry passage opened. Inspector Rusk Tal stands over an empty marker socket with one hand inside his coat. The first rank kneels toward Veyr. Behind it, the whole yard lowers faster than the gates can be moved.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

Several thousand Convocation pigs refuse one line through the eastern pens. Forced across it, they scream, wheel, and crush back against the rails; at dusk they turn toward Veyr and kneel pen after pen. The retaining wall bows, handlers pull injured drovers clear, and the cull crew waits outside the gate.

Buyers call it disease. Drovers cannot move their stock, and the watch must keep the gates open without allowing a riot. The Compact wants condemnation before the next pulse; Ina wants room to read what the herd refuses. Delay spends water, wall strength, and control of the yard.

## Cast

- **Seln Marr**, yardmaster, puts stampede prevention before every theory and cooperates with whoever lowers the immediate danger.
- **Ina Rill**, Harl's daughter and an expert handler, has recognized a buried boundary rather than a smell or visible obstacle.
- **Factor Osric Pell**, speaking for the Slaughterers' Compact, believes killing the eastern herd is the only way to save the market. Every delayed hour enlarges the loss he expects the city to pay.
- **Inspector Rusk Tal**, a junior water inspector, removed a bronze marker from beneath the eastern scale on Orr's orders. He now knows the order was irregular and fears both exposure and Dast.

## Immediate pressures

Poleaxes are being issued. The packed animals dehydrate beside a trough fed by the channel they refuse. A retaining wall has cracked and bows under the herd's weight. If rails or masonry fail, people die before the warning can be read.

Observation requires space made by work: suspend or delay the cull, reorganize pens with Ina, open the older dry passage, move gates, extract injured drovers, or place the animals under the commission's temporary responsibility. Handle the pigs as frightened livestock. Their weight, thirst, and panic are the danger.

## Running the encounter

The invisible boundary crosses masonry laid centuries after the original paving. A disused brass weighing frame vibrates as pigs approach. The removed marker left a socket stamped with an obsolete civic emblem. Drainage plans disagree with the water moving below the pens. Drovers and watch officers can reconstruct Tal's arrival, what he removed, and whose seal he carried.

At the sixth bell, thousands of bodies settle. Iron rails stop rattling. A low beat rises through boots, stone, and teeth. Instruments or magic confirm a vibration traveling through the ground rather than the air. After dark the animals rise into ordinary thirst and distress.

## Developments

Keeping the cull from beginning wins drovers who range across Rillcross and can carry later warnings. Saving workers while losing hundreds of animals leaves the evidence bloodied and the demand for blame sharper. If killing begins before the pulse, wounded pigs still struggle to kneel when it arrives. Keep the sight grave rather than lurid.

Tal may be protected, pressured, or surrendered. Under protection he describes the missing marker and admits that similar pieces were ordered removed near a roadside chapel. Careless exposure lets Orr recall him and turns Water Office obstruction public. Southgate should leave behind control or loss of the gates, a living urban reading on the third line, and a named chain of custody for the first removed instrument.
