# The Six-Line Bell

**Role:** necessary, end  
**Tags:** subterranean, finale, hazard

Six surface corridors let Veyr read a buried failure through living herds before officials can hide which district will pay.

## Opening View

The first message reaches the chamber before the inspection company does: third-line pigs have left water and pressed their flanks against the western rail. Then the Deep Bell sounds below Veyr, and dust lifts from six galleries at six different moments. A second runner reports rooting in Rillcross; a College needle scratches backward; the pump-house speaking pipe fills with river water. The Bell has not begun a neat countdown. It is asking the surface the same question through thousands of frightened bodies, and the answers are already diverging.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

The bronze Bell hangs above the buried river, wider than the Hall, with six galleries running toward Veyr and Rillcross. The company reaches only the controls its route exposes. The Bell may be silent between strokes, ringing under full load, or shuddering after an earlier intervention. Dust lifts from different galleries; the river hesitates, then surges; surface reports disagree.

The system is beyond tolerance. Its lowest tone travels through teeth and joints. Above, pigs may kneel, refuse water, root at pins, or panic as pressure moves. Do not assume all six lines still report, every route remains open, or a known control has a crew able to use it.

## Ways into the hollow

Each route enters a different part of the buried system and remains answerable to the surface above it.

- **The Rillcross pressure vent** descends through unstable earth beside an outer diaphragm. Farm messengers can still reach it with live herd reports while the lanes remain organized. Cave-ins, bad air, and sudden releases threaten anyone below.
- **The surveyors' stair** descends from the chapel to the swaying tuning crown. Its scratched sequence can align a report with the rod that produced it. The stair sheds stone whenever the crown changes phase.
- **The pump-house conduit** enters beside the hydraulic controls. It exposes load, phase, and pressure doors to direct intervention. Unless Nine-Mile was balanced, river water fills the conduit whenever the pumps unload.

A missed encounter does not erase a route. It removes the people, warning, or interpretation that would make the route intelligible. A company can descend through ignorance; the surface still bears the result.

## Cast and forces

Failing structure, public fear, and incompatible duties supply the opposition. Armed resistance arises only from officials or crews protecting incompatible orders.

- **Corven Dast** may arrive free, guarded, absent, or represented by loyal officers. He cooperates with plans that protect immediate water needs and, while he retains command, directs risk toward farms and poor districts before central service fails.
- **Tamar Vey** calls for staged shutdown unless a credible live reading supports retuning. She knows the pump phases and will operate the hydraulic doors herself.
- **Anja Veil** will not permit another district to be sacrificed from a buried room. She knows the surveyors' sequence and refuses any collapse whose surface corridor has not been named and warned.
- **Alda Mere, Olyra Sen, Harl and Ina Rill, Selya Quill, Oren Salk**, and other preserved allies carry writs, measurements, animal reports, labor, and public trust between the chamber and the people above.

The Bell has no will and offers no terms. Echoes can answer like speech, but every reply follows phase, load, moving water, a struck surface, or an animal reacting somewhere along a line.

## The living survey

No instrument below can show what all six lines are doing. Run the finale through reports arriving from the surface and changes made in the chamber.

- **Southgate** supplies the third-line herd: mass kneeling marks the Bell's central phase; refusal at one trough marks pressure passing through its channel; a turn toward a gate shows the pulse moving beyond the old weighing station.
- **Rillcross** supplies separate herds along all six corridors. Rooting finds ringing pins. A line of animals lying down can mark subsidence before masonry opens. Conflicting movement reveals that the network is separating rather than failing as one body.
- **The College** supplies calibrated needles and phase models. Its readings can distinguish a true reduction from a silence caused by a broken instrument.
- **The chapel** supplies the old sequence and the doctrine that a line is named by its surface passage, not by a number on a buried rod.
- **Nine-Mile** supplies pump load, reservoir consequence, and the moment a hydraulic change reaches the Bell.
- **The Hall** supplies temporary protection for messengers, evacuations, ration orders, and removal of an official who tries to suppress an unwelcome report.

Keep at least one means of communication visible: runners, drovers changing lanterns at shafts, speaking pipes, College wire, bells at the chapel, or magic. When a route is cut, the information becomes late, partial, or dangerous to obtain. Do not replace it with an omniscient diagram.

## Reading the failure

Begin with the latest animal behavior and ask what changed just before it. Every major intervention below produces a new report above. Every delay lets pressure choose another surface expression.

Use four kinds of development, in any order demanded by the fiction:

### A line changes its answer

Pigs that were kneeling rise and refuse water. A herd that rooted at one pin turns toward the next holding. Southgate and Rillcross disagree. The change may reveal a pulse moving, a line splitting, a pump phase arriving, or a false improvement caused by a broken corridor.

### A surface corridor becomes inhabited danger

A ditch swells, a warehouse floor heaves, a lane cracks, a ward main bursts, or a holding begins to settle. Name the people and animals there. Warning and evacuation can clear the corridor; secrecy converts it into an unwilling sacrifice.

### Veyr denies the reading

Dast's officers seize a runner, a ward refuses rationing, merchants call the farm reports fraud, or a clerk announces that quiet animals prove success. Evidence must travel through frightened institutions as well as through soil.

### The hollow answers an intervention

A pressure door opens, the Bell shifts phase, the buried river enters a gallery, or the tuning crown throws one rod out of sequence. The response may improve one corridor while worsening another. Report the corresponding herd before declaring the operation successful.

There is no fixed allotment of three cycles and no abstract grace extension. The failure continues until a remedy changes the pattern, the surface network is lost, or uncontrolled collapse makes the remaining choices immediate. Use the pigs to show whether Veyr has actually changed the system or merely stopped listening.

## Remedies

### Rest the load

Stop Nine-Mile in the sequence Tamar gives, damp the Bell, and let the buried river move toward its old course. The first proof is not quiet in the chamber. It is water refusal ending corridor by corridor while reservoir gauges fall above.

Hospitals, fire cisterns, and crowded wards can retain service if the Hall prepared priorities. Manufactories and upper districts go dry. Without public preparation, the safest structural remedy produces preventable fires, deaths, and unrest.

### Retune by living reports

Rephase the pumps and drive a controlled counter-ring while handlers, farmers, and College observers report each line's answer. The crown operator follows not a preset station schedule but the sequence made by current herds: which corridor kneels, which roots, which drinks again, and which must be isolated.

Every line must be heard; every line need not survive. A damaged corridor can be cleared and cut away while the other five return to phase. This preserves most water service and avoids deliberate destruction of an inhabited district, but places operators and surface observers in danger each time the network changes. Magic or instruments may replace one reading. They do not make absent households empty.

### Open a named corridor

Vent the hollow beneath one district or tract. Maps identify candidates; live reports show which corridor can take the release. The people above must be warned, moved, or knowingly abandoned before the vanes open.

Outer Rillcross fields, Southgate warehouses and pens, an industrial water yard, or a dense ward can each save the rest of Veyr at a different human cost. Anja demands the place be spoken aloud. Dast may try to call it only “the third line.” The operation becomes a civic crime when the buried number replaces the inhabited name.

### Break the warning

Shatter the tuning crown, wedge the resonator, or drop it into the buried river. The pigs may quiet because the signal has stopped reaching them. The pumps remain loaded, the river remains displaced, and the hollow remains unstable.

This choice buys a few hours in which officials can proclaim success. The last honest evidence may be a Rillcross herd still refusing water after every city gauge has gone still.

## Resolution

Do not end on the chamber alone. Call for reports from every surface corridor still reachable. Name where pigs drink, where they continue rooting, where a herd was moved, where water has stopped, and where people were warned before the ground opened.

A five-line retuning, a rationed shutdown, or an evacuated collapse is a complete outcome with a visible loss. If Dast works the controls, require him to speak every affected corridor by its inhabited name while Thane repeats the order and seals the changed gauge. He may turn the correct key and leave the chamber without it. A broken Bell may spare the company and leave the city blind. Total failure is the moment the reports cease because the messengers, animals, or surface passages are gone—not a single unsuccessful operation below.

The Hall can preserve evidence, protect witnesses, continue temporary ration and evacuation orders, and hold immediate offenders for review. Permanent water governance and criminal judgment come later. The finale ends when Veyr can state, in public and by inhabited names, which lines still carry water and who paid for the ones that do not.
