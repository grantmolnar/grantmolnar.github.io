# Party Design: The Lantern Measure

**Status:** Finalized and exercised by `play-state.example.json`.

## Purpose of the checked-in journal

The Lantern Measure demonstrates a traditional whodunnit in which good investigators are neither omniscient nor morally uniform. They begin with a plausible suspect, enter one inference too strongly in their notes, correct it without erasing the mistake, and later pursue a second wrong theory through the village accounts. They solve the murder by separating real misconduct from the sequence that killed Merrit Alder.

The journal exercises the following adventure and runtime behaviors:

1. the Lantern Measure opens all major investigative routes from the crime scene but visits only eight of the ten investigative encounters;
2. a suspect can lie about scene evidence and still be innocent of murder;
3. a financial discrepancy can be criminal or improper without belonging to the murder motive;
4. a mistaken working inference is voided by an append-only correction rather than deleted;
5. child testimony is separated and corroborated rather than treated as a single decisive statement;
6. the exact weapon is identified late and remains corroborative rather than indispensable;
7. all necessary revelations are established while two optional suspect secrets remain unresolved; and
8. the final public case protects the school beneficiaries without weakening the finding against Orlo.

The route leaves Moss Apothecary and Bramble Mill unvisited. Perrin Moss's altered dosage records and Cora Bramble's pre-supper confrontation therefore remain private uncertainties. The company does not pretend that solving Merrit's murder gives it a warrant to finish every other inquiry in Bramblewick.

## The Lantern Measure

The **Lantern Measure** is a four-person company that handles disputes where ordinary adventuring competence and civic process overlap: missing cargo with several owners, contested boundaries after floods, dangerous inheritances, accusations made beyond a magistrate's immediate reach, and violence whose evidence will not survive a week of rumor.

Merrit encountered them the previous spring during a toll-house dispute. He disliked their refusal to accept his private recollection as a public record and remembered that refusal as a qualification. His invitation to the Founders' Supper promised good food, a modest witness fee, and “an audit whose objections have cousins.”

The company takes its name from its ordinary field kit. A hooded lantern reveals texture and erasures; a folding measure tests distance, timing, and proportion. Neither tool decides what the facts mean.

### Teren Malk

Elian is a human former circuit bailiff and the company's formal speaker. He spent twelve years carrying warrants between villages whose customs differed more than their roads. He left after a magistrate ordered an arrest on a noble household's uncorroborated letter and later called the resulting death an unfortunate misunderstanding.

**Useful habits**

- Separates grounds for questioning, detention, public accusation, and final finding.
- Preserves custody and witness order before pursuing an elegant theory.
- States a case as propositions that can be challenged independently.

**Pressure point**

Elian distrusts informal mercy because he has seen powerful families call concealment reconciliation. When Pella argues that beneficiary names need not be read aloud, he initially hears a request to weaken the record. He must learn that a narrow exhibit can be complete rather than evasive.

**Likely concern in this adventure**

He treats Bram as the first serious suspect and is slowest to release that theory. At the moot, he presents the six-part reconstruction and insists that Orlo be remanded to the circuit magistrate. He also insists that the finding precede any discussion of Orlo's charitable purpose.

### Pella Reedbank

Pella is a halfling advocate from Reedbank, a larger share-village two valleys east. She apprenticed under its hearth keeper, resigned after discovering that publicly correcting a widow's irregular allotment would have left three children without winter grain, and then spent several years helping villages write procedures that did not require private need to become public humiliation.

**Useful habits**

- Reads kinship, inheritance, and common-share records without assuming that a technical irregularity has one moral meaning.
- Asks what must be disclosed to prove a proposition and what is merely available to expose.
- Hears the difference between a villager protecting a person and protecting an account of events.

**Pressure point**

Pella is inclined to preserve communal arrangements that keep people fed, even when they are unauthorized. Orlo's school fund attracts her sympathy before the murder evidence becomes complete. She must distinguish protecting the beneficiaries from protecting the person who made himself their secret patron and judge.

**Likely concern in this adventure**

She prevents the company from combining Hester's bridge spending, Mara's payments, and the ghost hearths into a single theory. She arranges sealed chapel and Common Chest exhibits, keeps the beneficiary box closed in public, and proposes a lawful replacement fund after Orlo's arrest.

### Sable Quoin

Sable is a dwarven factor, book examiner, and practical appraiser. She served a mining syndicate that measured every worker's debt precisely and every owner's obligation approximately. She departed with copies of both ledgers and has since made a profession of finding where a record changes hands, units, names, or purpose.

**Useful habits**

- Reconstructs a transaction from authorization, custody, redemption, and final use.
- Distinguishes missing money from concealed purpose and duplicate payment from a fictitious payee.
- Produces narrow exhibits that another clerk can reproduce without trusting her conclusions.

**Pressure point**

Sable regards common property as a promise made by every contributor. She reacts strongly to Hester's unauthorized spending and Orlo's forged hearths, even when both were directed toward defensible public ends. Her first accounting theory places too much weight on who controlled the chest and too little on the separate warrant chains.

**Likely concern in this adventure**

She briefly favors Hester as the financial culprit, then disproves her own theory by reconciling the bridge file. She traces the ghost-hearth warrants through Orlo's clean-copy hand to the school and designs the three-custodian relief fund adopted after the moot.

### Fen Harrow

Fen is a wood elf route surveyor, tracker, and former courier. He has crossed enough wet ground to distrust anyone who identifies a person from one footprint. He joined the company after an accused poacher was hanged on a boot comparison Fen had called merely consistent. Since then, he records what a trace establishes, what it excludes, and what it cannot know.

**Useful habits**

- Reconstructs movement from surfaces, weather sequence, doors, sightlines, and travel time.
- Tests an alibi by reproducing its route rather than debating confidence in a clock.
- Refuses to turn a class characteristic—blue cloth, a small boot, a common key—into a unique identity without convergence.

**Pressure point**

Fen trusts physical sequence more readily than testimony and can underweight why a person acted. The school-fund records are necessary to distinguish Orlo's route from the many villagers who own blue clothes or know the hedge. He must accept documentary motive as part of identification, not merely background.

**Likely concern in this adventure**

He clears the murder interval at Alder Orchard, times the dry North Hedge crossing at five minutes and forty seconds, and frames the route evidence narrowly at the moot. He never claims that the rounded track alone belongs to Orlo.

## Working disagreements

The fixed route grows from five disagreements.

- **Bram's traces.** Elian wants Bram held when the ash, cuff thread, inheritance dispute, and false statement converge. Fen insists on testing the orchard before the storm destroys the only independent chronology. Pella argues that a lie about the desk can be exactly what it appears to be: a lie about the desk.
- **Hester's accounts.** Sable believes the reeve's mark, custody request, and unauthorized payments make Hester the likely architect of the fictitious hearths. Pella requires the company to compare purposes and warrant series before treating all irregular money as one scheme.
- **The children.** Elian initially considers calling Jory Pike directly at the moot. Pella objects to making one child carry the accusation against his teacher. The final case uses separated statements, practice sheets, candle bands, and adult corroboration.
- **The beneficiaries.** Sable wants to inspect the full beneficiary box so the account can be closed completely. Pella argues that the murder case needs the path of the funds, not the names of children who received shoes or meals. Sable accepts a sealed verification by three custodians.
- **Orlo's purpose.** Pella and Fen both regard the school fund as evidence of a genuine civic failure. Elian insists that a compassionate motive does not convert a fatal blow and deliberate staging into an accident. The company ultimately protects the beneficiaries and accuses Orlo in the same morning.

None of these disputes is resolved by one character's specialty. The company succeeds because each member forces the others to narrow an overstatement.

## Fixed route

```text
Hearth Hall and the Map Room
  -> Alder Orchard
  -> Merrit Alder's Burrow
  -> The Common Chest
  -> Chapel of the Open Door
  -> Bramblewick School
  -> The Copper Kettle and Long Pantry
  -> The North Hedge
  -> Hearth Hall and the Map Room
  -> The First-Bell Moot
```

In shorthand:

```text
Hall -> Orchard -> Burrow -> Chest -> Chapel -> School
     -> Kettle -> Hedge -> Hall -> Moot
```

The first return to Hearth Hall identifies the preserved acorn weight and prepares the exhibits. Moss Apothecary and Bramble Mill remain available but unvisited. The company knows that each could provide additional support; it does not delay the moot merely to exhaust redundant evidence.

## The mistaken theories

### Bram killed for the inheritance

The first theory is reasonable. Bram argued with Merrit, searched the desk, lied about entering the map room, expected an inheritance, found the body, and left ash and cloth at the scene. The company records one inference too strongly: that the torn cuff proves a murder-interval return.

The orchard disproves the timing. The corrected journal preserves both the error and the reason for abandoning it. Bram remains guilty of trespass, attempted will-hunting, and lying to investigators. He does not become virtuous when he becomes innocent of murder.

### Hester killed to conceal the accounts

The second theory is broader and more dangerous. Hester keeps civic order, marked disputed payments, delayed opening the chest, and spent common money without a vote. The theory survives longer because it explains motive, procedural influence, and apparent financial concealment, even though Wil controls custody and Merrit's witness commission predates the murder.

The Common Chest separates the bridge file from the ghost-hearth warrants. Hester's entire discrepancy is present in delivered stone and bridge labor. The fictitious hearths use another sequence, another handwriting chain, and school redemptions. Hester's misconduct remains a public matter, but it no longer explains Merrit's threatened disclosure or Orlo's movement.

## The final case

The Lantern Measure does not ask the moot to infer guilt from character. It presents six propositions:

1. Merrit suffered a deliberate fatal blow before the last bell.
2. Someone moved the clock to nine, wedged it with school chalk, and broke the glass after Merrit bled.
3. The killer entered with an authorized key, left through the north garden door, and crossed the hedge before rain.
4. Orlo was absent for the first four rehearsal stanzas and arrived at the school side door shortly before the last.
5. Merrit was about to expose fictitious hearths created from dead children's names, written and redeemed through Orlo's school fund.
6. Orlo's key, lime, chalk, washed cuff, basin residue, changing statement, route, and threatened scheme explain the complete sequence better than any alternative suspect.

The acorn weight strengthens the account of the blow. It is not allowed to substitute for the other five propositions.

## Resulting village state

- **Orlo Vane:** found responsible for Merrit's murder and remanded to the circuit magistrate. His later admission explains his panic and intent but does not create the finding.
- **Bram Alder:** cleared of murder and exposed for searching Merrit's papers. His inheritance remains subject to the valid will rather than village sympathy.
- **Mara Kettle:** cleared of murder, fined for the hidden brandy trade, and publicly identified as one of Merrit's extortion targets only to the extent necessary to resolve her payments.
- **Hester Rowan:** cleared of murder, censured for unauthorized bridge spending, and stripped of unilateral emergency expenditure authority. The completed bridge work is separately ratified.
- **Cora Bramble and Perrin Moss:** neither cleared nor accused on their collateral secrets because the company never completes those inquiries. The murder record says only that the evidence used against Orlo does not require either secret.
- **The school:** remains open under Sister Amity and rotating village tutors.
- **The relief fund:** continues by vote, with public totals, private recipients, and three separately held keys.
- **Merrit's archive:** divided between murder exhibits, valid civic records, and unrelated leverage files. The last category is returned, destroyed, or referred separately rather than read into the public record.
