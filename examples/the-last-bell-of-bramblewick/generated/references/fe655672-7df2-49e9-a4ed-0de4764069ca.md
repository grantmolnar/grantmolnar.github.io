## Summary

Bramblewick's memorial sister keeps burial names and lending history, certifies copies, and protects children and living beneficiaries without turning pastoral authority into a verdict.

## Reference Material

## Names remain names

Sister Amity Thorne keeps the Chapel of the Open Door, its burial register, memorial ribbons, painted name-stones, annual notices, and lending slips. Her work preserves names as names rather than letting civic convenience turn a person into an entry or an entry into a household.

## Copies leave; the register stays

Amity may open the register after the proposition to be tested is stated, certify exact copies under her seal, witness sensitive access, and keep originals on their shelf. She protects pupils and living families from being made to pronounce upon murder or display private need when records can establish a narrower fact.

## A burial date carries no motive

The chapel can establish spellings, dates, memorial continuity, notices, and documented loans. Amity cannot certify why another person acted, what an unrecorded silence meant, whether an accusation is complete, or what the village must decide. Current disclosures, testimony, family consent, school arrangements, and moot findings remain live.
