## Reference Material

## A keeper with two hands

Merrit Alder keeps the Hearth Book, maintains working folios and clean public copies, and indexes births, deaths, debts, warrants, wills, and household succession. His small exact hand is familiar across Bramblewick's civic memory. The office makes him custodian and comparer of records, not owner of the people or obligations described.

## The paper he left alive

Before the Founders' Supper, Merrit signed an invitation naming outsiders as neutral witnesses to a morning audit. The paper survives him and permits preservation of common property, inspection of civic records with their custodians, questioning willing witnesses, and recommendation to the moot. It does not authorize forced private searches, unilateral guilt, punishment, or control of another custodian's book.

## Drawers are not a verdict

Merrit's archive joins public records to private correspondence, estate papers, and working notes. That breadth can expose contradictions, but one keeper's indexing, plan, or opinion is not itself a village finding. Which papers are found, opened, sealed, credited, or referred onward remains encounter evidence and play state.
