## Summary

Bramblewick's reeve keeps order, arranges lawful access, and convenes public process, but must recuse from her own accounts and cannot replace custodians, constable, witnesses, or moot.

## Reference Material

## Order that must survive morning

Hester Rowan is Bramblewick's reeve. She coordinates storm response, public readings, household access, and the practical work of keeping a closed village from turning fear into punishment. Her strongest instinct is procedural: the morning record should survive the morning argument.

## No hand holds both keys

Hester may invoke storm custom, summon custodians, request neutral witnesses, arrange access to common property, and call the First-Bell Moot when the flooded bridge keeps the magistrate away. She cannot open the Common Chest alone, order a forced private search, take physical exhibits out of Wil's custody, decide another custodian's record, or pronounce guilt by office.

## Her mark enters the same book

Hester's mark establishes that her office acted; it does not certify that every expenditure or decision was authorized. When her own accounts are questioned, she keeps order and recuses from judgment. Current confidence, censure, office, disputed transactions, accusations, and aftermath belong to evidence, the moot, and play state.
