## Reference Material

## A house measured in servings

Mara Kettle runs the Copper Kettle and its Long Pantry. She directs the ovens, public room, private alcove, school tray, cellar stores, serving order, and the staff who can tell one meal, crock, ribbon, or passage from another. In Bramblewick, her house is both livelihood and public shelter.

## Flour, trays, and the order of work

Mara keeps kitchen books, service cards, meal accounts, and household packets in the practical order of food prepared, carried, served, returned, or spoiled. Those records can reconstruct work and ordinary movement. They do not decide why a person used a passage or whether a witness's account has been credited.

## A quiet alcove is not silence

Mara may move pupils or staff out of a crowded room, offer a quieter place to speak, and protect the pantry from needless disruption. She cannot compel a statement, own another person's secret, control a civic record, or turn hospitality into investigative authority. Current testimony, private payments, disclosures, custody, and findings remain live.
