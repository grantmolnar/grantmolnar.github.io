## Summary

Bramblewick's civic register uses working folios and clean public copies to preserve household memory while leaving authorship, alteration, and legal effect open to proof.

## Reference Material

## One village, two copies

The Hearth Book is Bramblewick's continuing civic register. Working folios receive changes, comparisons, objections, and source notes; clean public copies preserve the accepted record in a hand villagers can consult. The two-copy practice permits correction without pretending that every draft and every published line have the same authority.

## A hearth carries names, debts, and bread

The book indexes births, deaths, burials, debts, relief warrants, wills, household succession, and other facts the village must carry across generations. Ordinary custodians maintain it, and source records remain with the offices that own them. A copied fact does not erase the authority or privacy of its source.

## Red ink still needs a witness

Hand, ink, sequence, source, correction, and custody may all matter before an entry is credited. The book preserves what was written and accepted through its process; its existence cannot determine motive, guilt, or the truth of a contested event. Current pages, discovered differences, certification, repair, and audit results remain in encounters and play state.
