## Summary

Bramblewick's two-key chest holds common coin, warrants, and sealed records under divided access, witnessed opening, numbered removal, and bounded disclosure.

## Reference Material

## Two doors and two keys

The Common Chest stands behind two oak doors in the reeve's office. One key belongs to the reeve's office and the second to the current autumn lot-holder. Neither key opens the village's coin, warrant bundles, and sealed records alone; divided access is the ordinary rule, not an emergency courtesy.

## Name the bundle before the lock turns

A lawful opening names the proposition or account to be tested, assembles the required custodians and witnesses, checks strings and seals, and numbers every bundle removed. Request, authorization, receipt, redemption, return, and copy may then be compared without spreading every private paper across the tables.

## One clean chain, few public names

Orderly custody proves what was opened, copied, moved, and returned. It does not make every entry true or every name public. Current seal condition, key possession, selected bundles, exhibit custody, privacy scope, and any conclusion drawn from the records remain encounter procedure and play state.
