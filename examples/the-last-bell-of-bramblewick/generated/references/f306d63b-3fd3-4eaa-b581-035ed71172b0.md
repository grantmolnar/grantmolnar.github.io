## Reference Material

## A shop of measured harms

Perrin Moss keeps Moss Apothecary. He compounds ordinary medicines, records household treatments, measures doses, tends sickness, and understands how wounds and bodily changes differ in age, cause, and severity. His shop is a place of care, commerce, and records rather than a magistrate's chamber.

## Measure the wound before the blame

Perrin may conduct a supervised examination, describe what he observes, compare ordinary instruments or preparations, and make exact copies of relevant shop cards. Another witness may watch the work and physical exhibits remain in their proper custody. Medical skill narrows possibilities; it does not convert inference into certainty.

## Wax records a bottle, not its whole history

A shop seal can identify origin, and a treatment card can record what was written. Neither guarantees that a preparation remained unchanged, a figure was correct, or a later explanation should be accepted. Current bottles, diagnoses, amendments, admissions, treatment choices, and public findings remain live evidence.
