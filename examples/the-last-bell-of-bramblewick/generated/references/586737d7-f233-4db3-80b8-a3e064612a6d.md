## A lane used by everyone

The North Hedge is a working boundary and lane rather than a sealed passage. It joins Hearth Hall's north garden to Alder Orchard's low gate, the mill's sluice approach, and Bramblewick School's covered wash yard. Orchard hands, school carriers, dogs, children, and neighbors use it in ordinary weather.

## Mud keeps order longer than ownership

Hazel gates, turns, surface height, garden doors, wash-yard access, and the old sheep tunnel give the route stable geometry. Rain and later traffic alter the ground quickly. Sequence, direction, and measured travel may remain useful when no footprint or fiber can honestly identify one traveler by itself.

## Cord the marks, not the village

The constable may mark, measure, cord, and reopen the lane as work and safety require. A route record should distinguish ordinary access from a particular crossing and preservation from permanent closure. Current tracks, weather damage, searches, pursuit, exit control, and demonstrated travel remain encounter evidence and play state.
