## Summary

Bramblewick's constable holds scenes, persons, and physical exhibits, preserves movement and custody, and may detain for evidence-backed risk without deciding guilt or controlling civic records.

## Reference Material

## One door, six stories

Wil Sloe is Bramblewick's constable. When storm custom is invoked, he holds the scene, controls the movement of persons, separates physical exhibits, watches exits, and records who handled what. His office keeps a disputed room usable; it does not select which of its stories is true.

## String, seal, and every hand

Wil may inventory, seal, move, compare, and present physical evidence with appropriate witnesses. Civic books remain with their ordinary custodians, private property keeps its own threshold, and willing witnesses keep their own voices. Weak custody burdens the affected exhibit rather than silently erasing every parallel source.

## A locked door needs a present danger

Suspicion alone does not justify detention. Independent connection plus a present risk of flight, tampering, witness access, or use of an unexplained route may support a temporary hold while the remaining claim is tested. Current watches, detainees, escape, pursuit, magistrate access, and final disposition belong to encounter procedure and play state.
