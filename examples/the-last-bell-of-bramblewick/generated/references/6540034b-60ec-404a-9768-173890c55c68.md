## Summary

## Reference Material

## A morning burden, not a trial

The First-Bell Moot is Bramblewick's emergency public hearing when flood or storm closes the bridge and an urgent civic act cannot wait. The reeve calls and orders the room, householders chosen by lot witness the proceeding, custodians present their own books, and the constable keeps physical custody. No single office supplies the finding.

## Lay the whole crossing on the table

Outside witnesses may call willing speakers, compare records with their custodians, stage a route or door demonstration, and recommend an action proportionate to the reconstruction. A strong recommendation names the act, time, means, reason, route, and evidentiary chain it relies upon rather than asking suspicion to carry missing propositions.

## Morning may hold; it may not punish

The moot may preserve people and evidence until ordinary authority returns, separate collateral matters, and protect private names when narrower proof suffices. It cannot force private testimony, make a conflicted officer judge their own account, or turn urgency into punishment. Current speakers, exhibits, votes, custody, remedy, and aftermath remain live.
