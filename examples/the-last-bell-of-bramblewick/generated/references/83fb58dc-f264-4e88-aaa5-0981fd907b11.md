# Bramblewick School

## Summary

Bramblewick's one-room school teaches letters and memorial recitation, administers ordinary supplies and meals, and protects pupils' independent voices from institutional ownership.

## Reference Material

## A room that belongs to its pupils

Bramblewick School is one turf-roofed room facing the green, with a covered wash yard and side door behind the North Hedge. Lessons use slates, household records, songs, and the annual Founders' recitation. The schoolmaster directs the day, but the institution serves the pupils and village rather than becoming one person's private room.

## Bread, slate, soap, red pencil

The school receives and records ordinary needs such as meals, slate, soap, lamp oil, shoes, winter cloth, and medicine reimbursement. Practice sheets, inventories, schedules, supply records, and adult supervision can corroborate one another. No single school paper decides who acted or why.

## Six pupils, six separate voices

Pupils may be questioned separately, with suitable adults and without making one child carry a public accusation. The school may remain open under lawful oversight even when an officeholder is absent or disputed. Current attendance, testimony, exhibits, records opened, leadership, beneficiaries, and school disposition remain live state.
