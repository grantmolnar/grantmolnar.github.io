## Summary

## Reference Material

## An open arch and a closed confidence

The Chapel of the Open Door is a round whitewashed memorial room whose entrance has no door. Ribbons, painted name-stones, annual notices, and the burial register keep the dead present as people before their names become evidence in another record. Physical openness does not abolish pastoral confidence.

## The register does not cross the threshold

The chapel keeps burial spellings and dates, memorial continuity, register loans, and exact certified copies. A stated proposition and suitable witness can permit narrow inspection while the original remains in place. Living relatives and children need not repeat what the records can establish without them.

## Three names, no verdict

The chapel can certify a name, date, notice, copy, or documented loan. It cannot infer another person's intent, compel a family disclosure, determine the public scope of private need, or decide a civic accusation. Current access, testimony, copies made, family consent, and later care remain live.
