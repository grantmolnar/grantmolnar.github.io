## The orchard keeps its own time

Bram Alder is Merrit's nephew and the working hand at Alder Orchard. He knows the terraces, brace cables, tools, dog paths, low gate, and weather work that keeps the trees standing. His competence makes him the right person to explain ordinary orchard labor; it does not make any claimed chronology true without independent marks.

## The green key opens a door, not an hour

Bram belongs to Merrit's household and may open ordinary family spaces. The orchard tool shed is the standing place for a duplicate used in estate work. Access explains how a door can open; it does not establish when it opened, what was sought, or what any trace means.

## The will waits for daylight

Bram is treated as Merrit's likely heir in daily village life, but succession belongs to the valid will and lawful settlement rather than expectation, debt, anger, or sympathy. Current keys, disclosed papers, admissions, searches, estate disposition, and accusation state remain encounter evidence and play state.
