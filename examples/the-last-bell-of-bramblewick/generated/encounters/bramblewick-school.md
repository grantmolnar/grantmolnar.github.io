# Bramblewick School

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** location, suspect, alibi, accounts, investigation, social

Practice marks, keys, ash, warrants, and a washed cuff turn Orlo's careful grammar into a route from Merrit's room to the final stanza.

## Opening View

Orlo Vane has put the school in order before the investigators arrive. Six pupils sit in wet cloaks; Tella Reed spreads rehearsal sheets across one desk, four stanzas marked in her hand before Orlo's red pencil begins. Nibs Fen guards a guttered practice candle. The wash yard stands open to the hedge, and an emptied basin has not dried. Orlo locks the record cupboard. Tella does not look up. 'We have not been asked yet.'

## Encounter Material

## Six pupils and one locked cupboard

High windows show only storm sky. Slate dust, apple peels, wet cloaks, and Founders' ribbons crowd the desks. The front door faces the green; the side door opens into the covered wash yard behind the North Hedge.

Tella Reed and Nibs Fen remain with four younger pupils because no parent wants a child walking home while a murderer is loose. Orlo stands between both doors and the locked record cupboard. He asks that adults be questioned first and offers to gather every school record himself.

They are rehearsal witnesses and possible fund beneficiaries, not accusers. Do not ask a child whether Orlo killed Merrit. Ask which stanza was underway, which door opened, whose pencil touched the page, and when the candle was relit. Speak separately, with a trusted adult near enough to protect and too far away to supply an answer.

## The rehearsal without Orlo

Tella and Nibs began at 8:35 from the note beneath the supper tray. They led four stanzas before Orlo entered by the side door, corrected Nibs's emphasis, and conducted the final stanza through the bell.

Three records survive:

- Tella's margins count four completed stanzas before Orlo's first red-pencil correction;
- Nibs drew rain beside the line underway when he arrived; and
- a practice candle burned through three measured bands before Orlo relit the conductor's lamp.

Jory Pike saw him enter at 8:54, but the alibi does not rest on Jory. A neighbor heard Orlo only in the final stanza. The children's continuous rehearsal became his continuous presence only when he retold it.

## Orlo's first interview

Orlo first says he was “with the recitation from its beginning” and entered by the front after checking the rain. He denies the wash yard and blames repetition for the pupils' confusion.

The marked sheets change his wording to “responsible for it from its beginning.” Jory or the side door brings a later admission: he arrived late after copying memorial text at the chapel. The lending history dates that work six years earlier. Orlo concedes one object at a time and changes the sentence around it: *with* becomes *responsible for*; *from the beginning* becomes *before the last stanza*; *memorial text* becomes *an old copy*. He never volunteers the next fact.

The two leaves missing from Merrit's folio are already ash. At 8:54, before Jory saw him, Orlo fed them through the rear door of the banked stove. Rag-paper ash, red ruling pigment, and short binding threads remain behind the lip. Nothing legible survives. Burrow, chapel, chest, mill, inn, and school copies preserve the chain the fire could not gather into one place.

Once the fund is joined to Merrit's intended disclosure, Orlo asks the investigators to read the recipient names aloud, though the sealed warrant numbers already prove the scheme. He wants refusal to look evasive and disclosure to look cruel. No child, he says, should go hungry so a legal chimney remains pure. He still denies the killing.

## Chalk, key, and copyist's hand

Blue slate chalk comes in paper-wrapped dozens. One packet lacks a stick; its surviving half fits the clock wedge by fracture face and paper dust. The chalk identifies school supply, not a unique hand. Orlo carries short pieces for outdoor boards.

His ring holds keys for school cabinets, the chapel coffer, and the authorized map-room lock. The ring is in his pocket. Pale lime sits fresh in the brass hall key's ward.

Clean Hearth Book extracts, school orders, and the first ghost-hearth authorizations share Orlo's open *g*, hooked final *r*, and narrow red ruling. Merrit's working hand is heavier and slopes left. A patient reader can see the difference; a scribe can certify it.

## The beneficiary box

A cedar box beneath Orlo's desk contains shoe measures, meal chits, medicine reimbursements, and family initials. The diverted shares reached real Bramblewick children, including some whose parents now stand outside demanding his release. No payment went to him.

Each distribution cycle cites the same three warrant numbers found at the Common Chest. The school seal and Orlo's receiving initials appear on surrendered originals. Seal the family names and the financial chain still holds.

If left alone after the chest opens, Orlo carries the box toward the chapel under Amity's supposed protection. He does not burn it. Interception shows control and obstruction; failure leaves the warrant trail elsewhere.

## The wash yard

The covered yard holds Perrin's waist-high basin, lavender soap, cloak pegs, and a grated drain toward the hedge. The water was refreshed recently, yet reddish-brown dilution remains beneath the rim. Fine green fibers matching Merrit's collar cling to a drain splinter. Orlo's right cuff lacks a dark blue thread and smells strongly of lavender beneath rain and chalk.

Blood magic may confirm blood according to the setting's ordinary limits. Lint, route, timing, and the washed cuff make the basin useful without turning it into a perfect identity test.

## When Orlo feels the case closing

Orlo's first weapons are moral and procedural. He isolates beneficiary records, challenges custody, and makes the inquiry answer for exposing poor families. Once early death, north route, and false alibi meet in his presence, he knows the moot may hold him.

Choose his response from the pressure already visible:

- remain and prepare a public defense while the case is mostly financial;
- carry the beneficiary box to the chapel if school records remain loose; or
- leave through the wash yard for the old sheep tunnel after route and basin evidence are preserved.

Flight changes custody. The murder still has to be proved.
