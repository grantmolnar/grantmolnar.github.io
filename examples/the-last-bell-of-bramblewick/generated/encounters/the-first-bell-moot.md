# The First-Bell Moot

**Role:** necessary, end  
**Tags:** location, resolution, social, accusation, terminal

At first bell, Bramblewick must decide which joined sequence can bear detention, accusation, and the cost of separating murder from every other village wrong.

## Opening View

The first bell stops, and Hearth Hall answers with silence. Hester removes the reeve's chain before sitting among Amity and three household witnesses. Wil keeps the suspects apart; the schoolchildren remain together near the middle hearth. Merrit's body waits behind the map-room door, and the stopped clock still says nine. Orlo lays his papers in one exact pile. Before the bridge opens, the outside witnesses must say what the evidence can bear.

## Encounter Material

## The chain on the table

By dawn the rain has thinned to grey. Hearth Hall is warmer than it was at the killing and less merciful. Benches fill the room. The pupils sit with their families near the middle hearth; six suspects sit apart under Wil Sloe's eye. Merrit's covered body remains beyond the map-room door. The clock still says nine.

Hester lays the reeve's chain on the table rather than around her neck. Amity and three lot-chosen householders sit beside her. Wil keeps the suspects apart; custodians keep their own books. Flood bars the magistrate. Hester recognizes speakers and records the vote, but cannot rule on her accounts or detention. Merrit's invitation seats the outsiders beyond Bramblewick's kin groups without giving them judgment.

The room has one immediate question: what Bramblewick knows well enough to do before the bridge opens. Wil keeps the scene and any detainee. The outsiders may call willing speakers, stage the door and route, recommend a proportionate action, or name Orlo first and build backward. No office supplies forced testimony, a private search, or a verdict. The evidence must carry the action requested.

## Six claims, one sequence

A complete case joins six claims. Orlo listens for whichever one remains loose.

1. **Merrit died before the last bell.** The focused wound, blood beneath rain, and medical interval place death earlier than the stopped clock.
2. **The clock was staged.** Glass fell after blood spread; school chalk wedged the pendulum; the clock had kept good time before supper.
3. **The killer entered lawfully and left north.** No lock was forced; the garden door pulls shut without latching; one pre-rain crossing runs toward the school.
4. **Orlo's school alibi begins too late.** Four stanzas passed without him. His first certain presence is the 8:54 side-door arrival.
5. **Merrit meant to expose a scheme Orlo controlled.** Dead children's names, school warrants, Orlo's hand, seal, key, and register loan join him to the ghost hearths.
6. **Orlo carried the crime from hall to school.** Map-room lime marks his key; the hedge thread matches his cuff; the basin holds blood dilution and Merrit's collar fibers; his account changes behind each discovery.

The acorn weight strengthens the first and sixth claims. It is not required. Nor must Bram's search, Mara's brandy, Cora's grain, Perrin's doses, and Hester's bridge all be resolved before the murder finding.

## Lay the case across the tables

If the players want a sequence, Hester offers one:

- break nine o'clock with body and rain;
- demonstrate the chalk wedge and garden-door strap;
- place the rehearsal sheets beside the mill slate and measured hedge walk;
- show one warrant chain, the burial entries, and Orlo's copyist hand; and
- end with key, cuff, basin, and his successive accounts.

Begin with money, speak Orlo's name immediately, or call him before any other witness. Whatever the order, the final account must carry the whole crossing from map room to school.

## One burden for each witness

Do not repeat every interview. Give each witness narrow work.

**Perrin Moss** identifies the focused blow, harmless dose, and earlier death. If his own alibi remains disputed, the Fen sickroom, used cloths, and measured bottle stand apart from his altered cards.

**Jory Pike** fixes the bell and Orlo's 8:54 arrival. If challenged, Tella's marks, Nibs's rain sketch, the practice candle, and a neighbor who heard only the final stanza remain.

**Tella Reed and Nibs Fen** describe what they did before Orlo arrived. Let their sheets speak first. Amity stops any attempt to make children pronounce upon murder.

**Elsie Sedge** places rain against supper service, sees Orlo moving toward the covered approach, and anchors Mara and Hester in the pantry.

**Cora Bramble** certifies the gauge and emergency stop. Her ergot quarrel may be entered without turning it into the killing.

**Nim Thatch** presents one complete warrant chain from authorization to school redemption. Six years of ledgers need not cover the tables.

**Sister Amity** names the dead children, dates Orlo's register loan, and keeps living beneficiaries out of the burial evidence.

**Bram Alder** demonstrates the garden-door strap and admits his earlier search. His lie becomes chronology when placed before rain and orchard work.

Useful exhibits include separated clock glass, chalk wedge, key register, route measurements, blue thread, frayed cuff, basin sample, certified warrants, and burial copies. Weak custody should burden the affected exhibit, not erase every parallel source.

## Orlo's defense

Orlo takes notes on a pupil's slate. He concedes what he can keep separate.

### Procedure

He asks whether outsiders may accuse a householder, whether a suspect reeve may call the moot, and whether children must testify against their teacher. These are serious objections bent toward self-preservation. Hester's recusal, lot-chosen witnesses, Merrit's invitation, and the pupils' physical records answer them without pretending Bramblewick has become a court.

### Separated facts

He had a key. He uses blue chalk. He controls school warrants. He arrived before the bell. He walks the hedge. He washed at school. Alone, each sentence sounds ordinary. His defense depends on keeping them from becoming a route.

### Alternatives

He assigns the early death to Bram or Cora, the missing leaves to Hester's accounts, blood near school to common injuries, and the thread to ordinary hedge traffic. Each explanation carries one object and drops the rest.

### The children

Once the warrant chain is shown, Orlo stops denying the ghost hearths. The shares bought shoes, meals, medicine, and winter fuel. He asks recipient families to stand so that judgment will look like taking food from the people in front of it. Some stand; others remain seated and weep. Hester reads only the warrant numbers. The village does not need the family names to prove the forged households or the murder.

Orlo forged the rule, kept sole control, killed the man who would transfer relief to another office, and placed the children between himself and judgment. Family names may remain sealed while the warrants, burials, hand, and receiving marks remain public.

### The gap

With an incomplete case, Orlo names the missing link. Motive does not place him in the map room. A route does not prove he walked it. A rehearsal lie does not name a killer. He is right about isolated clues. Convergence is the accusation's strength.

## How much weight the room will carry

### Suspicion

A motive, lie, or unexplained trace supports continued inquiry and record protection. It does not support a public finding.

### Detention

Independent connection plus present risk—flight, tampering, witness access, or an unexplained murder route—supports detention. Wil can hold Orlo while the remaining claim is tested.

### Public accusation

A public accusation should at least explain the earlier death, staged clock or false alibi, school-fund motive, and movement from hall to school. A major gap divides the hall rather than ending the adventure by fiat.

### Complete case

All six claims support a village finding without confession, perfect magic, or the weapon. Anything Orlo says afterward confirms or refuses a case already made.

## When the case is complete

Once every major gap closes, Hester asks three questions: Did Orlo hold the key? Did he arrive only for the final stanza? Were the false warrants in his hand? Preserved records make evasion more damaging than silence.

If exits are watched, Orlo remains seated. He admits the fund and says Merrit meant to destroy it. He may refuse the blow or say Merrit turned toward the cabinet and he struck before deciding to do so. The finding does not depend on either answer.

The householders bind him in the warm store room under rotating guard until the magistrate arrives. Amity and two parents keep the school open. The beneficiary box is sealed, not displayed. Bramblewick secures the murderer without pretending that hungry children were the error.

## When the name is right but the case is thin

A thin accusation divides existing loyalties. Parents defend the teacher who fed their children. Bram's kin demand equal scrutiny. Hester's opponents call the inquiry a diversion from her warrants. Orlo asks to return to school while the village waits.

Answer the actual gap with a preserved clue, one witness, a demonstration, or a smaller demand. Likely results include:

- **detention pending inquiry** after late alibi plus route, tampering, or removal of records;
- **freedom under watch** after motive and lies without physical opportunity; or
- **flight** when detention fails and Orlo knows the hedge, basin, or key will close the case.

The first presentation changes the room. It need not finish it.

## Private confrontation

Orlo will meet privately at the school, chapel, or Hearth Hall under the language of protecting children. Mere suspicion teaches him the case's weakness. Early death, late alibi, financial control, and north route produce a bargain: he surrenders keys, warrants, and himself if living beneficiaries remain sealed.

That term may be accepted without concealing the fraud or murder. Warrant numbers, burial entries, and Orlo's hand prove the scheme. A promise to blame another person or destroy the governing records is different; he will not accept the first and will exploit the second.

Do not let a private confession invent unseen clock, route, or weapon facts. Orlo may admit the fund and confrontation. Unsupported gaps remain unsupported.

## Flight and pursuit

Orlo flees only when detention is near and exits are loose. His path runs through the school wash yard, North Hedge, and old sheep tunnel. From the moot, he uses adult argument as cover and leaves through the map-room garden; he does not threaten pupils or create a theatrical disaster.

Fresh tracks cut deep into wet ground, unlike the rounded pre-rain crossing. Pursuit may end at the sheep tunnel, mill footbridge, or flooded lower meadow. Capture secures him; it does not prove the murder. Escape postpones custody while the moot may still reach a finding and the magistrate later issue pursuit orders.

## A wrong accusation

Let a wrong accusation break against evidence. The innocent suspect yields the hidden truth when silence becomes worse: Bram's search, Mara's ledger, Cora's ergot quarrel, Perrin's cards, or Hester's bridge accounts. Each admission explains a suspicious trace and creates its own civic problem.

Orlo supports caution, asks that the accused be watched rather than harmed, and uses delay to move the beneficiary box or prepare flight. Compare the innocent explanation with the clock, north route, and late arrival to reopen the case.

Knowingly accusing an innocent person to protect Orlo is concealment, not failed detection. Later consequences should treat it as such.

## Collateral findings

After the murder finding, Hester asks what else belongs in the morning record.

- **Bram:** unlawful search and debt; inheritance waits for magistrate review.
- **Mara:** duties, closure of the drain route, or a postponed brandy charge; her pantry alibi stands.
- **Cora:** inspection and restitution for contaminated grain; public handling may save the mill from rumor.
- **Perrin:** supervision or removal for altered doses; his wound analysis remains valid.
- **Hester:** resignation, confidence vote, or formal accounting after unauthorized bridge work.
- **Beneficiary families:** private audit, forgiveness of received aid, and replacement of the ghost hearths with a lawful school share.

The morning record may reveal or seal these matters separately. Family privacy does not hide the warrant scheme. A suspect's unrelated offense does not change the murder.

## What Bramblewick keeps

Orlo is identified through converging evidence, secured without harm to the pupils, and delivered to lawful authority. The records survive. Bramblewick keeps the obligation to feed poor children and loses the pretense that one schoolmaster may own the rule because he used stolen money well.

The outside witnesses may leave before every village quarrel ends. The whodunnit is complete when the record can say who killed Merrit, when and how he did it, why he staged the clock, how he reached the school, and why the last-bell alibi is false.
