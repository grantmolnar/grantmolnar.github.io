# Alder Orchard

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** location, physical-reconstruction, suspect, red-herring, investigation

Storm work, dog tracks, and wet wood account for Bram at the murder hour while his cuff and pipe confess an earlier trespass.

## Opening View

Nutmeg finds the investigators first and pulls them uphill through rain. Bram kneels beside a split pear tree, holding the dog and a freed brace cable that would have dragged two young apples from the terrace. His knife lies in wet grass. Dry sawdust survives beneath the cut; boot prints circle the work; the tether chain has wound twice around its marker. Nutmeg strains toward the lower gate.

## Encounter Material

## The orchard in the storm

Alder Orchard climbs the south hill in narrow terraces. Low apples follow stone lines, each family row marked with its common-share obligations. Rain runs toward the mill stream. An old pear has lost a main limb, dragging a brace cable across the footpath.

Bram came here after Merrit humiliated him at supper. He tells the truth about the murder interval and lies about the earlier evening. Let the orchard prove both.

## The damaged brace

Fresh cuts show where Bram freed the cable before it tore down two young trees. His pruning knife lies in the wet grass. Rain reached the exposed wood after the last strokes: sawdust is damp on top and dry where the cable compressed it.

Nutmeg was tethered to the next marker. The chain has wrapped twice around the post as the dog followed Bram's work. Paw prints and boot marks surround the brace; none continues toward Hearth Hall during the critical interval.

The site alone cannot account for every minute. Add the quarter chime, first rain, walking distance, and Bram's later return, and a trip to kill Merrit, stage the room, and resume work no longer fits.

## Bram's chronology

Bram heard the mill's quarter chime while cutting the final loaded section. Rain began a few minutes later and drove Nutmeg beneath the tool barrow. He secured the cable, then returned after the last bell when Hester sent for Merrit. The mill slate, pantry service, and Hearth Hall can test each marker.

Bram knows the north garden route and owns a household key. Broad opportunity remains; the orchard removes the necessary interval once nine o'clock is rejected.

## The earlier intrusion

A green-brown thread is missing from Bram's right cuff. It matches the thread on Merrit's archive drawer and another caught on the map-room desk catch. His pipe holds the same sweetgrass tobacco as the ash beside the desk.

Confronted here, Bram admits searching Merrit's papers before supper. Creditors had begun treating his inheritance as collateral, and he wanted the latest will. Merrit caught him in the map room at 6:10 and promised to read a disinheritance clause at the next moot. Bram left ash, thread, and anger behind.

The admission re-dates the scene traces; it does not absolve him of trespass or lying.

## Sightlines and routes

The north window of Hearth Hall shows between the upper trees. Bram noticed its lamp and open casement when he began work. He could not see the map-room door, but he saw rain strike the open window at 8:50 and no one close it before the body was found.

A lower path joins the North Hedge lane. Rain has rounded earlier shallow impressions; later searchers cut sharply into the mud. A dark blue thread, not Bram's green-brown wool, catches on the split hazel gate. The focused route evidence lies at the hedge, but the orchard points toward it.

A dry walk from the garden door to the school wash yard takes five or six minutes. Present mud makes it longer. That difference places the crossing before the rain.

## Orchard records

The tool shed holds Merrit's estate map, a duplicate burrow key, and common-share produce lists. The map leads to the private archive. The delivery lists show that Cora's chime and stream slate can fix the work interval independently.

Once his search is known, Bram surrenders the key. Before then, he pretends not to remember where Merrit kept it.

## Bram after the lie

Bram moves among grief, resentment, and relief that someone else has seen what Merrit did with private weakness. He may accuse Mara, Perrin, or Hester using genuine material from his uncle's files. He knows nothing of Orlo's route or the ghost hearths.

The narrow finding is enough: Bram had motive and earlier access, invaded Merrit's papers, lied about it, and was cutting a storm-torn brace when Merrit died.
