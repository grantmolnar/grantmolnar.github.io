# Moss Apothecary

**Role:** optional  
**Tags:** location, medicine, cause-of-death, red-herring, investigation

Perrin can place death before the bell and match the blow, but only by opening dosage records he altered for another wrong.

## Opening View

Three empty spaces interrupt Perrin Moss's exact blue shelves: a basin, cooling cloths, and a child's spoon. He fastens his medical case while Merrit's tincture bottle waits beside a fresh seal and an older dosage card rubbed nearly smooth at one figure. Perrin looks toward the Fen burrow. 'A sick child still has a fever. Bring me the body, the bottle, or your questions—but choose.'

## Encounter Material

## The shop after closing

Moss Apothecary is a dry, bitter burrow beneath thyme. Blue bottles fill the niches; every label appears twice because Perrin trusts neither memory nor assistants. Three absences disturb the order tonight: a basin, cooling cloths, and a child's spoon, all carried to the Fen household before supper.

Perrin wants his medical authority separated from his suspect status. He has called Merrit's death heart failure complicated by a fall, though he knows the wound will not support him. A proper examination also leads from the open tincture bottle to dosage cards he altered.

## Examining Merrit

Bring Perrin under supervision, use another healer, or examine the body directly. Competent work finds:

- a compact depression behind the left ear with radiating fracture;
- a pattern unlike a broad fall against the clock case;
- one measured dose of Merrit's usual tincture, enough for drowsiness but not sudden death; and
- cooling, blood settling, and first rigidity incompatible with death at nine and discovery six minutes later.

Strong expertise narrows death to roughly 8:40–8:50. Basic competence still places it materially before the bell.

## The bottle

Perrin's wax seals the dark bottle. Its remaining volume agrees with one spoonful. Poppy, valerian, and willow bark appear in Merrit's customary proportions, without poison or a lethal dose.

The current card says one spoon. Merrit's older copies and an erased figure in Perrin's ledger show an earlier prescription of three. Perrin later changed several entries, and Merrit kept the discrepancy over him.

That alteration may have endangered Merrit on other nights. It did not make the wound.

## The Fen household

Lottie Fen developed a high fever that afternoon. Perrin arrived before the founders' reading and remained until Hester summoned him after the body was found. Her parents place him there. So do four cooling cloths, doses marked against the sand timer, and the leaking bucket Lottie's father moved when rain began while Perrin held her.

Perrin's own log is unnecessary. He lied about the corpse because he wanted the bottle out of view.

## Matching the weapon

A wax impression or careful measurement joins the wound to the acorn weight with the crescent casting flaw. Perrin can compare an impression if the weight remains at Hearth Hall. A second cleaning may remove residue, not shape.

Use the group's ordinary procedure: automatic recognition for a trained healer, a check for exactness, or later confirmation by the magistrate. A poor result delays the match; it never turns the blow into a fall.

## Perrin under pressure

Perrin yields the altered records in steps: transcription error, excessive earlier dose, then repeated changes. He asks for malpractice and murder to be entered on separate pages before signing a clean statement.

The request serves him and clarifies the case. The cards may be sealed without announcing them at the inn. Threats of immediate exposure make him legalistic, not capable of changing the medicine. A promise to bury the records creates a later quarrel with Hester.

## The limits of medicine

Medicine establishes violence, earlier death, the harmless dose, Perrin's own wrongdoing, and perhaps the weapon. The clock, north door, and ghost-hearth money still belong to other rooms.
