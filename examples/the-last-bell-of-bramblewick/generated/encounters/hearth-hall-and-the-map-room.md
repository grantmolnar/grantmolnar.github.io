# Hearth Hall and the Map Room

**Role:** necessary, start  
**Tags:** location, opening, crime-scene, investigation, social

Merrit lies beneath a clock stopped at nine while rain, broken glass, and six suspects' genuine traces fight to become the room's first settled story.

## Opening View

The tallcase clock says nine to a room that will not agree. Merrit Alder lies beneath it; blood reaches toward the rain-dark window, and broken glass rests both on rug and blood. Flour, pipe ash, medicine, disputed accounts, and brass acorn weights offer six different stories. Constable Wil holds six villagers at the door while Hester raises Merrit's signed invitation, admitting outside witnesses before Bramblewick chooses one.

## Encounter Material

## Six stories under one stopped clock

Hearth Hall was built for halflings who distrust grandeur and wasted heat. Its public room sits half below the green: whitewashed stone, oak beams within reach, three hearths sharing one chimney. Founders' garlands hang above cooling pie, spilled cider, and villagers who have stopped pretending not to listen.

The map room opens through a screened clerk's passage at the north end. That passage joins the covered service way behind the long pantry, allowing a keyholder to reach the lock without crossing the public floor. A second door opens into the north garden.

Merrit's invitation lies open beneath Hester's hand. She has invoked storm custom; Wil holds the hall door, the six suspects, and the physical exhibits while each civic book stays with its keeper. The newcomers may preserve common property, question willing witnesses, and recommend detention. No paper in the room gives them a private door or a verdict. Beyond Wil, cousins argue over cellar passages, borrowed keys, and doors rarely locked.

Merrit lies on his right side beneath the tallcase clock, left shoulder pushed partly under the case. Blood crosses the rug toward the open north window. The hands show nine exactly. The pendulum is still. The lower pane is broken.

Keep six frightened people from turning one confused room into six settled stories.

## What the room gives up

Give the physical facts to patient examination. Merrit suffered a focused blow behind the left ear. Blood spread before some clock glass fell. Fine rain lies on top of the blood nearest the window. Training adds precision: a fall against the case would bruise more broadly; the rain arrived after the bleeding; the pendulum was wedged, not broken.

Several earlier visits remain in the room. Bram's pipe ash lies by the desk. Flour dusts the sill and chair rail. Perrin's medicine bottle stands open beside a spoon. Hester's mark appears on disputed payment pages. Mara's wine was served here. Each trace is genuine; together they describe a busy room, not a killer.

The map table holds nested brass acorn weights. One bears a crescent nick beneath its cap. A hurried wipe has polished the broad surfaces and left dark matter in the engraved seam. Orlo carried blue chalk for outdoor lessons, but the weight was already here when the quarrel turned violent. The case can stand on timing, access, route, motive, and alibi even if no one examines the acorns.

## Before the room changes

Fix a few facts early:

- record the north window and garden door before closing either;
- separate glass on bare floor from shards resting on blood;
- ask Jory Pike for the rain and bell times before he hears other accounts;
- list authorized keys instead of collecting every household key in Bramblewick; and
- keep the folio, weights, desk cloth, bottle, and kitchen scraps in distinct custody.

A mishandled object may weaken one route. Parallel evidence remains.

## The first statements

Each suspect's first account works best if nine o'clock is trusted.

**Orlo Vane** says he led the school rehearsal through the last bell. He asks whether children must be questioned tonight. He admits holding a copyist's key but says Merrit expected him after supper and he never went.

**Bram Alder** found the body. He says he argued with Merrit, went straight to the orchard, and had not entered the map room earlier. He watches the desk more than the corpse.

**Mara Kettle** denies private payments and insists no one poisoned Merrit through her kitchen. She wants a cellar ledger moved before Wil inventories the inn.

**Cora Bramble** recognizes the flour and calls it common mill dust. She hides the pre-supper struggle because an ergot scare could ruin the mill after murder suspicion passes.

**Perrin Moss** calls the death a heart seizure followed by a fall. Pressed on the wound, he retreats to uncertainty and reaches for the medicine bottle in the name of professional care.

**Hester Rowan** accepts Merrit's witness commission, places the scene under Wil, and asks that the Common Chest wait for its second keyholder and a neutral witness. The request follows law and buys her time to explain the bridge accounts.

The hall need not become six full interviews. Let these accounts open routes.

## The audit folio

Merrit's folio compares current Hearth Book entries with Common Chest warrants. Three lines bear a tiny chimney crossed out in red. Orlo tore away the final two leaves naming his clean-copy hand and the school redemptions, then burned them in the banked school stove. Broken stitching remains.

The missing leaves delay the simplest reading; they do not erase the chain. Marginal references lead to Merrit's working folios, chapel burials, and school-supply warrants.

Hester's bridge payments appear elsewhere in the book. Merrit kept them separate from the ghost hearths, though villagers will prefer one scandal with one culprit.

## The village presses inward

The storm first closes people around the room. Bram's cousins need him in the orchard. Parents want the pupils home and beyond questioning. Mara has food cooling in a flooded pantry. Perrin still has a feverish child. Hester wants a process the morning can survive. Orlo offers his help with Merrit's records.

Near midnight, if the weights remain untouched and Orlo thinks the clock is drawing attention, he urges a second cleaning: save the common rug, inventory the desk cloths, let a pupil carry a message about civic property. His concern becomes another observation. Residue deep in the acorn seam, the wound shape, and the rest of the case survive unless every route is abandoned.

## Where the evidence points

Credible first moves include:

- follow Merrit's notes to his burrow;
- take bottle and wound to Moss Apothecary;
- rebuild service at the Copper Kettle;
- test Bram's orchard work before rain changes it;
- compare flour, chimes, and stream rise at the mill; or
- secure the Common Chest before warrants move.

Orlo is already a suspect. His key, movements, and rehearsal can be questioned without waiting for the accounts to name him.
