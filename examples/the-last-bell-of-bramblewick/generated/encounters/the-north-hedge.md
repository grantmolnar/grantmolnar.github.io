# The North Hedge

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** location, physical-reconstruction, route, investigation

Rain has blurred the footprints but preserved their order: one dry crossing joins the unlatched garden door to the school wash yard.

## Opening View

The North Hedge loses its memory one footprint at a time. Wil's kitchen cord stops no neighbor and persuades no dog; rain works at every edge. Shallow impressions soften under newer heels. Pale lime remains in one print, and a dark blue thread catches on the schoolward gate. The lane joins Hearth Hall's garden to the covered school wash yard, but sequence—not destination—is what the mud is about to erase.

## Encounter Material

## A path being erased

Wil's kitchen cord has already failed. Neighbors have stepped over it, a dog has gone under it, and rain is rounding every edge. Beneath sharper post-rain heels, a shallower line still runs schoolward. Read what remains by depth and overlap, not by shape alone.

The path leaves Hearth Hall's north garden door, follows a limewashed retaining wall, passes the orchard's low gate, and reaches the school wash yard. Dry, it takes five or six minutes. In the present mud, eight or nine.

## The pre-rain crossing

Under the hedge overhang, shallow shoe impressions lie beneath deep post-rain heels. Their rounded track runs from the map-room lane toward the school without turning to the inn, mill, or green.

Pale Hearth Hall lime sits at the bottom of one old print beneath rain mud. At the low gate, dark blue wool catches on the inner thorn where a schoolward traveler would brush the right cuff. The weave and dye match Orlo's coat; the thread still needs timing and route around it.

The garden door pulls shut by an outer strap but cannot latch from outside. A reconstruction leaves it as Wil found it: apparently closed, latch open, no forced entry.

## The route and the alibi

A traveler leaving at 8:48 can pull the door shut, cross before rain at 8:50, reach the covered wash yard near 8:52, and enter the school by 8:54. Jory's sighting and the pupils' late fourth stanza fit that walk. A school departure at 8:45 cannot support a round trip before rain; testing the route only in current mud overstates the travel time.

The mill fixes the rain. Bram's orchard traces keep him above the lower path. Grain carriers explain older mixed traffic along the broad delivery lane, not the direct pre-rain crossing from the map-room garden.

## What the hedge carries

Lime, thread, timing, door, and Orlo's washed cuff join Hearth Hall to the school. Motive, the blow, and the ghost hearths come from elsewhere.

Tracking magic may distinguish the older crossing, later searchers, and animal traffic. Object-following divination may carry the thread to Orlo's coat or the lime to Hearth Hall. Neither needs to produce a glowing name.

## Keeping the path useful

Wil can close the path, but parents need the school, Cora needs the sluice lane, and Bram's cousins need the orchard gate. Mark the old impressions, sketch their order, lift the thread, and reopen the route. Preserving the sequence matters more than preserving every footprint.

If Orlo flees, his new tracks cut deep and sharp toward the old sheep tunnel. They cannot be mistaken for the earlier crossing.

## Carrying the route into the hall

A measured walk, the door-strap demonstration, the mill's 8:50 mark, the rounded track, and the school evidence placing Orlo at 8:54 make the exhibit. No one need believe that a single blurred footprint is uniquely his.
