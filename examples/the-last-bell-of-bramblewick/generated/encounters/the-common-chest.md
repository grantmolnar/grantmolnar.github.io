# The Common Chest

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** location, accounts, records, investigation

Two keys open two abuses of common money: Hester's hidden bridge and Orlo's six-year school fund built on dead children's names.

## Opening View

The Common Chest opens only after Hester Rowan and Nim Thatch disagree about how. Two keys turn before villagers packed onto the stair. Iron cabinets, tied warrants, a seal press, and an ignored coffer wait below. Bridge receipts take one table; school, grain, shoe, and lamp-oil warrants take another. Nim numbers each bundle before it crosses the threshold, making preservation public even when names must remain private.

## Encounter Material

## Two keys before the paper

Three ironbound cabinets wait behind two oak doors beneath Hester's burrow. Hester has one key; Nim arrives with the second key and rain on his shoulders. Before touching the lock, he asks which account the witnesses mean to test. Older bundles sit tied and sealed in open pigeonholes; the current-warrant cabinet remains shut.

Hester's request for witnesses is proper and convenient. She wants time to explain unsigned bridge warrants. Open with both keyholders and witnesses, or force the cabinet and spend the morning defending broken strings and substituted pages.

When the purpose is named, Nim numbers each removed bundle aloud while Hester writes. The first disagreement begins before the paper does: bridge warrants, household shares, or school redemptions?

## Six bundles, three accounts

Treat the work as comparison:

1. pull the six bundles cited by Merrit;
2. divide household shares, bridge expenses, and school redemptions;
3. compare request hand, authorizing seal, receiving initials, and surrendered warrant; and
4. decide whether beneficiary initials belong in the public packet or under seal.

Bookkeeping expertise finds altered totals and replaced string quickly. Patient sorting still builds a clean chain.

## The three impossible hearths

**Dolly Reed**, **Milo Tansy**, and **Pipkin Wold** begin receiving adult grain and winter-coin shares six years ago. None pays chimney levy, pasture tithe, or roof tax. None signs a receipt.

Tiny memorial crosses appear on the first warrants and vanish from later copies. Merrit's annotations pair each name with a childhood burial date and point to the Chapel of the Open Door. The chest shows sustained payment to impossible households. The chapel says who those names were.

## The school chain

The shares travel through ordinary hands:

- Bramble Mill releases grain to school kitchen helpers;
- Mara's enlarged rehearsal trays redeem meal warrants;
- craftspeople accept shoe and cloak warrants for family initials;
- Orlo's clean-copy hand orders slate, lamp oil, and soap; and
- surrendered originals return under the school seal and his receiving initials.

No warrant labels itself theft. Six years of the same nonexistent hearths feeding the same school network do the work together. Chest, mill, inn, and chapel can establish the chain even before the schoolhouse opens.

A narrow packet names beneficiary families. Leave it sealed, record only the number of recipients, or inspect it privately to confirm that Orlo took nothing for himself. The money served real children. The forged rule and the murder remained his.

## Hester's bridge file

Hester's file is untidy and finite. After spring floodwater exposed the east pier, she issued emergency warrants without a vote. Quarry tallies, mason marks, mill-cart entries, and stone in the new pier account for the sum. She hid the work because the bridge remains unsafe and rivals had rejected it twice.

Her keys and vouchers do not touch the dead children's names, school initials, or missing audit leaves. Hester kept her key while serving pie; Nim kept his in Hearth Hall's west alcove. Orlo created the older entries through clean Hearth Book copies and normal school redemptions, without opening this cabinet himself.

## Interference and custody

Once Orlo hears the cabinet is open, a pupil brings a courteous request to seal the “children's charity papers.” The request alone is advocacy. Left unsecured, the room draws Orlo later to the beneficiary packet, not the bundles that prove the scheme. Six years of copies elsewhere remain beyond one pair of hands.

For the moot, choose a short chain: one ghost-hearth authorization, one mill or vendor redemption, one school receiving mark, and the chapel burial. Three complete examples carry more weight than a cart of paper.
