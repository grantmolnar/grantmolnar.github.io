## Encounter Material

## The mill under storm water

Bramble Mill straddles the stream on a stone undercroft, low rooms tucked beneath a turf roof. Rain drums overhead; brown water hammers a locked wheel. Cora Bramble, apprentice Senn Barley, and two neighbors are shifting grain from the damp eastern wall. The work leaves a better chronology than a polished alibi.

At 8:45 the shaft bell rang its quarter chime. Five minutes later, the gauge jumped two painted marks and spray struck the inspection window. Cora ordered the emergency stop when a branch entered the race. Senn wrote both events before anyone knew Merrit was dead. The brake is still hot, the capstan rope bears three fresh sets of hands, and Cora's boot remains wedged beneath the sack shown on Senn's rough diagram.

These are ordinary facts made before anyone needed a story.

## The ergot notice

Cora first denies entering the map room. Shown the flour, Merrit's wrist abrasion, or Perrin's sealed sample, she admits the 5:40 visit. Her damp notice remains beneath the account stone. It identifies a bitter Crowbank lot, orders it held from grinding, and asks Merrit for a quiet recall before Founders' Supper.

Merrit refused secrecy. Cora tried to pull the sealed notice from his hand; its cord scraped his wrist, and flour shook from her sleeve across sill and chair. She feared a public ergot scare would destroy the mill before she could isolate the grain. Her lie protects the mill and her pride.

Preserving the sealed lot and arranging a controlled recall protects households with less panic. Immediate announcement costs contracts but reaches any sacks already distributed. The murder evidence survives either choice.

## The rain clock

The mill keeps three clocks without trusting memory:

- the shaft bell at 8:45 while the wheel still turned;
- the gauge slate's abrupt rise at 8:50; and
- the emergency brake set before leaves and roof straw reached the race.

Senn heard Jory test the bell rope after the stop and heard the true last bell ten minutes later. Cora cannot say whether water masked Hearth Hall's clock or it had already stopped. The mill fixes the rain, not the staged hands.

Blood beneath rain at Hearth Hall moves the death before 8:50. The dry-to-wet hedge route and Orlo's late school arrival then expose the crossing interval.

## Grain warrants and the school

For six years, three household names repeatedly drew small grain shares. No householder collected them. Rotating school kitchen helpers signed for the sacks under Orlo's standing instruction and charged Common Chest warrant numbers.

Cora assumed the hearths were quiet donors. She can name sacks, dates, and carriers. The chest identifies authorization; the chapel identifies the dead children. The sluice lane reaches the North Hedge and school wash yard, serving both grain deliveries and route reconstruction.

## Hester's bridge file

The mill cart ledger records three loads of cut stone carried to the east bridge under Hester's disputed vouchers. The stone stands in the new pier despite the unfinished parapet. Hester hid unauthorized emergency work among routine hauls; she did not pocket the money. The Common Chest completes the account.

## Cora at the sluice

Cora speaks plainly about machinery and badly about shame. Ask what happened at each chime, let Senn produce the slate, then place the sealed notice before her.

Once the murder interval clears her, she becomes a severe witness for the rain time, warrant deliveries, and bridge stone. She will expose her own concealment before calling Orlo's use of stolen shares lawful.
