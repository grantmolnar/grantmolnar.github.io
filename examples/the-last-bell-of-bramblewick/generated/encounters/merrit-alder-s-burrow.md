# Merrit Alder's Burrow

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** location, victimology, records, investigation, social

Thirty-four years of village memory separate Merrit's useful records, private leverage, and the ghost-hearth audit that made him dangerous to Orlo.

## Opening View

Bram turns the round green key twice. A prepared guest alcove and cold parlor yield to thirty-four years of wills, debts, burials, relief warrants, and private letters curved along the earthen walls. Three hearth names lie on a slate table beside burial dates and school expenses, the last sentence scored into the paper. Bram opens the archive, then keeps one hand on its door.

## Encounter Material

## A house built around memory

Merrit Alder's home is cut into the orchard hill behind a round green door Bram has painted twice and Merrit never praised. The front rooms offer a narrow kitchen, a cold parlor, and a guest alcove prepared with clean blankets and no needless comfort. The rear archive curves with the earthwork: thirty-four years of Hearth Books, wills, debts, burials, relief warrants, private letters, and packets in Merrit's small exact hand.

Here is the use and danger of Merrit. He kept the copy that could save a widow from convenient village memory. He also put human weakness in drawers and waited for it to acquire value.

Bram has a household key. Merrit's invitation authorizes review of the audit. The choice is breadth: take what serves the murder, inventory and seal the rest, or open every private packet because the door is available.

## The archive table

Merrit's slate table holds three hearth names beside six years of grain and coin. Each bears a childhood burial date in different ink and the question: *Who copied them forward?* Another column points to school meals, shoes, slate, and lamp oil. The final proof leaves went with him to Hearth Hall.

The table defines the ghost-hearth scheme before it identifies the hand. The ages and dates cannot describe adult households. The Common Chest follows the shares; the chapel restores the names to the dead children; the school explains the recurring initials.

One line is pressed harder than the rest:

> Charity administered in secret becomes dominion, even when the bread is real.

An agenda note rejects a sealed correction, demands recovery of the remaining shares, and places future school relief under the Hearth Book keeper. Merrit was right about the forgery and interested in inheriting its authority.

## Earlier visitors preserved in paper

### Bram's inheritance

Three incompatible wills leave Bram the orchard, divide it among cousins, or place it in common trust. The latest signed draft is absent from the drawer he searched. Green-brown cuff thread and disturbed dust fix an earlier intrusion, not an 8:44 presence.

### Mara's envelopes

Coin wrapped in excise slips for plum sugar and imported yeast sits under Merrit's index name, “Kettle smoke.” A drainage map marks the concealed brandy store. The payments were extortion; the latest demand was received before the murder.

### Perrin's dosage records

Older treatment cards preserve a previous overprescription and later altered figures. The current bottle can be measured against them. Perrin had reason to fear exposure and to misdescribe the body. The dose still did not kill Merrit.

### Hester's bridge disbursement

A copied warrant lists stone, willow piles, and night labor paid without a public vote. Merrit kept this file apart from the impossible hearths. The bridge and Common Chest account for the goods.

### Cora's grain notice

Cora's sealed ergot memorandum carries flour in its seal and Merrit's shallow wrist blood on its cord. Both belong to the pre-supper struggle. Her later movements remain for the mill to settle.

## The clock receipt and acorn set

A clockwright's receipt from three weeks earlier records cleaning, balancing, and all four quarter chimes; Merrit notes less than a minute lost in ten days. The packet also sketches the brass acorn weights and a crescent casting flaw. It cannot name the hand that stopped the clock or swung the weight. It makes accident harder to maintain.

## Bram at home

Bram arrives angry if excluded and ashamed when shown the wills. He begins with Merrit's changing intentions. The drawer thread brings out the archive and map-room searches. Keep Bram's debts behind the narrower question of what the thread proves.

He knows the filing system, orchard paths, and duplicate keys. He identifies other people's packets eagerly and his own reluctantly. Promise nothing about unrelated material until the sealed inventory is clear.

## What the archive can settle

Useful choices include:

- remove only audit files and leave private leverage for the magistrate;
- inventory suspect packets but seal unrelated contents from the moot;
- let Bram interpret while someone checks his references;
- compare Merrit's working hand with successive clean Hearth Books; and
- follow money to the chest, names to the chapel, or initials to the school.

Merrit was coercive, ambitious, and correct about the records that got him killed.
