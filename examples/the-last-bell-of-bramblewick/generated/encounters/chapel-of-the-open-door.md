# Chapel of the Open Door

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** location, records, victimology, investigation

Amity restores three ghost hearths to the children whose names were taken and preserves the loan that first put those names under Orlo's hand.

## Opening View

Sister Amity moves the dead before she moves the book. Rain enters the open arch, and she carries the painted stones of Dolly Reed, Milo Tansy, and Pipkin Wold beyond its reach. Her palm stays on the burial register. A lending slip, school memorial program, and red guide marks lie beside it while families watch from the alcoves. No record opens until the investigators speak the children's names as names, not entries.

## Encounter Material

## Three stones moved out of rain

Rain has crossed the open arch and darkened the nearest floor stones. Amity carries ribbons and painted name-stones farther into the memorial alcoves while relatives wait under the eaves. The register stays on its shelf.

Amity opens it only after hearing the proposition the pages must support. No cutting, charcoal rubbing, or carrying leaves into rain. She speaks the children's names before discussing the fraud made from them.

**Dolly Reed** died of winter fever at six. **Milo Tansy** drowned at nine while pulling a younger cousin from the mill race. **Pipkin Wold** died at four during the red cough. None lived to establish an adult hearth or claim common shares.

The Hearth Book revives the burial spellings exactly, including Amity's long-tailed *y* in “Dolly.” Household succession would continue a living line, not restore a child's full memorial name years later.

## The delayed entries

Dolly and Pipkin entered the chapel book promptly but waited until yearly review for Merrit's working Hearth Book. Their families were grieving, and Orlo asked Amity to spare them immediate civic visits. Milo died during a flood week when several books were being recopied.

Amity agreed to delay the notices. She never authorized adult hearths or saw the later warrants. The delay gave an authorized copyist room to carry the names forward as though they had matured elsewhere.

Six years ago, Orlo borrowed the register “for a school memorial roll.” His signature remains on the lending slip. Thin red guides mark the three entries; the same pencil appears on his school copy sheets. Amity remembers him asking which dead children had no living siblings likely to inherit their names.

## Independent links

The chapel cannot follow coin, but it preserves three joins:

- Founders' memorial ribbons carry the same names back to these entries;
- Amity's annual notices remove the children from family lists before the ghost hearths appear; and
- Orlo's school memorial program lists all three one month before the first false warrants.

Together they show selection rather than clerical duplication, without reading living beneficiaries into the hall.

## Amity's terms

Amity believes Orlo began with mercy and ended by treating grief, poverty, and consent as materials under his hand. She will testify to the register, lending slip, and delayed notices. She asks that the dead children not become accusations against their families.

She has no beneficiary list. For proof that the school helped real children, she points to its distribution box and the chest's sealed packet. At the moot she will say three things: the names belonged to dead children; Orlo selected them; the resulting hearths were never lawful.

## The cost of opening old grief

Relatives may arrive once word spreads. Some rage at the use of the names; others fear that condemning the scheme condemns the children it fed. Amity can certify copies under her seal and return every original to its shelf.

Orlo answers that delayed notices made the entries ambiguous and Merrit could have corrected them sooner. His lending slip and six years of warrants answer him without asking Amity to guess what he intended.
