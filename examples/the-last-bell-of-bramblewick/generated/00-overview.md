## Synopsis

At Bramblewick's Founders' Supper, Merrit Alder—the keeper who remembers every birth, debt, burial, and borrowed chimney—announces that three village households never existed. He promises to name the hand behind them before breakfast. Minutes after the last bell, he is found dead beneath a clock stopped at nine. The bridge is unsafe, the reeve's own accounts are under suspicion, and Merrit's invited outsiders must keep one wet room from hardening into six convenient stories before first bell.

## Premise

A village record keeper is murdered beneath a deliberately stopped clock after promising to expose three households that never existed. With the bridge closed by storm and the reeve among six compromised suspects, Merrit's invited witnesses must preserve a changing scene, test the alibis against rain and village work, and carry a complete accusation to the First-Bell Moot.

## Explanation

### Core answer

Schoolmaster **Orlo Vane** killed Merrit Alder. Six years earlier, Orlo copied three children who had died young into the Hearth Book as adult households. Their common shares bought school meals, clothes, slate, lamp oil, medicine, and small grants for poor families. Merrit discovered the ghost hearths and meant to read Orlo's name and the beneficiary families into the first-bell record, recover the remaining shares, and place future relief under his own office. Orlo asked for a sealed correction, repayment over time, and protection for the families. Merrit refused. Orlo killed him because Merrit would place the relief under another office and make Orlo one accountable steward among several. The meals were real; what Orlo would not surrender was authorship, sole discretion, and the right to decide which rule became mercy.

### Murder sequence

- **8:31 p.m.** Merrit withdraws to the map room with the audit folio.
- **8:33 p.m.** Orlo tells the pupils to begin without him, passes behind the long pantry, and takes the covered service way to Hearth Hall's screened clerk's passage.
- **8:38 p.m.** His authorized copyist's key opens the passage-side map-room door without requiring him to cross the crowded hall.
- **8:44 p.m.** Merrit refuses the sealed correction and says Orlo and the beneficiary families will enter the public record. Orlo strikes him with the nearest brass acorn map weight.
- **8:45–8:48 p.m.** Orlo wipes and returns the weight, tears out the two comparison leaves naming his hand and the school redemptions, moves the clock hands to nine, wedges the pendulum with blue school chalk, cracks the lower glass, and shifts Merrit beneath the case.
- **8:48 p.m.** He leaves through the north garden door and crosses the dry North Hedge path.
- **8:50 p.m.** Rain begins after blood has spread across the rug.
- **8:52 p.m.** Orlo washes his hand and cuff in the school yard.
- **8:54 p.m.** He feeds the stolen leaves through the banked stove's rear ash door; Jory Pike then sees him enter the school.
- **8:55 p.m.** Orlo joins the final stanza.
- **9:00 p.m.** The last bell makes his presence public at the hour shown by the staged clock.

### Suspects at a glance

- **Bram Alder** wants Merrit's inheritance and lies about searching the desk. Pipe ash and cuff thread place him in the room earlier; orchard work, tools, dog tracks, chimes, and rain account for the murder interval.
- **Mara Kettle** paid Merrit to hide an untaxed brandy route. The burned demand and cellar ledger are real; a burst crock, pantry workers, and supper service keep her away from the map room.
- **Cora Bramble** struggled with Merrit over an ergot-tainted grain lot before supper. Flour and a wrist abrasion belong to that quarrel; the mill brake, apprentice, stream rise, and work slate place her later.
- **Perrin Moss** altered dosage records and first misstates the cause of death. The tincture is nonlethal, the wound is a focused blow, and the Fen sickroom accounts for his time.
- **Hester Rowan** spent common funds on an emergency bridge without authorization. Stone, carts, and service witnesses account for both the money and her movements.
- **Orlo Vane** controls the threatened scheme, carries the relevant key and chalk, has no alibi before 8:54, and brings the map room to the school in his key, cuff, wash basin, and revisions.

### Investigative authority

Merrit's signed invitation appoints the newcomers as outside witnesses to his audit. Bramblewick's storm custom divides the rest. Constable Wil Sloe holds the scene, persons, and physical exhibits. Record custodians open civic books. Willing witnesses answer questions. The outside witnesses may preserve common property, inspect those records, and recommend detention to the moot. They may not pronounce guilt, force a private burrow, or let Hester judge her own accounts. Hester keeps order and arranges access; Sister Amity, Nim Thatch, or householders chosen by lot witness sensitive openings and searches.

### What constitutes a complete case

A complete accusation joins six claims:

1. Merrit died before the last bell.
2. The clock was deliberately staged.
3. The killer entered lawfully and left by the North Hedge.
4. Orlo's school alibi begins only after the murder.
5. Merrit was about to expose a scheme controlled by Orlo.
6. Orlo carried the crime from Hearth Hall to the school.

The case does not require every innocent suspect cleared, every beneficiary named, the exact weapon found, or a confession.

### When the investigation stalls

Bring authored evidence forward through village work. Hester asks which claim still lacks support. Elsie remembers the school tray. Amity offers certified burial entries without family names. Rising water sends Senn with the mill slate. A pupil asks why Orlo corrected only the final stanza. Wil requests a measured walk from the garden door to the wash yard. Each intervention exposes an existing route and returns the next choice to the table.

### Privacy and collateral wrongdoing

Beneficiary names may remain sealed. Bram's search, Mara's brandy, Cora's grain concealment, Perrin's altered doses, and Hester's bridge warrants survive their exoneration from murder. Protecting a child and hiding Orlo's warrants are separate acts; innocence does not require exposing another suspect's offense.
