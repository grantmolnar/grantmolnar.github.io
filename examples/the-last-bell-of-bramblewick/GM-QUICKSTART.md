# GM Quickstart: The Last Bell of Bramblewick

Use this document first. The generated packet supplies the complete encounter sheets, clue list, revelation list, validation report, and example play summary; this guide tells you what must remain clear while running them.

## The mystery in one paragraph

Schoolmaster Orlo Vane created three fictitious halfling households from the names of children who died young and diverted their common shares into a secret fund for poor pupils and their families. Merrit Alder discovered the fraud and planned to name Orlo and the beneficiary families at first bell, recover the remaining shares, and place future relief under his own office. Orlo came seeking a sealed correction and repayment. At 8:44 p.m., after Merrit refused, Orlo killed him with the nearest brass acorn weight, staged the clock at nine, removed the final audit leaves, crossed the North Hedge before the 8:50 rain, washed, burned the leaves at the school, and joined only the final rehearsal stanza. His visible presence at the nine o'clock bell is the alibi he manufactured.

## The table contract

- The killer is one of the six named suspects introduced at Hearth Hall.
- Every physical clue is genuine. Innocent suspects leave real traces for real reasons.
- Basic evidence is not gated behind a roll. Checks add speed, precision, or custody, not access to the mystery.
- No single clue, child witness, confession, spell, weapon identification, or suspect-clearing scene is indispensable.
- A fresh group can win cleanly: identify Orlo, preserve beneficiary privacy, keep the school open, and refer collateral wrongdoing separately.

## Temporary authority

Merrit's signed invitation makes the newcomers outside witnesses before the murder. Afterward, Bramblewick's storm custom divides responsibility: Constable Wil Sloe controls the scene, custody, and movement of persons; ordinary custodians control civic records; Hester keeps order but cannot rule on her own accounts; and the outside witnesses may preserve common property, question willing witnesses, inspect civic records with their custodians, and recommend detention. They cannot compel private searches, pronounce guilt by themselves, or punish anyone. Sister Amity, Nim Thatch, and householders chosen by lot can witness sensitive access.

## Suspect sheet

### Orlo Vane — schoolmaster and murderer

Run his defense as grammatical retreat. He concedes one object, revises the sentence around it, and never volunteers the next fact. Once the ghost hearths are proved, he asks beneficiary families to stand so judgment appears to take their food. Keep their names sealed; the warrants, burials, hand, and receiving marks prove the scheme without making the children his shield.

**What looks suspicious:** copyist's key, blue chalk, school warrants, missing rehearsal interval, washed cuff, changing account.  
**What he says:** he led the rehearsal and never went to the map room.  
**What is true:** he created the fund to help children and fears exposing their families.  
**How he defends himself:** he concedes isolated facts while denying that they form one sequence. He attacks gaps, not the evidence itself.

### Bram Alder — indebted heir

**What looks suspicious:** public quarrel, inheritance motive, pipe ash, cuff thread, discovery of the body, false denial of an earlier visit.  
**Hidden wrong:** he searched Merrit's desk for the latest will.  
**Clearing routes:** fresh brace cuts, dry sawdust, tools, dog tracks, mill chime, and rain place him in the orchard during the murder.

### Mara Kettle — innkeeper

**What looks suspicious:** private payments, burned demand, hidden cellar ledger, control of food and drink.  
**Hidden wrong:** untaxed brandy brought through an old drain; Merrit extorted her.  
**Clearing routes:** Merrit remains alert after his measured tincture, and multiple witnesses plus the burst crock place Mara in the pantry.

### Cora Bramble — miller

**What looks suspicious:** flour at the scene, earlier struggle, threatened mill, Merrit's wrist abrasion.  
**Hidden wrong:** she concealed an ergot-tainted grain lot.  
**Clearing routes:** the abrasion and flour predate supper; the hot brake, apprentice account, gauge slate, and emergency stop place her at the mill.

### Perrin Moss — apothecary

**What looks suspicious:** open medicine bottle, altered records, false initial diagnosis, attempt to take custody of the bottle.  
**Hidden wrong:** prior overprescription and falsified dosage cards.  
**Clearing routes:** the dose is customary and nonlethal; the focused wound killed Merrit; the Fen household accounts for Perrin's interval.

### Hester Rowan — reeve

**What looks suspicious:** disputed payments, procedural delay, control over access to the Common Chest.  
**Hidden wrong:** she funded the emergency bridge without a village vote.  
**Clearing routes:** the bridge expenditure is fully accounted for, and witnesses place her serving pie in the hall.

## The six propositions of the accusation

The six propositions may be proved in any order. Orlo listens for whichever strand remains loose.

1. **Earlier death:** the wound, blood beneath rain, medical interval, and 8:50 rain clock place death before nine.
2. **Staged clock:** glass lies on blood, chalk wedges the pendulum, and the clock had recently kept good time.
3. **Lawful entry and north exit:** no forced entry, the unlatched garden door, and the pre-rain track connect hall to school.
4. **Late alibi:** four stanzas precede Orlo; Jory sees him enter at 8:54; another witness hears him only in the final stanza.
5. **Orlo's threatened scheme:** dead children's names become ghost hearths; their shares flow through school warrants in Orlo's hand.
6. **Hall-to-school linkage:** key lime, blue cuff thread, washed cuff, blood dilution and collar lint in the basin, and Orlo's revised story join the route to him.

The optional acorn-weight evidence strengthens the method and linkage. It is not required.

## When the investigation stalls

First identify the missing category, then surface an existing route toward it.

- **They accept nine o'clock:** have Elsie report the 8:50 rain crossing, the mill crew bring its gauge slate, or Perrin concede that the body had been dead longer.
- **They fixate on one suspect's secret:** ask what still explains the chalk, north route, late school arrival, and ghost warrants together.
- **They have motive but no opportunity:** direct attention to the school rehearsal, North Hedge, key register, and wash basin.
- **They have route and alibi but no motive:** let Nim request the Common Chest warrants or Amity offer the burial register.
- **They fear questioning children:** use marked practice sheets, the candle, the neighbor's hearing, and separate factual statements; Jory is never indispensable.
- **They want to go straight to the moot:** Hester permits it. The hearing exposes the precise gap, and the outside witnesses may repair that gap publicly or seek detention rather than conviction.

Do not create a new clue, make Orlo blunder theatrically, or force a confession. Village activity should carry authored evidence into view: a tray arrives, a slate is produced, a custodian offers certification, or a witness asks to correct a statement.

## Running the two commonly unvisited encounters

### Moss Apothecary

Use it when the table is caught between poison, accident, and deliberate violence, or when Perrin becomes the leading suspect. The scene should produce two separations: medicine did not kill Merrit, and Perrin nevertheless falsified medical records. Perrin's expertise remains usable evidence after his lie is exposed. The exact weapon match is optional.

### Bramble Mill

Use it when the rain time, Cora's flour trace, Bram's quarter-hour chronology, or the grain-warrant chain needs independent support. The mill is a physical clock: 8:45 shaft chime, 8:50 gauge jump, emergency stop, hot brake, and contemporaneous slate. It should not merely repeat the hedge; it fixes when the hedge surface changed.

## First-Bell Moot thresholds

- **Suspicion:** one motive, lie, or unexplained trace; enough to continue questioning.
- **Detention:** serious connection plus present risk of flight, tampering, or witness access.
- **Public accusation:** a named, coherent sequence including earlier death, false time or alibi, motive, and opportunity.
- **Complete case:** all six propositions joined. The village can find against Orlo without confession or exact weapon proof.

A correct but thin accusation does not end the adventure. Orlo identifies the gap; the hall divides; the outside witnesses may recall evidence, call another witness, demonstrate the route, reduce its demand to detention, or continue investigating.

## Privacy and aftermath

The beneficiary box can remain sealed. Prove the fraud with names of the dead, warrant numbers, Orlo's hand, and aggregate redemptions. After the murder finding, handle Bram's trespass, Mara's brandy, Cora's ergot, Perrin's dosage fraud, and Hester's spending as separate civic matters. A clean ending keeps the school open under lawful oversight and does not require making the innocent suspects morally spotless.
