# Demonstration Company: The Blackbriar Commission

The demonstrated company consists of four outsiders carrying Canon Ysra Vale's sealed commission: Sabren Holt, Lysa Marrin, Toma Brack, and Neris Kade. They did not know Mara Sedge before reaching Blackbriar Vale, and they are not presumed to be intimate friends. Ysra dispatched the original inspection before Mara's arrest; a parish rider carrying Caldus's addendum meets them before dawn and reports the accelerated execution. The commission gives them a common duty, several narrow legal permissions, and a non-accidental reason to stand between Judith Crowl and an execution without deciding their methods in advance.

Village witnesses begin calling them **the Blackbriar Commission** because Sabren repeatedly states the instrument's exact limits in public and because the four continue acting together after Judith tries to separate inspector, healer, graveworker, and tracker into different obligations.

Canon Ysra asks them to determine:

1. whether Mara Sedge's emergency conviction rests on lawful evidence;
2. why burial fees and parish graves do not agree;
3. where Nell Sedge has been taken; and
4. whether Saint Mercy House is obstructing inspection.

The commission permits questioning, inspection of parish and charitable records, preservation of evidence, and temporary suspension of an execution while evidence is reviewed. It does not authorize unrestricted seizure, killing, command over the reeve's guards, or permanent rule over the vale. Every expansion of practical authority therefore has to come from public evidence, willing local cooperation, immediate rescue necessity, or ordinary self-defense.

## Sabren Holt

**Function:** Canon examiner and public challenger.

**What Sabren brings:** Sabren reads warrants, household instruments, debt terms, custody forms, and the distance between a lawful sentence and a clerk's imitation of one. He carries Ysra's commission, blank witness sheets, sealing wax, a narrow writing board, three copies of the valley burial ordinance, and a small iron knife used to cut bindings without destroying pages.

**Opening position:** Stay Mara's execution, preserve the record, compel the reeve to state the legal basis of every order, and avoid turning one unlawful burning into an excuse for unbounded outsider rule.

**Private pressure:** Sabren once invalidated a seizure on a technical defect and left before the dispossessed family could recover the house. He fears winning the argument while leaving the people who testified exposed.

**Red line:** A frightened official may be protected long enough to testify; protection does not become immunity from accounting.

**Temptation:** Judith offers Sabren a complete confession in exchange for accepting one signed meal and promising to judge only acts committed after the famine. The confession would be genuine, useful, and designed to erase fifteen years of victims.

**Table voice:** Exact, restrained, and most forceful when naming what another speaker has avoided. “You have described necessity, Beadle. I asked who signed the order and where the body went.”

**Growth demanded:** Sabren must use law to preserve rescue and evidence rather than make rescue wait for perfect jurisdiction. His best ending transfers temporary authority to named local custodians and preserves charges broader than Judith alone.

## Lysa Marrin

**Function:** Field physician and household protector.

**What Lysa brings:** Lysa can separate poison, contagion, transferred illness, fear, and deprivation without treating every symptom as proof of witchcraft. She carries cooling wraps, waxed labels, a folding litter, clean cord, bitter disinfectant, a hand lens, and a ledger that records patient, bottle, carrier, and receiving bed as separate facts.

**Opening position:** Keep Mara, Tomas, the Mercy House residents, and the infirmary patients alive. She refuses to make the children serve as bait for Judith's channels or as moral symbols in an adult prosecution.

**Private pressure:** Lysa once moved medicine from a dying prisoner to three patients judged more likely to survive. The triage was defensible; the prisoner's family was never told who decided. Judith learns this through a stolen name and offers to make the decision disappear from every record.

**Red line:** A rescued body without food, care, shelter, and a receiving person is a transfer, not a completed rescue.

**Temptation:** Judith can return Reeve Pell's illness to herself for one hour if Lysa publicly certifies Saint Mercy House as medically necessary and delays evacuation.

**Table voice:** Practical and intolerant of abstract reassurance. “Which child, which bottle, which bed? ‘The infirmary is safe’ is not an answer I can carry.”

**Growth demanded:** Lysa must accept that witnesses may choose risk after informed preparation. Her best ending keeps care visible without using medical authority to silence victims who want to testify.

## Toma Brack

**Function:** Gravewright, bell-rigger, and occult breaker.

**What Toma brings:** Toma knows bodies, lime, burned timber, load-bearing frames, bell fittings, witness rites, and the difference between symbolic closure and completed physical work. He carries lifting hooks, canvas masks, chalk, wedges, a short pry bar, a bronze pin blank, a mason's square, and a packet of burial tags that cannot be erased without cutting them apart.

**Opening position:** Find the missing dead, identify them honestly, and deny Judith the convenience of calling difficult bodies spiritually resolved before they are lifted and cared for.

**Private pressure:** Toma supervised a fever burial where six unidentified dead were recorded under one household name to finish before rain. No pact followed, but the surviving families never received a correction. The Worm in White offers him exact identifications if he leaves four Blackbriar bodies in the lime.

**Red line:** “Known enough” must identify a person, the evidence used, and the uncertainty that remains. A rite cannot launder a guessed name.

**Temptation:** The Worm offers to hold every corpse still and prevent exposure if Toma rings the honest bell without naming who denied burial.

**Table voice:** Material, patient, and severe about unfinished labor. “The bell can witness a body only after someone says whose hands put it here.”

**Growth demanded:** Toma must let many imperfect workers share the burial operation rather than making correctness depend on his personal control. His best ending leaves a repeatable record and trained local crews.

## Neris Kade

**Function:** Tracker, infiltrator, and channel hunter.

**What Neris brings:** Neris reads wagon compression, bird handoffs, ash packets, hidden road cuts, serviced locks, false rooms, and the small delays produced when several systems pretend to be one omniscient intelligence. She carries dull mirror cloth, red and white route cord, a compact bow, wax for taking impressions, small shears, climbing hooks, and folded cards bearing chosen aliases rather than full names.

**Opening position:** Find Nell's route, learn how Judith receives information, and turn the witch's finite couriers and service roads against her before the village assumes she sees everything.

**Private pressure:** Neris spent two years carrying sealed messages for a magistrate whose prisoners disappeared. She never opened a packet and told herself ignorance limited responsibility. Judith offers proof that one vanished prisoner survived if Neris delivers a single covered vessel to Blackbriar Hall.

**Red line:** A messenger, route, or familiar is evidence only when its physical handoff and destination are recorded. Intuition about “where the witch must know” cannot become fact.

**Temptation:** The Child Behind Glass offers Neris a reflected route straight to Judith's unguarded body in exchange for borrowing her face until dawn.

**Table voice:** Brief and positional. “One crow left. Two ash packets remain. Judith knows the bridge failed; she does not know which children crossed the drain.”

**Growth demanded:** Neris must expose her own prior service rather than using secret competence as a substitute for trust. Her best ending makes routes and channels legible to local people who will remain after the outsiders leave.

## Demonstrated company tensions

The four agree that Judith is the central perpetrator and that the children must be rescued. They disagree about sequence and acceptable risk.

- Sabren wants a public stay and copied evidence before the village's frightened officials can rewrite events.
- Lysa wants the Mercy House patients moved before Judith can release stored illnesses.
- Toma argues that the White Pits cannot be postponed until after the feast because every untreated body remains a weapon.
- Neris wants to exploit Crow Wood and Blackbriar Hall before Judith understands which channels have failed.

The demonstration does not resolve these differences by assigning one permanent leader. Each operation names the person responsible for law, care, material rite, and route control, then records what the others must know before the company divides.

## Questions for other companies

Before play, ask each player one question:

- What kind of gift is hardest for the character to refuse?
- Which public accusation would most damage their standing if Judith made it convincingly?
- What obligation do they believe survives even when the recipient is hateful?
- What would persuade them that a frightened collaborator has crossed into willing cruelty?

Use the answers to tailor Judith's offers and attacks. Do not use them to remove agency or declare that a character accepted a magical gift without the player's choice.
