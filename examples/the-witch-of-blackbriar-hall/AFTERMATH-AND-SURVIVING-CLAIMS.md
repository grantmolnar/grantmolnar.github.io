# Aftermath and Surviving Claims

The demonstrated playthrough ends Judith Crowl's three pacts, kills Judith after she refuses surrender, collapses the common welcome, restores the seven current names, and honestly disposes of the thirty-one founding plague dead. It does not make Blackbriar Vale administratively, medically, legally, or socially whole in one night.

This file records the demonstrated branch. Other tables should derive their own aftermath from the exact people, carriers, evidence, pacts, and feast components that survive.

## Immediate night

### Authority

Canon Ysra's sealed commission remains temporary. Sabren Holt does not inherit Judith's household machinery or Reeve Pell's office. Pell's emergency powers are suspended because he signed false seizures, concealed Judith's influence, and failed to protect witnesses. He remains available under guard to explain dockets, tokens, and cure receipts.

A temporary six-seat council controls relief and evidence until outside authorities arrive:

- Caldus Merrow holds chapel and burial records.
- Cora Pike controls the Hall stores and ordinary staff.
- Matron Eda Wren coordinates Mercy House residents and medical care.
- Dena Moor coordinates hidden witnesses and western routes.
- Mara Sedge represents falsely prosecuted households without speaking for every victim.
- One rotating household delegate records food, fuel, beds, and token revocations.

The council cannot decide criminal judgment, permanent property title, child custody, or new parish office by itself. It can keep people alive, preserve records, prevent reprisals, and request outside adjudication.

### Judith's body

Judith's body remains in the Underhall under a double custody seal: one civil witness and one chapel witness must be present whenever it is moved or examined. Her throat wound stays on her body. No ash door, reflected substitute, corpse exchange, or stolen name removes it.

Lysa Marrin records that she attempted to preserve Judith for custody after the first wound and that Judith deliberately reopened it. This preserves the difference between a failed execution, combat injury, suicide under attempted arrest, and an unexplained magical death.

### The Underhall

The Underhall is not burned immediately. It contains the Mercy Book, token table, household sockets, pact chairs, prisoner records, prepared gifts, and physical evidence of the feast. The service tunnel is controlled by Cora Pike and Neris Kade's route record. The public Hall entrance remains open for relief distribution under witness.

The feast table is dismantled object by object. No household token is destroyed until its owner, carrier, signature, and local claim are recorded.

### Saint Mercy House residents

Thirty visible children, nine adult dependents, five infirmary patients, and the hidden captives survive the evacuation. Twelve residents spend the first night in supervised unmirrored public rooms because the chapel and household beds are full. This is a capacity decision, not continued institutional custody under Judith's rules.

Matron Wren and Ressa Pold continue food, medication, and resident counts. Fen Corren supervises record comparison. Sister Harl is removed from authority and held in a prepared chapel room with two guards, access to counsel, food, and medical care. Her decision to hold the bridge and expose the feast is recorded alongside years of punishment, name movement, and concealment.

### Infirmary patients

Eda Lorn, Dall Oat, Ysa Bell, Marric Soil, and Noma Feld remain with their matched bottles until an independent medical examination can determine whether the stored symptoms have dissipated after the Worm pact's severance. No bottle is opened for demonstration. Linn Pell remains ill from Judith's returned cure and receives care without conditioning treatment on her husband's testimony.

## Pacts and patrons

### Guest in Ash

The Guest pact is severed. The original refuge doors remain open. The restored roll and the ashes of the false ledger are kept separately. The granary is not converted into a cheerful public memorial before descendants and local households decide how the site should be marked.

The nineteen dead can be remembered without the Guest speaking for them. Their released fragments do not become complete posthumous testimony about unrelated events.

### Worm in White

The Worm pact is severed only after the final four founding bodies receive independent witnessed disposition during the Underhall confrontation. The founding thirty-one are fully accounted for.

The five identified renewal victims return to household custody for public rites. Two additional renewal remains are preserved separately while the council seeks names. The end of the founding pact does not identify every later body or excuse later collaborators.

### Child Behind Glass

The Child pact is severed. Seven current and nine older recoverable names are returned. Three damaged unidentified vessels remain covered in separate padded boxes under three-key custody: one key with Caldus, one with Matron Wren, and one with a rotating former resident.

The boxes are not displayed, opened for curiosity, or described as harmless. Identification work may continue through former resident lists, household memory, and unreflected physical records.

## Feast machinery

### Mercy Book

The master binding between names and concealed obligations is severed. The pages and index remain legible and admissible as evidence. Sabren prepares three certified copies before any page leaves the chapel.

A household may request its own entry, but no household may remove or destroy the only record of another person's coercion, gift, custody, or signature.

### Token network

The common network no longer joins the vale. Five households remain actively aligned with Judith or unwilling to revoke. Several other signatures are uncertain because the signer is dead, absent, a child at the time, or no longer lives in the household.

Each remaining claim requires a local procedure:

1. identify the physical token, ash, gift, or written carrier;
2. identify who accepted or signed and who currently lives at the hearth;
3. separate refusal, private revocation, and public revocation;
4. provide replacement food, fuel, medicine, or shelter where withdrawal creates a real need;
5. record any continuing ordinary debt without preserving Judith's hidden magical terms.

Destroying all tokens in a communal fire would erase evidence and may expose households that still depend on the goods attached to them.

### Captive choir

The choir is permanently neutralized in the demonstrated branch. All seven bodies and names are free before the Underhall entry. The servants Odo substitutes cannot supply owned child names and are not treated as equivalent ritual components.

## Collaborators and witnesses

### Odo Vane

Odo is captured with the Mercy Book. He offers testimony against Judith, Cleft, Pell, and Harl. Sabren promises protection from retaliation and no immunity. His false charges, substituted ordinances, suppressed dockets, and attempt to preserve office remain chargeable.

### Varro Cleft

Cleft is captured after continuing violence through both willing and commanded guards. He receives medical care and guarded custody. His command responsibility, direct coercion, prisoner transport, and enjoyment of lawful violence are documented separately from magical command affecting subordinates.

### Garran Pike

Garran's cooperation exposes Crow Wood, the prisoner wagon, Olla's condition, several transports, and the Hall tunnel. He remains in custody. Families of transported prisoners do not lose standing because his information was useful.

### Sister Harl

Harl's bridge action saves residents and evidence. Her administration of punishment, transfers, name vessels, and institutional lies remains answerable. She is protected because her testimony and knowledge matter and because Judith's surviving allies may target her. Protection is not absolution.

### Reeve Pell

Pell's public stay, release orders, and later cooperation preserve lives. His cure bargain, false seizures, concealment, and years of deference remain. He cannot adjudicate his own case or resume ordinary authority during the review.

### Marked guards

Each guard receives an individual record distinguishing:

- acts performed under visible command;
- prior willing acceptance of Judith's service;
- ordinary paid duties;
- cruelty before or after command;
- concrete refusal or aid during the final operations.

No guard is treated as wholly innocent because command occurred, or wholly voluntary because a token existed.

## Property, relief, and institutional survival

Blackbriar Hall contains food, fuel, medicine, bedding, confiscated tools, household goods, and property seized through false prosecutions. The temporary council uses the current inventory to meet immediate needs while recording every withdrawal.

Priority order for the first three days:

1. food and clean water for displaced Mercy House residents;
2. medicine, cool storage, and staffed beds for infirmary patients;
3. fuel for households that revoke Judith during cold weather;
4. burial and identification supplies;
5. witness guards and messenger routes;
6. return of clearly identifiable personal property where doing so does not deprive dependents of immediate survival goods.

Longer-term title disputes require outside adjudication. Judith's theft does not make every object ownerless, and emergency use does not settle compensation.

## Public account

The first public statement names facts rather than declaring the vale purified:

- Mara Sedge was unlawfully condemned on planted evidence.
- Judith Crowl directed the prosecutions and willingly entered three murderous pacts.
- Seven current children and additional former residents had names taken through Saint Mercy House.
- Thirty-one pox dead were denied burial and used to sustain Judith's power.
- Nineteen famine refugees were murdered in promised shelter.
- The Hollow Feast and valley-wide invitation were stopped.
- Judith is dead and the three pact channels are severed.
- Local token claims, unidentified victims, displaced care, property restoration, and collaborator judgments remain.

The statement does not call every Saint Mercy worker a witch, every token-holder willing, every child healed, or every surviving household safe.

## Seasonal states

### One month

A favorable month includes stable food distribution, outside investigators present, the five active household claims reduced or contained, the two later bodies identified, and former Mercy residents choosing whether the institution should reopen under a new name and charter.

A difficult month includes retaliatory fires by Judith loyalists, contested property returns, illness among displaced residents, and public pressure to burn the Hall before records are copied.

### One winter

A favorable winter produces a locally controlled relief trust funded from adjudicated Blackbriar assets, separate civil and care offices, a public burial record, and child-governed rules for name and testimony use.

A difficult winter recreates Judith's system without her magic: one committee controls food, custody, evidence, and moral reputation, and frightened households accept new opaque obligations because visible need remains.

### One year

The most important question is not whether Judith returns. All three pact channels are severed and her body is accounted for. The question is whether Blackbriar Vale learns to distribute care, authority, records, and refusal so that no new patron can make indispensability into ownership.
