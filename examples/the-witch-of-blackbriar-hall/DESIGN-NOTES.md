# Design Notes: The Witch of Blackbriar Hall

The current adventure contains ninety-five leads. The unchanged demonstration encounters eighty-three of them; twelve remain unseen alternatives.


## The antagonist is the adventure's center

Judith Crowl should not become an absent mastermind whose personality exists only in old records. She begins in the opening view, can confront the party at Saint Mercy House and Blackbriar Hall, retaliates after major discoveries, and remains an active opponent during rescues and pact work.

Her presence does not mean unlimited knowledge. Judith learns through accepted gifts, written names, stolen hearth ash, mirrors, servants, and crows. Every channel is physical enough to discover, disrupt, redirect, or feed false information.

## Judith is culpable without qualification

The adventure should not resolve into a revelation that Judith was coerced by the Unwelcome Court, sacrificed a few to save many, inherited an unavoidable curse, or remains the only barrier against something worse.

She sought the patrons. She understood that the bargains required murder, sacrilege, and the exploitation of children. She renewed each pact after acquiring alternatives. She keeps real relief work functioning because dependence is more useful than indiscriminate ruin.

Her motive can be intelligible without becoming exculpatory. Judith fears insignificance, despises people who accept limits, and takes pleasure in proving that gratitude can be made stronger than love. None of that reduces her agency.

## Hate should arise from witnessed choices

Do not rely on adjectives such as “cruel” or “evil” when an action can show the fact.

- She orders Tomas to burn his mother so that survival itself becomes complicity.
- She cures one household by transferring its sickness into another and then cites the cure as evidence of benevolence.
- She shelters children, learns exactly what comfort each needs, and uses that knowledge to make the Glass Nurse persuasive.
- She preserves ordinary charity inside Saint Mercy House so that rescued residents can be portrayed as abducted ingrates.
- She makes public accusations from facts obtained through private dependence.

Judith can be witty, patient, and socially exact. Avoid making her shrill, random, or incompetent merely to signal villainy.

## The dark powers remain distinct

The Unwelcome Court is a useful collective name, but each patron governs a different violation and a different family of effects.

| Patron | Founding betrayal | Powers | Severance obligation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Guest in Ash | Promised shelter, barred the doors, burned nineteen refugees | Hearth movement, inward fire, threshold denial, concealment in ash | Open the refuge, restore the names, burn the false ledger in a freely offered hearth |
| Worm in White | Stole thirty-one plague dead from burial and erased them in quicklime | Transferred sickness, corpse command, rot, wound displacement | Identify, lift, and honestly bury or burn the dead beneath a truthful bell |
| Child Behind Glass | Copied children's names and delivered them through Moonless Mere | Dream entry, borrowed voices, command through names, false reflections | Speak names back in unborrowed reflections before breaking the vessels |

One patron's method should not casually duplicate another's. This makes Judith's capabilities legible and lets earlier work change the finale materially.

## Killing Judith is allowed but not sufficient by itself

The adventure should neither moralize against killing a plainly murderous witch nor make combat an automatic complete solution. Judith's body is protected by distributed pact effects. Once the Hollow Feast has enough accepted invitations, the Court can continue through the Mercy Book, household tokens, and captive choir after her death.

This creates several legitimate final shapes:

- sever the pacts, revoke the feast, and kill or capture a mortal Judith;
- disrupt the feast, kill Judith with one or more pacts intact, and face surviving local claims;
- force Judith into conflict with her patrons while rescuing the captives;
- destroy the invitation machinery and drive her into flight;
- fail to stop every claim but preserve named households and witnesses for a continuing struggle.

Do not turn these into a menu. The available operations arise from what the party learned, rescued, repaired, destroyed, and persuaded others to revoke.

## The victims are people, not proof objects

Mara, Tomas, Nell, the Mercy House residents, infirmary patients, refuge dead, and plague dead should retain separate interests. Rescue requires destinations, food, care, witnesses, and protection from recapture. Evidence may be lost because someone reasonably chooses life over documentation.

The children should contribute information, plans, refusals, and mutual obligations. Do not make them a homogeneous choir of innocence. The unburied dead may be angry, confused, practical, forgiving, or unwilling to serve the party's preferred timing.

## Institutional rescue has several independent states

Saint Mercy House should not collapse into a locked door followed by a mass escape. Track physical access, resident trust, hidden captives, name vessels, infirmary safety, evidence custody, receiving households, and public legitimacy separately. Success in one state may coexist with failure in another.

Residents ask practical questions because those questions are morally and operationally correct. A party that opens the doors but cannot name beds, food, guardians, medical care, or protection from Judith has not completed the rescue. This does not make Judith's ownership legitimate; it makes the work of replacing it real.

Sister Harl may turn against Judith without becoming absolved. Her defection requires proof that Judith plans to spend the residents, a viable evacuation, and a specific sacrifice she can no longer disguise as institutional necessity. Survivors retain the right to judge what Harl knowingly did.

## Irregular clues should express evidence density

The clue counts are deliberately unequal.

- Judith's identity receives seven sources because her acts leave evidence across the whole valley.
- Saint Mercy House and the invitation machinery receive six sources because many institutions interact with them.
- Exact name-return procedure receives three sources because only Nell's experimentation, the mere itself, and the chapel manual preserve it.
- Pact-severance procedures receive four sources because they are narrow material operations.

Do not add clues to make the graph visually regular. Every clue should be something the source encounter can actually contain, demonstrate, remember, or carry.

## Second-look causal chain

The original inspection predates Mara's arrest. Canon Ysra acts because duplicate burial fees, missing graves, and returned Mercy House letters already justify an inquiry. Caldus's addendum reaches the commissioned company before dawn after Nell vanishes and Mara names Judith. Judith knows the expected inspection day through an intercepted writ, advances the execution before the customary answer-night, and fails to predict the rider who changes the company's road.

The Hollow Feast is not a routine annual ceremony that happens to become important during play. Judith converts the Renewal of Mercy into final consolidation because Mara's seven-case comparison, Nell's mirror knowledge, and the incoming inspection threaten to make her distributed system publicly legible. Slow ownership is becoming evidence.

Keep four feast facts separate:

1. sixty-three copied doors represent possible household claims, not automatic invitations;
2. the Mercy Book and physical token network remain distinct components;
3. seven prepared name vessels do not equal seven living choir voices; and
4. at least two of the Mercy Book, token network, and four-voice captive choir must remain active for the common welcome.

The opening occurs shortly after dawn. Midnight creates real triage, but the company need not personally perform every hour of labor. Once a material procedure has named workers, tools, witnesses, care, authority, and a reporting route, local crews may continue it in parallel. Delegated work remains vulnerable and incomplete until recorded; it does not become automatic success.

The commission remains bounded. It supports inspection, evidence preservation, the execution stay, immediate witness protection, and later emergency action only through discovered unlawful custody, willing local cooperation, public local orders, or ordinary defense. It cannot become permanent government or owner of the Hall by narrative convenience.

## Failure changes control, evidence, and victims

Failure should usually produce one or more of the following before it ends the adventure:

- Judith moves captives or destroys one class of records.
- A witness becomes publicly discredited but remains privately useful.
- A household accepts a token under pressure.
- A pact site becomes more dangerous or can be only partially repaired.
- One child-name is returned while another vessel is moved.
- Judith loses one travel channel and intensifies another.
- The village believes the party abducted residents from Saint Mercy House.
- A final operation must be performed while also containing illness, fire, or commanded retainers.

Avoid single checks that decide whether an entire encounter exists.

## The estate is a household system, not a dungeon skin

Blackbriar Hall should preserve the distinction between Judith's useful stores, stolen property, legal machinery, captive routes, pact records, servants, and personal defenses. Taking one room or defeating one lieutenant should not silently settle all of those states. Conversely, a failed assault should expose movement and carriers rather than causing every record and captive to disappear.

Crow Wood follows the same rule. Its streams, ridges, ruts, road grades, wagon, and tunnel are fixed. Judith's marks redirect attention and route choice through physical carriers; they do not grant arbitrary geography. Crows carry finite objects and Garran needs keys, roads, and time.

## The finale joins two independent structures

The pact structure determines which patrons and power families remain. The feast structure determines whether the village's household welcomes can be joined into one invitation. They interact without becoming identical.

- Severing pacts removes Judith's powers, protections, and patron manifestations.
- Neutralizing any two of the Mercy Book, token network, and captive choir collapses the common welcome.
- Killing Judith removes her coordination but does not by itself perform either operation.
- Household revocations can protect particular homes even when the common rite or a patron survives.

Run the Underhall by applying these rules to prior play. Do not present a menu of prescribed endings or conceal a mandatory sequence behind the apparent choices.


## Knowledge, access, readiness, and control are separate

A revelation records knowledge. It does not automatically open a road, assemble labor, move a witness, recover a vessel, prepare a free hearth, or neutralize a feast component.

Track at least four questions independently:

1. What has the party actually established?
2. Can the party physically reach the relevant person, site, object, or route?
3. Does it possess the witnesses, care, tools, transport, records, and time required to act?
4. Who currently controls the people, carriers, and machinery involved?

This distinction is especially important on compact routes. A social party may understand all three severance obligations without visiting any pact site. A pact-first party may make Judith mortal while leaving Saint Mercy House and the Mercy Book under her household's control.

## Judith has one body

Judith begins physically at Saint Orra's Gallows. Thereafter, record where her body goes. An ordinary road, a valid Guest in Ash hearth passage, or a complete Child Behind Glass reflected route moves the body. Quill, a commanded guard, a borrowed voice, a hearthling, the Glass Nurse, and a patron manifestation carry her intention without duplicating her.

Between substantial party operations, Judith spends at most one major response through one surviving channel. A split party does not grant her one response per group. Site clocks may still advance when a group lingers, alarms remain active, or personnel complete an already declared movement.

Every appearance or reprisal should answer four questions:

- What channel carried the information?
- What did that channel actually reveal?
- What asset, route, or pact permission did Judith spend?
- What trace lets the party understand or contest the action?

## Direct assault remains a real route

The party may reasonably decide that Judith's public murder justifies immediate violence. The estate and Underhall therefore remain reachable without a compulsory tour of the pact sites.

Direct attackers can seize stores and records, rescue captives, divide Judith's household, expose pact carriers, neutralize feast components, force an escape, or contain a local manifestation. They do not receive missing remote severance procedures for free. The route is faster and less informed rather than forbidden or secretly complete.

## Uneven support is allowed to produce uneven knowledge

The irregular clue distribution should remain visible at the table. Social and rescue-first routes encounter broad redundancy because households and institutions touch Judith's public system. Direct, covert, and combat-heavy routes may know exact counter-rites from one source or not at all.

Do not normalize the route by moving an extra manual into the Underhall or letting one manifestation explain every missing step. An active patron can reveal its carrier and violated obligation under pressure. That is not the same as knowing the full sequence, people, and materials required at a remote site.

## Completed work persists through retaliation

Judith can attack workers, caregivers, transport, uncopied records, unsecured vessels, active carriers, and remaining claims. She cannot erase an act that has been materially completed.

- Copied evidence remains copied.
- A witnessed public refusal remains part of the public record.
- Opened refuge doors remain open.
- Identified and lifted dead remain identified and lifted.
- Honest final disposition removes a body from the Worm.
- A returned name stays returned.
- A neutralized feast component stays neutralized unless a distinct replacement operation is shown.

Retaliation should alter the next problem rather than reset the previous scene.

## Governing voice

Prefer household, legal, bodily, and material language over generalized occult fog. Judith keeps receipts, placements, custody papers, meal rolls, token ledgers, copied names, and samples of hearth ash. Her power is frightening because recognizable work has been bent toward ownership.

Give Judith a domestic volume and an accountant's memory. She names the benefit, the recipient, and the person she has chosen to pay. She does not argue that she is innocent. She argues that everyone else is dishonest about cost. Her cruelty should answer that claim before narration does: the payer is always someone she made easier to spend.

Keep the patrons verbally distinct. The Guest asks permission and closes doors. The Worm counts unfinished bodies and transfers one burden into another. The Child borrows a trusted cadence and offers to substitute for someone absent. None should dissolve into generic whispers, visions, or omnipotent darkness.

A pact does not merely “weaken.” A copied door swings outward. Ash stops crossing a sill. A wound remains on Judith's body. A child answers in an unborrowed voice. A corpse ceases turning toward the supper bell. Let physical change carry the supernatural conclusion.

## Voice disposition

The final pass rewrites the adventure's governing prose without changing its graph or demonstrated play. Encounter openings now establish distinct sensory and institutional registers. Revelation titles function as table claims. Selected clues show the object, witness, route, or bodily change that earns each conclusion. Principal actors have separate sentence habits and vocabularies.

The voice pass preserves Judith's deliberate culpability. She receives no coercive origin, tragic necessity, redemptive final act, or claim that the vale requires her survival. Her useful work remains real because its usefulness explains the depth of dependence; it does not offset the murders, pacts, stolen children, or pleasure she takes in assigned suffering.

## Demonstration decisions

The demonstration uses Sabren Holt, Lysa Marrin, Toma Brack, and Neris Kade as outsiders joined by Canon Ysra's narrow commission. Their route is rescue-first but requires public evidence, care logistics, finite-channel exploitation, Hall infiltration, material pact work, and a final component operation.

The route deliberately leaves the Worm in White pact partial after the White Pits. All thirty-one founding bodies are identified and lifted, but four await independent witnesses. This preserves completed labor while carrying a visible, bounded defense into the finale. The final four dispositions sever the Worm during the confrontation.

Judith dies only after the choir and Mercy Book have been neutralized and after all three pact channels are severed. Her death is permitted and decisive, but it does not perform any missing rescue, revocation, name return, burial, evidence, care, or property operation.

The demonstrated journal classifies the original eighty-three opportunities: seventy-two spotted and eleven missed. Twelve later leads remain unseen alternatives. The demonstration does not retroactively add discoveries to repair its route.

The aftermath preserves five active local household claims, three unidentified damaged vessels, two unidentified later bodies, displaced institutional care, property disputes, and differentiated collaborator judgments. The antagonist is conclusively ended without converting the valley into a frictionless moral or administrative settlement.


## Second-look Voice I

Voice I returns the authoritative adventure to receipts, doors, bodies, names, records, care work, witness, and assigned cost. Canon Ysra's **commission** remains the source of bounded authority; a fresh **company** carries that authority without becoming the grammatical owner of every action. Each encounter now speaks through its own work: docket and count at the Gallows, household evidence at the Croft, beds and transfers at Mercy House, petition and confiscation at the Hall, threshold at the Refuge, tagged bodies at the Pits, separate public functions at the Chapel, recognition at the Mere, carried routes in Crow Wood, and accumulated claims in the Underhall.

The completed player-facing openings, revelations, topology, procedures, and demonstrated journal remain unchanged. Voice II completes that reconciliation: three narrowly scoped source edits remove the last generic clue actor and two editorial abstractions, while current operating documents now use encounter, commission, company, household, carrier, pact, and feast-component language. Dated construction records remain explicit history.
