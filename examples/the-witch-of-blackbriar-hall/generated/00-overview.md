## Synopsis

Blackbriar Vale remembers Judith Crowl by what arrived when no one else would send it: grain during the famine, beds during the pox, burial money when a household could not pay. Fifteen years later, each kindness has a receipt. Judith selects the vale's witches, removes their children, stores inconvenient dead, and keeps every household close enough to answer her knock. She bought that power through three chosen betrayals: nineteen refugees barred inside a burning granary for the Guest in Ash, thirty-one plague dead erased in quicklime for the Worm in White, and stolen children's names lowered into Moonless Mere for the Child Behind Glass. Canon Ysra's examiners were already approaching when Mara Sedge linked seven false prosecutions and Nell Sedge mapped the mirror dormitory. Judith took Nell, moved Mara's burning before the customary answer-night, and turned the annual Renewal of Mercy into the Hollow Feast. At first light she places the torch in eleven-year-old Tomas Sedge's hands. By midnight, the Mercy Book, household carriers, and four controlled living voices can join every door still answering Judith's claim. The commissioned company must keep the Sedge family alive, find the people and records Judith has moved, expose the roads behind her apparent omniscience, complete the material obligations she inverted, and break a feast built to continue after its hostess dies.

## Premise

Canon Ysra Vale sent your company to compare burial fees with parish graves and inspect Saint Mercy House after two letters went unanswered. Before dawn, Brother Caldus's rider finds you on the road with a sharper report: Nell Sedge vanished three nights ago, Mara Sedge named Judith Crowl as the taker, and the reeve has advanced Mara's execution before the customary answer-night. You reach Saint Orra's Gallows as Judith orders Mara's eleven-year-old son to light the pyre. The death roll has been cut and rebound. The reeve hides a blackthorn token. Every household owes Judith a sack, cure, bed, burial, or life. Canon Ysra's seal can hold Mara alive, preserve evidence, and protect immediate witnesses; it cannot govern the vale for you. The Renewal of Mercy begins before dusk. At midnight Judith means to turn the vale's separate debts into the Unwelcome Court's common invitation.

## Explanation

Judith Crowl is the witch. Fifteen years ago she sought the Unwelcome Court because affection could be withdrawn, while debt could be recorded, inherited, and enforced. She chose each bargain, renewed it when it weakened, and built ordinary relief work around the access it purchased.

The Guest in Ash was her first account. During the famine, Judith entered nineteen road families on Saint Vey's refuge roll, took their labor on the granary roof, barred the doors on the third night, and welcomed the Guest across the threshold they trusted. The fire burned inward. The Guest now carries her across marked hearths, turns domestic flame against a household, closes claimed exits, and folds passage into soot. Her private ledger still records all nineteen as safely discharged.

The Worm in White followed during the pox. Families paid Judith to carry thirty-one bodies to honest ground. She cut off the tags, laid the dead in quicklime beneath the orchard, and denied each body a final witness. The Worm moves sickness, works abandoned flesh, spoils stores, and lays Judith's wounds on the unburied. Later patients whose suffering inconvenienced her accounts became renewal bodies.

The Child Behind Glass came with Saint Mercy House. Judith records children under their whole names, copies chosen names onto silvered slips, and lowers the vessels into Moonless Mere. The Child lends voices, dreams, false recognition, written command, and reflected roads. It named the price before Judith paid it; she returned with more names and built forms, dormitories, transfers, and receiving beds into a steady acquisition system. Saint Mercy House is not an independent good she later corrupted. Judith made the care effective because every cured fever, occupied bed, and winter meal placed another person inside a room, record, or obligation she could inventory.

Judith also manufactures the witches she condemns. Petition paper becomes a planted poppet. An illness moves into a household. The dependent most likely to be believed disappears. Ordinary officers receive enough fear or favor to finish the accusation, and Judith offers the survivors grain, medicine, custody, or protection. Tokens, receipts, copied names, and stolen hearth ash extend a network that crows, servants, mirrors, wagons, and marked people must physically carry. Her knowledge is formidable because the network is busy, finite, and well supplied.

That distributed system kept any one failure from exposing the whole. It also ended when Caldus's duplicate burial figures reached Canon Ysra, Mara connected seven prosecutions, and Nell mapped the mirror dormitory. Judith's couriers learned when the examiners would arrive. She took Nell, framed Mara, advanced the burning, and rewrote the Renewal of Mercy as a final consolidation. Slow ownership is about to become public evidence.

Forty-seven households display a blackthorn token. Eleven more have signed the Mercy Book. Five have done neither. Judith means the reeve's Renewal oath to answer for the vale and catch the unmarked doors, but an explicit household refusal blocks a new connection and can turn an existing copied door outward once its material carriers are revoked. The Mercy Book names hosts. Tokens and stolen ash carry household claims. Seven silvered names prepare the choir bays and coordinate the Glass Nurse, but a vessel is not a voice: at least four living, controlled speakers must stand in the Underhall. The Hollow Feast operates while any two of three components—the Mercy Book, token network, and captive choir—remain joined. Midnight reaches only the doors still connected.

Ysra's writ authorizes record inspection, evidence preservation, a temporary stay of an unlawful execution, and immediate witness protection. It grants no permanent rule, general command over the reeve's guards, title to Blackbriar property, or power to dispose of victims without their families and witnesses. Later operations rest on discovered unlawful custody, immediate rescue, ordinary defense, willing household or parish cooperation, and narrow public orders from local officers who accept responsibility. Government, property settlement, and final judgment wait for outside civil and chapel authorities.

Each pact ends only when living people complete the obligation Judith inverted. Open the Burned Refuge without welcoming the Guest, restore the nineteen names, and burn the lying shelter ledger in a hearth freely offered by someone Judith betrayed. Identify the pit-dead, lift them safely from quicklime, and give them burial or burning beneath a bell that states where they were found and who denied them. Speak each stolen name back in an unborrowed reflection before breaking its vessel. Doors, records, bodies, witnesses, transport, care, and final disposition are the rite.

Two severed pacts make Judith's body mortal. Three prevent the Court from taking up her claim cleanly. Killing her may be necessary, but the common welcome can outlive her through the Mercy Book, household tokens, and captive choir. Removing any two components collapses the valley-wide claim. Unrevoked household carriers may still support smaller claims afterward.

Keep Judith in the work. At the gallows she makes Tomas hold the torch. At Saint Mercy House she names the meals, medicines, bottles, and receiving beds a rescue will require. At Blackbriar Hall she offers exact benefits whose costs she has assigned to someone else. Retaliation strikes a person the company promised to protect and arrives as an accounting correction. Judith remembers debts perfectly and wrongdoing only when it belongs to another person.

Her voice stays controlled and domestic. Captives are residents, commands are placements, stolen property is relief stock, and coercion is the part of mercy that sentimental people refuse to name. She uses names as receipts: ‘Ansel whom I restored,’ ‘Caldus whose sister I housed,’ ‘Tomas whom I am still willing to forgive.’ Her claim is not innocence. It is that everyone else lacks the nerve to choose who pays. The relief remains real because false usefulness would bind no one.

The clue distribution follows the work. Public machinery leaves many witnesses; exact severance procedures survive in fewer hands and places. Every clue should come from a household, record, victim, route, tool, patron, or worker able to know it. The finale supplies no compensating manual for discoveries missed elsewhere.

Judith has one body and bounded information. Track where she goes after the gallows. Roads, valid Guest crossings, and complete reflected routes can move her. Crows, borrowed voices, commanded guards, hearthlings, the Glass Nurse, and patron manifestations carry intention without duplicating her. Between substantial company operations, spend at most one major response through one surviving channel and leave its receipt: soot, paper, silver scoring, a returning symptom, a courier, a moved wagon, or a witnessed handoff.

The opening begins shortly after dawn, and midnight is a working deadline. One company cannot personally complete ten large operations in sequence. Once procedure, materials, witnesses, care, and authority are established, named local crews may continue persistent work while the company moves to the next critical decision. Record who was entrusted, what remains incomplete, and which routes carry reports. Parallel labor never becomes automatic success, and travel never empties an encounter offstage. At midnight, advance the Underhall to the components actually assembled.

Keep knowledge, access, readiness, care, pact work, feast machinery, and Judith's control separate. Copied evidence remains copied. An opened door remains open. A body honestly buried does not return to the Worm. A restored name stays restored. Direct assault can win rescues, records, routes, or component losses early; it cannot perform a remote severance or conjure the people and materials that work requires.
