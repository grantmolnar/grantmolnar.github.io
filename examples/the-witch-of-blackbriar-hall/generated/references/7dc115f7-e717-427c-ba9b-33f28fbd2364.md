# Canon Ysra Vale

**Reference ID:** `7dc115f7-e717-427c-ba9b-33f28fbd2364`  
**Kind:** Person  
**Aliases:** Ysra Vale, Canon Vale, Ysra  
**Tags:** Canon Ysra, inspection commission, burial records, bounded authority

## Summary

The canon whose burial discrepancies and unanswered Mercy House letters produced a narrow inspection commission able to preserve evidence and witnesses without governing Blackbriar Vale.

## Reference Material

## A commission sent before the pyre

Canon Ysra Vale acted before Mara Sedge was accused. Duplicate burial fees, missing graves, and two unanswered letters from Saint Mercy House were enough for her to send examiners into Blackbriar Vale. Brother Caldus's later addendum sharpened the emergency; it did not create the original inquiry. That chronology matters whenever Judith claims the commission was invented to rescue one favored prisoner.

## What the seal can hold

Ysra's writ authorizes inspection of parish and charitable records, preservation of evidence, protection of named witnesses, and a temporary stay while a defective capital charge is reviewed. It does not dismiss the reeve, command every guard, open every private room, transfer custody of every child, seize Judith's stores, or make the commissioned company the vale's government. New authority requires a new basis: an unlawful detention, a household's invitation, a public local order, chapel witness, or ordinary defense against violence.

## Law must be received somewhere

A seal can demand a docket; it cannot carry a patient, guard a threshold, copy a ledger, feed a household, or make a frightened official obey. Ysra's authority is strongest when local people accept bounded responsibility for those acts and weakest when outsiders treat the writ as a substitute for consent or care. The commission's bearer, recognition, exercised scope, orders, and legal effect remain matters of play.
