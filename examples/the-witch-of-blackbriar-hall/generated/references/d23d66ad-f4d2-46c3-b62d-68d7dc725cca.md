# Reeve Ansel Pell

## Summary

Blackbriar Vale's frightened civil magistrate, cured through another man's suffering and practiced at hiding his own false seizures inside passive grammar and borrowed ordinances.

## Reference Material

## The reeve whom Judith restored

Ansel Pell suffered a wasting sickness until Judith moved it into Elian Darr. Pell lived, Darr was accused of blighting him, and Blackbriar Hall received the Darr lease after conviction. Pell later signed false seizures and emergency charges while telling himself that the documents arrived already decided. He speaks in passive grammar: a charge “was brought,” an ordinance “was found,” a child “has been asked.”

## Civil power with a narrow honest use

The reeve's office can recognize a lawful stay, demand a docket, preserve evidence, release or reassign guards, authorize a bounded evacuation, and place local acts on a public record. It cannot make a grain ordinance govern witchcraft, turn Judith's accusations into sworn testimony, or grant one household's consent on behalf of another. Pell knows more of that boundary than his conduct admits.

## Fear is an explanation, not an acquittal

Judith can threaten Pell through the cure receipt, stolen hearth ash, and the sickness she once displaced. He wants a lawful reason to stop and someone else to bear the cost of stopping. Whether he retains office, cooperates, confesses, falls ill, issues a useful order, enters custody, or resumes collaboration remains live. The stable fact is the compromised magistrate and the authority he repeatedly misused.
