## Summary

Blackbriar parish clerk, compromised burial witness, and keeper of the dates, inks, fees, and chapel fragments that turn missing bodies into a provable institutional pattern.

## Reference Material

## The clerk who counted absent bodies

Brother Caldus Merrow remembers a missing page by its date, ink, fee, and place in the parish sequence. Burial payments arrived for bodies that never reached parish ground. Later leaves were substituted, the Chapel of the Free Witness was closed under the wrong ordinance, and records that should have met were kept apart. His first addendum put those discrepancies beside Nell Sedge's disappearance and Mara's accelerated sentence.

## Concealment under threat

Judith threatened Caldus's widowed sister Adela and her son Lark until he hid discrepancies and falsified later records. That coercion explains his silence; it does not make the altered pages true or erase his responsibility for helping conceal them. Caldus can identify the false famine ordinance, locate burial fragments, explain the closure chronology, and distinguish an original parish hand from a substituted leaf.

## A witness, not a complete ritual book

Caldus knows where the chapel's threshold, burial, and naming offices were divided, but he does not carry every rite from memory. The honest bell and counter-rites require surviving texts, material repair, and free witnesses rather than clerical rank alone. His cooperation, protection, record custody, route, testimony, and family safety must be established in play rather than supplied by this record.
