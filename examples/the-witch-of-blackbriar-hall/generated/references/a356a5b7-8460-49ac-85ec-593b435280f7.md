# Beadle Odo Vane

**Reference ID:** `a356a5b7-8460-49ac-85ec-593b435280f7`  
**Kind:** Person  
**Aliases:** Odo Vane, Beadle Vane, Odo  
**Tags:** Blackbriar court, beadle, false charges, Mercy Book

## Summary

A willing court collaborator who turns Judith's injuries into charges, custody, and household obligations, and whose love of office survives exposure more readily than his loyalty to any person.

## Reference Material

## Injury made procedural

Beadle Odo Vane drafts charges, cites ordinances, prepares seizures, keeps seals, and supplies the phrases by which coercion becomes an official act. He knew the famine ordinance did not authorize Mara Sedge's execution. He has repeatedly chosen substitution over error: accused for person, dependent for child, disposition for murder, beneficiary for household under threat.

## The daughter whose place excuses everyone else

Odo's daughter received a place through Saint Mercy House. He treats preservation of that advantage as permission to ruin other families, close the chapel, falsify custody, and join Judith's household account. The motive is intimate; the collaboration is willing. Exposure may make him bargain over a record or captive, but it does not reliably make him repent.

## A seal is not the law it imitates

Odo can control access to records, lecterns, ink, prepared charges, and the mechanics of a public reading. He cannot create lawful authority by quoting an unrelated ordinance or make a household welcome Judith by naming it from a page. Separate him from ink, knife, seal, and prepared text when his procedural skill matters. His allegiance, control, movement, survival, bargains, and use of the Mercy Book remain live adventure state.
