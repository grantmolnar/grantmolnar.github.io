## Summary

Mara Sedge's thirteen-year-old daughter, an observant captive cartographer who counted the mirror dormitory, named seven linked children, and learned the safe order for returning a stolen name.

## Reference Material

## A map scratched where adults would not look

Nell Sedge is thirteen. She drew a long house, hanging mirrors, and crows flying toward a wood where the sun appeared in the wrong place, then hid the map beneath her bed. She also listed seven children who sometimes answered in voices not their own: Nell, Amey Fen, Jo Rusk, Lark Merrow, Perrin Mett, Sella Darr, and Corin Pike. The list establishes a shared naming system without pretending all seven bodies or vessels occupy one room.

## Count the mirrors before breaking one

At Saint Mercy House, Nell counted every mirror, found the sealed east stair, and learned where the iron key could be hidden. She witnessed failed attempts to free stolen names and knows the necessary order: establish identity through the owner or a free witness, recover an unborrowed reflection, speak the name back, wait for the reflection to belong to the person, and only then break the vessel. Violence against the glass before that sequence harms the owner rather than defeating Judith cleanly.

## A child with evidence, not an evidence object

Nell knows other children, rooms, voices, and routes because she watched and remembered. Her knowledge does not authorize adults to move her body, speak for her, or decide that a rescued name is the same as a rescued child. Her location, custody, bodily safety, testimony, name-vessel condition, consent, healing, and final guardianship remain live adventure state.
