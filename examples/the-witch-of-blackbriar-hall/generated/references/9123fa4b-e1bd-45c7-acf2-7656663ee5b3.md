## Reference Material

## Names, hours, complications

Mara Sedge is thirty-eight and records births by mother, witness, hour, and complication because memory becomes generous after danger passes. She applies the same discipline to illness, cures, custody, gifts, and accusations. Her children are Nell, thirteen; Tomas, eleven; and Pate, seven. She refuses any account that turns one of them into a symbol before naming where that child will sleep and who will answer for the door.

## Seven prosecutions laid side by side

Mara compared seven cases by household rather than theory: a Judith gift, a convenient symptom or planted object, a missing dependent or body, an officer's altered story, and property or custody moving toward Blackbriar. Red thread marks dates; birthing blue marks names. The packets do not prove that every case used the same magic. They prove that Judith repeatedly joined magic to ordinary offices, frightened witnesses, and beneficiaries who preferred not to ask what paid for their relief.

## Refusal has a material form

Mara understands the household rule Judith suppresses: accepting aid grants no permanent entry. A resident may name the gift, withdraw access to their own name, room, bed, threshold, or hearth, and remove or cover the physical carrier. Mara's execution clock, stay, custody, injuries, testimony, standing, children, and household outcome remain live. Her record preserves the woman capable of recognizing the pattern, not the result of her attempt to expose it.
