## Summary

Physical household and command carriers—tokens, splinters, coins, marked gifts, cure receipts, oaths, and stolen ash—through which Judith can identify a claimed door, redirect a bearer, or seize a marked body.

## Reference Material

## A claim needs something that can be carried

Judith’s blackthorn system uses carved tokens, splinters, coins, marked lamps, cure receipts, lodging oaths, stolen hearth ash, and comparable accepted gifts. A carrier may identify a household door, support a marked road, warm near a claimed threshold, or give written command access to a particular body. The objects differ; none is a universal license over everyone nearby.

## Heat is evidence, not consent

A token warming, bleeding soot, burning the skin, or pulling attention reveals an active connection. It does not prove that the present resident accepted the gift, that the bearer remains willing, or that a reeve may answer for a whole household. A resident may revoke their own welcome and remove, cool, cover, isolate, or publicly surrender the carrier.

## The network must match real households

At the Hollow Feast, token echoes matter only while the table can match them to actual household carriers and written hosts. Warmth proves a connection, not ownership or consent. Record the owner, bearer, revocation, command, household exposure, and socket separately; the network survives only where those carriers still meet the written account.
