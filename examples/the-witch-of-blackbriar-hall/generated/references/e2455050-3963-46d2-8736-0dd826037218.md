# Chapel of the Free Witness

## Summary

Blackbriar’s deliberately open chapel, preserving the honest bell and divided threshold, burial, and naming offices that require free witnesses, material repair, and public responsibility.

## Reference Material

## A threshold that refuses hidden ownership

The Chapel of the Free Witness was built so that no patron, reeve, household, or priest could quietly convert another person’s dependence into title. Its open threshold, nave, bell beam, vestry records, three incomplete manuals, and undercroft preserve procedures rather than a sovereign answer. Judith closed it under a famine ordinance because public witness disrupted command and separated gifts from consent.

## Three offices kept apart

The threshold office tests household welcome and revocation. The burial office joins name, body, witness, and final disposition. The naming office distinguishes an owner’s identity, an unborrowed reflection, and the order in which a stolen name may safely return. The honest bell carries those acts into public hearing; clergy alone cannot complete them from memory.

## Openness exposes what enters

The chapel can reveal a token, stolen ash, written command, or uncovered vessel without neutralizing it automatically. Its threshold and surviving offices explain how work can be done; they do not reopen the building, repair or commission the bell, remove hidden tokens, assemble free witnesses, or make the roof a safe refuge.
