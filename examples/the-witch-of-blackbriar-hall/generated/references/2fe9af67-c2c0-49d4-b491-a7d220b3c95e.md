## Summary

The black water where Judith lowers silvered names to the Child Behind Glass, preserving reed shore, vessel hooks, an unborrowed basin, drowned records, and reflected routes that depend on controlled identities.

## Reference Material

## Water that borrows a face

Moonless Mere is a reed-bound black pool with vessel hooks, an unborrowed basin, a standing reflection, and a drowned cabinet of older names. Judith brings written names here so the Child Behind Glass can lend voices, dreams, false recognition, command, and reflected passage. The water is a pact site and a route surface, not a mind that knows every traveler.

## An unborrowed reflection

A safe restoration establishes the owner through the person or a free witness, finds a reflection not supplied by the Child, speaks the name back, waits until the reflection belongs to its owner, and only then breaks the vessel. An absent person’s name can be protected and prepared without pretending their body, consent, or guardianship has been resolved.

## Reflections have endpoints

The Mere can join only corresponding uncovered surfaces and controlled names. Covering a mirror, separating the written name, or breaking the pact closes that route rather than rewriting geography. Read each body, vessel, witness, bargain, recovery, and destination from the current state; the water never promises that a name or person remains where first found.
