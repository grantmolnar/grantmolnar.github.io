# Blackbriar Hall

## Summary

Judith Crowl’s public household government, where charity stores, petitions, seizures, accusation work, service routes, pact channels, and the descent to the Hollow Feast meet under one roof.

## Reference Material

## A house that governs by receiving

Blackbriar Hall is manor, relief office, court lobby, storehouse, and private household at once. Petitioners enter beneath gifts Judith has made visible; seized goods and charity stores pass through rooms where gratitude, title, and punishment are written beside one another. The house makes her claim feel domestic even when it is performing government.

## Twelve rooms and several roads

The thorn terrace, petition hall, charity stores, counting room, dining room, ash gallery, study, guest rooms, servants’ passage, warm cellar, thorn court, undercroft gate, and locked stair form the stable house. Public petition, formal inspection, service tunnel, reflected corridor, marked hearth, and direct assault reach different parts of that geometry and expose different carriers.

## Occupants remain unsettled

The Hall can hold records, stores, patients, staff, guards, prisoners, pages, prepared gifts, and pact apparatus without guaranteeing that any remain when reached. Its geometry opens routes and custody problems; it does not place Judith, open the Underhall, preserve a record, command a servant, or settle who receives the estate afterward.
