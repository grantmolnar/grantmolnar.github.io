# Tomas Sedge

**Reference ID:** `69daecdb-c3fe-4aab-99ad-4a9feafe9720`  
**Kind:** Person

## Summary

Mara Sedge's eleven-year-old son, a direct witness to Judith's public coercion who knows Nell's hiding places and Quill's route and responds to concrete protection rather than adult moral summaries.

## Reference Material

## The child Judith asks to carry the sentence

Tomas Sedge is eleven. Judith's method at Saint Orra's Gallows is to place the torch in his hands, threaten his siblings, and call whatever follows a son's consent. Tomas therefore understands one part of Judith's rule with unusual clarity: an adult can manufacture obedience by making a child responsible for the harm done to the people he loves.

## Plain questions before brave plans

Tomas wants to know whether Mara can breathe, where Pate will sleep, who will stop Captain Cleft, and what happens if a promised refuge fails. He has no patience for adults discussing him as cargo or praising his courage while leaving the immediate danger intact. Give him one credible action and a named adult responsibility, and he acts quickly.

## What he noticed at the croft

Tomas knows where Nell hid drawings, which crow watched the chimney, and how Quill approached the Sedge hearth. He is also a public witness to the terms Judith offered and the officers who helped stage them. None of that makes his testimony automatically safe, believed, or available. His torch, location, custody, willingness to speak, immediate safety, and family outcome remain decisions and consequences of play.
