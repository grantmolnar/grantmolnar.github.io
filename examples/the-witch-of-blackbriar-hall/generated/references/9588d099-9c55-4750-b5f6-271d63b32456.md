## Summary

Judith’s orchard burial ground, where thirty-one plague dead were erased in quicklime and later bodies were added, joining false fees, transferred illness, unburied remains, and the Worm in White’s claim.

## Reference Material

## Bodies denied a final witness

The White Pits lie beneath Judith’s orchard: lime shed, founding trenches, renewal trench, old cart approach, and sunken bell post. During the pox, families paid for honest burial. Judith removed tags, laid thirty-one bodies in quicklime, denied each one a final witness, and used the missing account to welcome the Worm in White. Later victims and inconvenient patients entered a separate renewal trench.

## Identification before lifting

The founding dead retain household marks, fee fragments, objects, bodily distinctions, and surviving family records. Safe work requires names, separate labels, protective equipment, labor rotations, prepared transport, and a real destination before remains leave the lime. The honest bell states where the dead were found and who denied them; it does not replace careful identification or disposition.

## A site, not an automatic conclusion

The Pits preserve the founding betrayal and the material conditions needed to answer it. Count progress in protected workers, honest names, lifted remains, labeled transport, bell witness, and final disposition. The orchard’s existence supplies no labor, cart, audience, safe trench, or completed burial.
