# The Burned Refuge

**Reference ID:** `46c9f075-d08e-4251-8fba-c86f47b30411`

## Summary

The granary where Judith barred nineteen promised refugees inside and welcomed the Guest in Ash, leaving an outward-opening door, restored names, and a freely offered hearth as the material terms of repair.

## Reference Material

## A shelter made into a betrayal

During the famine, nineteen road people worked on Saint Vey’s refuge granary after Judith entered them on the refuge roll. On the third night she barred the outer doors, accepted the Guest in Ash across the threshold they trusted, and recorded every victim as safely discharged. The fire burned inward because the promised shelter itself had been turned against them.

## Doors, names, and a truthful fire

The stable site includes the barred west doors, surviving measures and household marks, the roll niche, loft, ash ring, and the false shelter ledger’s connection to the place. Repair has three distinct obligations: open the original doors outward, restore all nineteen names under free witness, and burn the lying ledger in a hearth freely offered by someone Judith betrayed.

## Ruin is not repair

Breaking a wall, burning the granary while barred, scattering unnamed ash, or using coerced fire repeats the wrong rather than completing the obligation. Measure repair in doors opened outward, people named under witness, the false ledger handled truthfully, ash controlled, and a hearth freely offered. The ruined granary grants none of those acts by itself.
