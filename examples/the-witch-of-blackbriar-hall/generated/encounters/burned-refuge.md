# The Burned Refuge

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** ruin, pact-site, fire, undead

Nineteen barred refugees still knock inside the granary where Judith converted promised shelter into the Guest's title.

## Opening View

Saint Vey's granary stands untouched inside its burn scar. Nineteen soot-dark handprints knock from behind doors whose iron bar melted into the oak. Beneath the north wall, a human mouth opens under hard soot and cannot draw breath. Grain measures in the lean-to bear the names of the travelers who repaired this place. When someone speaks of shelter, a door-shaped absence rises in the yard and answers gently: there is room; no one need sleep exposed. The outer bar sinks deeper.

## Encounter Material

## What Judith did here

The west doors are still barred from outside. Nineteen handprints knock from within; Orra Kelm's soot-bound body can answer when named; Judith's private shelter ledger still records every traveler as safely discharged. The lie remains active: ash returns to a name scraped from the living copy, and the door-shaped absence in the floor offers exactly what a visitor has just said they need.

The founding act is specific. Nineteen road travelers repaired Saint Vey's granary under Judith's promise of shelter, then died when she barred both doors and welcomed the Guest across the threshold they trusted. The roll, household marks, Orra's testimony, and false discharge ledger let the company prove who was received and betrayed. The scene begins with those doors and with a harder practical question: where the living will sleep after they are opened.
## Approaching the granary

The refuge stands west of the vale beyond Dena Moor's coppice. Three approaches are practical.

- **The famine road** is broad enough for a wagon and visible from Crow Wood. Judith's crows can warn her unless the familiar route has been disrupted or travelers move under cover.
- **The charcoal path** begins behind Dena Moor's kilns. It is narrow, avoids the crows' usual perches, and reaches the north wall near the soot chamber.
- **The lime ruts** leave the yard toward the White Pits. Clearing them exposes the old corpse-cart connection but stirs ash hounds sleeping beneath the road surface.

Before anyone crosses the yard, ask where everyone will sleep afterward and whether Judith possesses ash, a token, or a signed invitation there. The Guest tries to make the next shelter part of the scene.

## The site

### The outer yard

The west doors are barred by the original iron beam, now warped into the oak. Nineteen shallow handprints mark the door from inside. The prints knock in groups corresponding to the five traveling households. Clearing the old wagon ruts reveals quicklime pressed beneath refuge ash and points toward the White Pits.

A roofless lean-to contains broken grain measures. Each measure bears one or more initials cut by the refugees while they repaired the granary. These are physical identities, not a complete roll.

### The north wall

A human shape lies beneath six inches of hard soot. This is Orra Kelm's body. She died with the others, but the Guest preserved her final attention because Judith needed a witness who could be forced to repeat that the doors had been lawfully closed. Orra can speak when clean air reaches her mouth and someone addresses her by name. Her testimony survives; her life does not.

Orra refuses to recite all nineteen names for strangers who treat the dead as a code. She first asks who is outside, what shelter they have offered the living victims, and whether anyone intends to burn the building around the bodies. If answered plainly, she identifies the household groupings and explains Judith's exact invitation.

### The granary floor

The interior cannot be entered safely through the west doors until the external bar is lifted. Other breaches open into folded interior space: a person climbing through the roof descends repeatedly past the same loft, while a hole cut in the wall opens into the nearest marked household hearth.

Once the doors are opened correctly, the floor is thirty paces long. Grain bins form two aisles. Fire runs beneath the boards without consuming them. A soot outline of a freestanding door appears whenever someone says that they need shelter, warmth, or a way home.

### The loft and roll niche

The surviving parish copy of the refuge roll once rested in a wall niche above the east bins. Judith removed the full roll, but scraps remain beneath the nesting boards. The scraps identify household groupings and four names. The complete chapel copy is wrapped around the honest bell's clapper.

### The inward ash ring

At the center of the floor, nineteen handprints surround a black circle where the Guest first stood. A door-shaped absence rises from the circle when the pact is challenged. It has no face, but a host's voice comes from the other side of it. It offers exactly what the listener most recently said they needed.

## The Guest's invitations

The manifestation may make one offer to each person who enters the yard and another to anyone who crosses the opened doors. Use concrete offers:

- a warm room for children evacuated from Saint Mercy House;
- a hidden route into Blackbriar Hall;
- immunity from Judith's hearth fire;
- the voice of a dead refugee as a guide;
- safe sleep for someone whose name the Child has taken.

An offer does nothing until accepted. “I hear you,” “not now,” silence, threats, and questions are not acceptance. “Come in,” “we accept,” taking the offered coal, or deliberately crossing a door the Guest creates constitutes acceptance. State the proposed exchange plainly before treating an action as assent.

A person who accepts gains the promised immediate benefit, but the Guest acquires passage through that person's next accepted shelter. This is leverage, not permanent possession. The claim can later be revoked at the relevant threshold if the person and household understand what they are withdrawing.

## Opening the original doors

This is the first severance act.

The bar must be moved from outside while the west doors remain the route being opened. It requires leverage, cutting or cooling the warped iron, and enough labor to prevent the beam from rolling onto the workers. Four able people with sound tools can do the work steadily. Fewer can succeed by taking longer, constructing a brace, or exposing themselves to the ash hounds.

During the operation:

1. the first lifted inch releases smoke containing the refugees' last pleas;
2. halfway clear, two ash hounds emerge from beneath the beam and try to drive workers across a soot door;
3. when the bar leaves both sockets, Judith can no longer make these doors refuse an exit;
4. the doors must then be pulled outward. Pushing them inward repeats Judith's act and closes the pact around the workers.

Opening the doors frees the dead to speak beyond the granary and exposes all copied doors as soot outlines. It immediately weakens Judith's threshold denial: she can still use marked tokens and accepted gifts, but she cannot silently hold a person inside a room merely because its hearth is claimed.

## Restoring the names

This is the second severance act.

Write the nineteen names on a new refuge record in the presence of their remains or surviving marks. The record states that Judith Crowl accepted them, barred the outer doors, and did not release them. Clergy, magistrate, villager, traveler, or examiner may make it. Authority comes from witnessed truth, not office.

At least one free witness reads each household group aloud. Orra may witness the Kelm names. The chapel roll, household objects, and surviving marks support the others. If no living person remembers a victim, the material evidence is sufficient when the recorder states the uncertainty rather than inventing a relationship.

Each restored name changes one handprint from black to gray and releases one intelligible memory. Once all nineteen are recorded, Judith loses concealment through refuge ash. Her soot trails remain visible, erased names creep back into copies, and the refuge dead can contradict her without the Guest translating their speech.

Copy and separate the restored record. The original may remain at the open granary, travel to the chapel, or be read publicly. The pact requires no sacred storage place.

## Burning the false shelter ledger

This is the third and final severance act.

The private ledger is usually in Judith's study or carried to the Hollow Feast. An ordinary duplicate will not suffice: clean firelight reveals the living copy because ash crawls back onto every erased name.

The hearth must be freely offered by a living person whom Judith betrayed after promising aid, shelter, custody, cure, or protection. Mara Sedge, Sila Darr, Dena Moor, Reeve Pell, and several Mercy House residents may qualify. Consent, knowledge of Judith's possible counterstroke, and freedom from examiner coercion make the offer valid.

Prepare the hearth by removing Judith's tokens, old relief fuel, stolen ash, and blackthorn. Light it from fuel belonging to the household or openly given without debt. Read the restored refuge record while the ledger burns. The fire turns outward, the nineteen handprints open, and the Guest's standing door becomes an ordinary rectangle of smoke before collapsing.

Once the ledger is consumed in this way, the Guest in Ash pact is severed. Judith loses hearth passage, inward-burning fire, threshold denial, and refuge-ash concealment. Existing mundane arson, locked doors, debts, and household fear remain.

## Defenders and pressure

Use only the defenders that follow from current circumstances.

- **Two ash hounds** sleep beneath the outer bar. They are dog-shaped concentrations of hot soot. They pursue whoever most recently accepted an offer and collapse when submerged, dispersed, or named as Judith's servants before the open doors.
- **The door-shaped Guest** protects assent rather than Judith. It bargains, separates, and redirects. It becomes violent only when someone tries to destroy the ring while the founding acts remain unrepaired.
- **Hearthlings** may arrive through a marked coal, lamp, or offered fire if Judith has warning. They ignite inward and try to force the doors shut again.
- **Blackthorn Men** can reach the site by the famine road. Cleft prefers to burn the building from outside and blame the examiners for destroying refugee evidence.

Do not combine all four by default. Choose what Judith could have learned and deployed.

## Judith's response

When Judith learns the refuge is open, she does not mourn the pact. She attacks the people performing the repair.

- She sends relief wagons to the vale and announces that the examiners burned winter stores.
- She ignites one blackthorn lamp in a household that publicly supported the rescue.
- She offers safe lodging to evacuated children and names their current refuge inadequate.
- She orders Cleft to seize the restored roll as fabricated evidence.
- If the false ledger remains in her possession, she writes one living witness into it as having accepted shelter at Blackbriar Hall.

Every response uses a known channel and creates a protectable victim or document.

## Partial success and careless destruction

Record the three acts separately.

| State | Material result | Effect on Judith |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Doors still barred | The dead remain contained and copied thresholds stay hidden | Full Guest powers remain |
| Doors opened outward | Dead can speak beyond the granary; copied doors become visible | Silent threshold denial ends; marked passage remains |
| Nineteen names restored | Refuge record contradicts Judith and ash no longer conceals identity | Soot trails and false erasures remain visible |
| False ledger burned in a free hearth | The Guest's claim collapses | Guest in Ash pact severed |

Careless destruction does not count as repair.

- Burning the granary while barred feeds the Guest and may create a new betrayed shelter.
- Breaking through roof or wall opens into folded space or a marked hearth rather than releasing the dead.
- Scattering unnamed ash lets Judith collect it through crows.
- Burning the false ledger in ownerless, stolen, or coerced fire destroys evidence while the living copy reforms at the refuge ring.

Failure costs safety, records, or one channel of access; completed acts persist. Once the doors have been opened correctly or a name honestly restored, Judith cannot reset that work offstage.

## Leaving the refuge

Before departure, record:

- whether the original doors stand open;
- which of the nineteen names are securely restored;
- where the restored roll and copies are held;
- whether Orra's testimony was heard or preserved;
- where the false ledger is believed to be;
- whose hearth might be freely offered;
- which ash, token, familiar, or accepted bargain still gives Judith access.

Lime ruts, the chapel roll, the crows' route, the false ledger, and soot doors leaning toward Blackbriar Hill all point onward.

## If the refuge is reached late

The Guest can defend unfinished work but cannot restore completed betrayal.

- Judith may move loose ash, steal one uncopied name list, send one copied threshold against a worker, or bar an auxiliary opening through a surviving household claim.
- The original granary, the nineteen dead, and the living false ledger remain at the site until the ledger is physically moved.
- Doors already opened outward stay open. Names already restored in independent records remain restored. A false ledger already burned in a freely offered hearth does not reform.
- If the Guest pact is severed while Judith is elsewhere, her hearth passage, inward fire, and threshold denial fail immediately. Judith learns the loss only when she attempts the channel or receives an ordinary report.

A late operation may be more dangerous and less private. It remains materially completable.
