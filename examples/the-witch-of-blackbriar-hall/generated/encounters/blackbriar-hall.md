# Blackbriar Hall

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** estate, social, infiltration, antagonist

Petitioners queue beside winter stores and confiscated property while Judith's ledgers, couriers, pact records, and stair below remain in active use.

## Opening View

Blackbriar Hall's petition line moves between two desks. Dain Orm weighs grain for a coughing woman; Odo Vane records the sack, the cure, and her son's winter labor in separate columns. Confiscated cradles prop the doors open. Corin Pike crosses the hall with a loose Mercy Book leaf while Captain Cleft seals the private stair. Seven chimneys smoke without fuel. A brass speaking tube clicks below. Odo tears need from debt and sends Corin toward the undercroft.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

Petitioners wait at the front doors while side-court clerks relabel confiscated stores and Corin carries separated Mercy Book leaves toward the private stair. Odo can still make the house look like an office; Cleft can still turn that office into a cordon; Judith may be receiving challengers, spending one surviving channel elsewhere, or already descending. The first problem is not proving that the Hall is corrupt. It is holding people, records, and relief goods in public custody before the machinery moves below.

The hall remains usable under Canon Ysra's commission, through the petition line, behind the red-curtained wagon, across a mirror, by the root-cut tunnel, or through a broken gate. Each route exposes a different piece of the same household.

The stores are real and many families need them. Much of the stock was bought with seized property, transferred suffering, coerced work, or debts designed never to close. Fire and indiscriminate looting punish the households before Judith. An inventory, named custody, and public redistribution separate relief from the woman who insists relief proves ownership.

Judith ordinarily remains here until the Renewal nears completion. She may receive the examiners before witnesses, spend one surviving channel to answer trouble elsewhere, descend when the feast has enough components, or leave Odo and Cleft to hold the house while she corrects a public refusal. Mark every departure. Blackbriar Hall is her center, not proof that she occupies every room at once.
## The household

Blackbriar Hall ordinarily contains twenty people before transferred patients, prisoners, or petitioners are counted.

- **Judith Crowl** receives petitioners, chooses punishments, and controls the pact channels that remain intact.
- **Beadle Odo Vane** keeps the legal, seizure, and accusation records. He is a willing collaborator. He bargains when cornered, but his first offer is always someone else's safety.
- **Captain Varro Cleft** commands six Blackthorn guards at the hall and can call eight more from the village. He enjoys turning official language into permission for violence.
- **Steward Dain Orm** runs the charity stores and confiscated-property auctions. He knows the stores are stolen and profits from every shortage. He will flee with portable silver before defending Judith personally.
- **Merrit Coil**, Judith's housekeeper, accepted a cure for her brother and then spent twelve years making rooms ready for threatened guests. She knows the house's ordinary locks and schedules. Her complicity is substantial, but she has never carried a name vessel or knowingly delivered a child.
- **Corin Pike**, fifteen, is called an apprentice and used as a page. He carries separated Mercy Book leaves between the study, petition hall, and Underhall. His own name vessel remains elsewhere, so commands through his written name can turn his route against rescuers.
- **Eda Lorn** begins at Saint Mercy House. If Harl's transfer reaches the hall, Eda is placed in the warm cellar because Judith uses her body to receive illnesses that would otherwise expose the White Pits. Otherwise the cellar contains her prepared bed, labeled lines, and transfer order.
- Eight cooks, porters, cleaners, and clerks work for wages or debt relief. Three have accepted marked gifts, two know Judith is a witch, and none knows the whole machinery.
- Six guards hold the public entrances. Cora Pike or Hen Joss may be among them if prior events have not separated the ordinary guards from Cleft.

Do not make the whole staff fight to the death. Ask what each person knows, has accepted, fears losing, and can physically reach.

## Ways into the hall

### Public petition

The front doors remain open while the Renewal is publicly underway. Petitioners are searched for weapons but not for copied documents, household tokens, or concealed tools. An official commission, a person Judith expects to control, or evidence that can embarrass her in front of petitioners buys time inside.

Public entry places witnesses around Judith. It also gives her the opportunity to offer real relief, expose private facts obtained through her network, and portray any seizure as an attack on food and medicine.

### Formal inspection

A lawful stay from Pell, Canon Ysra's commission, Caldus's burial records, or proof of false seizure can compel Odo to open the petition hall, counting room, and public stores. It does not by itself open Judith's study, the ash gallery, or the undercroft. Every refusal, substituted record, and moved object becomes evidence.

### Service tunnel

The concealed road through Crow Wood enters the receiving cellar below the charity stores. The tunnel is wide enough for one wagon. Its iron gate can be opened from Garran's road key, from Corin's page key, or by forcing the counterweight. A forced gate rings the ash-gallery service bell and advances the hall response, but it does not collapse the tunnel.

### Reflected corridor

While the Child Behind Glass remains active, a known mirror route from Saint Mercy House or Moonless Mere can reach the guest corridor. Covering the arrival mirror prevents immediate pursuit. An unguarded traveler may arrive in a prepared guest room rather than the intended corridor.

### Marked hearth

A person carrying accepted ash or a household token can be drawn through the ash gallery when Judith opens the matching hearth. Deliberately using the channel provides a rapid route but briefly exposes the carrier's household door in the Underhall. A fully revoked household cannot be used this way.

### Assault or siege

The terrace can be climbed, the gates broken, and the hall surrounded. A direct assault makes Judith spend visible channels and may keep her from moving records quietly. It also places petitioners, servants, stores, and captives between intruders and the estate's defenders. Cleft uses that fact deliberately.

## Hall response

Track the hall's response by changed control, not by an abstract alarm that makes every door impossible.

1. **Observe.** Clerks record names, crows leave the roof, and Judith chooses whether to receive the examiners personally.
2. **Separate.** Odo moves petitioners to the stores, Cleft closes the private stair, and Corin is ordered to carry one packet toward the undercroft.
3. **Preserve the machinery.** The Mercy Book or its separated leaves move downward; accusation drafts and the network board remain behind because they are too useful to burn blindly.
4. **Move vulnerable people.** If Eda Lorn has arrived, she is prepared for the service wagon; otherwise an order is sent to bring her from Saint Mercy House. One present captive or servant is moved, and Merrit is ordered to destroy a single named evidence class.
5. **Use an intact pact.** Judith crosses a hearth, transfers a wound, or opens a false corridor. Use only a channel she still possesses and reveal its physical carrier.
6. **Commit to the feast.** Judith descends, Cleft holds the stair or retreats to the thorn court, and Odo begins reading the household names below.

Interrupting a step creates an opportunity. A courier can be followed, a moved captive can be rescued, a selected document can be preserved, and an intact channel can be traced.

## Areas

### 1. Thorn terrace and front doors

Clipped blackthorn hedges form waist-high lanes around the approach. Forty-seven household emblems are cut into the stone curb; each corresponds to a currently displayed token. Eleven blank recesses await households that have signed but not yet displayed one.

The front doors swing inward under the Guest in Ash. If the refuge doors have been opened correctly, soot outlines become visible around the hinges and the doors can no longer close without someone physically moving them. If the Guest is severed, they are ordinary heavy doors.

Petitioners waiting outside can witness official refusals, Judith's admissions, or violence. They are not a passive crowd. Several have come for food that cannot safely be delayed.

### 2. Petition hall

Two desks face each other. At the first, people state what they need. At the second, Odo records what they owe. The room contains cure receipts, custody forms, grain extensions, and gratitude signatures in separate drawers. The separation lets Judith publicly display need while privately joining obligation.

A brass speaking tube reaches the Underhall lectern. During the feast, names read below can be answered or corrected here. A free household representative speaking through the tube can publicly revoke a signature if the relevant page is present or accurately identified.

Judith receives challengers here when she wants witnesses. She does not deny being useful. She asks which patient should lose medicine, which child should lose a bed, and which household should feed a refugee if her stores are seized. The honest answer is a distribution plan, not an argument that the needs are unreal.

### 3. Charity stores

The north rooms hold grain, lamp oil, dried apples, winter blankets, fever bark, bandages, and six locked chests of parish silver. Every stack bears two marks: the household from which it was confiscated or purchased under pressure, and the household promised relief.

Destroying the stores punishes both sets of people. An inventory made with independent witnesses allows the goods to be redistributed without preserving Judith's claim. Dain Orm knows which measures are short, which medicines are genuine, and which casks carry blackthorn splinters.

Three marked gift crates are prepared for the Renewal. Removing their blackthorn linings prevents them from creating new household carriers. Burning them while the Guest remains intact sends their ash to the gallery.

### 4. Counting room and accusation press

Odo's room contains the seizure ledgers, seven old prosecution files, prepared accusations for six future targets, blank confessions, parish seals, and the small press that makes petition-paper poppets.

The prepared cases are not vague threats. Each names an incident Judith intends to create: a mill fever, a chapel fire, a missing infant, spoiled grain, a guard's seizure, and a failed cure. The evidence lists leave spaces for whichever frightened witness can be coerced afterward.

The room also contains Odo's private index of who knows each legal substitution. Seizing the index exposes witnesses to danger unless protection plans exist. Copying and dividing it preserves the pattern without concentrating every name in one vulnerable bundle.

### 5. Public dining room

Judith feeds twelve petitioners here every evening. Three unused chairs stand at the head of the table. Their undersides bear the titles **Guest in Ash**, **Worm in White**, and **Child Behind Glass**, together with dates and payments for each voluntary renewal.

The meals are ordinary and nourishing. Judith uses the room to insist that appetite proves consent to her whole order. A person can eat food without inviting her patrons. Stating and witnessing that distinction weakens her social argument but does not replace physical revocation.

If a pact has been severed, its chair splits along the grain and cannot be repaired. If weakened, the corresponding payment marks blur or bleed their material sign: soot, white moisture, or false silvering.

### 6. Ash gallery

Forty-seven shallow hearths line a long room. Each bears a household name, a sample of stolen hearth ash, and a socket matching a blackthorn token. Eleven additional hearths hold signed paper but no ash. Five cold bays correspond to households that have avoided both.

The gallery is a communications network, not a bank of portals Judith can use without cost.

- A lit hearth lets Judith observe flame and sound in the matching marked home.
- Crossing requires an intact Guest pact, a warm matching token, and enough open floor on both sides for a body.
- Carrying a message requires paper, ash, or a crow. Thoughts do not travel by themselves.
- A privately revoked household causes its hearth to smoke and show no image.
- A publicly witnessed revocation turns the household name outward on its iron label and prevents the Underhall from counting that door in the common welcome.

The **network maintenance board** lists every current carrier: token, ash sample, written name, servant, crow route, and known failure. Removing it does not end the network; it identifies what must be tested and where Judith has already lost access.

Pulling ash samples from the hearths is safe only if they are covered separately. Mixing or burning them while the Guest remains active can create a temporary common passage between households.

### 7. Judith's study

The study contains five crucial records that Judith cannot replace before midnight.

1. **The living false shelter ledger.** Ash restores erased names until it is burned under the proper refuge conditions.
2. **The Mercy Book master index.** It maps every signature to its household, token, dependents, and underhall door. The full ceremonial book may be here, in transit, or already below depending on earlier events.
3. **The three injury ledgers.** They record where Judith sent wounds, illnesses, and reflected harms. Current entries reveal which pact still carries each protection.
4. **The contingency matrix.** It states plainly that the common welcome survives one lost feast component but collapses when any two of the Mercy Book, token network, and captive choir fail.
5. **The accusation drafts.** They establish that Judith planned crimes before their supposed evidence existed.

The desk has no single self-destruct device. Judith values these records and relies on them. If she must destroy something, she chooses one class, uses a specific channel, and leaves the action visible.

A small portrait shows Judith at twenty-three before the first pact. Behind it is her first rejected petition for an ordinary parish office. It explains the injury to her pride; it does not excuse what she chose next.

### 8. Prepared guest rooms

Five rooms contain gifts tailored to common adventuring wants. Choose only those that fit actual characters.

- A military guest finds a restored standard and a written promise that one dead comrade can answer orders again.
- A healer finds a sleeping patient whose illness Judith can remove immediately by naming another recipient.
- A scholar finds an accurate page from a lost work, with the remaining leaves promised after the feast.
- A disgraced or guilty guest finds a confession already signed by someone willing to bear public blame.
- A claimant finds a deed transferring confiscated land and the testimony needed to make it lawful.

The gifts are real enough to tempt. None takes effect without a legible act of acceptance: signing, carrying, naming the beneficiary, drinking, opening the parcel, or inviting Judith to complete it. Examining, refusing, preserving as evidence, or redirecting a gift does not silently bind someone.

The Child Behind Glass can make a room appear to contain a loved or trusted person only through an uncovered mirror and a written name. Covering the mirror leaves the physical gift and removes the false person.

### 9. Servants' passage and page room

Corin Pike sleeps beside shelves of separated Mercy Book leaves. Each leaf contains three to six household entries so no servant carries the whole plan. Corin has hidden copied page numbers under the bed slats and marked every route where Cleft cannot see him.

Corin wants his name returned, his household protected from the grain debt, and the other captives freed. He will not accept being used as bait merely because he knows the routes. If commanded through his written name, he can still resist by taking longer paths, dropping page corners, or knocking the Pike mill rhythm on the walls.

Merrit Coil keeps ordinary keys here. She can identify which staff accepted gifts, which rooms Judith enters by ash, and which guest preparations were made for the examiners. She will help only after seeing a viable way to prevent Cleft from punishing the servants collectively.

### 10. Warm cellar and stillroom

If Harl's transfer has occurred, Eda Lorn lies behind a false medicine cupboard. Otherwise her labeled bed stands ready, and a sealed order directs Mercy House to send her before the feast. Three copper lines enter the frame from the infirmary, White Pits, and Judith's private bath. While the Worm remains intact, Judith can move fever or wound shock through this junction. Disconnecting occupied lines without stabilizing the patient returns the stored harm to the last living body in the chain.

The room contains honest medicines as well as transfer apparatus. A healer can isolate each line, identify who currently bears the harm, and close the circuit in a chosen order. If the honest bell has sounded, white movement becomes visible in the copper and interruption is easier.

Rescuing Eda here, intercepting her transfer, or preserving the empty bed's order establishes that Judith's reported recoveries conceal human recipients. The line labels join the hall, Mercy House, and White Pits.

### 11. Thorn court

A roofless court contains a blackthorn tree trained into the shape of a many-handed person. Marked guards and servants can be arranged around it so Judith's written commands reach them together.

Cleft prefers to fight here. He places unmarked guards in front, commanded guards behind, and petitioners or servants at the exits. The tree is a distribution carrier, not the source of the commands. Cutting it interrupts simultaneous orders for the encounter, but written names and personal carriers remain.

A free guard can ring the small court bell to signal refusal. Piers Vey recognizes its metal as stolen from the Chapel. Replacing the bell with the honest bell, or sounding the honest bell nearby, releases anyone whose command depends on Judith's false closure order.

### 12. Undercroft gate and locked stair

The service tunnel ends at a wagon platform above the Underhall. A side chamber holds red curtains, prisoner hoods, covered mirror panels, lime tools, and two spare token racks. The platform makes the movement between Mercy House, Crow Wood, the pits, and the hall undeniable.

The inner stair is marked with three empty chairs. It can be opened in four ways:

- Judith or another person whom the feast currently recognizes as a host opens it from above.
- A stolen written name is placed in the glass recess. This opens the stair but exposes that person to command until the name is removed or returned.
- Blood or material freely carried from two pact sites is placed on the two side plates. Correctly completed pact work causes the signs to reject Judith and open outward.
- The counterweight can be forced from the service platform. This breaks the stair mechanism, alerts the Underhall, and advances the feast one stage, but it creates a permanent physical route.

No access method is a required puzzle answer. Prior work determines which costs remain acceptable.

## Judith in her house

Blackbriar Hall is where Judith expects gratitude to sound reasonable. She begins with the winter coal ledger, not a denial of the dead or children, and asks which household a seizure will leave cold.

In conversation, give her three moves.

1. **Present a receipt.** State one private fact learned through a surviving channel. If challenged, the route can be traced: crow strip, written name, token, servant, mirror, or ash sample.
2. **Assign the price.** Offer one concrete good—medicine, testimony, a captive, a road, a confession—whose cost has already been placed on a named other person.
3. **Leave room for consent.** Threaten only someone Judith can presently reach, then wait. She wants refusal to feel like the cause of the harm.

Judith may admit the false prosecutions or even the pacts in private. She believes records and injured witnesses remain weaker than a full storehouse and a winter schedule. She never asks forgiveness. Her house phrases include:

- “Love says forever and forgets by winter. Gratitude keeps accounts.”
- “A rescued child is a mouth. Which household have you volunteered?”
- “You call it coercion because I have named the payer.”
- “I did not make the need. I merely stopped pretending no one must own the answer.”

The last sentence is a lie: Judith repeatedly manufactured the need. Let evidence and living witnesses answer it.

If violence begins, she uses one intact power to protect one position: a hearth crossing to change range, one wound sent to a known body, one written-name command, one false recognition in a mirror. Do not let three pacts become three simultaneous turns.

## Failure-forward outcomes

A failed infiltration, lost debate, or repelled assault should change the estate without erasing its content.

- Judith descends with the Mercy Book, leaving the accusation drafts and network board above.
- Cleft captures one isolated intruder or witness and moves them through Crow Wood, creating a rescue route.
- Odo burns one prepared accusation packet, proving deliberate destruction and leaving five others.
- Dain Orm flees with parish silver, exposing a pursuit and weakening store control.
- If Eda is present, she is moved toward the wagon; if not, her transfer order leaves by crow or servant. Either action exposes the apparatus and route.
- Corin is commanded to carry pages below but leaves the page-number key behind.
- The public stores are locked while petitioners remain outside, turning Judith's claim of indispensable charity into a practical distribution crisis.
- Judith uses a pact power whose carrier can now be traced or severed.

Do not remove every ledger, captive, route, or witness at once.

## Leaving the hall

Record separately:

- which rooms are controlled;
- which stores are inventoried, redistributed, sealed, or damaged;
- which staff defected, fled, remained, or were captured;
- Corin and Eda's condition and destination;
- which five critical records were preserved, copied, moved, or destroyed;
- which ash-gallery household links were tested or revoked;
- whether the service tunnel and stair are open;
- Judith's location and remaining pact channels;
- and the current stage of the Hollow Feast.

A strong operation can secure evidence, rescue captives, divide the staff, preserve relief stores, and open the Underhall. A partial operation may achieve any subset. Blackbriar Hall should make Judith's system legible even when she retains tactical control of part of the estate.

## Early or direct assault

The estate may be attacked before the village investigation or pact work is complete. Set the Hall response to the warning that actually reached it.

For each completed response step, Judith's household may move one selected critical record class, one captive movement, or one ordinary staff group. It does not move all five record classes, Corin, Eda, the relief stores, every servant, and the feast machinery together. Record the carrier and destination.

A direct attacker can:

- seize or distribute relief stores while identifying their marked linings;
- divide ordinary staff from Cleft and Odo;
- rescue Corin or intercept an Eda transfer if either is present;
- capture the network maintenance board, accusation drafts, or one of the five critical record classes;
- reach the Underhall through the study stair, undercroft, service tunnel, or Corin's page marks; and
- force Judith to expose one intact pact channel while defending or escaping.

Visible machinery can be acted on immediately. Speed does not supply the remote work at the refuge, pits, chapel, or mere. An early assault may collapse feast components, force Judith into flight, or leave the estate in examiner hands while all three pacts remain dangerous.
