# Sedge Croft

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** household, investigation, familiars, refuge

Quill and three hearthlings tear through Mara's stripped house while seven case packets, Nell's route, and two planted witnesses remain inside.

## Opening View

Rain blows through Sedge Croft's open door. Quill, a crow with a finger-bone tongue, tears the blue-thread index from Mara's case packets while three soot-thin hearthlings divide the house: chimney, children's beds, root cellar. Someone below pounds twice and chokes. Nell's route map lies beneath a wet black footprint. Quill frees the strip bearing her name and hops toward the fire. Behind it, the cellar latch lifts once.

## Encounter Material

## The Sedge household

The croft is both evidence room and endangered household. Pate begins at Adela Merrow's cottage; Tomas's presence depends on what happened at the gallows; Nell remains missing after the blackthorn horse drew her through the hearth. Mara's records, the children's rooms, and the stolen household ash can expose several of Judith's routes, but every useful fact names someone Judith can punish.

Seven red-thread case packets lie unfinished in Mara's workroom. Each joins a gift, a convenient accusation, a missing dependent or body, and a transfer toward Judith's institutions. Mara was taken before she could send the comparison to Canon Ysra. Quill, fire, or a careless evacuation can still separate the records from the witnesses who make them intelligible.

The croft lies below a wet alder bank half a mile from the gallows. It has a kitchen, workroom, two sleeping rooms, root cellar, byre, and one yard gate that can be watched from both pear trees. Ten people can sleep here without crowding. Sixteen can endure one night if the byre is cleared. The pantry will feed the Sedge family for twelve days; every additional mouth turns refuge into an obligation someone must provision.
## Arrival under attack

Quill reached the croft first. The crow, fitted with a human finger-bone for a tongue, tears names from Mara's notes and feeds them into the chimney. Three soot-thin **hearthlings** search the bedrooms for the blackthorn horse. Captain Cleft left two coerced villagers in the root cellar after forcing them to plant the poppet.

Run the intrusion as a short clock.

1. Quill tears the index from one case packet.
2. A hearthling finds Nell's map but cannot interpret it.
3. The chimney fire carries one written name to Blackbriar Hall.
4. The hearthlings seize Tomas or Pate if either child is present.
5. Quill withdraws toward Crow Wood when seriously threatened.

The intruders fight only long enough to destroy evidence or seize a named person. Captive Quill is a vicious but knowledgeable witness. Dead, it leaves a bone tongue that repeats the last three names Judith ordered it to carry.

## People at the croft

- **Mara Sedge**, if alive and free, wants Nell found before she wants Judith exposed. She will cooperate with a larger plan only if it includes a real destination and guard for Tomas and Pate.
- **Tomas Sedge** knows where Nell hid drawings and which crow watched the chimney. He wants to help and will take reckless risks if adults discuss him as cargo.
- **Pate Sedge** may arrive from Adela Merrow's cottage after the gallows. He remembers that the blackthorn horse spoke in Nell's voice one night before she vanished.
- **Sila Darr**, twenty-four, is conscious in the root cellar. Cleft threatened her younger sister Sella at Saint Mercy House until Sila placed the poppet. She can identify Cleft, Odo Vane, and the Blackbriar petition form used to make it.
- **Iven Rusk**, fifty-six, is the other cellar prisoner. Judith has partly removed his name. He remembers facts but cannot say “I,” “me,” or his own name without choking. He knows that his son Jo was moved from Saint Mercy House in a red-curtained wagon.
- **Adela Merrow**, Caldus's widowed sister, can bring Pate, food, bedding, and a cart if the route is secure. Her son Lark is one of the seven name-captives, so she will not accept plans that treat an assault on Mercy House as expendable chaos.

Each witness protects something beyond the case. Sila may prioritize Sella over public testimony. Iven may refuse to be exhibited while nameless. Mara may burn a packet if preserving it would expose a child.

## The rooms

### Kitchen and hearth

The hearth contains no natural ash. A faint ring of hot gray powder surrounds the interior lintel: one of Judith's traveling marks. The blackthorn horse lies split beneath a loose flagstone. Its hollow body contains a curl of paper bearing Nell's full baptismal name in Judith's hand.

Breaking the toy without first removing the paper causes Nell to feel the injury at Mercy House. Burning the paper sends a warning through every linked gift. Washing the name in running water, enclosing it in clean wax, or carrying the whole toy without accepting it as a gift prevents immediate use.

The croft's own ash was taken in a small brass warming pan three nights ago. Replacing it with ash from a freely shared fire and withdrawing Judith's welcome blocks her direct hearth passage here. The old ash must still be found or rendered useless elsewhere.

### Mara's workroom

Mara organized her evidence by families rather than theories. Seven packets compare a Judith gift, a later accusation, a disappearance or illness, and a transfer into Saint Mercy House.

1. **Auda Vey**, keeper of the Chapel of the Free Witness, was accused after its bell broke a commanded man's trance. She died in Pell's gaol; the chapel was closed.
2. **Elian Darr**, tenant farmer, received Pell's wasting sickness after Judith's cure. He was convicted of blighting the reeve and died under restraint. Pell seized the Darr lease for Blackbriar Hall.
3. **Dena Moor**, charcoal burner, reported children at the upper Mercy House windows. A poppet was found in her kiln. She escaped into the western coppice and remains a possible witness.
4. **Bram Kett**, parish gravedigger, recorded burial fees for bodies he never handled. He disappeared on the road to the old orchard. Judith claimed he fled with parish silver.
5. **Ilyra Fen**, laundress and mother of Amey, challenged a cure that moved fever between beds. She was burned after Odo produced a petition-paper poppet. Amey entered Saint Mercy House the same day.
6. **Toman Rusk**, cooper and father of Jo, confessed to cursing Judith after she threatened both his children. He died in a red-curtained prisoner wagon in Crow Wood.
7. **Mara Sedge** accused Judith after Nell vanished. Her case repeats the old sequence quickly enough that Mara recognized it before Judith completed the seizure.

The pattern is not perfectly uniform. Judith has several kinds of mark and sometimes uses ordinary coercion instead of magic. The packets support comparison; they are not a single master proof.

A hidden board contains Mara's most dangerous conclusion: Judith was present at the famine refuge fire, the pox burials, and the first Mercy House naming ceremony fifteen years ago. These are not three local legends. They are three transactions.

### Nell's room

Nell scratched a map beneath her bed after dreaming of the other taken children. The map shows a long house, a room of hanging mirrors, and crows flying toward a wood where the sun appears in the wrong place. Her window sill bears white lime under the black feathers.

Nell also kept a list of seven children who sometimes answered in voices not their own: Nell Sedge, Amey Fen, Jo Rusk, Lark Merrow, Perrin Mett, Sella Darr, and Corin Pike. The list does not say where all seven are now. It establishes that they know one another and have been moved through the same naming system.

### Root cellar

Sila and Iven are bound behind stacked turnip bins. The cellar door carries a Blackthorn Men seizure tag stating that the contents are “contaminated evidence.” The tag grants Judith's marked servants permission to enter if the household accepts the seizure as lawful. Tearing it down is necessary but not sufficient; someone of the household must reject the seizure aloud.

Sila can testify immediately, but moving her sister is her condition for public accusation. Iven's partial naming makes him vulnerable to mirrors and written questions. He can communicate safely by choosing objects, drawing routes, or having another person repeat words he confirms.

### Byre and yard

Two sets of wheel ruts cross the yard: the reeve's seizure cart and a narrower red-curtained wagon from Crow Wood. A loosened byre board creates a route to the alder bank that can move people unseen one at a time. It cannot evacuate a crowd quickly.

## Evidence under pressure

Do not require one exhaustive search. The hearth mark, gift-name, family packets, cellar witnesses, Nell's map, Quill, lime, and wagon tracks all point outward independently. Weak or rushed investigation can preserve some evidence while losing other pieces.

When evidence is threatened, force a priority. A person, packet, familiar, marked gift, and burning room cannot all be secured by one vague action. Losing a document does not erase what a living witness already knows.

## Revoking Judith's welcome

Revocation is a material household act, not a password.

1. **Name the claim.** Identify the token, signature, gift, seizure tag, stolen ash, or person through which Judith asserts welcome.
2. **Withdraw consent.** Each present resident withdraws their own welcome. A child may revoke a gift accepted in their name. No householder may silently speak for an unwilling resident.
3. **Remove the carrier.** Take down tokens and tags, isolate gifts, and clear marked ash without feeding it into another household fire.
4. **Restore the hearth.** Place ash or flame freely shared by someone who understands the use and demands no claim in return.
5. **Witness the act.** One independent witness records who revoked what. Public revocation before midnight also removes the household from the Renewal oath; private revocation blocks direct entry but leaves Judith room to lie about the record.

A suitable declaration is: “No gift purchased this door. Judith Crowl and every claim carried by her token, ash, writing, or servant are refused here.” Exact wording is not required. Intent, authority over one's own welcome, and the physical carriers matter.

At the Sedge Croft, successful revocation produces an immediate sign. The lintel cools, the fire bends outward, and Judith cannot cross directly even if the Sedge names remain in the Mercy Book. She may still send crows, commanded people, smoke, legal officers, or violence from outside.

## Making a refuge

A defended threshold becomes a safehouse only after five decisions:

- who stays and who moves;
- who watches the yard, roof, and hearth;
- which gifts and written names are isolated;
- where food, water, medicine, bedding, and waste go;
- how witnesses give testimony without all occupying one target;
- how a messenger reaches Caldus, Adela, the chapel, or another household; and
- what happens if Judith attacks through law rather than magic.

The croft can become the first public proof that revocation works. It can also become a trap if every witness, child, and packet is concentrated behind one door.

## Judith's attacks on the croft

For each major period spent here, choose one attack through a channel Judith still possesses.

- **Ash passage:** Judith or a hearthling attempts to use the stolen Sedge ash. This fails after proper revocation but reveals where the old ash has been carried.
- **Commanded entry:** a marked guard or frightened neighbor asks for shelter while carrying a concealed token or written name.
- **Legal seizure:** Odo Vane arrives with a warrant naming the croft, residents, and evidence as parish property.
- **Transferred harm:** Pell's illness, a child's fever, or a guard's wound appears in someone placed under protection.
- **Crow fire:** Quill drops a coal wrapped in a copied petition page onto the thatch while another familiar attacks the evidence route.
- **Public accusation:** Judith gathers Mercy households outside and claims the examiners have abducted children and nameless patients.

Every attack creates a choice among people, evidence, secrecy, and public standing. A properly secured refuge never fails by declaration alone.

## Leaving the croft

Before moving on, record:

- Mara's state and chosen priority;
- the location and consent of Tomas and Pate;
- whether Sila and Iven are protected, moved, or willing to testify;
- which of the seven packets survive and who copied them;
- the condition of Nell's name vessel and map;
- whether Quill lives, dies, escapes, or has been redirected;
- whether the Sedge threshold is privately or publicly revoked; and
- what Judith learned through the intrusion.

The croft does not need to become headquarters. It needs to produce concrete people, evidence, and one tested method of refusing Judith's claim.

## If the croft is reached late

A warned Croft changes one asset at a time.

- Quill may destroy one uncopied note group, carry away one named strip, or mark one witness for seizure.
- Cleft's people may move Sila or Iven, but only through the yard and an actual Crow Wood route. The other witness, the cellar construction, and the seizure tag remain.
- One of Mara's seven packets may be removed or burned. The other packets, household comparisons, and witnesses continue to support the pattern.
- A completed household revocation, copied page, protected witness, or distributed packet does not reset because Judith retaliates.

Name what moved, who carried it, and where it went. Leave a track, order, crow strip, damaged hiding place, or witness to the movement. Late arrival narrows the evidence and creates pursuit; it does not make Judith's fifteen-year system leave no trace.
