# Saint Orra's Gallows

**Role:** necessary, start  
**Tags:** village, public, antagonist, execution

Judith places Mara's torch in Tomas's hands, making an illegal execution a public receipt for obedience.

## Opening View

First-light rain has blackened Saint Orra's green. Mara Sedge names her missing daughter from the pyre while her eleven-year-old son grips an unlit torch. Brother Caldus holds a death roll whose cut edge has been rebound; Reeve Pell hides a blackthorn token beneath his coat. Judith Crowl waits under a dry black umbrella. “Tomas,” she says, “a good son proves his innocence by burning the witch who bore him.” One guard lowers her spear. Captain Cleft says, “Three.”

## Encounter Material

## The immediate wrong

Mara Sedge has been condemned without a lawful witness. Her thirteen-year-old daughter Nell vanished from the family croft three nights ago. Mara named Judith Crowl as the taker. Before the hour turned, Judith had produced a blackthorn poppet from Mara's chimney, supplied an emergency ordinance, and given Reeve Ansel Pell the courage to sign what she had already decided.

Now Judith has placed the torch in the hands of Mara's eleven-year-old son, Tomas. If he lights the pyre, she promises to receive him and seven-year-old Pate into Saint Mercy House instead of examining them as a witch's accomplices. The bargain kills more than Mara. It makes Tomas carry the act, teaches Pate what refusal costs, and gives the crowd a child whose obedience can be called consent.

## Why the commission reaches the pyre

Ysra's original writ predates Mara's arrest. Duplicate burial fees, missing graves, and two returned Mercy House letters had already caused the canon to send examiners into the vale. After Nell vanished and Mara named Judith, Brother Caldus sent a short addendum by a parish rider who met the commission before dawn. Judith's crow route intercepted enough of the first writ to know the expected day of arrival, but not which road the examiners would use. She therefore denied Mara the customary answer-night and moved the burning to first light. Their arrival before Tomas raises the torch is the consequence of two competing courier networks, not chance.

The commission reaches the green under the original inspection writ and Caldus's addendum. The seal neither dismisses the reeve nor commands his guards; it demands the docket, names witnesses, preserves evidence, and holds Mara alive while the charge is reviewed. Judith expected scrutiny after the ash cooled. She did not expect a rider to put the examiners on the correct road before dawn.
## The execution clock

Run the opening in visible steps rather than resolving it with one check.

1. **The oath.** Beadle Odo Vane reads the charge and makes the crowd repeat that every household “receives mercy and its mistress.”
2. **The torch.** Captain Varro Cleft gives Tomas the torch. Tomas does not move until Judith names Pate.
3. **The challenge.** Present the commission, accuse Judith, question the process, interfere physically, address the crowd, or pretend to cooperate.
4. **The first order.** Judith tells Pell to proceed, orders a marked guard to seize one examiner, or offers a public bargain.
5. **The break.** If resistance holds for even a few moments, Mara or Brother Caldus can put a concrete irregularity before the crowd. Judith must then retreat, escalate openly, or risk losing her appearance of lawful calm.

Questions do not shorten the count. Judith wants witnesses to see Tomas obey, so she leaves enough silence for the boy's choice to become public—and enough time for the examiners to act.

## The legal fault

The emergency conviction fails Blackbriar's own parish custom in four ways:

- no sworn accuser names an act they personally witnessed;
- the poppet was produced by Judith rather than found by the reeve or parish clerk;
- Mara was denied the customary night to answer a capital charge;
- no record explains why Nell Sedge is absent or who holds custody of her.

Canon Ysra's seal authorizes a temporary stay while those defects are examined. Pell knows this. Odo Vane insists that famine ordinances let the reeve ignore it; the cited ordinance concerns spoiled grain, not witchcraft. Put the docket in Caldus's hands and he can expose the substitution before the crowd.

## The commission and later authority

The writ permits four things at the opening: inspect parish and charitable records, preserve evidence, suspend this execution while defects are reviewed, and protect named witnesses from immediate seizure. It does not transfer civil rule, command every guard, authorize unrestricted searches, or make the commission owner of Judith's stores and estate.

Practical authority may broaden only when the fiction supplies a new basis. A discovered unlawful detention supports immediate rescue. A household may invite protection and revoke its own carriers. Pell may issue limited guard-release, evidence-preservation, or evacuation orders in public. Caldus and chapel witnesses may commission the bell and counter-rites. Violence by Judith's people supports ordinary defense and detention. None of those bases authorizes permanent government, secret punishment, or final disposition of property and children. Record which person or institution accepts responsibility for every expansion.

## People on the green

- **Mara Sedge** is burned, exhausted, and exact. She names children, dates, remedies, and the point at which an officer's story changes. She will not ask Judith for mercy. “My Nell is thirteen. She was taken on the ninth night after first frost. Put that in your book before you call her gone.”
- **Tomas Sedge** is eleven and has no patience for an adult's moral summary. He wants to know where Pate is, whether Mara can breathe, and who will stop Cleft if he drops the torch. Give him one credible adult action and he acts at once.
- **Judith Crowl** speaks at household volume. She uses debts as surnames—“Ansel whom I restored,” “Caldus whose sister I housed,” “Tomas whom I am still willing to forgive”—and leaves silence after each one so the crowd must either accept the account or break it aloud.
- **Reeve Ansel Pell** hides decisions inside passive grammar: the charge “was brought,” the ordinance “was found,” the child “has been asked.” Judith cured his wasting sickness by moving it into Elian Darr, and Pell signed the first seizure order afterward. He wants a lawful reason to stop. He also wants someone else to be the person who stopped.
- **Brother Caldus Merrow** remembers missing pages by date, ink, and burial fee. Judith threatened his widowed sister Adela and her son Lark until he concealed discrepancies. Under protection, he can name the substituted leaves and the day the Chapel of the Free Witness was closed.
- **Beadle Odo Vane** prefers offices to people: accused, dependent, household, beneficiary. He drafted the emergency charge knowing the famine ordinance did not authorize it. His daughter received a Mercy House place, and he has chosen repeated cruelty to preserve that advantage.
- **Captain Varro Cleft** speaks in distances and commands. He enjoys the moment when a lawful phrase gives him permission to hurt someone. If Judith leaves, he is the person most likely to continue the burning because the crowd has already been arranged for it.
- **The marked guards**—Edrin Bale, Samet Kett, Will Noll, and Piers Vey—carry tokens or signed cure receipts. Written names can force their bodies, but command arrives visibly: teeth lock, eyes gray, and the mark heats before movement.
- **The unmarked guards**, Cora Pike and Hen Joss, are paid retainers rather than enthralled servants. They can refuse Cleft once the commission is public and the crowd stops lending silence.

## The crowd is not one thing

Use four visible groups.

- **Sedge patients** remember births Mara attended without payment. They will not begin a fight, but two will cut ropes or carry Mara if someone else breaks the first line.
- **Mercy households** owe Judith food, medicine, fuel, or custody. Some defend her sincerely because the aid was real. They become uncertain when asked whether aid authorized Tomas's punishment.
- **The prosecuted families** stand at the edges: Darr, Rusk, Mett, Vey, and Fen households. They possess fragments of older cases and are watching to see whether any challenger survives public speech.
- **Judith's dependents** include paid servants, token-bearing patients, and people with children in Saint Mercy House. They repeat her accusations quickly because they fear what silence will cost.

A crowd response should follow a witnessed act. One villager speaking does not create a mob; one villager speaking and remaining protected makes a second speaker possible.

## Evidence present on the green

The green yields evidence before anyone leaves.

- Mara can direct them to the packets beneath her floor and accuse Saint Mercy House.
- The trial docket shows the false ordinance and Blackbriar Hall as recipient of seized property.
- Caldus can name the old chapel bell and the burial discrepancy.
- The poppet uses Blackbriar petition paper, though the clove-waxed thread is easier to identify at the croft.
- Pell's blackthorn token warms when Judith crosses the courthouse threshold without a spoken invitation.
- Judith's retreat through hearth ash leaves inward-bending flame and warm soot.
- Quill and the other crows depart toward Crow Wood carrying red prisoner-wagon cloth.
- A commanded guard continues speaking in Judith's voice after she has crossed the green.

Award observed facts immediately. Their implications can wait until someone investigates them.

## Ways to stop the burning

### Invoke the commission

Reading the seal aloud and naming one procedural defect forces Pell to acknowledge a stay unless Judith openly overrules him. Mara then needs a custodian and a protected destination. Pell's gaol leaves her within reach of the reeve's token.

### Separate Tomas from the torch

A character can reach Tomas, block Cleft, soak or seize the torch, or give the boy a believable place for Pate and Mara. Tomas is not a test of persuasion skill; he is a child being threatened. Remove the immediate threat or accept the danger of rescuing him physically.

### Turn the process against Judith

Questioning Caldus, examining the poppet, or forcing Odo to cite the ordinance can expose a lie before the whole green. Judith answers with a counteraccusation and an offer: surrender Mara to Saint Mercy House and the burning stops. Acceptance saves Mara now but places her inside Judith's strongest institution.

### Use force

Violence can rescue Mara. Distinguish Judith, Cleft, the four commanded guards, the two ordinary guards, and the crowd. Initiative grants Judith no free massacre. If she loses the green, she retreats through ash and sends retainers after the rescuers.

## Failure forward

If the execution stops only at a cost, choose the smallest consequence that follows.

- Tomas drops the torch after the pyre catches, leaving Mara burned and in urgent need of care.
- Cleft wounds or arrests one rescuer while Mara is cut free by someone else.
- Pell grants a stay but orders Mara confined in an unsafe location.
- Judith escapes with the poppet or docket while Mara is carried clear.
- The crowd accepts Judith's accusation that the examiners endangered children, making Saint Mercy House harder to inspect openly.
- Mara dies only after a meaningful chance to reach her. Her directions survive through Tomas, Caldus, and the croft, but the village loses its strongest living accuser.

## Public result

Record the strongest witnessed refusal, not a numerical morale score.

- **Silence broken:** someone publicly contradicts Judith and survives the scene.
- **Authority contested:** Pell recognizes the stay, a guard refuses Cleft, or the false ordinance is exposed.
- **A witness protected:** Mara, Tomas, Caldus, or another speaker leaves under credible protection.
- **A household commits:** a named household offers food, shelter, transport, testimony, or public revocation.

These commitments determine what villagers will risk later. Judith may punish a person or break a refuge, but she cannot make the crowd forget that resistance occurred.

## Judith's first retaliation

Once the stay is invoked or Mara is removed from the pyre, Judith sends Odo the altered Renewal schedule and commits the feast clock. That ordinary order starts preparations below Blackbriar Hall; it does not grant her an extra remote attack. She then chooses one response supported by a channel she still possesses.

1. She names the examiners as paid witch-finders who manufacture guilt for coin and has Odo post the accusation.
2. She orders Saint Mercy House sealed because outsiders have threatened the children.
3. She sends Quill and three hearthlings to the Sedge Croft to destroy Mara's notes and seize Tomas.
4. She transfers Pell's old sickness back into him and blames the stay.
5. She commands one marked guard to accuse the examiners of bewitchment while the guard's unmarked comrades watch the possession begin.

Every retaliation reveals method. Judith turns dependency, gratitude, or duty into evidence against the victim.

## Leaving the green

Possible departures include Mara under temporary ecclesiastical custody, Tomas as a protected witness, the reeve's records in examiner hands, or flight after a violent rescue. Name every vulnerable person's destination, escort, food, care, and any token, name, gift, or hearth ash Judith owns there.

Unless she is physically restrained, Judith leaves the green for Blackbriar Hall by carriage after the public break. If she instead uses a hearth or reflected route, show the valid carrier, name the destination, and move her one body there. Do not place her at Saint Mercy House and Blackbriar Hall at the same time.

Whatever the route, the village has seen someone refuse Judith publicly. Fear remains, but her claim that resistance is impossible has been broken once.

## Route branches from the green

The opening does not prescribe a visit to the Croft before every other response. Once the immediate burning is resolved, five actionable branches are visible.

- **Sedge Croft:** Mara, Tomas, Quill's flight, the clove-waxed poppet, and the stolen hearth ash point toward the family evidence and refuge problem.
- **Saint Mercy House:** Nell's disappearance, Judith's custody threat, and the promise made to Tomas point directly toward the children and dependent residents.
- **Blackbriar Hall:** the trial docket names the estate as recipient of seized property, and Judith's own departure or servants provide a route for direct confrontation.
- **Chapel of the Free Witness:** Caldus, the false ordinance, and the burial discrepancy point toward the closed parish protections and truthful bell.
- **Crow Wood:** Quill, the red prisoner-wagon cloth, and marked guards point toward the moving captive and familiar network.

Record the first destination, every vulnerable person sent elsewhere, and Judith's last confirmed physical location. One chosen branch gives her time to spend a surviving channel against another. The unchosen encounters remain occupied and active.
