# The White Pits

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** orchard, pact-site, burial, disease

Thirty-one pox dead wait beneath orchard lime, still carrying the illnesses, wounds, and bodies Judith charged to them.

## Opening View

White fruit hangs over the abandoned orchard while a Blackthorn burial crew opens the first trench. Their foreman reads Odo Vane's quarantine notice and orders every unidentified body removed before noon. A water barrel stands beside loose quicklime. One laborer hooks a pale wrist whose tag was cut away; another reaches for the barrel. A tiny ankle bell rings under the soil. Thirty-one buried faces turn toward it together. The barrel tips.

## Encounter Material

## What Judith did here

The wind is turning east, two Blackthorn watch posts still overlook the orchard lane, and no trench can be opened safely until the company chooses a working side, labels, labor, carts, and receiving ground. Fourteen household trenches hold the founding thirty-one; a narrower renewal trench holds five later victims. The honest bell can expose a transfer, but it cannot lift, identify, carry, or dispose of anyone.

Judith promised the pox dead honest ground, stripped their tags, and laid them in quicklime instead. That denied witness is the Worm's founding account. Work here therefore proceeds person by person: control the lime, establish names, interrupt current transfers through bodies, roots, bottles, or wounds, and give the remains a stated destination.
## Reaching the orchard

Three routes reach the White Pits.

- **The orchard lane** accepts wagons and laborers but passes two Blackthorn watch posts.
- **The old lime road** runs from the Burned Refuge. Its ruts establish the sequence of Judith's first two pacts.
- **The infirmary track** descends from Saint Mercy House and Crow Wood. It is narrow but exposes later transfer traffic.

The wind usually travels east toward the vale at dusk. Choose the working side of each trench before opening it. Without masks, shrouds, tools, water kept away from loose lime, a plan for remains, and a receiving burial or pyre site, the dead can still be found and identified, but mass lifting is not yet safe.

## The orchard

### The outer trees

Thirty-one winter-white apple trees stand in fourteen family groupings. Their roots enter the founding trenches. Fruit cut open contains lime and a strip of erased paper. Eating it transfers fever, coughing, or wound pain from the nearest pit-dead for several hours.

The trees are not the pact anchor by themselves. Felling them may expose graves, but burning the whole orchard before identification destroys tags and drives preserved sickness into the smoke.

### The lime shed

The shed contains long-handled boards, iron hooks, six intact waxed shrouds, broken masks, and invoices for quantities far beyond orchard use. A false tally lists the loads as wall plaster for Saint Mercy House. Under clean waterless light, scratched initials match several Vey Bellhouse and Pike Mill carts.

### The founding trenches

Fourteen long troughs hold the thirty-one pox dead by household group. The lime is dry near the surface and damp beneath. Water poured directly into it produces heat, caustic spray, and a white vapor in which the Worm moves freely.

The bodies are not all equally obedient. Three can resist long enough to communicate: Meya Merrow counts with her fingers, Hal Rusk turns his face toward family voices, and infant Iri Pike rings a tiny ankle bell whenever Judith tries to move a wound into the trench.

### The renewal trench

A narrower trench contains five people Judith denied burial after the pox year: Auda Vey, Elian Darr, Ilyra Fen, Bram Kett, and Toman Rusk. The first three died through Judith's false prosecutions; the last two disappeared after uncovering burial or transport evidence. These later victims matter morally and as evidence, but they are not substituted into the founding count of thirty-one.

### The sunken bell post

A rotted post marks where burial carts were once announced before entering the parish ground. Raising the repaired honest bell here brings every founding body within sound. The post can be replaced with a tripod, wagon frame, or carried yoke; the rite requires audible witness, not the original timber.

## The Worm's behavior

The Worm appears as coordinated white threads moving through lime, roots, wounds, and sealed illness bottles. It does not speak with one mouth. It communicates by arranging dates, names, and prices in moving lines.

It protects denied burial, not Judith's personal safety as such. When confronted, it offers practical bargains:

- take one named body and leave the rest;
- move a fever into a condemned collaborator;
- close Judith's current wound in exchange for one unrecorded corpse;
- reveal a missing person's trench if another name is omitted from the bell record.

Acceptance must be explicit. Refusal triggers no arbitrary disease. The Worm attacks when bodies are lifted without controlling lime, when the bell is silenced, or when someone knowingly treats a person as interchangeable remains.

## Preparing the extraction

A safe large operation normally needs:

- the repaired honest bell and a stable carrying frame;
- one reader or recorder who can remain outside the lime work;
- six to twelve laborers in rotating pairs;
- long-handled boards, hooks, dry brushes, masks, gloves, and eye covering;
- one shroud or separate fuel bundle for each body;
- two carts or several litters that keep remains apart and labeled;
- a cool clean place for exposed workers and Mercy House patients;
- a burial ground or controlled pyre prepared before lifting begins.

Improvisation is possible. Missing resources create specific exposure, delay, or carrying problems rather than forbidding action. Pike Mill can supply labor and carts, Rusk Cooperage can build frames, Darr Washhouse can prepare marked cloth, Mett Tallowhouse can provide waxed coverings, and the chapel can supply the bell and recording office.

## Sounding the honest bell

This is the first operational protection, not the final severance.

A free witness sounds the bell and states:

1. where the bodies were found;
2. that Judith Crowl denied them the burial promised to their households;
3. that the workers intend to identify and release rather than use them.

The statement may be brief and can be corrected as facts emerge. The bell must continue at each trench opening and when remains leave the orchard. Under its sound:

- resistant dead can move or speak without Judith's command;
- wound transfers become visible as white threads before they cross;
- a worker can cut or interrupt one transfer;
- the Worm cannot move unnoticed between labeled bodies.

If the bell stops, completed identifications remain. The next transfer or corpse command simply becomes harder to prevent until it rings again.

## Identifying the dead

Attach a durable name tag to each body before it leaves its trench. The tag states uncertainty honestly: “believed to be,” a household relation, or identifying marks are valid when exact certainty is unavailable. A knowingly false convenient identification does not release the body.

Read each name under bell sound. A correctly identified corpse ceases rehearsing the Hollow Feast response and no longer accepts Judith's wounds. The person's illness history and bodily damage remain; the corpse is not restored to life.

Household witnesses may grieve, refuse public speech, or delegate reading to another free witness. Do not require a victim's relative to perform for the village in order for the record to be true.

## Lifting from quicklime

This is the second severance obligation.

Work from the windward side. Remove dry lime with boards and brushes. Do not flood the trench. Slide each body onto a labeled shroud or litter while the bell sounds. Separate exposed clothing and tools rather than returning them directly to the vale.

Each lifted founding body produces a visible change:

- one white root blackens and breaks;
- one orchard tree loses its false fruit;
- one route by which Judith transfers wounds closes;
- one denied voice becomes available for testimony or final wishes.

The dead do not all want the same disposition. Most accept burial or burning. Meya Merrow asks to lie with her household. Hal Rusk wants the village to hear that Judith used fear of contagion to hide ordinary theft. Iri Pike's family may choose burial once the ankle bell is returned.

## Honest burial or burning

This is the third severance obligation.

A common grave is permissible if each body remains separately shrouded and named. Individual graves are permissible. Controlled cremation is permissible where disease, land, or household custom makes it safer. The bell sounds as the named remains enter earth or fire, and the recorder states who denied burial and what disposition was chosen.

The rite does not require a priest, but it does require a witness who is not speaking under Judith's command. Copies of the final register should go to at least two independent holders.

When all thirty-one founding dead have been identified, lifted, and honestly buried or burned, the Worm in White pact is severed. Judith loses wound displacement, magical sickness transfer, command of abandoned bodies, and pact-driven rot. Existing infection, poison, injury, and ordinary decomposition remain real.

Recover the renewal dead when possible. Leaving one does not restore the founding pact, but it leaves evidence, grief, and a lesser corpse hazard in Judith's hands.

## Hostile interference

Use opposition according to Judith's warning and remaining channels.

- **Pit-dead under command** grab tools, roll into workers, or try to enter the wrong shrouds. Named resistant dead can help restrain them.
- **Worm knots** cross wounds or illness bottles. They can be cut under bell sound, isolated with dry ash, or followed to their source.
- **Cleft's corpse-cart team** attempts to seize the bell or disperse laborers under a quarantine order.
- **Judith** may transfer sickness into a public supporter, reopen an old wound in a laborer, or appear through an accepted hearth carried on a relief wagon.

Opposition forces choices between protection, evidence, pace, and pursuit. Thirty-one completed releases never reset.

## Judith's response

Judith's strongest defense is to make honest burial look like reckless contagion.

- She circulates a true warning about caustic lime while concealing the safer method.
- She sends Odo with a quarantine proclamation that gives Cleft custody of bodies and records.
- She returns Pell's old sickness during public labor and calls it proof that the pits were disturbed.
- She offers paid burial crews whose shrouds carry blackthorn tags.
- She orders one commanded relative to beg the workers to stop.

A public answer names the current safety practice, the bodies already identified, and where exposed people are receiving care. Bare reassurance is weaker than visible competence.

## Partial success and careless destruction

Track progress by body and by operational stage.

| State | Material result | Effect on Judith |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bell absent or silent | Dead remain hard to distinguish from Judith's commands | Full Worm powers remain |
| Bell sounding | Transfers are visible; resistant dead can act | Wound movement can be interrupted locally |
| A body identified | That corpse stops taking Judith's wounds or supper commands | One transfer route closes |
| A body lifted | Its root and false fruit fail | Local corpse command and rot weaken |
| A body honestly buried or burned | Its denied death is closed | That share of the pact cannot be reclaimed |
| All thirty-one completed | Founding obligation satisfied | Worm in White pact severed |

Careless destruction creates consequences without satisfying the obligation.

- Flooding lime releases heat, caustic spray, and moving white vapor.
- Burning the orchard before lifting spreads disease smoke and destroys identities.
- Burying unnamed bodies in one unrecorded trench repeats Judith's concealment.
- Treating later renewal dead as substitutions for unidentified founding dead creates a false count.
- Smashing the bell removes protection but does not undo names already recorded.

## Leaving the pits

Record:

- which founding bodies are identified, lifted, and finally disposed;
- which renewal dead were found;
- where the bell and burial registers are held;
- which workers were exposed and where they receive care;
- which households supplied labor, carts, cloth, fuel, or witness;
- which injuries or illnesses Judith can still transfer;
- whether Cleft, Odo, or a familiar learned the operation's next destination.

Infirmary tags, the chapel rite, refuge ash, Crow Wood's corpse cart, resistant testimony, and the supper response beneath Blackbriar Hall point onward.

## If the pits are reached late

The founding thirty-one cannot be removed from the site by an offstage order.

Judith may move renewal dead, steal tools, threaten workers, collapse one access ramp, foul one staging area, or send Cleft with a seizure order. Each response names the people and route involved. The lime trenches, founding bodies, excavation evidence, and honest records remain.

Identification, lifting, and final disposition persist separately. An identified body does not become anonymous again. A lifted body does not return to the trench. A body honestly buried or burned under truthful witness is no longer available to the Worm. Late arrival changes labor, exposure, and opposition; it does not reset completed care.
