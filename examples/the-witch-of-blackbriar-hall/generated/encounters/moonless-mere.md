# Moonless Mere

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** wetland, pact-site, dream, names

Silvered names hang above a pool that keeps its own night, where the Child lends Judith voices, recognition, and reflected roads.

## Opening View

Moonless Mere keeps a starless night beneath noon. Small mirrors along the reeds show a dormitory, wagon cage, sickbed, and hall stair; their sounds arrive several breaths late. Seven iron hooks pull toward hidden silvered vessels, and the hook marked for Nell Sedge trembles hardest. A servant uncovers a crate as two child-shaped shells of broken glass wade ashore. Every reflected child opens its eyes. In a borrowed mother's voice, the standing reflection offers one captive's location for another person's name.

## Encounter Material

## What Judith does here

Begin with the state actually reached. The seven hooks may pull toward vessels still at Saint Mercy House, vessels already secured, or names carried elsewhere; the corresponding bodies may be in different encounters. The standing reflection is watching, the drowned cabinet still holds older names, and every uncovered surface can become a route or a bargaining table.

Before anyone approaches the water, record which bodies, vessels, witnesses, and safe carriers are present. The operation can restore an absent person's name, protect a secured vessel, open a reflected road, or recover older victims without pretending those are the same success. Breaking glass before a name reaches an unborrowed reflection still injures the person whose social handle remains inside.
## Reaching the mere

The mere lies beyond reed marsh south of Blackbriar Hill. Four approaches are possible.

- **The causeway** carries Judith's covered carriage and is watched by crow perches.
- **The fen path** begins near Rhea Fen's laundry pools and reaches the vessel shore without crossing a reflected route.
- **The chapel drainage ditch** enters near the one patch the honest bell does not reflect.
- **A borrowed corridor** from Saint Mercy House or Blackbriar Hall can be traversed through a hanging mirror, but the Child controls where an unguarded traveler exits.

Before approaching, cover recovered vessels in dull unpatterned cloth. Do not read written names aloud within sight of water or polished glass. Ask each participant what name or title they will use during the operation.

## The site

### The reed shore

Dozens of small mirrors hang from bent reeds. Each copies a room where Judith has a name, accepted gift, or uncovered mirror. The images are current enough to show movement, but sound arrives several breaths late. Cutting a reed drops its route into the water and leaves a silver knot that can be examined for the linked person or place.

The shore is firm only where a person's own reflection follows them. Borrowed patches repeat another person's steps and can turn a walker back toward the causeway.

### The vessel hooks

Seven iron hooks stand beneath a black awning. Judith hangs newly delivered vessels here before lowering them into the mere. Scratches record Nell, Amey, Jo, Lark, Perrin, Sella, and Corin in Judith's private abbreviations. Empty hooks still pull weakly toward the current location of each vessel.

This distinguishes a secured vessel from a body rescued without its name. The pull ends when the name has been returned correctly.

### The unborrowed basin

A shallow stone basin lies where the chapel bell has no reflection. It fills from the mere through a narrow channel but does not copy distant rooms. The Child has cracked its rim and hung mirrors around it so that most visitors never see a reflection belonging only to the present person.

Clear or cover every mirror that can see the basin. Flush the channel once, let the surface settle, and test the reflection before beginning a return. The basin is a tool, not sacred ground; another still surface can work if it passes the same test.

### The standing reflection

At the end of a submerged stone walk, a child-shaped figure stands in the water while the shore behind it remains empty. It appears differently to each observer, usually as a younger person whose trust they regret losing. Its mouth moves before it speaks.

This is the Child Behind Glass. It cannot leave the mere bodily. It can act through name vessels, reflected routes, dreams, and anyone who explicitly lends a voice, face, or name.

### The drowned cabinet

A black cabinet rests below the standing reflection. It contains twelve older vessels, Judith's first bargain scratched onto silver, and lists of names she considered but never successfully took. Some vessels belong to living people, some to the dead, and three cannot yet be identified.

Opening the cabinet requires following its real reflection rather than the visible object. A covered mirror, the honest bell, or a person whose returned name cannot be copied can identify the correct latch.

## The Child's bargains

The Child offers accurate information because exact truth makes its bargains more attractive.

- It can show where Jo, Perrin, or Corin is currently held.
- It can replay Judith's first naming bargain.
- It can reveal which household token carries a person's written name.
- It can expose a private fact Judith intends to use publicly.
- It can open a reflected route into Blackbriar Hall.

The price is equally exact: say a full chosen name, let the Child borrow a voice until dawn, leave one vessel unreturned, look into its standing reflection while accepting the face shown, or carry a mirror into a refuge.

Questions, insults, silence, false names, and observing a demonstration are not consent. Make the exchange clear before resolving acceptance. A bargain may be tactically useful and still dangerous; an accidental phrase never counts as acceptance.

## Name exposure without a vessel

The Child's leverage has visible stages.

- **A spoken name overheard at the mere** lets it mimic the speaker's voice once within the marsh.
- **A name written and reflected** lets it send a dream or produce a false image of the person.
- **A knowingly lent voice or face** lets it speak or appear through the lender for the agreed duration.
- **A silvered vessel made through Judith's process** permits sustained command, false recognition, and route control until the name is returned.

Destroying an ordinary mirror does not erase a spoken name. Conversely, the patron cannot create a full vessel from hearing a stranger introduce themselves once.

## Testing an unborrowed reflection

A reflection is unborrowed when it belongs to the present person rather than to Judith, the Child, a distant room, or another name.

Perform three tests:

1. the person makes an unannounced small movement; the reflection follows at the same moment rather than before or after;
2. the person speaks a chosen form of address; the reflected mouth forms the same words in the same voice;
3. the person places a hand beyond the reflecting surface's edge; no second hand remains in the image.

If any test fails, cover nearby mirrors, clear silver knots, change the water, or move to another surface. The honest bell can interrupt borrowed reflections for several breaths but does not make a false surface permanently safe.

## Returning a name to its owner

Use this sequence for a present and willing owner.

1. Keep the vessel covered until the unborrowed reflection is ready.
2. The owner states which name is theirs and which forms they reject.
3. Uncover only the face of the vessel and place it beside, not inside, the reflection.
4. The owner speaks the name into the reflection while a free witness answers with the relationship by which they know the person.
5. Wait until the reflection moves and speaks in exact time with the owner. Warmth returns to the vessel and the iron hook for that name goes slack.
6. Wrap the vessel in cloth and break it downward into a tray or bag. Collect the silvered fragments so the Child cannot house another copy in them.

The owner may choose a current name rather than the exact form Judith stole if the witness can identify the continuity honestly. The rite restores ownership; it does not force a victim to keep Judith's preferred wording.

## Returning an absent or dead person's name

A free witness may act when the owner is absent, unable to speak, or dead.

The witness brings an unborrowed possession or a clearly identified record, stands in their own unborrowed reflection, and states:

- the person's name and chosen form of address;
- the relationship or events by which the witness knows them;
- why the owner cannot speak here;
- that Judith Crowl has no authority to use the name.

For a living absent owner, the vessel becomes warm and dormant. Judith loses command and recognition effects, but the owner must later accept or revise the returned name in person to complete healing. For a dead owner, the name returns to memory and record; the vessel can then be broken without pretending the dead have been restored.

A witness who only read a list and knows nothing else cannot satisfy the clause. Several partial witnesses may combine their knowledge.

## Older vessels and recoverability

The drowned cabinet contains twelve older vessels. Nine remain identifiable. Leta Orm, Corren Bell, Miri Voss, Jessa Rill, and Harn Coil survived Saint Mercy House and now live elsewhere under incomplete names. Pella Vane, Orric Bale, Nessa Kett, and Tavi Moor are dead but remain known through household and chapel records. Three vessels are too damaged to distinguish during ordinary play.

“Recoverable” means that an owner or free witness can identify the person with enough specificity to distinguish them from a mark on silver. The nine identifiable older names remain recoverable before every witness is assembled. Keep the three unidentified vessels covered, separately recorded, and outside Judith's custody. Breaking them blindly injures whatever social memory remains and gives the Child a new shard through which to bargain.

Judith's pact is severed when the seven current names and all nine identifiable older names have been returned in proper order. The three unidentified vessels do not preserve Judith's command because neither she nor the Child can still distinguish whom they bind, but they remain unresolved victims and dangerous objects.

## Mirror navigation

The hanging mirrors can provide routes into Saint Mercy House, Crow Wood, and Blackbriar Hall.

To use one deliberately:

1. identify the room and confirm the image is current;
2. cover or cut neighboring mirrors so the route cannot be switched;
3. anchor the traveler's name outside the image through a witness, written card, or returned vessel;
4. enter in physical contact with the mirror frame or water knot;
5. leave a free witness at the mere to keep the route named.

Without an anchor, the Child may exchange the destination for another reflected room. If it already owns the traveler's vessel, it can close the route behind them. A successful route is still infiltration into a real guarded place, not teleportation past every obstacle.

## Opposition at the mere

- **Reed mirrors** copy allies, captives, or Judith and create false paths. They can be covered, cut, turned, or tested.
- **The standing reflection** bargains, reveals, and tries to make one person's rescue depend on abandoning another.
- **Glass waders** are child-sized shells assembled from broken vessels. They carry uncovered names toward the drowned cabinet and collapse when the relevant name is spoken back.
- **Judith's carriage team** may arrive with new vessels or remove old ones if warned.
- **Judith herself** can appear in a borrowed reflection, speak through a stolen voice, or enter by carriage. She cannot bodily step from a mirror unless the Guest in Ash also supplies an accepted threshold.

Choose opposition that follows from intact channels. The mere should remain legible enough for careful work.

## Judith's response

Judith attacks relationships around the names.

- She speaks in a parent's voice and gives a child a plausible but false evacuation instruction.
- She publicly claims that returned names have made residents delirious or unrecognizable.
- She moves an absent body while work continues on its vessel.
- She orders Sister Harl or Cleft to break one vessel early and blame the injury on the rescuers.
- She reveals a true secret obtained through a stolen name to make a witness hesitate publicly.
- If one current name is restored, she concentrates dream pressure on another rather than reversing the completed return.

A restored name stays restored. Judith's retaliation changes custody, trust, or the danger to remaining victims.

## Partial success and early breakage

Track each vessel separately.

| State | Material result | Effect on Judith |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vessel uncovered and active | Reflection and command channel remain open | Full name-based powers for that person |
| Vessel covered and freely carried | Observation and easy route use blocked | Command remains but requires another active channel |
| Unborrowed reflection established | Safe return can begin | Child cannot substitute a copied image unnoticed |
| Name spoken back correctly | Hook goes slack; voice and recognition begin to return | Judith loses command and mimicry through that name |
| Vessel broken afterward | Silver copy cannot be reclaimed | Return is stable |
| All recoverable names returned | Standing reflection loses Judith's bargain | Child Behind Glass pact severed |

Breaking a vessel before returning the name produces one specific consequence: fractured voice, loss of recognition by one loved person, compelled sleepwalking, or movement of the name into the nearest mirror. The injury is serious but not a reason to remove the victim from play permanently. The name can still be pursued to the mere and returned.

Smashing the drowned cabinet, draining the mere, or burning the reeds without identifying vessels scatters reflected routes across the vale. It may deny Judith one local station while making recovery harder; it does not count as returning names.

## Severance

When the seven current names and all nine identifiable older names have been returned before their vessels are broken:

- every iron hook goes slack;
- borrowed rooms vanish from the reeds;
- the standing reflection finally copies Judith rather than the observer;
- Judith's first bargain appears in her own handwriting across the water;
- the Child pronounces her name once and withdraws beneath the surface.

The Child Behind Glass pact is severed. Judith loses sustained name command, borrowed voices, dream entry through stolen names, false recognition, and reflected routes. Ordinary lies, disguises, remembered secrets, and trauma remain.

## Leaving the mere

Record:

- the location and state of all seven current vessels and bodies;
- which older vessels were identified, returned, preserved, or lost;
- who served as owner or free witness for each return;
- any early-breakage injury and where the displaced name went;
- which reflected routes remain usable or exposed;
- whether Judith, her carriage, or the Child made a bargain;
- which names or titles company members used at the shore;
- whether the standing reflection and Judith's pact remain intact.

The reflected Blackbriar corridor, absent captives, Judith's original bargain, the chapel's missing reflection, and the feast assembled from stolen voices point onward.

## If the mere is reached late

Judith can move only vessels and people still carried by her actual network.

- An unsecured current vessel may be sent toward Crow Wood, Blackbriar Hall, or the Underhall through a named servant, wagon, crow load, or prepared mirror route.
- The shore, drowned cabinet, older hooks, standing reflection, and Judith's original bargain remain at the mere.
- A name already spoken back in an unborrowed reflection stays returned. A hook already slack does not tighten again. A vessel broken after a correct return cannot be reclaimed.
- Covering or destroying one reflected exit closes that route; it does not make the physical shore inaccessible.

Record every moved vessel separately. A late arrival may require pursuit and a divided operation; it never finds an empty lake and a reset pact.
