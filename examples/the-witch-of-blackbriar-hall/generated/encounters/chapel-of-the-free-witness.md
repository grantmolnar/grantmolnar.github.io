# Chapel of the Free Witness

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** chapel, records, counter-rites, refuge

The doorless chapel preserves the rolls, bell, and witness rites that restore shelter, burial, and naming to the people who must answer for them.

## Opening View

The Chapel of the Free Witness has no door. Blackthorn and strips of Judith's closure order cross the empty frame while Brother Caldus holds two record pages together against a stitching root. Inside, Hett Vey braces the cracked bell beam. Piers Vey advances on her in a guard's coat, gray-eyed, repeating Captain Cleft's command; each step moves the clapper wrapped in a waxed roll of names. Riders appear on the public road. One carries a fitted door.

## Encounter Material

## What the chapel preserves

The chapel stands open because its door is gone, not because anyone has reclaimed it. Judith's closure strips still grow through the blackthorn threshold; the honest bell hangs disabled with the refuge roll wrapped around its clapper; the three offices survive in scattered pages; six hidden household tokens make the undercroft unsafe as a refuge.

Choose the first act. A public reopening can break the false ordinance before witnesses. Quiet record recovery preserves evidence. Bell repair prepares the White Pits. Clearing tokens and assigning beds prepares shelter. The chapel supplies procedures and a roof, but none of those four kinds of work completes the others or turns every person beneath that roof into a free witness.
## Approaching the chapel

The chapel stands on low ground between the village and north orchard. It can be reached from the public road, the Merrow footpath, or the old burial lane.

- The **public road** makes reopening visible and allows Judith to answer through officials.
- The **Merrow footpath** reaches the vestry window and gives Caldus or Adela a private entry.
- The **burial lane** is choked with blackthorn but permits a cart to carry the bell toward the White Pits once cleared.

Anyone carrying a blackthorn token, Mercy gift, stolen ash, written command, or uncovered name vessel should decide whether to bring it inside. The chapel's openness exposes channels; it does not automatically neutralize them.

## The site

### The open threshold

The original door, hinges, and latch are gone. Blackthorn branches form a false door across the opening. Each branch bears a strip from Judith's closure order. Reading her formal titles aloud causes the thorns to grow toward the speaker's mouth and hands.

The barrier may be cut, unwoven, burned, or legally dismantled. Cutting under bell sound makes the closure strips readable. Burning destroys some evidence and sends thorn smoke toward any marked household downwind. Publicly reading the false authority before removal gives villagers a concrete reason to regard the chapel as reopened.

Do not replace the door. The building's operating principle is that shelter is offered without imprisoning the sheltered. A curtain or weather screen may be hung so long as no one claims power to bar an occupant inside.

### The nave

The nave contains plain benches, a cold central brazier, and a wall ledger naming households that offered winter shelter without acquiring ownership over guests. Judith scratched her own name across several entries, but clean rubbing reveals the original hosts.

When a commanded person enters, the old bell gives one dull movement even if its clapper is still wrapped. This is warning, not automatic release.

### The cracked bell beam

A small bronze bell hangs from a split oak beam. Its clapper has been wrapped in the complete Saint Vey refuge roll. The leather hanger is rotten, one bronze pin is missing, and the clapper cannot swing far enough to ring clearly.

Removing the paper carelessly tears the nineteen names. Soften the outer wax with hand warmth or a covered low flame, support the roll on cloth, and unwrap it rather than cutting through it. The bell can then be lowered for repair.

### The vestry records

Three cabinets hold:

- Canon Ysra's unanswered inspection letters concerning Saint Mercy House;
- the pox procession chronicle and fragments of the missing burial roll;
- Auda Vey's trial and the false famine ordinance;
- the old priest's memorandum recording Judith's request to bind three guests;
- household hospitality law distinguishing aid from welcome;
- the closure order that transferred chapel fittings to Judith's custody.

Blackthorn roots have stitched some pages together. Pulling a page free without first cutting the root at both ends causes ink to migrate onto the closure order. The information is not destroyed, but custody becomes harder to prove.

### The three manuals

The threshold, burial, and naming offices were separated so no one officiant could conceal all three obligations. Judith's closure scattered their pages.

- The **threshold manual** lies beneath the brazier stones and in strips on the blackthorn barrier.
- The **burial office** is split between the vestry cabinet, bell inscriptions, and the reverse of the pox chronicle.
- The **naming office** lies beneath the altar cloth, inside the east wall, and in Nell's copied notes at Saint Mercy House.

Each operation has a short material test; no complete reconstruction of the manuals is required.

### The undercroft

A shallow undercroft stores bell fittings, spare litters, old shrouds, and the door's removed hinges. Judith's servants hid six household tokens here after the closure so the Guest could enter any future refuge established in the chapel. Finding and returning or destroying those tokens is necessary before using the site as a dependable shelter.

## People connected to the chapel

- **Brother Caldus Merrow** can locate the burial fragments and explain where he falsified later records. He does not know the full rites from memory.
- **Hett Vey** remembers Auda Vey's trial and can identify the bell's original fittings. She wants the closure order read aloud before the chapel is called open again.
- **Piers Vey** can be commanded through his guard token. Bell sound interrupts the command long enough for him to choose whether to remove the token or continue serving Cleft.
- **Dena Moor** knows the hidden refuge path and remembers Judith asking which chapel documents named accepted travelers.
- **Mara Sedge** understands the practical household revocation clause and has compared it with Judith's cure receipts.
- **Odo Vane** knows the closure order is fraudulent. He may arrive to defend it, bargain over it, or destroy the cabinet that proves his role.

No single person is the required priest. The chapel works through distributed witness because that is the doctrine Judith tried to suppress.

## Repairing the honest bell

The bell must be made portable enough to reach the White Pits and clear enough to distinguish truthful witness from command.

Required work:

1. lower the bell without tearing the refuge roll;
2. replace the missing bronze pin or fit a sound iron substitute;
3. replace the rotten hanger with clean leather or cord not taken from Judith's stores;
4. fit a carrying yoke, tripod, or wagon frame;
5. ring it once at the open threshold while a free witness states what work it will accompany.

Vey Bellhouse has a matching pin. Rusk Cooperage can build a yoke. Mett Tallowhouse has clean wax and leather. Improvised parts work if they are sound and their origin is stated honestly.

A cracked or makeshift bell may still function. The crucial distinction is whether it records openly rather than conceals. Judith's own bell, a stolen funeral bell, or a sound produced by a commanded witness does not satisfy the burial office.

Under the bell's sound:

- a commanded person shows the physical carrier of command;
- a copied reflection falls briefly out of sequence;
- the pit-dead can resist Judith long enough to be identified;
- an ash door becomes visible but does not automatically close.

## The threshold procedure

The threshold manual distinguishes accepting aid from granting entry.

A household withdrawal requires:

1. the threshold, hearth, or specific token through which Judith claims entry;
2. a person who lives there or knowingly accepted the relevant gift;
3. a plain statement naming what was accepted and what access is withdrawn;
4. removal, return, covering, or destruction of the physical carrier.

The chapel cannot revoke every household's welcome. It can preserve the procedure, test a token, train witnesses, and establish publicly that Judith's claim is neither natural nor irrevocable.

If a whole household cannot agree, one resident may withdraw their own name, room, bed, or hearth use. This narrows Judith's access without pretending family conflict is solved.

## The burial procedure

The burial office gives the White Pits operation its truthful form.

For each body or household group, a free witness states:

- who the person is, including honest uncertainty;
- where the remains were found;
- who denied the promised burial;
- whether the body will be buried or burned;
- where the record and remains will go.

The bell sounds as the body leaves concealment and again at final disposition. A common grave is acceptable when names remain separate. Fire is acceptable when bodies remain identified. Clerical rank is not required.

## The naming procedure

The naming office supplies three tests.

1. **Identity:** the owner or free witness must know the person, relationship, and chosen form of address rather than reciting marks from a list.
2. **Unborrowed reflection:** the reflection must follow the present person's voluntary movement and voice instead of anticipating, delaying, or copying another room.
3. **Order:** speak the name back, wait for the reflection to belong to the person, then break the vessel. Reversing the order injures the owner and lets the Child move the name into another mirror.

For an absent owner, a free witness may hold an unborrowed possession and state the relationship. This disables Judith's command and preserves the name for the owner's later acceptance. For a dead owner, the act returns the name to memory and record; it does not restore life.

## Reopening the chapel

Practical preparation can reopen the chapel as a refuge, record office, and staging point.

- Remove the blackthorn barrier and read or preserve the closure strips.
- Search the undercroft and remove Judith's six hidden tokens.
- Build a fresh brazier from unclaimed or freely given fuel.
- Establish an entry roster recording who brings gifts, tokens, ash, names, or illness bottles.
- Assign beds, food, medical care, watches, and destinations for anyone sheltered.
- Keep the threshold physically open or guarded by people who do not claim ownership over occupants.

This makes the chapel defensible, not invulnerable. Judith can attack through a carrier someone brings inside, through ordinary servants, through legal seizure, or through patrons whose pacts remain intact.

## Chapel hazards

- **Title-thorns** grow when Judith's formal titles are read without exposing the false authority attached to them. Naming the underlying act—closure, seizure, coercion—halts the growth long enough to cut.
- **Migrating ink** moves evidence onto Judith's closure order when root-bound pages are torn. Copies and witnesses can preserve meaning even if provenance becomes contested.
- **Command resonance** causes marked servants to repeat the last instruction heard from Judith. Bell sound exposes the carrier and gives one voluntary interval.
- **The offered door** appears if someone tries to make the chapel secure by accepting a new barred door from Judith or the Guest. Installing it would turn refuge back into custody.

The hazards protect concealment and ownership; they are never generic animated vines filling every room.

## Judith's response

Judith treats reopening as theft of an institution she claims to have replaced.

- She sends Odo with a seizure order naming the bell as Blackbriar property.
- She commands Piers Vey to strike Hett or Caldus before the bell can be repaired.
- She offers new food, bedding, and a fitted door to anyone sheltering at the chapel.
- She has Cleft arrest one witness for spreading plague panic.
- She sends a familiar to steal one manual page or exchange it for a convincing false copy.
- If public support is growing, she appears and calmly asks who will feed every dependent after this “ceremony” ends.

Her question identifies a real obligation. Her claim of ownership remains false. Answer with visible assignments of labor and care where possible.

## Partial success and failure forward

Track the chapel by functions rather than one restored/not-restored state.

| Function | Incomplete state | Completed state |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Refuge roll | Wrapped, torn, or uncopied | Nineteen names available in independent copies |
| Honest bell | Hanging but muffled or unportable | Clear, portable, and publicly commissioned for witness work |
| Threshold office | Fragmentary | Household revocation test understood and usable |
| Burial office | Fragmentary | White Pits identification and disposition record usable |
| Naming office | Fragmentary | Identity, reflection, and sequence tests usable |
| Chapel refuge | Tokens, no supplies, no roster | Open threshold, carriers screened, beds and care assigned |
| Public standing | Closure order uncontested | Fraud exposed or reopening witnessed |

A raid may steal the bell after the manuals are copied. Fire may destroy one cabinet while Caldus preserves the relevant chronology. The bell may be repaired without making the chapel a refuge. Preserve each completed function.

## Leaving the chapel

Record:

- who holds the refuge roll and each manual copy;
- whether the bell is repaired, where it is mounted, and who carries it;
- which hidden tokens were removed from the undercroft;
- whether the closure order was exposed publicly;
- who is sheltering in the chapel and what care they have;
- which witnesses Judith can still command, discredit, or reach;
- which operation—refuge, pits, mere, household revocation, or final feast—comes next.

## If the chapel is reached late

Judith can attack one chapel function at a time.

- A servant or familiar may steal one uncopied manual page, move the portable bell, plant one marked token, or arrest one named witness.
- Independent copies remain independent. A witnessed reopening remains public. An open threshold does not silently grow a new door.
- If the bell is moved, record its carrier and destination. It can be pursued, recovered, or used elsewhere.
- Refuge beds, food, watchers, and medical care remain practical requirements even after the doctrine is understood.

The chapel can still support household revocation, refuge, burial, or naming work through whatever functions remain completed. Do not close every function because one raid succeeded.
