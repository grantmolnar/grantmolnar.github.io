# Saint Mercy House

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** institution, children, rescue, names, antagonist

Saint Mercy House turns bread, beds, medicines, copied names, and transferred illness into a custody system disguised as charity.

## Opening View

Saint Mercy House is warm enough to steam in the rain. Thirty children sit before untouched bowls; nine adults wait with bottles, crutches, and folded blankets. Sister Harl displays clean public accounts while Tib Wale turns the rear-yard key. Behind the sealed east stair, a girl whispers each visitor's whole name in order. Harl asks who will feed thirty-nine dependents if breakfast becomes an inspection. Two empty crates lined with red cord strike the floor beside the stair.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

Lunch is being served in the petition hall while Sister Harl keeps a sealed transfer order ready for Crow Wood. Five captives remain hidden above thirty children and nine adult dependents; all seven name vessels still hang in the mirror dormitory; the kitchen can support four ordinary days or one hurried evacuation. The house is feeding people at the same moment it prepares bodies, names, and records for Judith. An inspection that proves abuse but supplies no receiving beds, medicines, carriers, or guarded destination gives Harl the argument she needs to move them herself.

Arrival may come under Canon Ysra's seal, as kin or donors, through the laundry drain, behind a rescued witness, or in open force. Every entrance raises three questions:

- Which records, hidden rooms, and name vessels remain reachable before Harl begins moving them?
- Which people require bottles, stretchers, chosen carriers, familiar faces, or protected names before they can cross the door safely?
- Who will receive them, feed them, and answer Judith when she calls the evacuation abandonment or abduction?

A single inspection roll cannot answer all three. Let access, trust, evidence, medical safety, hidden captives, and departure routes change on their own clocks.
## Current occupancy

The visible population consists of thirty children and nine adult dependents. Four paid staff run the house. Five captives are concealed upstairs:

- **Nell Sedge**, thirteen, has counted every mirror and learned the order in which a stolen name must be returned.
- **Amey Fen**, twelve, is forced to answer in dead Ilyra Fen's voice after dusk.
- **Lark Merrow**, nine, is kept near the infirmary so Judith can compel Brother Caldus.
- **Sella Darr**, eight, is held because her sister Sila witnessed the planted poppet.
- **Eda Lorn**, thirty-six, is an infirmary patient whose family was falsely told she recovered and left.

All seven silvered name vessels are still in the mirror dormitory. The bodies of Jo Rusk, Perrin Mett, and Corin Pike are elsewhere, but their written names remain here. Judith can therefore punish or command them at a distance, and their vessels matter before their bodies are found. The Glass Nurse also coordinates seven copied identities through the silver, but vessels alone cannot form the captive choir. Harl's transfer order must bring at least four living, controlled children to the Underhall; securing a vessel neither rescues its owner nor silences that owner's physical voice.

## Principal people

- **Sister Harl**, steward, knowingly collects names, orders punishments, and prepares transfers. She believes dependency should be used without apology. She is culpable, but she has told herself Judith preserves the institution rather than spending it. Proof that the feast will consume residents, combined with a credible evacuation plan, can make her turn on Judith. Mere exposure of abuse makes her defend the house more fiercely.
- **Matron Eda Wren**, day matron, has tolerated coercion because she can point to children who were fed. When immediate harm to a named child is placed before her, she chooses the child. She can organize the visible residents, identify medical needs, and keep a frightened evacuation from becoming a stampede.
- **Ressa Pold**, cook, hides extra bread and has memorized which children cannot return home. She will help quietly before she will testify publicly.
- **Tib Wale**, porter and night guard, accepts Harl's discipline and enjoys deciding who is grateful enough to enter. He controls the front keys and the bell pull to Crow Wood.
- **Old Fen Corren**, resident cobbler, has copied Mercy Book entries into shoe soles. He refuses to leave the infirmary patients behind and knows which service shoes conceal pages.
- **The Glass Nurse**, a joint reflection made from seven stolen names, occupies the mirror dormitory after dark or whenever Harl uncovers the central glass. It imitates desired voices, repeats marked-room conversations, and gives commands that sound like trusted people.

Use four or five resident voices in play rather than presenting the house as a crowd. Good choices include:

- **Henn Lye**, fifteen, who portions food and will organize older children if given an actual destination;
- **Mira Coil**, eleven, who remembers the full names Harl made residents repeat;
- **Bess Orm**, eight, who can distinguish the Glass Nurse because its reflection smiles before the speaker does;
- **Tamsin Pike**, fourteen, who knows Corin was taken across the east bridge and wants the vessel bearing his name protected;
- **Sera Quill**, a blind basketmaker, who can identify Harl and Judith by step and perfume and does not rely on false reflections;
- **Dall Oat**, an adult laborer with transferred lung sickness, who can walk only if his sealed bottle travels with him.

Residents do not become loyal because a door opens. They ask where the youngest will sleep, whether siblings remain together, who carries medicine, whether Judith can enter the destination, and whether their rescuers will return for those still hidden.

## House plan

### 1. Petition hall

A polished public room holds the donor board, intake desk, and six visible gift cabinets. Harl receives inspectors here. The displayed ledger is genuine but incomplete: it records food and beds while omitting transferred illness, hidden custody, and mirror vessels. Tib can lock the outer doors from the desk. A blackthorn lamp above the hearth lets Judith hear formal petitions spoken beneath it.

Reading Canon Ysra's commission publicly opens the refectory, kitchens, visible dormitory, and public accounts. Harl calls the upper east wing unsafe because of roof damage. Pressing that boundary begins the inspection clock.

### 2. Refectory and schoolroom

Thirty children sit in six assigned groups. The arrangement lets staff count absences instantly and makes private conversation difficult. The slate lesson repeats the Renewal phrase: “We receive mercy and its mistress.” Children have altered several letters so the sentence reads differently from the doorway than from the tables.

A resident speaks honestly only after seeing how speech will not punish the next table. Move Harl, Tib, and the blackthorn lamp out of earshot before offering broad promises.

### 3. Kitchen, pantry, and rear yard

Ressa keeps enough food for four ordinary days or one hurried evacuation. The rear gate opens onto the parish granary yard, but Harl has stacked it with winter fuel to make it appear unusable. A thin old wall behind the flour bins can be opened into the granary passage without collapsing the kitchen.

The pantry contains gifts marked for particular households. Removing a gift may block one known channel, but do not treat the pantry as every token in the vale. The inventory is useful for identifying carriers and planning household revocations.

### 4. Cobbler room and laundry drain

Fen's bench contains shoe soles copied from the Mercy Book. The copies are partial but independent of Judith's records. A stone laundry drain runs beneath the old Darr wash channel. Small residents and unarmored adults can crawl it; stretchers and the infirmary bottles cannot.

The drain is the safest covert exit for a few children and the worst route for a mass flight. Sila Darr can receive people at the far end if warned.

### 5. Visible dormitory and roof schoolroom

These rooms are clean, crowded, and genuinely used. The roof schoolroom has a shuttered loading window above a cooper's awning. A rope crossing can move a few able children when the lower floor is controlled, but rain, panic, and the visible drop make it unsuitable for the sick or very young.

A false wall behind the copybooks opens onto the sealed east stair. Nell persuaded Mira and Bess to hide its iron key inside a hollow counting rod.

### 6. Records room

The records room contains the **Mercy Book**, token lists, donor correspondence, altered admission dates, infirmary transfers, and Sister Harl's feast checklist. The shelves are arranged so that removing the central book releases a blackthorn catch and rings the Crow Wood bell unless the side weights are replaced.

The clue-bearing evidence is distributed rather than bundled into one confession:

- admission and trial dates expose Judith's manufactured prosecutions;
- gift columns connect accepted aid to later access incidents;
- token lists identify specific household carriers;
- the feast checklist names the Mercy Book, hearth tokens, and seven stolen-name voices;
- Harl's sealed transfer order sends captives and vessels through Crow Wood if the upper floor is breached.

Copying, dividing, or publicly reading records may be more useful than carrying one vulnerable book. The Mercy Book itself remains a component of the final rite.

### 7. Punishment passage

Four narrow closets have no interior handles. The walls contain written names beneath limewash. Speaking a captive's name in the passage makes the matching door seem to move farther away. Scraping away one inscription gives that person a brief interval in which Judith cannot stop them at a marked threshold, but it also alerts the Glass Nurse.

The closets preserve immediate evidence of abuse: fingernail dates, punishment tallies, and phrases residents were forced to copy. They do not replace the broader records needed to establish the system.

### 8. Mirror dormitory

Seven narrow mirrors hang from blackthorn frames. Each silvered name vessel is a folded slip sealed between the backing and the glass. The central mirror has no independent vessel; it combines the seven reflections into the Glass Nurse.

The Nurse first uses comfort. It speaks in Mara's voice to Nell, Ilyra's voice to Amey, or one examiner's own voice to another company member. It can then:

- repeat a command through a voice Judith has borrowed;
- show a false ally opening the wrong door;
- report speech from a marked household;
- hold one named child at a threshold;
- inflict sharp pain when Harl scores the matching vessel.

The Nurse cannot invent knowledge absent from Judith's channels. It repeats overheard speech, recorded names, resident memories, and personal information given through accepted gifts. Covering the central glass with dull cloth prevents it from coordinating the seven vessels, though individual bonds remain.

### 9. Suspended infirmary

Five patients lie in a warm locked room above the kitchen flue. Judith has transferred illnesses from paying households into dependent bodies, then drawn the worst symptoms into sealed glass bottles so the recipients remain alive enough to serve. Each chain consists of a payer entry, a recipient entry, a wax-sealed bottle, and the living patient. The treatment is not a cure.

Current patients include Eda Lorn; Dall Oat, carrying a brewer's lung sickness; Ysa Bell, carrying seizures removed from a magistrate's niece; Marric Soil, carrying a wasting fever; and Noma Feld, carrying the recurring chills of a Blackbriar tenant.

Do not smash or casually open the bottles. Heat, breakage, or separation from the matching patient releases stored symptoms into the patient or the nearest breathing body. Safe movement requires keeping each bottle upright, cool, labeled, and beside its recipient. The hidden infirmary book identifies failed transfers sent to the White Pits and restores eleven erased identities.

Medical rescue is possible without solving the Worm pact. It requires stretchers, blankets, clean water, receiving beds, and people willing to care for contagious-looking patients whom Judith has publicly described as cursed.

### 10. East bridge and transfer cabinet

A covered bridge joins the upper floor to an old service tunnel leading toward Crow Wood and Blackbriar Hall. The transfer cabinet holds red cords, covered mirror crates, prisoner hoods, and Harl's emergency order. A pull-bell summons Garran Pike's wagon.

This is Judith's extraction route, not a convenient secret exit. Seizing it can intercept the wagon, rescue transferred captives, or send false instructions. Failing to control it gives Harl a concrete way to move vessels and children rather than allowing captives to vanish arbitrarily.

## The inspection clock

Advance the clock for prolonged delay, a loud accusation with open exits, the record-room catch, or unrestricted staff messages. Careful questions consume table time, not fictional minutes by themselves.

1. **Polite containment.** Harl produces public accounts and separates inspectors from residents.
2. **Quiet preparation.** Tib bars the rear yard; Eda Wren is ordered to assemble visible children for a “health count.”
3. **Records move.** Harl sends the feast checklist and several token pages toward the east bridge.
4. **Captives move.** Nell, Amey, Lark, and Sella are hooded; the seven vessels are packed into two crates.
5. **The wagon answers.** Garran's people enter the service tunnel. If the route is blocked, Harl prepares the roof and front-door alternatives instead.
6. **Judith intervenes.** Judith arrives through the petition-hall hearth or a mirror if the relevant pact remains intact and she is not already committed at another site.

Every step leaves something actionable. A moved ledger creates a courier to follow. Hooded captives can resist, leave marks, or delay staff. The arriving wagon exposes Crow Wood. Judith's entrance reveals a channel and places her in reach.

## Approaches

### Inspection under commission

Harl obeys the letter of the commission while narrowing every request. She offers clean rooms, real food stores, and selected residents who will truthfully say they were helped. Compare records, separate staff, test the sealed wing, and ask residents about absences; a demanded confession only strengthens her posture.

A lawful inspection provides the best public evidence and the slowest access. It becomes much stronger if Mara, Caldus, Sila, or another protected witness is present.

### Donor, applicant, or kin entry

A false donor can reach the gift cabinets and petition process. A claimed relative must answer details Harl can compare to the Mercy Book. An applicant is offered food or lodging and asked to sign. Accepting is a real choice: Judith gains a channel only through an explicitly named gift and carrier; ordinary hospitality does not acquire a hidden cost after the fact.

Residents judge visitors by practical commitments. A believable rescue conversation names beds, guardians, food, transport, and how siblings stay together.

### Covert entry

The laundry drain reaches Fen's room. The granary passage reaches the kitchen. The roof reaches the schoolroom. Covert movement can bypass the public shield but creates a harder problem once people must leave. Infiltrators who secure records and routes before revealing themselves give Eda Wren and residents something around which to organize.

### Assault

The front hall makes violence look like an attack on children. Tib locks doors, Harl places visible residents between armed intruders and the stairs, and the Glass Nurse imitates company voices. Control of hearth, bell, and exits can make a direct assault succeed, but residents, records, and routes bear separate consequences.

If the Guest in Ash pact is intact, Judith can turn the petition hearth inward or move through it. If the Worm pact is intact, she can force one stored symptom into a nearby body. If the Child pact is intact, she can command through a vessel or false reflection. A weakened or severed pact removes that response visibly.

## Name-vessel handling

The seven vessels are evidence, hostages, and people’s stolen property. Treat each one separately.

1. Cover the mirror with dull, unpatterned cloth before opening the frame.
2. Do not read the written name aloud in a reflected room.
3. Keep each vessel with a free carrier who is not acting under Judith's written command.
4. Mark which body is present, missing, or already located elsewhere.
5. Do not break the vessel before the owner or a free witness returns the name in an unborrowed reflection.

Covering a vessel blocks sight and coordinated use. It does not return the name. Scratching or breaking it early causes pain, disorientation, loss of voice, or impaired recognition in the owner; it does not provide a clean shortcut. Nell knows the correct sequence because she witnessed two earlier failed attempts.

Bodies can be rescued now and vessels carried toward Moonless Mere later. Jo, Perrin, and Corin's vessels may be secured while their absent bodies remain unresolved.

## Evacuation procedure

Before the first large movement, establish four facts:

- a receiving place for children who cannot safely return home;
- a medically suitable destination for infirmary patients;
- a protected carrier and route for each name vessel and copied record;
- an accountable adult for every group leaving the house.

Then move in priorities rather than one undifferentiated crowd:

1. prevent transfer of hidden captives and vessels;
2. stabilize infirmary patients and pair each with the correct bottle;
3. move the youngest children and anyone Judith can command directly;
4. move witnesses and records through different routes where possible;
5. move remaining residents with food, blankets, and named receiving households.

Available exits:

- **Front court:** highest capacity and best for public legitimacy; unsafe while Tib, Cleft, or Judith controls the crowd.
- **Granary passage:** accepts stretchers and supply carts once the flour-bin wall is opened; leads to parish ground and can support the infirmary evacuation.
- **Laundry drain:** discreet and defensible for six small or able people at a time; cannot take stretchers, bottles in cases, or a panicked mass.
- **Roof schoolroom:** useful for a few trapped children when lower floors are lost; slow and hazardous in rain.
- **East bridge:** quickest route for the vessel crates but leads into Judith's prisoner network. Use it after seizing the tunnel, intercepting the wagon, or deliberately setting a pursuit.

A disorderly rescue still saves people. Record who escaped, who remained, which route Judith now knows, what evidence was secured, and which dependents lack a safe destination. Do not erase progress because the evacuation was imperfect.

## Sister Harl's decisions

Sister Harl has spent years turning people into beds, bottles, meal places, and transfer lines. She is neither a secret rebel nor a zealot eager to die for Judith. She tells herself that someone must keep the institution functioning and has let “functioning” excuse punishments, hidden rooms, stolen names, and patients stored as liabilities.

Calling her a hypocrite does not move her. Three concrete facts can:

- the feast list identifies named residents as voices to be spent, not people to be housed;
- the rescuers can show where the hidden residents will sleep, who carries each bottle, and who answers for the next meal;
- Judith orders her to sacrifice one person Harl has personally kept alive.

Before that break, Harl delays by count and category. She narrows answers, moves records, threatens to divide kin, and deploys the Glass Nurse while insisting that disorder is the greater cruelty. If she turns, she gives up the record-room weights, calls Eda Wren by name instead of title, opens the flour-bin route, and holds the east bridge for one evacuation wave.

Her help does not acquit her. Survivors may still charge her for transfers, punishments, and name custody she knowingly administered. If she escapes, she carries either the feast checklist or one vessel crate—never the entire encounter's evidence—and leaves a route that can be pursued.

## Judith's response

Judith prefers to arrive after care has been promised. She walks the receiving plan line by line and points to every empty space.

Under inspection, she admits the bread, beds, medicine, and forms, then requires proof for each concealed abuse. Under infiltration, she names the trespass and offers one examiner a tailored benefit for leaving the upper floor closed. Under assault, she spends the narrowest surviving pact channel and retreats only after rescuers remove something she cannot rename as temporary confusion.

During public exposure, Judith does not say Saint Mercy House is harmless. She asks who feeds thirty children tomorrow, which cottage accepts a fever patient, and whose winter grain the rescuers have volunteered. Then she punishes the first person who answers: a vessel scores until a child recants, a stored symptom returns to that speaker's favored household, or a marked guardian reaches for their own dependent. Exposure does not invite confession or reform. It tells Judith which relationship to turn into the next correction.

Her claim is always the same: mercy consists of choosing who pays, and only she has the honesty to keep the account. Her answer is the person she hurts. Judith created the scarcity, concealed the alternatives, and calls ownership the price of solving what she arranged.

She can leave through the petition hearth while the Guest channel survives, through prepared glass while the Child channel survives, or over the east bridge if her body can reach it. She cannot answer a simultaneous crisis in person. If another pact site already holds her attention, Harl runs the house and Judith's response arrives through one bounded channel.

## Outcomes to record

At the end of the encounter, record:

- each hidden captive rescued, moved, or left behind;
- the current location and condition of all seven name vessels;
- which infirmary patients and bottles were matched and moved safely;
- whether the Mercy Book, copied pages, token lists, feast checklist, and transfer order were secured, destroyed, moved, or publicly read;
- which staff aided, resisted, fled, or were captured;
- which residents have named receiving households and which remain dependent on improvised refuge;
- whether Judith's public legitimacy strengthened, fractured, or became divided by household;
- which route now points toward Crow Wood, Moonless Mere, the White Pits, the Chapel, or Blackbriar Hall.

A strong result rescues everyone onsite, preserves the vessels, secures distributed evidence, and exposes Judith's machinery. A partial result may save people while losing the Mercy Book, preserve evidence while Harl moves captives, or force a pursuit into Crow Wood. Every result leaves changed lives and actionable routes, never only a solved room.

## If the house is reached late or skipped

Saint Mercy House cannot be emptied offstage by one order.

- One completed transfer wave can move up to four ambulatory captives, one vessel crate, or one matched infirmary patient and bottle through the east bridge. It cannot move all three categories at once.
- Every transfer requires custody papers, staff, the bridge route, and either Garran's wagon or a smaller actual conveyance. Its destination is Crow Wood, Blackbriar Hall, the service route, or the Underhall.
- Thirty visible children, adult dependents, most stores, the suspended infirmary apparatus, and records too bulky for the chosen wave remain available.
- Harl may take the feast checklist or one vessel crate if she flees, never every vessel, patient, and record.
- People already evacuated, vessels already covered or removed, and evidence already copied remain in their recorded states.

If Saint Mercy House remains unvisited until the finale, use the transfer record to determine which stolen-name bodies physically reached choir bays. Populate no bay by assumption. The untransferred residents remain a live rescue, testimony, and care problem above ground.
