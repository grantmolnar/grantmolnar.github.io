# Underhall of the Hollow Feast

**Role:** necessary, end  
**Tags:** undercroft, finale, ritual, antagonist

Beneath sixty-three carved doors, Judith joins signed gratitude, household carriers, captive voices, and three old betrayals into one invitation.

## Opening View

Below Blackbriar Hall, sixty-three freestanding doors show household hearths as they are tonight. Blackthorn tokens glow on mantels; paper-thin thresholds wait for signatures; other doors remain dark. Odo Vane fits Mercy Book leaves around a four-sided lectern. Captain Cleft presses warm iron echoes into the root table. Living captives are strapped beneath silver mouthpieces as the wagon hoist lowers another covered shape. Judith sits at a table laid for four, with three empty chairs behind her. One household door swings inward. A frightened woman asks who has entered her home.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

Begin by inventorying what actually reached the chamber: Mercy Book pages and index, warm token sockets, living controlled voices, pit-dead or white roots, Judith, her lieutenants, and each surviving pact. Set the feast stage from elapsed time and prior responses. A missing body, page, carrier, witness, or commander stays missing; the reference library does not preload the finale.

Four struggles proceed at once; one ritual roll or Judith's remaining endurance cannot settle them together:

- Judith's body, position, and ability to issue commands;
- the **common welcome**, which needs at least two of its three components to remain joined;
- the captives, prisoners, and households exposed through the chamber; and
- the patrons whose pacts still possess a material claim.

Earlier work must be visible. Revoked doors face outward. Returned names leave silent choir bays. Honest burials take white roots from the table. An opened refuge makes copied thresholds swing away from the Guest. Seized records, controlled routes, divided lieutenants, and protected witnesses change who can act before the first weapon is raised.
## Entering the Underhall

The main stair descends from Judith's study. The service tunnel enters a wagon gallery above the household doors. Reflected routes can reach the choir bays while the Child pact remains intact. A hearth crossing can emerge through a corresponding household door while the Guest remains intact and that household has not revoked.

Forcing the inner stair advances the feast one stage but creates a permanent route. Entering through a household door exposes that household until its carrier is removed or the door is turned outward. Following the wagon gives access to the prisoners before the main defenders can fully redeploy.

## The chamber

### Wagon gallery

The service tunnel ends on a stone gallery with room for two wagons. Prisoners, Mercy Book packets, spare tokens, and bodies from the White Pits are received here. Two hoists descend to the choir bays and central table.

Controlling the gallery prevents new captives, guards, or records from reaching the rite. It does not by itself stop components already in place.

### Sixty-three household doors

The doors stand in seven rows of nine; only doors with a surviving signature, token, stolen ash, or another actual household carrier can connect. Through a connected door lies the matching hearth as it currently exists, not an illusion of an ideal home.

- A fully marked and unrevoked household shows a clear room and warm token.
- A signed but tokenless household shows a paper-thin doorway that cannot carry a body until a token is placed.
- A privately revoked household smokes and gives no stable view.
- A publicly revoked household faces outward and cannot be counted in the common welcome.
- A household whose carriers were destroyed carelessly may show uncontrolled fire, a false reflection, or a sick resident depending on the channel disturbed.

A person at a doorway can speak to the household, move a small object, or cross if the Guest and matching token permit. This creates opportunities for warning and revocation as well as danger.

### Mercy lectern

The Mercy Book rests on a four-sided lectern. The master index joins separated pages carried by Corin and other servants. Odo reads household names and obligations from one side; Judith signs acceptance from another.

The book can be neutralized in several materially different ways:

- remove it from the lectern and keep the master index apart from the pages;
- cut or tear out accurately identified household entries after their residents revoke;
- correct the entries under independent witness through the petition-hall speaking tube or honest bell;
- soak or otherwise render the signatures unusable after preserving necessary evidence;
- destroy the book after removing stolen names and evidence needed for the pact operations.

Burning it while the Guest remains intact sends signed ash into the household hearths and strengthens that component for one stage. Smashing it without separating written child names leaves those names available to the Child Behind Glass.

### Token table

A blackthorn root spreads across a low table and ends in sixty-three iron sockets. Forty-seven hold warm token echoes. Eleven hold empty paper rings for signed households. Five are cold.

The token component remains operational while the root can match enough actual hearth carriers to the Mercy Book. It can be neutralized by severing the central root after the Guest pact is broken, by removing and separately containing the warm sockets, or by making the relevant household doors face outward through witnessed revocation.

Cutting the root while the Guest remains intact sends inward flame toward every connected hearth. Protection requires opened doors, cooled sockets, guarded households, or acceptance of the burn. Removing sockets one by one is slower but does not spread the fire.

### Choir bays

Seven narrow bays surround the table. Each is marked for one stolen name: Nell Sedge, Amey Fen, Jo Rusk, Lark Merrow, Perrin Mett, Sella Darr, and Corin Pike. Judith needs at least four controlled voices from living captives speaking at once for the captive-choir component to function. A vessel, copied strip, borrowed recording, commanded servant, or remote voice may help control or imitate a child, but none counts as that child's physical voice in a bay.

A captive whose name has been returned cannot be used even if physically present. A bay can also be disabled by freeing its captive, removing the written-name strip, covering the silver mouthpiece, or substituting a free witness who openly refuses the welcome. Judith can move a controlled child between bays, but she cannot make one child count as two.

Captives are not helpless ritual objects. They can interrupt rhythm, exchange names, bite through wax, relay instructions through doors, and choose whether to remain long enough to help other households revoke. Do not require them to risk themselves merely because doing so is tactically useful.

### Dead table

Thirty-one white place settings correspond to the founding pit-dead. Five smaller settings correspond to the renewal trench. If the Worm remains intact, abandoned bodies or white roots answer the supper bell and carry Judith's wounds.

Each honestly identified founding body removes one setting from Judith's protection. Each lifted body leaves a chair that cannot rise. Honest final disposition of all thirty-one breaks the Worm's claim. The later five no longer strengthen the founding pact, but recovering them prevents Judith from using their bodies as immediate servants and provides living families with evidence.

The honest bell can be sounded here if it has been brought from the chapel or mounted at the pits. Its sound makes every wound transfer visible and causes the dead to answer with their own names rather than Judith's feast response.

### Three patron chairs

The chairs stand behind Judith.

- The Guest's chair is charred beneath intact varnish.
- The Worm's chair is white and damp without being cold.
- The Child's chair reflects a seated child who is never present in the room.

Completed pact work changes the chairs permanently. Weakened work exposes remaining obligations. A severed pact splits its chair and prevents that patron's full manifestation. Two broken chairs make Judith bodily mortal. Three broken chairs leave the feast without an inheriting patron even if household claims remain.

### Judith's place

Judith sits at the fourth side of the table with the contingency matrix beneath her plate. Her position joins command of the components but is not itself a component. Killing, displacing, binding, or forcing her from the place stops her personal coordination. The Court can continue if two feast components and at least one pact remain.

## The three feast components

The **common welcome** exists while at least two of these three components remain operational:

1. **Mercy Book:** written hosts and accepted obligations joined by the master index.
2. **Token network:** physical household carriers connected through the token table and ash gallery.
3. **Captive choir:** at least four controlled stolen-name voices speaking the welcome.

Neutralizing any one weakens and delays the rite. Neutralizing any two collapses the valley-wide welcome into isolated household claims. Those remaining claims can still endanger individual homes until revoked, but the Court can no longer enter the vale as one invited household.

Reveal this rule through Judith's contingency matrix, the chamber's behavior, or prior clues—not as a menu of endings. Available operations come from the people, evidence, tools, and routes actually secured.

## Feast stages

Begin at the stage justified by elapsed time and prior Judith responses. Advance when Judith completes an action, prolonged work goes unopposed, or a failed operation gives one component control. A round ending by itself changes nothing.

### Stage 1: Places laid

Odo joins the Mercy Book pages. Tokens warm in their sockets. Captives are placed in the bays. Records and people remain interceptable until the components are physically joined.

### Stage 2: Households named

Odo reads the sixty-three household names. Connected doors clarify. Publicly revoked households turn outward. Correcting an entry through witness or bell removes it from the common count and may cause Odo to lose his place.

### Stage 3: Gifts answered

Cleft, Harl if still loyal, or household servants recite what each household accepted. The token table sends heat through matching hearths. Removing sockets, opening doors, or distributing warnings creates immediate local choices.

### Stage 4: Children speak

The controlled voices answer for the vale. Judith needs four. Returned names speak freely, not in unison. A captive's clear refusal can guide a household through a doorway but does not substitute for rescuing or protecting that child.

### Stage 5: The dead attend

The Worm's bell sounds. White settings rise, injuries leave Judith's body, and stored illness moves toward the doors. The honest bell, named bodies, or a severed Worm prevent or reduce this response visibly.

### Stage 6: Betrayed welcome closes

The Guest turns connected doors inward and tries to bar exit from each represented home. Correctly opened refuge doors force copies outward; restored names let the refuge dead state what Judith did; the burned living ledger leaves the Guest without a host claim.

### Stage 7: Common invitation

If at least two feast components and at least one pact remain, the doors join into one threshold. Manifestations enter according to surviving pacts. Judith claims mastery or, if dead, becomes the first thing the Court consumes.

### Stage 8: Midnight possession

If the common welcome remains and no one contests control, the surviving patrons establish continuing access. This is not an automatic campaign end. Publicly revoked households remain outside, rescued names resist command, severed patrons are absent, and witnesses retain evidence. Record exactly what the Court gained rather than declaring the whole vale simply lost.

## Judith's capabilities by pact state

Judith normally takes one decisive action through one intact channel and one ordinary action through a servant, weapon, position, or command. Do not let three patrons become unlimited action economy.

### Guest in Ash

**Intact:** Judith crosses connected hearths, denies a threshold, turns one domestic flame inward, or hides a movement in soot.

**Doors opened:** Threshold effects leave visible soot and require a physical door to move. She cannot silently imprison someone behind a claimed threshold.

**Names restored:** Refuge dead can contradict her, copied doors repeat their names, and her soot concealment leaves a speaking trail.

**Severed:** Hearth crossing, inward fire, and threshold denial end. Ordinary fire and doors remain.

### Worm in White

**Intact:** Judith transfers one significant wound or illness into a known pit body, animates abandoned remains, or sends stored sickness through a prepared line.

**Honest bell sounded:** Transfers become visible white threads and can be intercepted. The dead can answer with their own gestures or names.

**Bodies identified or lifted:** Judith has fewer valid recipients. Use the actual remaining bodies; do not invent replacements.

**Severed:** Wounds remain on Judith, abandoned bodies fall, transferred illness stops moving, and pact rot ends.

### Child Behind Glass

**Intact:** Judith commands one person through a controlled written name, borrows a known voice, opens one reflected route, or creates one false familiar figure.

**Individual names returned:** That person cannot be commanded, imitated, or used to open a route. Their bay fails.

**Mirrors covered or tested:** The affected route or false figure ends even while other names remain controlled.

**Severed:** Borrowed voices, name commands, false recognition, and reflected routes end. Mirrors become ordinary.

### Bodily protection

- With all three pacts intact, lethal harm can be distributed among ash, dead flesh, and stolen reflection. Judith can be driven off or restrained only by controlling the carriers she would use.
- With one pact severed, she loses that defense and power family but can still divert fatal harm through the other two.
- With two pacts severed, Judith is bodily mortal. She bleeds, cannot leave by a lost channel, and remains where physical restraint places her.
- With all three severed, she is an exceptionally prepared but ordinary mortal practitioner with servants, weapons, poisons, records, and no supernatural patron defense.

## Judith's conduct in the finale

Judith remains socially exact under pressure. She names practical costs, not cosmic abstractions. She offers a cure that would move illness into Eda, a child's release in exchange for another household page, or safe departure while the village remains claimed. She never offers restitution, shared custody, or surrender of a claim. Every concession substitutes a new victim or preserves her title to the old one.

When a component fails, she does not shriek or become incoherent. She changes ownership strategy.

- If the Mercy Book fails, she tries to preserve tokens and force the children to name individual hosts.
- If the token network fails, she moves to signed pages and reflected access through named residents.
- If the choir fails, she uses Odo, Harl, or a commanded household representative as witnesses, but those voices cannot restore the choir component.
- If a pact severs, she abandons that patron's chair and exploits the others.
- If two pacts sever, she seeks a physical exit, hostage, or negotiated surrender because she knows she can now be killed.
- If all three sever, she may attempt to burn evidence, kill a particular witness, or flee through an ordinary route. She does not gain a hidden fourth patron.

Judith prefers punishments that make someone participate: ordering Cleft to put a child in a parent's doorway, making Odo read his own prepared accusation, or offering a household medicine only if it speaks the welcome. Keep the target reachable and the choice contestable.

## Lieutenants and divided loyalties

### Odo Vane

Odo controls the Mercy lectern, seals, and phrases by which injury becomes procedure. He calls children dependents and murder disposition. Exposure makes him offer one record or captive in exchange for his daughter's place and his own office. He will falsify surrender unless separated from ink, knife, and seal.

### Varro Cleft

Cleft holds the token table and armed defenders. He treats each open household door as leverage against a person elsewhere and orders ordinary guards to stand where marked guards can be spent. Loss of command, isolation from the table, or physical defeat ends his role. Conscience does not.

### Garran Pike

If loyal and present, Garran controls the wagon gallery and the only orderly retreat. If broken, he can turn the road, cut reinforcement, or bring Olla under the bell. His aid changes movement and timing, not his culpability.

### Sister Harl

Harl's state follows Saint Mercy House. Loyal, she counts choir bays and calls restraint continuity of care. Broken with Judith, she knows every buckle and can hold one route. Absent, Tib Wale or Merrit Coil performs the work badly and with less resident trust.

### Ordinary staff and guards

They do not share one courage or one excuse. Resolve each by prior commitment, dependent, payment, witnessed pact effect, and whether someone has offered a survivable refusal. Some flee. Some free captives. Some continue for wages after command is gone.

## Patron manifestations

Manifest only patrons whose pacts still possess a substantial claim. Each speaks and acts through the obligation Judith inverted.

### The Guest in Ash

The Guest appears as hands closing doors from the household side and as an occupied emptiness in every offered chair. Its speech is impeccable invitation: “May I keep the door you asked me to close?” “Will you leave these people outside so that your own may be safe?” It tries to turn rescue into trespass and refuge into title. Open doors, restored refuge names, freely offered hearths, and the burned ledger answer it in the only language it honors.

### The Worm in White

The Worm arrives as damp lime beneath plates, white roots in abandoned flesh, and wounds entering the wrong body. It counts rather than argues: bodies unnamed, witnesses absent, dispositions unfinished. Its bargains offer speed, stillness, and exact identifications in exchange for one person left without a final account. Names, careful lifting, the honest bell, and completed burial remove one item at a time from its tally.

### The Child Behind Glass

The Child appears in each uncovered surface as the person the viewer most expects to trust. It speaks in borrowed cadences and asks apparently small questions: “Which name should I keep when you leave?” “May I answer as your mother once?” “Would you know the prisoner if I wore the face?” It seeks recognition, repetition, and permission to stand for someone absent. Returned names, covered surfaces, unborrowed reflections, and free refusal leave it visible but handleless.

A manifestation is not a second all-purpose villain. The Guest closes, the Worm transfers and tallies, and the Child substitutes. Press the old violation; do not invent unrelated powers.

## Stopping the feast

The feast is stopped cleanly when the common welcome collapses and no intact patron can inherit Judith's coordinating claim. This can occur through many sequences of actual play.

- Earlier household revocations may have turned enough doors outward that the token component is easy to dismantle.
- A Mercy House rescue may already have removed four choir voices.
- Captured pages, Corin's key, and public witnesses may neutralize the Mercy Book quickly.
- Severed pact chairs may make direct confrontation with Judith and the manifestations tractable.
- Control of the wagon gallery may prevent moved captives or replacement records from arriving.
- A direct assault may kill Judith first, leaving the surviving components to be stopped while the Court tries to inherit them.

Keep these consequences behind the screen until action exposes them. Apply the component and pact rules to what actually happens.

## If Judith falls

Judith can be killed while pacts remain once two have been severed, or if every surviving recipient of her distributed harm is physically controlled. If Judith is killed or rendered unconscious, remove her personal coordination and ordinary actions immediately.

- If two or three feast components remain and at least one pact survives, the patron chairs turn toward the Mercy Book and the rite continues.
- If fewer than two components remain, the common welcome collapses even if a patron survives.
- If all three pacts are severed, no patron can inherit her place. Remaining household carriers are dangerous local objects, not a valley-wide invitation.
- Judith's body may still contain evidence: written names under the tongue, ash in old burns, white lime beneath healed scars, and silver behind one eye. Handle those carriers rather than assuming death purifies them.

Judith may be killed, captured, abandoned to the patrons, or driven into flight. Record the result and every obligation that remains.

## Authority after the confrontation

The commission may continue Mara's stay, preserve records, protect rescued people, and detain Judith or an officer caught attacking witnesses, captives, or evidence. It may not award Blackbriar Hall, appoint a permanent government, compel a child to testify, settle every confiscated title, or pronounce final judgment on collaborators.

A temporary local council is legitimate only if named household, parish, care, and civil representatives accept the work publicly, its powers are limited to food, refuge, medical care, evidence custody, immediate safety, and contact with outside authorities, and its end condition is stated. If no such coalition forms, the ending must name the narrower custodians who actually hold each person, store, record, and dangerous carrier.

## Failure-forward outcomes

Failure in the Underhall produces a specific claim, loss, or rescue problem.

- One feast component becomes fully active while another remains contestable.
- A patron manifests through a subset of household doors rather than the whole vale.
- Judith escapes through a surviving hearth, road, or mirror and leaves evidence behind.
- Odo carries a page packet into the village, creating a pursuit and local household danger.
- Cleft takes one choir captive to the wagon gallery while other bays are freed.
- Stored illness enters three named connected households, which can be isolated and treated.
- The common welcome collapses, but several private household claims remain unrepealed.
- Judith dies before the pact sites are repaired, leaving patron manifestations to be contained.

Avoid replacing a mixed result with “the ritual succeeds” or “the ritual fails.” State exactly which components, pacts, people, doors, and channels remain.

## Final record

At the end of the confrontation, record:

- Judith alive, captured, dead, escaped, or taken by a patron;
- Odo, Cleft, Garran, Harl, and principal staff states;
- Guest, Worm, and Child pacts intact, weakened, or severed;
- Mercy Book operational, neutralized, preserved, corrected, or destroyed;
- token network operational, fragmented, contained, or severed;
- each of the seven choir bays and name states;
- household doors connected, privately revoked, publicly turned outward, or locally endangered;
- Mara, Tomas, Nell, other captives, Eda Lorn, and pit-dead outcomes;
- patron manifestations remaining in the vale;
- evidence and relief stores preserved;
- immediate medical, burial, refuge, food, and legal needs;
- and who has authority to undertake those tasks.

A complete victory exposes and ends Judith's system without making fifteen years of harm vanish at midnight. A partial victory may save most people while leaving local claims, a fugitive witch, an intact patron, or contested institutions for the aftermath. The chamber reflects every person, record, route, and obligation preserved earlier.

## Arriving with partial knowledge

The Underhall remains physically legible after a direct assault.

- The Mercy Book, token table, and choir bays visibly perform different parts of the invitation. Reachable machinery may be disrupted, seized, covered, freed, disconnected, or destroyed without first checking a revelation box.
- The first time Judith displaces a wound, crosses an inward-turning door, or commands a stolen name, show the actual recipient, carrier, and trace. This reveals an immediate tactical problem and may point toward a remote site.
- A patron chair or manifestation can demonstrate the violated obligation it still uses. It does not complete the refuge opening, pit burial, or name-return sequence elsewhere.
- A missing counter-rite remains missing. Do not convert broad knowledge that Judith has three pacts into the exact material steps for all three severances.
- Direct attackers can still collapse the common welcome by neutralizing two feast components, free captives, divide lieutenants, preserve evidence, force Judith through a traceable exit, or contain a local manifestation.

Partial information changes preparation and available remedies, not permission to act. State what is visible, what each action changes, and which obligations remain elsewhere.
