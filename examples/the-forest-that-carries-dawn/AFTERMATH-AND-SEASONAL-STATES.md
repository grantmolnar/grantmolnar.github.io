# Aftermath and Seasonal States

The dawn ending is not one success track. Human rescue, caravan survival, ecological repair, seed condition, forest injury, Merewash's future, eastbound travel, and property obligations can each resolve differently.

Use the immediate record from the Glass Verge before narrating later consequences. Do not improve a damaged seed, restore an absent person, forgive a property claim, or declare the eastern forest safe merely because the session has ended.

## Immediate aftermath: the first day

### West of the verge

Each westbound group begins from the route and burdens it actually carried. A divided caravan does not automatically reunite. Groups need a meeting place, water allocation, animal count, and a record of who remained aboard.

Orra's first practical questions are:

- Which people are physically present?
- Which group holds the authoritative current roster copy?
- Which injuries changed during the crossing?
- Which animals, wagons, water, food, tools, and private cargo survived?
- Which people intentionally continued east, and which remain unresolved?
- Who is responsible for reporting the forest, seed, losses, and outstanding claims?

A group that escaped without a seed still achieved a real rescue. A group that brought a seed but cannot account for missing people does not erase that obligation.

### A carried rain-seed

A mature seed is a living circulating burden, not a gem or sealed relic. For transport it needs:

- a wet flexible cradle that does not compress the outer seam;
- an open return vessel or wick so water passes through and comes back;
- brief dawn exposure rather than continuous reflected glare;
- the seed’s retained living course filament or a replacement path kept moist; and
- a named custodian empowered to halt transport when circulation fails.

Two people can carry the cradle. One calm draft animal can bear it as a balanced load. A sealed chest, stagnant bucket, dry packing, or rigid iron cage causes a visible decline before it kills the seed: the inner cloud stops returning water, salt forms, the course wanders, or the pulse becomes irregular.

A stormglass or torn knot requires the same care but is not promoted to viable by careful transport alone. Its missing or damaged relationship must be rebuilt.

### The forest east of the verge

The forest does not disappear when ordinary access ends. State the last evidence the characters actually possess.

- If a mature knot remains attached, the crown continues cycling as the roots move onto glass.
- If a demonstrated surplus was separated, the remaining crown and basins settle after the socket closes.
- If the sole mature knot was taken, basin returns weaken, organisms crowd the remaining wet routes, and the crown travels east without the only completed continuation the party observed.
- If a knot was torn free, the linked current visibly falls a state and the crown carries an open scar.
- If runaway fire reaches the eastern lattice, smoke and reflected ignition continue beyond the party's ability to contain them from the salt.
- If people remain aboard, they can continue rescue, cooling, repair, and observation. They are exposed and cut off, not automatically dead.

Do not claim more certainty than the evidence permits. The old account describes one earlier crossing, not the whole Glass Waste.

## Property, repair, and testimony

Within the first day, Hessa's board becomes the basis of a claims record. For every disputed use, list:

- owner;
- material or animal;
- immediate purpose;
- whether the use was life-saving, ecological repair, seed work, transport, or convenience;
- who authorized and witnessed it;
- what remains recoverable; and
- whether the owner ratifies, disputes, forgives, or postpones the claim.

Repairing the soil road does not erase the caravan's accidental damage. It changes the record from unaddressed harm to harm recognized and materially answered. Likewise, a justified emergency use may still leave compensation due.

Sio and Nahl preserve separate records:

- the measured migration and river-course evidence;
- the observed condition of each current and crown knot;
- the exact basis for calling a seed mature or surplus;
- the separation cues and method; and
- uncertainty concerning the forest beyond the verge.

These records matter whether or not a seed was taken.

## Seed condition states

### Mature surplus seed

The seed completed all six currents, separated cleanly, and left another mature knot attached while soil delivery continued.

- **Immediate:** circulation remains stable in a proper cradle.
- **Establishment:** the seed can found a new watershed if Merewash provides a compatible receiving site and maintains the six relationships during planting.
- **Risk:** transport neglect, a false receiving course, fouled water, or an uncontrolled germination burn can still kill it.
- **Forest cost:** no immediate loss of the forest's only demonstrated continuation was observed.

### Mature sole seed

The seed is fully viable and separated cleanly, but no other mature knot remained attached.

- **Immediate:** transport and planting prospects are as strong as the seed's physiology permits.
- **Forest cost:** the party knowingly removed the only completed continuation visible at the crown; eastern basin and crown stress was already observable.
- **Social consequence:** Merewash may receive real relief under a claim that remains morally and politically contestable.
- **Uncertainty:** the forest may form another knot east of the verge, but no evidence at departure established that it would.

### Stormglass seed

All six currents touched the knot, but one remained weak, interrupted, or incorrectly related.

- **Immediate:** the shell may produce mist, a local shower, or a repeating miniature storm.
- **Establishment:** it cannot yet sustain a watershed. The damaged relationship must be diagnosed and rebuilt before the first planting.
- **Risk:** treating spectacular weather as proof of viability can flood one site, salt another, or exhaust the remaining pulse.
- **Value:** it preserves living structure and may be repairable; it is not a worthless failure.

### Torn seed

The knot was removed before the exhale, after hardening, or through a failed support that tore a living feeder.

- **Immediate:** name the inherited injury: lost return, unstable course, weak pulse, burned coat, or dead soil center.
- **Establishment:** repair requires both the missing relationship and treatment of the tear. Simply supplying more water or heat is likely to worsen it.
- **Forest cost:** the crown carries a corresponding scar and linked current loss.
- **Risk:** irregular local weather may continue even if the seed cannot root.

### No seed taken

No rain-seed reaches Merewash.

- **Immediate:** the survivors may still carry exact river measurements, a compatible course, soil and water observations, blackgrass germination knowledge, and evidence of the ninety-year migration.
- **Practical value:** those records can improve wells, storage, overflow planning, and future catchment work without promising miraculous rain.
- **Forest consequence:** every attached mature knot continues east in the state the party left it.
- **Human consequence:** saving the caravan, repairing the road, or refusing an unjustified taking remains a substantive outcome.

## Establishing a viable seed at Merewash

A viable seed does not make rain merely by arriving. It must be received by another living relationship.

### Choose the receiving course

The planting site must materially match the selected remembered course:

- **Fall:** enough elevation change for water to move without scouring the seed bed;
- **Storage:** soil, stone, roots, or constructed basins that retain the first water;
- **Ground:** a substrate that can accept infiltration rather than sealing into salt or clay;
- **Overflow:** a route for excess water that does not destroy homes or fields; and
- **Interval:** a plausible cycle of filling, rest, and release.

A site can be prepared to improve one relation. It cannot be declared compatible because the valley needs it.

### Rebuild the six relationships

Planting requires:

1. living local soil joined to the old-ground core;
2. dawn exposure through leaf, cloth, or an equivalent moderated release rather than Glass-Waste glare;
3. a circulating water loop whose return remains visible;
4. the seed’s retained course filament rooted into the receiving ground;
5. a narrow controlled germination burn around, not through, the wet cradle; and
6. the seed's internal pulse taking hold in new roots.

The seed carries the Bearer's pulse as an inherited beat. Merewash does not need another Bearer, but it must not clamp, pave, or drown the first roots before the beat transfers to the ground.

## Seasonal development at Merewash

These are expected tests, not guaranteed rewards.

### First seven days

A well-received mature seed keeps a stable inner cloud, darkens nearby living soil, and returns slightly more water than is needed to keep its cradle wet. This is establishment evidence, not yet rainfall.

A failing seed shows its defect plainly: salt, stagnant water, wandering condensation, cold sealed coat, dead center, or irregular pulse. Early correction is possible if the missing relationship survives.

### First month

The first roots define a small catchment. Dawn mist and short local showers may follow the selected fall. Storage and overflow must be watched before anyone expands fields or channels.

A mature seed can still be lost here through celebration-driven overwatering, premature channel digging, uncontrolled fire, or treating every damp patch as public surplus.

### First wet season

A successful planting should produce a reliable small watershed: springs, fog capture, or repeated local rain sufficient to stabilize one district, orchard belt, or reservoir system. It does not immediately end a six-year drought across the whole valley.

The first serious overflow is the decisive public test. If the remembered course and receiving site agree, excess water enters the prepared route. If they do not, the community must alter channels and storage rather than blame the seed as capricious.

### First following dry season

The measure of success is retained cycle, not spectacle. A living watershed continues returning some water, keeps its root soil alive, and preserves the course through drought. Merewash must decide how much is reserved for the growing system, human drinking, animals, fields, and downstream obligations.

A politically celebrated seed can still be exhausted by taking every early surplus.

### Third year

Under good care, the watershed may support expansion to neighboring catchments through daughter roots and copied course work. This is the earliest point at which the seed's benefit can plausibly become regional rather than local.

If the planting remains stormglass, the community may still gain intermittent weather and valuable knowledge, but it should not claim a self-sustaining watershed that has not appeared.

## Eastbound aftermath

People who continue with the forest enter another phase of the adventure rather than an epilogue written for them.

Record:

- names and explicit reasons for remaining;
- water, food, shade, tools, animals, and carriers aboard;
- whether the basin, canopy, burn margins, and crown remain usable;
- unresolved people or animals still being sought;
- whether a seed or damaged knot remains reachable;
- the last known western meeting place; and
- what evidence might permit a future return or message.

Jun or any other young traveler is neither a trophy of wonder nor proof that the forest chose someone. Continuing east is a costly human decision under uncertainty.

## Immediate outcome combinations

Several of these may hold together:

- all forty-two travelers accounted for and westbound;
- the caravan divided by informed choice;
- some people or animals unresolved but still aboard;
- a functional caravan core survived without intact wagons;
- the soil road was restored and all seeds left attached;
- a mature surplus seed travels toward Merewash;
- a mature sole seed was taken cleanly;
- a stormglass or torn knot was carried west;
- crown, basin, course, or fire damage continues east;
- Merewash receives only records and methods;
- volunteers continue east with supplies and stated obligations; and
- owners retain compensation or accountability claims.

Do not summarize these as “good,” “mixed,” or “bad.” Name the people, living systems, and obligations that make the state what it is.

## Final campaign record

Preserve:

- the complete traveler and animal disposition;
- every ecological current's final state;
- crown knot count and condition;
- clean versus forced separation and surplus evidence;
- seed custodian, transport method, and receiving plan;
- measured Merewash course and surviving transcription;
- forest injuries and eastbound observers;
- private and company property claims;
- promises of compensation, return, report, or continued search; and
- the first future date on which the seed, watershed, caravan, or eastbound group must be checked again.

### First Merewash decisions

The demonstrated westbound group reaches Merewash with three claims that must not be merged.

- The seed is presently mature and circulating.
- The selected river course is compatible with measured Merewash needs.
- No local establishment site has yet proven all five relations.

Shai therefore recommends a temporary shaded cradle at the reservoir excavation rather than immediate ceremonial planting. Nahl requires local fall, storage, ground, overflow, and interval tests before opening the cradle into Merewash soil.

The settlement may be generous, contentious, or delayed. The important demonstrated fact is that the successful ecological outcome does not erase the ledger.
