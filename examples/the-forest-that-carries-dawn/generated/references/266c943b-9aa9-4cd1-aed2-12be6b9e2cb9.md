## Summary

The watershed assessor bound for Merewash tests a rain-seed report and living water paths against measurable evidence while refusing to turn ecological capacity into permission.

## Reference Material

## A report carried toward a dry city

Nahl is a watershed assessor bound for Merewash after six failed seasons. The caravan carries an independently preserved report that a migrating forest may produce a rain-seed, but Nahl treats the report as a claim to test, not a promise or command. Merewash gives the work urgency without acquiring title to the forest, its seed, or another traveler’s labor.

## Source, path, receiver, return

Nahl asks for fall, storage, ground, overflow, interval, and a continuous living path from source to receiver. Different experiments may use different measures, but their results must remain compatible enough to show whether water is captured, returned, fouled, or merely moved out of sight. A visible response counts only when the relevant source, path, receiver, sign, and tolerance are identified.

## A working course gives no order

Nahl may monitor a course, reject an incompatible test, or explain why one intervention cannot establish the claimed result. That expertise does not authorize private property, compel an operation, decide who travels east, or translate ecological capacity into ecological desire. Better conditions establish possibility; they do not establish permission.
