## Summary

The drought-struck Cairnroad destination lies in clay and limestone above old salt lenses; any carried water course must provide durable storage and safe overflow rather than one destructive flood.

## Reference Material

## Six failed rainy seasons

Merewash lies below a stony northern rise, above old salt lenses, and around clay channels that carry sudden water but crack open through long drought. Its upper springs no longer run through summer, and its surviving wells are deepest west of the settlement. The Cairnroad carries seed grain, copper pipe, reservoir tools, and hired labor toward a place whose need is real before any rain-seed claim is proved.

## Ground that can receive a course

A useful living course must answer four stable questions. Its fall must move water without cutting directly through inhabited clay. It needs a durable storage reach above or within compatible deep ground. Its roots and banks must tolerate Merewash clay, limestone, and old salt. Its overflow must spare the central houses and fields. These receiving conditions test a proposed course; they do not prove that a crown knot is portable, surplus, or available.

## What the Cairnroad may bring

Merewash may receive rescued travelers, grain, tools, labor, copied measurements, a human-readable course record, or a defensibly separated living seed. Those are distinct outcomes. Current cargo, seed condition, custody, establishment, seasonal water, and surviving obligations belong in the play journal and aftermath record rather than this place sheet.
