# The Bearer

## Summary

The immense root animal carries the forest east through traction, venting, one deep exhale, and inward draw; its bodily responses can be accommodated but never treated as speech or consent.

## Reference Material

## A forest carried, not one will

The Bearer is an immense root animal beneath the migrating ecology. Soilbearers, lantern flowers, warm basins, fungal cords, blackgrass, crown stems, people, wagons, and animals all travel across or within its load, but they do not become one intelligence. A bodily response may reveal pressure, cooling, obstruction, or rhythm. It does not turn the forest into a patron, bargain, witness, or sovereign.

## Traction, venting, one deep breath

Traction pulses draw the root mat east. A venting rise sheds heat and admits cooling. One long central exhale slackens the mature crown sockets and opens the only clean separation interval. The inward draw then closes vents, reverses water, and hardens eastern tissue. A preliminary breath may test one gate or burden; forcing repeated responses cannot safely create a second deep exhale.

## Burden without command

The Bearer incorporates old stone, bone, tools, wagon iron, and scars. Some are harmless; others bridge pressure, block a channel, or strain neighboring roots. Support, cooling, load redistribution, removal along an entry path, and rhythm matched to the existing cycle can alter access and favor one load band. They cannot turn the Bearer around, make it desire a seed operation, or authorize anyone’s eastward journey. Current burdens, cooling, favored band, pulse quality, and missed windows remain live state.
