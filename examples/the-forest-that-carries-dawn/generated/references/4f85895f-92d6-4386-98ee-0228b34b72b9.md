## Summary

The caravan’s roster keeper separates sight, report, injury, movement, and informed departure so no person disappears inside “everyone” or “the caravan.”

## Reference Material

## The board across two grain chests

Hessa keeps the forty-two names on a board laid across two grain chests. She distinguishes the people at camp, the missing wagon pockets, the injured, the witnesses, the people moving between regions, and anyone whose route remains unresolved. Her count is exact because rescue and accountability both fail when a category becomes a person.

## Sight, report, and the next mark

Hessa records what each group saw, heard, or learned by report, and where that report came from. A fresh mark is not a living person; a repeated voice is not testimony from the person it resembles; kinship is not consent; survival is not a departure decision. The roster can preserve distinctions. It cannot manufacture facts or choices that no one has established.

## A voice is not a person

In the Hollow of Kept Voices, Hessa keeps the living Tovan distinct from the work-pattern the forest repeats. At the verge she can read unresolved and eastbound names aloud, but the board records decisions only after rescue, current information, and a specific alternative make those decisions informed.
