# Lantern Canopy

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** ecology, light, canopy, moving-region

Glasswings lend stored dawn along wet living stems; severed brilliance still shines, but no longer reaches a crownward receiver.

## Opening View

Six clicks pass from flower to flower. On the last, one pale cup opens and a glasswing moth falls out in a wash of old gold. It circles a severed bloom blazing on the broken bough, then settles on the dim wet stem below. Other cups remain shut around their tapping. Farther east, their seams begin opening too soon.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

The Lantern Canopy hangs above a steep web of living roots. Hundreds of pale cups remain closed around light gathered at the previous dawn. Their seams shine through the green dark. Inside many of them, glasswing moths strike the petals in measured intervals.

Below, broad roots carry people and animals during Long Shade and draw inward thereafter. Wet stems connect some cups to a pollen lattice; dry branches close others in groups. Moths leave opened flowers dusted with bright living pollen and follow click sequences through the canopy. Crownward filaments appear only where stored light, living stem, water, moth, and receiver remain joined.

A cut cup can illuminate a hand all night and still feed nothing. Ordinary reflected light can make every surface brighter without replacing borrowed dawn. Run the region by tracing where the light goes, not by counting how much of it is visible.

## What can be learned without intervention

Observation through one complete leaf-click sequence establishes several facts.

- The leaves click in descending groups of six before an orderly opening.
- Warm rain rises through translucent stems immediately before the moths emerge.
- Moths leave with bright pollen on their wings and follow wet, shaded filaments.
- Pollen settles on barren pale roots but travels over roots darkened by delivered old soil.
- A severed cup on the broken bough shines strongly but sends no living filament.
- Cups exposed to harsh eastern reflection tighten shut or split without releasing pollen in sequence.
- A line of early openings advances from the east as blackgrass heat approaches below.

These facts distinguish captured dawn from ordinary brightness and show why the canopy depends on both soil and circulating water.

## The light current

Use the captured-dawn line in `ECOLOGY-OPERATING-SHEET.md`.

- **Source:** intact cups holding the previous dawn.
- **Path:** glasswing moths, bright pollen, wet stems, and shade.
- **Receiver:** one or more developing crown knots.
- **Visible sign:** sixfold clicks, orderly moth emergence, and pollen traveling through warm rain rather than falling locally.
- **Tolerance:** an intact cup may be rehung or moved a short distance with its stem and root sheath; pollen may be redirected along wet living or prepared thread; cut cups and dried stems do not recover during the adventure.

The canopy begins **working but exposed**. Left alone, enough light reaches the forest's own crown. Smoke, portable-light use, animal damage, dry basin branches, or Glass Waste glare can reduce it. The practical question is which living route remains aligned with the intended knot.

## Running the region

Before moving a branch, answer five questions.

1. Which intact cup branch is being protected, opened, moved, or redirected?
2. Is warm rain reaching its stems?
3. What keeps the branch shaded from smoke and reflected glare?
4. How will the moths receive or imitate the six-click release sequence?
5. Which crownward receiver will the pollen reach, and what proves that it arrived alive?

Climb, imitate signals, move stems, guide moths, build wet pollen lines, rescue an animal, or spend severed light on an immediate human need. Whenever the evidence permits, show the difference between local illumination and ecological delivery before the choice.

## Observation and bounded experiments

### Severed and living light

Compare one already torn cup from the broken bough with an intact cup entering its natural sequence. The torn cup floods the lower lattice with light and fades. The intact cup opens dimly, releases moths, and sends a bright filament away.

**Weak result:** the observers must choose whether to use the dying torn cup for a rescue route before it fades. The distinction is still learned; the remaining local light becomes a practical resource with a deadline.

### Wet-stem test

Lay two pollen-catching threads from the same low branch, one wetted with warm basin water and one dry. Bright grains crawl or wick along the wet line while the dry line gathers inert dust. Living plant fiber works best, but clean flax, hair, fine cloth, magic, or another capillary medium can demonstrate the effect.

**Weak result:** the wet line reaches an unintended root patch. The transport rule is clear; recover the line before the next release or accept a small misdirected branch.

### Shade test

Expose one unopened cup briefly to reflected light with a mirror, polished copper, or lifted shade while keeping a neighboring cup shaded. The exposed seam tightens and may split; the shaded cup continues the click sequence. Ordinary reflected light does not replace stored dawn.

**Weak result:** the exposed cup opens early and is spent. The experiment proves the danger but reduces the branch unless pollen is immediately caught and redirected.

### Click-sequence test

Repeat the descending six-click pattern on leaves or stretched cord near a ready branch. Moths answer only when warm rain is present and the final interval matches the root pulse. A correct imitation can release one branch; an incomplete pattern produces agitation without opening.

**Weak result:** moths leave their roost but do not find an open cup. They gather on the nearest warm surface and can be guided back before smoke or reflection reaches them.

### Receiver test

Mark pollen from one branch with harmless mineral trace or observe its distinct hue. Follow it through the wet bridge. A viable receiver darkens, holds the pollen, and emits a faint crownward glow after the next pulse. Pale untreated roots shed it.

**Weak result:** the marker reaches two receivers. The fork becomes visible. Narrow it, support both receivers, or accept a divided release.

## Intervention families

### Restore the natural release

Return warm rain to dry stems, clear smoke from the cup seams, stabilize the lattice, and let the six-click sequence proceed.

- **Best use:** the canopy is fundamentally intact and only one dependency has been strained.
- **Material:** water return, rope, clean cloth, labor, or a repaired lower support.
- **Persistent cost:** little if the work is timely; delayed stems may miss the intended crown knot even after reopening.
- **Later option:** a natural release keeps the moths available as visible guides toward the descending crown.

### Rehang an intact cup

A cup with its stem sheath and a handspan of living root can be moved to an adjacent wet branch. It must remain shaded and upright. Cutting the sheath converts it into a local lamp rather than a living source.

- **Best use:** a branch is crushed, dry, or aimed at a dead receiver.
- **Material:** two steady climbers or a lifting method, wet wrapping, and a prepared attachment point.
- **Persistent cost:** the donor branch remains reduced; a moved cup can release only once during this migration.
- **Later option:** a correctly rehung cup creates a redundant light path and may make the current strong.

### Guide the moths

Guide glasswings with warm-rain scent, the six-click rhythm, shaded cloth, bright pollen, or a carried roost fragment.

- **Best use:** cups are ready but the normal route is blocked by smoke, animals, or a broken lattice.
- **Material:** little property, but careful timing and exposed movement.
- **Persistent cost:** moths driven through smoke lose scales and carry less pollen; moths drawn to ordinary fire may die.
- **Later option:** guided moths can reveal a crown path or carry pollen across a gap no person can safely bridge.

### Build a wet pollen bridge

Stretch clean flax, hair, fine cloth, living vine, copper mesh, or another capillary line from an opening branch to a working rain stem above living soil. Keep it wet until the release passes.

- **Best use:** the original stem route is broken or points toward a weak receiver.
- **Material:** line, water, anchors, and access to the intended receiver.
- **Persistent cost:** dry fiber catches pollen without transporting it; oil or smoke fouls the line and the branch.
- **Later option:** a successful bridge can route a reduced lantern release into the same knot supported by repaired soil and rain.

### Spend light for rescue

An already severed cup, or a deliberately cut healthy one, provides intense portable light for several minutes. It may make a dangerous climb, medical procedure, or animal extraction possible.

- **Best use:** a named immediate human danger cannot be addressed with reflected or ordinary light.
- **Material:** one cup is spent; a healthy cut also severs its moth path.
- **Consent:** no human owns the cup, but the ecological cost should be stated as clearly as a spent planter or cut wagon.
- **Later option:** the rescued people or animals may provide labor worth more than the lost branch. The loss is still recorded rather than morally erased.

## Human pressure and negotiation

Light looks abundant here, which makes spending it easy to misname. A closed cup can guide a trapped climber or calm a draft animal before it ever reaches the crown. Della Myr will lend wet cloth, planters, and careful hands for a living repair; she will not accept “the forest needs it” as an ownerless claim on medicine.

Nahl asks where the light travels after release. The property recorder asks what has been cut. A character testing the ecology asks whether a bright intervention still leaves a living filament. Survivors under immediate threat may justify spending a cup that the seed work needed. Record that as a rescue cost, not as a failed puzzle or a moral stain.

## Animal complication

If Moth and Reed have not been recovered at the Warm Rain Basins, one or both may enter the lower lattice at Red Dusk.

- Moth follows rhythmic clicks and can be guided along a broad branch.
- Reed shies from upward rain and may lunge toward a dry support.

Calm, lead, or temporarily tether them. Driving them hard succeeds in moving them but crushes one lower stem and leaves the team strained. Letting them find their own route preserves the canopy but may carry them east beyond the next ordinary crossing. A calm recovered team can later lift a rehung cup assembly, carry wet line, or move injured travelers.

## Smoke, heat, and reflection

Three different light hazards should remain distinct.

- **Smoke** coats seams and moth wings. It can be cleared with clean moving air or washed from lower stems before opening.
- **Blackgrass warmth** advances from the east and triggers early opening. Cooling the lower stems delays it; total chilling may prevent opening during the proper sequence.
- **Glass reflection** is harsh ordinary light. Shade, angle, bark screens, mud, or cloth can block it. Reflecting more of it into the cups does not replenish captured dawn.

At Root Night, the natural release is nearly complete. At Glass Dawn, unopened cups harden or split and no longer provide a living current.

## Damage and repair states

Record each lantern branch as **orderly**, **early**, **dark**, or **destroyed**.

- **Orderly:** warm rain, click sequence, moth emergence, and living pollen path all function.
- **Early:** the cup opened before its receiver was ready. Pollen may still be caught and redirected if wet soil and rain are already in place.
- **Dark:** the cup remains intact but lacks rain, moth access, or a viable receiver. Restoring the missing relation before Root Night can recover it.
- **Destroyed:** the cup or stem sheath was cut, crushed, burned, or dried beyond recovery during the adventure.

The overall current is absent, weak, working, or strong according to how many viable branches reach the chosen receiver and whether a redundant path exists. Do not count flowers mechanically; describe whether the intended knot receives no living dawn, a late or reduced thread, an ordinary complete release, or a protected surplus.

## Stage changes

### Long Shade

The lattice is broad and mostly shaded. The broken bough provides safe comparative evidence. Intact cups can be rehung with ordinary climbing and heavy support.

### Red Dusk

The six-click sequences begin. Smoke and blackgrass warmth approach from the east. The lower lattice narrows, and unrecovered draft animals may enter. This is the best stage for release, redirection, and rescue-light decisions.

### Root Night

Most cups have opened or gone dark. Pollen bridges remain useful, but moving intact cups is difficult. Following glasswings becomes one of the clearest routes toward the descending crown.

### Glass Dawn

Reflected light floods the exposed canopy. Emptied flowers fold; unopened flowers harden or split. Local severed light may remain for moments, but the ecological release is over.

## Results and later options

- **Absent:** no living pollen reaches a viable receiver. Ordinary light cannot replace the current.
- **Weak:** an early, reduced, smoky, or divided release reaches the crown. Strong working rain and soil may keep one knot viable at a later cost.
- **Working:** one complete living release reaches the intended knot over wet, living ground.
- **Strong:** a protected main path and a redundant branch reach the same or compatible receivers. The current can absorb one later strain, expose the moth route to the crown, or support a marginal soil or rain delivery without replacing an absent relationship.

Show the result through moth behavior, pollen movement, and receiver response. The flowers thank, accuse, and authorize no one.

## Late access and moving evidence

Once the lower lattice folds, glasswings, wet pollen, and intact cups may move inward even when ordinary climbing access is gone. A living pollen line or an intact cup still connected to wet tissue can continue the captured-dawn current at the crown. A cut cup may demonstrate the difference between stored light and a living path, but it cannot become that path.

Record a clue carried by moth, pollen, or cup as Lantern Canopy evidence and state how the carrier reached the group. After the natural release ends, no ordinary flame, mirror, spell, or sunrise reflection replaces missed captured dawn. Light already transferred inward remains part of the established state.
