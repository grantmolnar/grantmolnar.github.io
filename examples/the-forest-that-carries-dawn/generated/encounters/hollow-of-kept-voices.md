# Hollow of Kept Voices

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** ecology, memory, river-course, moving-region

Fungal cords keep gradients, storage, ground, and overflow after names decay, allowing a defensible river course without mistaking memory for a surviving person.

## Opening View

“Twelve steps,” says a child behind the fungal curtain. The ford is twelve steps wide. A white cord repeats the measure in an old man’s voice, then in words nobody knows; slope and interval survive every change. From deeper in the hollow, Tovan Clay gives the right route home. Asked his own name, he answers with the depth of a well.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

The Hollow of Kept Voices lies where warm rain seeps through curtains of pale fungus. Wet a cord and it speaks: a slope counted in steps, the depth of a well, the taste of stone, the interval between flood marks. The same measure may return in another language or another familiar cadence.

The cords do not contain the dead. They keep associations among sound, moisture, mineral taste, distance, and route. Personal details fray first. Names and relationships exchange places. Depth, fall, color, storage, and interval persist because those were the repeated measures that once guided water.

At Long Shade the hollow is cool and sparse. Red Dusk separates the cord families with warm rain. During Root Night the chamber speaks in layers: biography becomes least reliable just as compatible river measures become easiest to compare. At Glass Dawn, reflected heat dries the outer curtains and leaves only the deepest wet branches active.

## What the cords retain

Read each useful fragment as a measurement attached to a physical condition. Voice is the pattern’s carrier, not its preserved subject.

| Measure family | What a usable fragment provides | What changes between repetitions | What makes it relevant to Merewash |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fall | A sequence of rise, drop, and distance | Speaker, place-name, unit, or metaphor | Whether water can move from the proposed head to inhabited ground without cutting a destructive gorge |
| Storage | A basin, marsh, deep soil, snow pocket, or shaded reach that holds seasonal water | Local plants, season names, and exact duration | Whether the first watershed can survive a dry interval instead of releasing one flood and failing |
| Ground | Soil color, mineral taste, root response, salinity, and bank behavior | Familiar nouns and comparisons | Whether the course can take hold in Merewash clay, limestone, old salt, and planted ground |
| Overflow | A second channel, floodplain, spill height, or sacrificial reach | Human landmarks and household references | Whether exceptional rain has somewhere to go without destroying the settlement it is meant to save |
| Interval | Repeated travel time, pulse count, bend spacing, or sequence of wet and dry reaches | Voice rhythm and counting form | Whether separate fragments belong to one coherent course rather than several incompatible rivers |

A viable selected course needs a coherent **fall**, **storage**, **ground**, and **overflow** relationship. Interval evidence confirms that the fragments belong together and can strengthen the current, but it cannot substitute for a missing safety or storage feature.

## Opening a cord family

A cord family is a set of strands that brighten, tighten, and speak under the same water temperature and mineral trace. Opening one should be an observation before it becomes an extraction.

1. **Establish the quiet baseline.** Listen to a dry or cool cord. It may whisper a cadence, but it should not provide a complete route.
2. **Choose one measured feed.** Admit a cup, gutter, or capillary thread of working warm rain. Mark the source and preserve a return path.
3. **Mark what remains stable.** Record numbers, relative distances, colors, tastes, slopes, and repeated transitions rather than transcribing every sentence.
4. **Change one condition.** Warm or cool the feed slightly, move it to a neighboring strand, or add a harmless mineral marker. A real relation changes predictably.
5. **Check a second family.** One vivid voice is not enough. Compare at least one independent strand before treating a fragment as a course feature.
6. **Return or continue the water.** A selected living course needs circulation. Water pooled in the hollow produces volume, not clarity.

Once the setup is clear, state what the cord preserves. Use a system check for speed, exposure, water loss, or whether a second family remains reachable before Root Night.

## Testing whether a voice is a person

The hollow offers several material tests. Put evidence before metaphysics.

- Ask a fresh repeated voice a present-tense question it would understand. Tovan Clay's roster call can repeat the missing names and their order, but it cannot answer Hessa's new question or update the location after the wagon pocket moves.
- Listen to one measurement through several cord families. The same flood depth may appear in three voices, languages, or counting systems.
- Repeat the same feed after the hollow warms. Slope and stone color remain while kinship, names, and emotional framing drift.
- Redirect a narrow water channel. The spoken pattern changes with the flow, even when no listener addresses it.
- Compare a remembered route with current terrain at the Glass Verge. A convincing guide may direct listeners toward a bend that ceased to exist long ago.

Let grief, hope, and speech remain possible. The boundary is practical: the fragment cannot consent, testify about events it did not observe, reveal current danger, absolve anyone, or make decisions for the dead.

## Choosing a river course

Nahl Reed can reject obviously incompatible courses, not identify one perfect answer. Build a defensible course from evidence.

Nahl spreads Merewash's notes beneath the cords: inhabited clay, limestone, old salt, and deep western wells. The hollow need not reproduce one remembered river. It must supply a course this ground can receive, tested through four questions:

1. **Where does the first water fall?** The fall must be enough to move, but not so steep that it cuts directly through inhabited clay.
2. **Where is water held?** The course needs one seasonal storage reach above the settlement or within deep compatible ground.
3. **What ground receives it?** A course associated only with peat, ice, or fresh volcanic soil may be accurate and still unsuitable.
4. **Where does excess go?** The course needs an overflow that does not run through Merewash's central fields and houses.

A course does not have to reproduce an old river exactly. The fungal current supplies a tendency: a sequence of gradients, storage behaviors, ground associations, and overflow responses around which the rain-seed can organize its first watershed.

### Living braid

The most reliable method is to bring three or more compatible cord families into one wet braid without cutting them from their surrounding network. Route a measured rain feed through the chosen strands, keep incompatible branches physically separate, and carry the joined outflow toward a crownward root channel.

- **Requirements:** working warm rain, at least four compatible measure families, a continuous living path, and someone checking the source and the crownward receiver.
- **Visible result:** the braid repeats shorter and shorter fragments until only distances, gradients, ground signs, and overflow intervals remain. A pale pulse travels crownward through the outflow.
- **Persistent cost:** the selected branch becomes temporarily quiet behind the transfer and cannot support another full course during this migration.
- **Later option:** the crown can organize a weather-knot around a working river tendency, and Nahl can defend why that tendency suits Merewash.

### Wet transcription

Copy stable measurements onto a board, cord gauge, marked pipe, knotted line, or field sheet while the branch remains alive.

- **Requirements:** patient comparison and at least two independent repetitions of each copied feature.
- **Visible result:** the record stays intelligible after the voice changes.
- **Persistent cost:** transcription consumes time and may miss a late-opening family.
- **Later option:** Merewash receives a human-readable record even if no seed is taken. The copy does not itself supply the living memory current to the crown.

### Cut living branch

A cord can be cut and carried wet, but severing it strips away neighboring relations.

- **Requirements:** a sealed wet wrap and a clear choice about which family is being sacrificed.
- **Visible result:** the cut branch repeats one short sequence with great clarity, then begins substituting voice and interval.
- **Persistent cost:** the source family is broken and the carried current is weak at best.
- **Later option:** a difficult route can still carry partial course evidence, but the crown may form a smaller or unstable watershed pattern.

### Flood the hollow

Opening several basin feeds at once wakes almost every cord.

- **Visible result:** the hollow becomes loud enough to feel through bone and root; many valid measures overlap with incompatible courses.
- **Persistent cost:** warm rain pools instead of returning, distinctions wash out, and the cords may remain weak after drainage.
- **Later option:** Several historic courses become undeniable, while selection becomes slower and less precise. Flooding is no shortcut to a perfect answer.

## Current states

Record the remembered river course as one of the six ecological current states.

- **Absent:** no living selected branch reaches the crown, or all available branches have been dried, scorched, severed, or mixed beyond distinction.
- **Weak:** a stable fall and ground relation reach the crown, but storage or overflow remains missing, contradictory, or dependent on a severed branch.
- **Working:** coherent fall, storage, ground, and overflow relations travel through a living wet path to the crown.
- **Strong:** the working course also has interval confirmation, a redundant compatible family, or a complete human transcription. It can absorb one later strain such as a shortened transfer or one damaged strand.

A strong memory current cannot replace absent rain. The cords require living circulation to speak and to transfer their pattern.

## People in the hollow

**Nahl Reed** marks fall, storage, ground, overflow, and interval beside every braid. A beautiful familiar voice that fails one mark remains evidence, not a course.

**Sio Tern** taps the inherited measure against each returning cadence. When slope and interval hold while kinship changes, Sio keeps the measure and lets the intimacy drift.

**Hessa Clay** may hear Tovan's fresh roster call before he reaches camp. Once Tovan is safe, she can place the living man and the repeated work side by side: the cord kept how he called, not the person who called.

**Jun Alder** listens where leaf-clicks and cord-counts disagree. A useful measure surviving its maker makes east imaginable; Jun still has no road until rescue, supplies, danger, and a western alternative are named.

A player character may hear a familiar cadence. Ask for one practical thing that person repeated—a flood mark, a well depth, a weather sign, a field boundary, a road interval. Keep the observation. Let private detail drift. The hollow has no final message to deliver.

## Complications and failure-forward consequences

- A selected course is accurate but associated with ground unlike Merewash. The party must seek another family or accept a weak current.
- A wet branch crosses the safest return route. Preserving the living transfer makes movement harder rather than impossible.
- A character follows a familiar voice into a tightening cord loop. Rescue may break one strand, delay selection, or require another person to hold the route.
- Fire heat reaches an outer cord. The party can cool the edge, sacrifice the affected family, or move the selected braid sooner.
- Flooded voices produce two plausible overflows. The party can compare interval evidence, copy both for Merewash, or choose one and record the uncertainty.
- A cut branch remains clear enough to use but cannot carry all four required relationships. It creates a weak course rather than no course.

Leave a legible record of what remains true. A mistaken choice alters the course; it does not summon an unrelated curse or a lying ghost.

## Stage changes

- **Long Shade:** one voice at a time can be distinguished, but several important families remain dry.
- **Red Dusk:** warm rain exposes the network and allows careful cross-comparison.
- **Root Night:** course relations become vivid, personal details blur quickly, and the living braid can reach the descending crown.
- **Glass Dawn:** outer cords dry and crack. A course already transferred remains; an unselected course becomes inaccessible from ordinary routes.

## Results and later options

Record more than “the voices are not ghosts”:

- which measure families were observed;
- which familiar voices appeared and what practical observation each carried;
- which present-tense or flow test demonstrated the hollow's limits;
- which course was selected and why it suits Merewash;
- whether the transfer path is absent, weak, working, or strong;
- what warm rain was returned, pooled, spent, or fouled;
- whether a human transcription survives; and
- which route remains open from the hollow toward the chamber, crown, or exit.

The hollow offers evidence, a living course, and difficult grief—never permission from the dead.

## Late access and moving evidence

After the outer cords become inaccessible, only a living braid already connected to water or a surviving transcription carries the selected course westward. The living braid can supply the remembered-course current. A transcription preserves measured fall, storage, ground, overflow, and interval relations as evidence and a construction template, but it is not itself living memory.

A clue heard through a carried braid remains sourced to the Hollow of Kept Voices. Personal details that washed out near dawn cannot be reconstructed from the stable measurements, and a late cadence does not become a whole person merely because it is emotionally recognizable.
