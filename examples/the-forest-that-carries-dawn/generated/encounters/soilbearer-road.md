# Soilbearer Road

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** ecology, repair, soil, moving-region

Beetles carry living ground across oil, iron, and closing gates; a repair must bear two-way traffic, preserve the substrate, and survive the coming burn.

## Opening View

The road is no road until the beetles arrive. Laden ones stream east; empty ones return along the margins. At the wagon scar, the procession folds around a shining band of spice oil and a gate crushed under iron. One beetle tests the slick, retreats, and begins pushing its dark pellet toward the only hinge that still moves. Beyond it, a pale root waits.

## Encounter Material

## Situation

The Soilbearer Road appears when the beetles move. Fist-sized, bronze-green, and nearly silent, they travel east with thumb-sized pellets of living ground. Empty beetles return along both margins. At the wagon scar, the two streams lose their order: spice oil cuts the upper track, iron rims have crushed the road lip, and salt and scale jam two gates that should open one after the other.

The western mounds hold five old landscapes—pine duff, river clay, black marsh, shell sand, and red upland loam. The upper-wheel spill is slick with oil and broken grain. Beneath the copper wagon, the split gate still flexes under its packed hinges. Farther east, pale receiver roots darken wherever a pellet arrives; then warm rain enters them and lantern pollen catches on their wet skin.

Pelen Marr can read the broken frame and moving load. Nahl Reed can tell living substrate from a dark dead substitute. Either may be absent. No designated expert is required. Watch what the beetles avoid, what the gates answer, and whether delivered ground changes the receiver.

## What can be learned without intervention

A careful circuit of the region establishes the following facts without a roll.

- Empty beetles travel west and laden beetles travel east. They are carriers, not consumers of the forest.
- The five mound soils contain living fungal threads, seeds, shells, and mineral grains from different landscapes.
- Roots receiving regular deliveries are dark, flexible, and wet. Deprived roots are pale and brittle.
- Warm rain crosses ground mixed by the beetles but beads and runs away from untreated White Salt.
- Lantern pollen settles in bright lines only above living delivered soil.
- Crownward gates begin to open when the central mound exhales warm air, then close in the same order.
- Soil from an earlier narrow burn scar is darker and more active than soil beyond it.

These observations reveal relationships. They do not by themselves establish how much soil the crown can spare or whether a repair will survive the approaching fire.

## The road's current

Use the soil line in `ECOLOGY-OPERATING-SHEET.md`.

- **Source:** the five old-ground mounds.
- **Path:** loaded soilbearers, the polished road, and the paired root gates.
- **Receiver:** the eastern crownward roots.
- **Visible sign:** beetles cross under burden, gates open on the exhale, receiving roots darken, and translucent soil mist rises.
- **Tolerance:** one shallow tray may be taken from a mound without breaking its continuity; pellets survive shade, moisture, and ordinary handling but not oil, sterile salt, crushing, or prolonged heat.

The road begins **weak**. Some beetles detour, enough for continuation; too little old ground reaches the crown in time for a defensible surplus unless the path improves or an equivalent living route replaces it.

## Running the region

Before committing material, answer five questions.

1. Which break is being addressed: oil, structure, gate timing, or heat protection?
2. What surface will bear two opposing beetle streams through a root pulse?
3. Where does the living substrate come from, and how is it kept moist and unpoisoned?
4. How will beetles recognize the continuation of the road?
5. What will happen when the blackgrass heat reaches the scar?

Resolve them separately and in any order. A later solution may compensate for an earlier partial result.

## Observation and bounded experiments

Several bounded tests are available. Each takes one arriving beetle stream or one Bearer exhale unless an appropriate ability accelerates it.

### Scent-water fork

Place two short surfaces beside the surviving road, wet one with water that has run over living pellets, and leave the other dry. Empty beetles inspect both; laden beetles follow the scented branch if its grade and temperature are tolerable. This proves that the route can be redirected without handling every animal.

**Weak result:** the branch attracts beetles but is too steep or hot under burden. The failed limit becomes visible, and dropped pellets remain recoverable living material until oil or salt reaches them.

### Shade and heat test

Place equal living pellets under shaded canvas and on exposed board through one warm pulse. Healthy pellets remain springy and threaded; overheated pellets crust and no longer open receiver rootlets. This establishes why a bright, dry bridge can appear successful while delivering dead substrate.

**Weak result:** wind or a root shift takes one sample into the scar. The experiment still distinguishes the conditions, but one small portion is spent or fouled.

### Gate-timing test

Mark the paired gates and watch a complete exhale. The first hinge loosens as warm air rises; the second opens only when the first stream crosses. A pole, rope, wedge, spell, or counterweight can hold the first gate long enough for a reduced stream to trigger the second.

**Weak result:** the held gate closes behind the operators, separating them east of the scar until the next pulse. The gate pattern is learned; the cost is route exposure and time.

### Living-ground comparison

Offer equal amounts of old mound soil, medicinal planter soil, and darkened sterile salt to receiver rootlets. Old mound soil is accepted fastest. A healthy medicinal planter works more slowly. Dyed or damp salt is refused. This distinguishes biological function from appearance.

**Weak result:** the rootlets close around the viable sample before it can be recovered. The knowledge is secure, but that sample is spent in the receiver.

## Repair families

Other methods are possible. These are legible families, not prescribed solutions.

### Shaded wagon-bed bypass

Untreated wagon boards or a dismantled bed bridge the crushed lip. Canvas below or above maintains shade and catches dropped pellets. A shallow layer of old ground or planter soil connects the surviving scent trace.

- **Best use:** stable structural repair with recoverable caravan material.
- **Consent:** company boards are available through Orra; private frames require an owner or a defensible emergency rule.
- **Persistent cost:** dry canvas becomes dangerous when the burn arrives unless removed, wetted, buried, or shielded.
- **Later option:** a broad working road can also carry hand loads and one calm draft animal toward the crown at Root Night.

### Copper scent-rill

Uncut copper pipe or opened trough sections carry warm scent-water across the bad surface. Beetles walk beside the rill while the water keeps pellets cool and marks the receiving gate.

- **Best use:** contaminated or irregular ground where a full deck would be costly.
- **Consent:** the pipe belongs to Ira Senn. An uncut returnable run is a different proposal from hammered troughs or abandoned sections.
- **Persistent cost:** copper left against tightening roots may kink and become unrecoverable after Red Dusk.
- **Later option:** a working rill can become a return line for basin water or a cooling margin during the burn.

### Hand ferry and gate crew

Travelers collect dropped pellets on shallow trays, carry them around the spill, and feed them into an opened gate. This can restore delivery without a continuous beetle road.

- **Best use:** late rescue-first routes or parties with abundant labor but little material.
- **Consent:** no private ecological material is required, but the work competes directly with departure and treatment labor.
- **Persistent cost:** handled pellets are more easily crushed or overheated; the stream remains dependent on people.
- **Later option:** a successful manual delivery can raise the road from weak to working but rarely to strong.

### Root-gate bypass

Open a new receiver slit in dark roots beyond the crushed gates with pressure, moisture, careful cutting, magic, or a temporary wedge. Beetles need not use the old gate if the new path responds to the same exhale.

- **Best use:** the road surface is viable but the gate cannot be restored in time.
- **Consent:** little caravan property is needed.
- **Persistent cost:** a cut made across the root grain leaks living mist and may close permanently after one stream.
- **Later option:** a clean new slit can reveal the Bearer's pulse and provide a narrow access toward the Root-Breath Chamber.

## Testing a repair

Every repair receives the same three-part test.

1. **Approach:** Do empty beetles enter the new surface without circling or turning back?
2. **Burden:** Can laden beetles cross without dropping, crushing, or overheating their pellets?
3. **Gate:** Do crownward roots recognize the arriving stream and open on the Bearer's exhale?

State each result separately.

- Passing **approach only** creates a recognizable but nonfunctional detour.
- Passing **approach and burden** delivers living soil to a closed receiver; manual gate work can still complete the current.
- Passing **approach and gate** but failing burden delivers dead or damaged substrate and does not create surplus.
- Passing all three makes the current **working**.
- Passing all three with a protected return stream, intact mound continuity, and burn preparation makes it **strong**.

A failed test reveals the failed condition. It may strain animals, cost material, close a route, advance the stage, or foul a bounded patch; it never erases the work or makes the beetles inexplicably hostile.

## Borrowing and damage

### Sustainable use

A shallow tray from any one mound leaves that mound intact. Small samples from several mounds are also sustainable if the fibrous roots remain covered and moist. Beetle pellets that have been dropped but not fouled may be returned to the current.

### Spending

A mound stripped until its binding roots show is broken for this migration. Its soil may still support the repair, but that line of old-ground continuity no longer travels east. A medicinal planter emptied into the road is spent from later treatment even if the soil survives ecologically.

### Fouling

Spice oil, axle grease, soap, sterile salt, and prolonged bare heat foul living soil without removing it. Covering fouled ground does not make it viable. Remove it from the active surface, isolate it behind a raised bridge, or route around it.

### Structural damage

Iron left across the road bruises beetles and root gates through every pulse. A repaired bridge that still shifts under burden keeps the road weak. A bridge that settles harmlessly into the root rhythm can remain after the caravan leaves.

## Fire preparation

At Red Dusk the blackgrass heat reaches the scar. The full burn can wait, but the soil repair must already have a way to meet it.

- **Wet margin:** return basin water along both sides of the route.
- **Bare mineral break:** expose a narrow strip of nonliving stone or clean salt without letting it contaminate the carried soil.
- **Living scar:** route the stream through the earlier burn line, where the roots already tolerate bounded heat.
- **Removable bridge:** finish delivery, then lift canvas and dry boards before the flame arrives.
- **Heat conductor:** use pipe, wet clay, or another medium to bring warmth to the receiver without letting flame cross the road.

Total smothering protects the road but may prevent the later seed coat from opening. Unbounded oil or canvas fire may make the soil current absent. Record the preparation rather than resolving the burn in advance.

## Stage changes

### Long Shade

Both scar sites are reachable. Intact wagon beds, heavy pipe, planter soil, and draft teams can be brought to the road. The old-ground mounds remain low and cool.

### Red Dusk

The beetle detour lengthens. The first blackgrass warmth reaches the upper-wheel oil. Heavy material must already be in place or arrive by a working animal route. Mounds begin lifting away from the western scar.

### Root Night

The gates climb crownward and open only on the deeper breath. A working road becomes a narrow route for people, trays, or one calm animal; an untested bridge is too dangerous for heavy loads. Manual ferry remains possible but exposes labor to the closing root bands.

### Glass Dawn

Old-ground delivery is complete or missed. Remaining western mounds fold into the moving root mat. The result remains legible at the crown, but no new full road can be built on the Glass Waste.

## Results and later options

Record the soil current as absent, weak, working, or strong.

- **Absent:** no living old ground reaches the receiver. The forest may preserve an earlier knot, but a new defensible surplus cannot be attributed to the party's repair.
- **Weak:** late, reduced, or manually delivered soil reaches the crown. The ordinary migration can continue; later strong rain or light may keep one marginal knot viable, but there is no clear surplus yet.
- **Working:** living soil reaches the crown before the gates climb. The convergence can form normally if the other currents work.
- **Strong:** the route carries a protected surplus, retains living mound continuity, and survives or bypasses the burn. Additional translucent bladders rise along the road, a crownward service path remains open at Root Night, and more than bare continuation becomes physically demonstrable.

Show beetles continuing east, roots darkening, mist rising beyond the first receiver, and later organisms using the repaired ground. None of those signs is approval.

## Late access and moving evidence

After heavy access closes, only materials already staged, a maintained hand ferry, or a root gate opening on the Bearer's breath can alter the road. Do not permit a new wagon-bed bypass to appear during Root Night unless its pieces and labor were physically placed before the detour climbed.

Soilbearers, old-ground mist, and mound samples can carry road evidence crownward. A clue discovered from them remains sourced to the Soilbearer Road. If the scar itself is burned or folded before it was examined, its unobserved evidence is gone unless the same authored clue is still materially visible in the moving stream. At Glass Dawn old-ground delivery has succeeded, weakened, or failed; no new western road repair begins.
