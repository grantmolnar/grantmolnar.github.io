# Camp Under New Leaves

**Role:** necessary, start  
**Tags:** start, caravan, rescue, moving-region

Hessa's roster turns a stranded camp into thirteen named rescues, two westward routes, and a commission that must be renewed before seed work begins.

## Opening View

Hessa Clay calls thirteen names into the clicking leaves. Four answers come back from four directions; the ninth name gets only a mule’s bray. On her board, twenty-nine people are marked in camp, six wagons lean in roots that grew during the night, and White Salt slides west beneath them. Orra Venn taps the unanswered column. “Bring me news while it is still true.”

## Encounter Material

## Situation

The **Cairnroad caravan** bedded down on bare White Salt. At the middle watch, the ground answered with a long shudder. Bells rang without wind. A dark line crossed the stars. Now six wagons sit in a bowl of braided roots, canvas is snagged high among wet branches, and the western salt moves backward beneath the camp because the whole forest is walking east.

Forty-two people began the night here. Twenty-nine answer the first count. Five wagons and three draft teams are gone. Far off, a mule brays; a human voice answers it and then is swallowed by the clicking leaves.

**Orra Venn** starts with names. She has twenty-nine people in sight, four voices heard beyond the camp, six people believed to be with named wagons, and three whose location is unknown. She refuses the phrases “the missing” and “the survivors” whenever a more exact list can be made.

**Hessa Clay** lays the largest serving board across two grain chests and marks every name: present and able, present and injured, heard but unreached, believed with a wagon, missing without location, or departed after hearing the available choices. When someone says “everyone,” Hessa turns the board around.

**Nahl Reed** sets a closed shining flower between a twice-translated watershed report and Sio's six-line song. One claims a portable rain-seed; the other names dawn, warm rain, memory, fire, and glass. Neither supplies a route, and nobody present has seen the seed. Nahl asks for source, path, receiver, and visible return.

The Cairnroad runs west and south toward drought-struck Merewash with grain, pipe, tools, and labor. The forest bears it east toward the Glass Waste. West returns toward the intended road; east accepts another journey.

For fresh play, the adventurers occupy or replace an equal number of present-and-able roster entries. Do not add them on top of the forty-two. Keep the opening total and location categories fixed; do not invent a surprise survivor, erase an inconvenient injury, or move a missing person to simplify the night.

## Why the party is sent

Orra needs people who can leave the camp, return, and distinguish observation from hope. The adventurers may have been hired for different reasons and may barely know one another. She gives them three immediate tasks:

1. establish where the missing people and draft teams went;
2. identify at least two routes by which people can leave the moving forest; and
3. determine whether the forest's eastward movement will stop before it reaches the shining ground visible at the horizon.

Orra's commission is narrow. She can assign caravan labor and release company property. She cannot spend privately owned cargo without its owner, compel someone to pursue the rain-seed, or declare that a missing person has accepted a dangerous route.

## The first physical facts

A careful look establishes several facts without a roll.

- The root mat moves in pulses separated by stillness. The pulses are not footsteps. Long root bands tighten in sequence, drawing the whole ecology east.
- Fine white dust on the western bark proves the forest crossed miles of exposed salt during the night.
- Dew falls upward from lower leaves toward warm channels in the canopy.
- Small bronze-green beetles carry pellets of dark soil in the same direction as the forest.
- A thread of smoke lies far to the east. It is moving across the forest rather than rising from one fixed blaze.
- The eastern horizon shines like water, but no water appears on Orra's route maps. Sio Tern identifies it as the Glass Waste.

The forest is not silent. Leaves click in repeating sequences. Root cavities exhale warm air. Far away, one of the missing mules brays and receives a human answer.

## Immediate hazards

The camp is stable only during the first migration stage, **Long Shade**. The outer roots are already drawing inward. Problems can be handled in any order, but delay changes them.

- A suspended canvas holds **Lio Darn** and **Sen Voss** above a widening root seam. Lio can move with help after splinting; Sen must be lowered before they can safely walk.
- A grain wagon has split and is feeding pale rootlets that curl away from metal fittings. Its boards are immediately useful, but the seed grain is part of Merewash's failing reserve.
- **Thistle**, a draft mare from a surviving team, is loose. She follows Mara Doss's voice but kicks at soilbearers. Calming her provides an early animal litter; driving her hard leaves her strained at the finale.
- Several thirsty travelers have found warm rain dripping from a channel and want to cut it open. Nahl can test the flow, but no one yet knows whether the channel replenishes.
- Rusk Vale, Ira Senn, and Corven Ash disagree over whether to abandon the surviving wagons. Each will spend property under some conditions; none has authority over the others' cargo.

Saving people may cost tools; preserving tools may make later rescue or repair possible; water taken now may weaken an unknown circulation. State every visible cost before the choice when the characters can perceive it.

## Camp operating sequence

Run the camp as a working hub rather than a council scene that settles the adventure in advance.

1. **Count:** Hessa updates names and functional movement states: walking, assisted, carried, stabilize first, heard but unreached, believed with a named wagon, missing without location, or departed by informed choice.
2. **Treat:** Vey Dorr states what the injured need and what movement will worsen. Treatment creates capacity; it does not automatically decide where a person goes.
3. **Route:** Name one route recorder. They record where ordinary ground lies, what burdens a route can take, and what visible change will close it.
4. **Assign:** Orra asks for one named operation at a time. She can release company stores and labor but cannot conscript a speculative seed party.
5. **Record property:** Name one property recorder. They identify the owner of any proposed material. Immediate life-saving use may proceed under necessity; repair, investigation, and seed work require consent or a rule the party is willing to defend later.
6. **Carry the plan:** Bera Holt or another messenger takes revised routes and risks to separated groups. A plan is not accepted merely because it was announced at camp.

After each substantial intervention or major delay, show a migration sign before advancing the stage: outer roots draw inward, wagon bells shift position, upward dew becomes warm rain, or the bright eastern line climbs the underside of the leaves. Advance the stage when the forest moves: outer routes narrow, basins climb, the burn arrives, or eastern tissue hardens.

## Available people and materials

The camp can support several efforts, but not all at full strength.

- Vey and Cal can staff treatment or accompany one difficult extraction, not both.
- Bera and two uninjured route hands can maintain reports while the remaining teamsters handle animals.
- Company rope, canvas, common tools, and fittings are available on Orra's authority.
- Five medicinal planters contain living soil. Della will surrender one after a demonstrated need; taking more threatens later treatment.
- Copper pipe, seed grain, spice, oil, masonry tools, reservoir tools, and private wagon shares retain named owners.
- Thistle can carry one litter or two balanced loads once calmed.

When scarce people or material move to one operation, state which other operation loses support.

## Sio's six carried lines

**Sio Tern** writes six remembered lines beside Nahl's report, learned from a grandparent who survived the previous crossing:

> Old ground walks under the green.
> Dawn is kept where noon cannot reach.
> Warm rain climbs before it falls.
> What the dead remembered becomes a river.
> Fire opens what roots must loose.
> Leave before the road turns to glass.

Sio does not know which phrases are literal. They are embarrassed by how useful the fragment appears and more embarrassed by how easily desperate people may treat it as complete instruction. If pressed, Sio insists that every line be tested against something material.

## Nahl's copied claim

After six failed rains, Merewash's upper springs no longer run through summer. Nahl's account says a mature “rain-seed” from an earlier migration began a northern tributary. It does not say every knot can travel, the forest can spare one, or the caravan's people and goods belong to seed work.

Nahl asks for no immediate seed expedition. They state three uncertainties:

- no one present has seen a living rain-seed;
- the account may describe a complete ecological process rather than a portable object; and
- taking the wrong thing could kill part of the forest without helping Merewash.

The possibility matters because this crossing will not recur within the lifetime of anyone on the roster. The immediate duty to find people remains.

## Renewing the commission

Orra's first commission covers rescue, routes, and the dawn deadline. It does not silently expand when the rain-seed becomes plausible. Once at least one usable western route has been reported and every missing name is either physically reached or assigned to an active rescue, Orra calls a work muster rather than a binding vote.

At the muster, record four things separately: who intends to leave on the known route, who remains for unfinished rescue, who volunteers for ecological repair or investigation, and which owners permit their material to be used. Orra may keep assigning company labor one operation at a time. Authority for seed work comes from named volunteers and specific consent. A rescue-only departure is complete if nobody accepts the later burden.

## Using the camp

The camp is both information hub and exit staging ground. Change it as groups return.

Record:

- the exact roster state;
- which people have heard which proposed routes;
- what company and private cargo remains available;
- which animals are controlled, missing, or loose;
- what water has been taken from the forest; and
- which groups intend rescue, early departure, seed work, or continued travel.

At Red Dusk, the camp begins sliding toward the western edge. At Root Night, it becomes easier to leave from here but harder to reach the crown. At Glass Dawn, anyone still using the camp must depart immediately or accept being carried east.

On every return, ask for the four-part report from the rescue ledger: who was physically seen, what route is open now, what change will close it, and what plan each person actually heard. This keeps rescue, consent, and topology from collapsing into one vague update.

## Late access and carried reports

After Red Dusk the camp is no longer a universal hub. It slides toward the western edge and becomes a departure staging ground. A returning group reports named people, clues actually observed, materials committed, route or current states changed, and the next visible closure. Knowledge does not pass to another group merely because the players know it.

Hessa's slate, Sio's copied lines, Nahl's notes, and samples already brought to camp can travel with a named departure group. They preserve observations already made; they do not supply unheard bells, unseen tracks, or tests no one performed. If the camp itself becomes unreachable, record which person carries each report and which eastbound travelers never heard it.
