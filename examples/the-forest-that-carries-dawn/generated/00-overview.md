## Synopsis

At middle watch, the Cairnroad caravan wakes inside a forest walking east across the White Salt. Five wagons, thirteen travelers, and three draft teams hang in divided roots behind the surviving camp. At dawn the Bearer will reach the Glass Waste, harden the eastern forest, and lift the last ordinary road home. Merewash, the caravan's destination, has endured six failed rainy seasons; two damaged records suggest this migration can make a living rain-seed. No seed waits to be picked. Old ground, borrowed dawn, returning rain, a remembered river, bounded blackgrass heat, and the Bearer's one deep exhale must meet in the crown. The wagons have broken the beetle road that feeds it. Rescue, repair, departure, and the hope of rain now spend the same hands before the last salt disappears.

## Premise

You travel with the Cairnroad caravan, one group among its forty-two named travelers. Orra Venn sends you because you can cross moving roots, distinguish a route from a hope, and return with news while it is still true. Find the missing people. Mark ways west. Learn what the forest carries toward its crown, then decide what labor, water, cargo, and danger anyone will spend for Merewash. The forest offers no bargain. Beetles turn from oil, lantern cups open to wet stems, water returns or fails, fire follows fuel, and the Bearer breathes once whether anyone is ready or not.

## Explanation

The forest crosses the White Salt once in ninety years on the back of an immense root animal called the Bearer. Its western mat passed beneath the sleeping Cairnroad, lifted eleven wagons, and kept moving. Six wagons remain near camp; five lie in three root pockets with thirteen people and three draft teams. At direct dawn the Bearer reaches the Glass Waste. Reflected heat hardens the eastern tissue, the last salt footing narrows, and anyone still aboard travels east beyond ordinary return.

The forest is not one mind. Bronze soilbearers carry thumb-sized parcels of living earth. Lantern flowers lend stored dawn through glasswing moths and wet pollen. Warm basins send water outward and receive it back. Pale fungal cords keep slope, depth, interval, and mineral taste while names and biographies wash away. Blackgrass opens hard coats and clears young roots unless oil, canvas, or pollen gives the flame another road. The Bearer bears, cools, strains, vents, and exhales. None of these responses is consent.

A rain-seed is made where six paths meet: living old soil, captured dawn, circulating rain, a remembered river course, bounded blackgrass heat, and the Bearer's root pulse. During the one long central exhale, a mature knot loosens in its socket; before or after that breath, the crown tears or hardens. Sio Tern's family route-song and Nahl Reed's independently preserved watershed report agree only in fragments. Both are instruments to test against present matter.

The caravan has already altered the crown. Iron rims crushed the Soilbearer Road and spice oil fouled its upper track. If that road stays weak, enough old ground should reach the crown for the forest's continuation, but not enough to demonstrate a surplus. Repair the corridor and keep it alive through the burn, and several knots may mature. One can then separate cleanly while another mature knot remains attached. That proves capacity, not permission.

Orra Venn begins with forty-two names and will not trade one for an elegant plan. Hessa Clay keeps the roster exact and records what each group has actually heard. Nahl names the evidence threshold and where it ends. Pelen Marr reads frames, iron, and load; guilt makes him quick to offer his own property, never another person's. Sio tests inherited lines against matter. Jun Alder may choose the eastward road only after rescue, supplies, risks, and a real western alternative have been named. No one secretly caused the crisis.

The authored clue graph remains stable beneath routes that change through Long Shade, Red Dusk, Root Night, and Glass Dawn. Knowing a region exists does not guarantee a safe current route. Discovery persists after bark burns or roots move; access does not. Evidence travels only through a carrier already established in the fiction: a survivor, bell, animal, soilbearer, mist, glasswing, pollen line, marked water, living braid, fire front, ember, vent, feeder ring, or crown defect. A failing receiver can diagnose an absent path, but it cannot supply that path after the repair window closes.

At the Glass Verge, put the night's residue on the ground: rescued names, informed departures, usable animals and wagons, borrowed or spent property, functioning currents, mature knots, returned water, and the approach or loss of the deep exhale. The ending is the shape made by the work, damage, and choices that came before.
