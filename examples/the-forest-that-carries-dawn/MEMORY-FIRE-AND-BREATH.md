# Memory, Fire, and Breath Operating Sheet

Use this sheet when the party enters the Hollow of Kept Voices, prepares or runs the Blackgrass Burn, or opens the Root-Breath Chamber. It operationalizes the remembered river course, bounded blackgrass heat, and Bearer's root pulse without turning voices into intact dead persons, necessary fire into harmless fire, or the Bearer into a speaking sovereign.

## Governing distinctions

- A preserved voice is a carrier for measured associations, not a complete person.
- A bounded heat current is distinct from a runaway fire. More flame is not a stronger current.
- The Bearer's bodily response can be observed and accommodated without being interpreted as consent.
- Each current still has a source, path, receiver, visible sign, and tolerance.
- A strong late current can absorb one supported strain. It cannot replace an absent soil, light, rain, memory, heat, or pulse relationship.

## Remembered river course

### Four required relations

A working selected course needs all four:

| Relation | Table question | Merewash constraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fall | Where does water descend, and how sharply? | Must move without cutting directly through inhabited clay |
| Storage | Where is seasonal water held? | Needs one durable reach above or within compatible deep ground |
| Ground | What soil, mineral, and salinity receive it? | Must tolerate Merewash clay, limestone, and old salt lenses |
| Overflow | Where does exceptional water go? | Must not route the first great flood through central houses and fields |

Interval evidence—repeated distances, travel times, bend spacing, or pulse counts—confirms that fragments belong to one course and can strengthen it.

### Voice test

1. Establish a cool or dry baseline.
2. Feed one cord family with measured working warm rain.
3. Mark numbers, slopes, colors, tastes, and repeated transitions.
4. Ask a present-tense question or alter one narrow flow condition.
5. Compare a second independent family.
6. Return the water or continue it through the selected living path.

Names and relationships drift. Measurements persist. A voice cannot update a route, answer for the dead, reveal events it never observed, or consent to present action.

### Transfer methods

| Method | Current result | Main cost | What survives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Living braid of compatible wet cords | Working or strong | Selected branch becomes quiet behind the transfer | Living course reaches crown; evidence remains in the network |
| Wet human transcription | No living-current upgrade by itself | Time and possible missed late family | Defensible record for Merewash |
| Cut wet branch | Weak at best | Source family broken; relation stripped from neighbors | One partial portable sequence |
| Flood the hollow | Usually weakens distinction | Pools rain and mixes incompatible courses | Broad proof that several courses exist |

### Memory-current states

- **Absent:** no living selected path reaches the crown.
- **Weak:** fall and ground are stable, but storage or overflow is missing, contradictory, or carried only on a severed branch.
- **Working:** fall, storage, ground, and overflow travel through one living wet path.
- **Strong:** the working course also has interval confirmation, a redundant family, or a complete human transcription.

## Bounded blackgrass heat

### Burn survey

Before ignition or admission, name:

1. leading edge;
2. left margin;
3. right margin;
4. intended receiver; and
5. escape route.

Then mark exceptional fuels: oil, spice dust, dry canvas, chaff, bedding, cut lantern stems, animal hair, resinous wood, and lamp wick.

### Three segments

| Segment | Receiver | Principal risk | Useful preparations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wagon scar | Renewed scar above damaged soil road | Oil, spice dust, salvage, trapped people or animals | Clear lateral fuel, mineral break, returned wet line |
| Restored corridor | Living soil route after beetle passage | Heat arriving with rather than after old soil | Living-soil guide, lifted bridge, crew withdrawal, delayed segment |
| Crownward seam | Prepared filled shells beyond root gap | Early pollen jump, dry memory cords, closed seam | Ember hold, cooled lips, deep-exhale timing |

### A defensible corridor

A working corridor requires:

- continuous intended fuel;
- two independent margins;
- a prepared receiver; and
- a maintained escape route.

Useful operations include clearing lateral fuel, laying a returned wet line, building a living-soil guide, making a clean mineral break, ferrying a held ember, or delaying one segment until its receiver is ready.

### Burn sequence

1. Name segment and receiver.
2. Show both margins and their keepers.
3. Move nonparticipants and animals.
4. Admit the existing front or a prepared ember.
5. Check source, margins, receiver, and one neighboring current.
6. Record the new scar and any separate runaway front.

### Heat-current states

- **Absent:** the intended corridor is smothered, severed, diverted, or missed after hardening.
- **Weak:** intermittent or late heat reaches only part of the prepared path.
- **Working:** a low continuous front reaches renewed scar and prepared crownward receiver after the other currents.
- **Strong:** the working front has redundant margins and a second living branch or held ember.

A runaway fire is tracked separately by its own fuel, margins, and threatened receivers. It is never counted as strong bounded heat.

## Bearer's root pulse

### Four phases

| Phase | Reliable signs | Main opportunities |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Traction pulse | Root bands tighten; lines draw taut; dust moves east | Move with pulse; measure; redistribute light burdens |
| Venting rise | Vents open west to east; chamber warms; cooling water descends | Clear vents; cool tissue; test gates; induce one preliminary breath |
| Long central exhale | Traction stops; air moves outward; crown stems slacken | Transfer heat; complete convergence; realign one band; safely separate a mature seed during late slack |
| Inward draw and hardening | Water reverses; cords tighten; eastern tissue shines | Release work, clear chamber, commit to departure or continued migration |

Use at least three independent cues to announce the safe interval: suspended tension, vent sequence, water temperature, cord behavior, or opening of the fire seam.

### Burden diagnosis

| Burden class | Correct response | Do not assume |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exposed foreign object | Support, cool, and remove along entry path during venting | Every embedded object is harmful |
| Incorporated scar | Leave stable tissue intact | The chamber is a treasure vault |
| Active pressure bridge | Brace and redistribute load before removal | Pulling the object free will relieve pressure |
| Blocked channel | Open the existing vent or water path | A new cut or blind flood is safer |

### Useful interventions

- Remove exposed iron under support.
- Redistribute wagons, cargo, people, and animals among load roots.
- Restore an existing cooling channel with returned basin water.
- Peel dead sheath without cutting live load tissue.
- Match the existing pulse with rope, wheel, footfall, drum, or tool vibration to induce one small test response.

A preliminary breath can open one gate, slacken one wagon line, test the fire seam, expose a burden pocket, or shift one region. It cannot safely release a mature seed. Repeated forcing shortens or distorts the unique deep exhale.

### Load-band redirection

| Favored band | Opens | Strains |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Western carry | Mass departure, late rescue, animal extraction | Crown and burn access from camp |
| Middle circulation | Basin, hollow, chamber, and split-party return | Outer exits and intact heavy salvage |
| Crownward lift | Convergence, fire transfer, and seed access | Direct camp return and undecided departure time |

The Bearer still moves east. Redirection changes adjacency and timing, not destination.

### Root-pulse states

Before assessment, mark the pulse **unassessed**.

- **Absent:** the deep exhale has passed or no usable slack interval remains.
- **Weak:** the interval is short, asymmetric, overheated, or burdened; one major operation completes cleanly.
- **Working:** venting, cooling, slack, and inward cues form a readable sequence long enough for prepared transfers and one safe separation.
- **Strong:** burdens are balanced, cooling works, vents are open, and redundant cues provide a longer or correctable interval.

## Cross-current checks

When one late current changes, show the receiver and one neighboring dependency.

- Warm rain wakes memory cords, cools fire margins, and lengthens root tissue flexibility.
- A selected memory path must remain wet while it crosses the burn's threatened area.
- The burn should pass after soil, light, rain, and memory begin filling crown shells.
- Root-breath opens the final fire seam and the only clean seed-separation interval.
- Fire, memory, and pulse all become less forgiving at Glass Dawn, but none should fail merely because the GM wants a climax.

## Session 4 control points

Before proceeding to the rain crown and Glass Verge, the GM should be able to answer:

- Which four river relations were selected, and why do they suit Merewash?
- What voice tests demonstrated that the cords are patterned records rather than whole people?
- Is the memory current absent, weak, working, or strong?
- What human transcription, cut branch, or living braid survives?
- What are the states of the wagon-scar, restored-corridor, and crownward burn segments?
- What holds each fire margin, and does a separate runaway front exist?
- Is bounded heat absent, weak, working, or strong?
- Which burden objects were removed, supported, or deliberately left?
- Which basin branch cools the chamber?
- Which load band was favored, and what route narrowed?
- What three cues announce crown slack, and what sign ends it?
- Is the root pulse absent, weak, working, or strong?

Do not decide the final rain-seed outcome yet. Record the late currents precisely enough that the crown can display what the party actually built.
