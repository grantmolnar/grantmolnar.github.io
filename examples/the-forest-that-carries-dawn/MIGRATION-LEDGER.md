# Migration Ledger: The Forest That Carries Dawn

Use this sheet to keep the living route distinct from the static clue graph.

## Migration stages

| Stage | Trigger | Physical changes | Opportunities under pressure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Long Shade | Opening until two substantial interventions or a major delay | Broad root roads remain open; the missing wagons are reachable; lantern light is still stored | Rescue, count survivors, identify several regions, make low-cost experiments |
| Red Dusk | After Long Shade | Outer roots draw inward; warm basins climb; the blackgrass burn reaches the old wagon scar | Restore the soil route, control fire, choose early departures, recover heavy cargo |
| Root Night | After Red Dusk or when the party induces the Bearer's breath | The central mound opens; the crown descends; pale cords brighten; some earlier paths separate | Enter the root-breath chamber, select a river memory, complete convergence, divide the caravan |
| Glass Dawn | Finale | Eastern roots cross reflective glass; ordinary exits close from east to west; the crown hardens or travels on | Separate a mature seed, take the remaining exits, travel with the forest, accept damage already done |

Advance a stage because the fiction changes, not after a fixed number of die rolls. Announce the physical evidence before closing an opportunity.

## Rescue pressure and stage advancement

During Long Shade, the party normally completes two substantial interventions before Red Dusk, unless a major delay advances the forest sooner. This is not a hidden action budget. Show the changing fiction before the stage changes:

- outer root roads draw inward;
- wagon bells shift relative to the camp;
- upward dew warms into circulating rain;
- the eastern brightness climbs the underside of the canopy; or
- one missing-wagon pocket reaches its stated warning condition.

The three wagon pockets have separate local pressures. The upper wheel loses intact salvage as oil reaches the soil track. The split freight bed separates Bell and the eastern copper load. The low hollow loses its wagon-sized western climb. These consequences change method and capacity rather than killing unresolved people offscreen.

Use `CARAVAN-AND-RESCUE-LEDGER.md` for the exact roster, rescue operations, movement states, animal capacities, and emergency property rules.

## Ecological interventions and stage advancement

Use `ECOLOGY-OPERATING-SHEET.md` for observation, experiments, current states, sustainable taking, borrowing, spending, fouling, and cross-current effects. Use `MEMORY-FIRE-AND-BREATH.md` for remembered-course selection, the three burn segments, separate runaway-fire tracking, burden diagnosis, load-band redirection, and the deep-exhale interval.

One bounded observation normally fits inside the current stage. A substantial ecological intervention includes building or dismantling a road, rehanging a lantern cup, guiding a full moth release, installing a water loop, recovering the grain team, or repairing a basin branch. Two such interventions may occupy the same broad interval when different groups work in parallel and the routes support them. Do not advance the stage merely because the party asked several good questions.

Before a stage closes, show the local physical sign:

- soilbearers begin using the longer detour;
- old-ground mounds lift from the western roots;
- leaf clicks begin or skip a branch;
- smoke reaches lantern seams;
- basins climb beyond ordinary terraces;
- the eastern cooling shelf warms; or
- a root pulse moves a prepared structure under load;
- repeated voices shed names while retaining measurements;
- blackgrass whitens along one margin or jumps exceptional fuel; or
- vents open west to east before the central exhale.

When the party changes one current, state the effect at a receiver and one neighboring dependency. A repaired soil route may darken basin roots and catch lantern pollen. A restored basin branch may reopen cups and cool the burn margin. A strong current supplies one concrete redundancy or absorbs one later strain; it does not replace an absent current.

## Discovery, access, and mobile carriers

Track three questions separately for every region:

1. **What is known?** Record clues actually observed and which group knows them.
2. **What is reachable?** Record burden class, special access, and the visible sign that will narrow or close the route.
3. **What still functions?** Record the current, receiver, damage, and any living path or prepared bypass that persists after the source region moves.

A clue remains known after its evidence is destroyed. A route does not remain open because its locator revelation was established. Evidence that travels with survivors, bells, animals, soilbearers, old-ground mist, glasswings, pollen, marked water, a living braid, fire, a held ember, vents, tension, or crown defects retains the authored source region where the process occurred.

Use four access states beside each region: **open**, **narrow**, **carried**, or **closed**. Carried means the region itself cannot be visited normally, but a specific organism, material, report, or living path may bring evidence or function onward. It is not a general substitute for access.

## Region movement

### Camp Under New Leaves

- Long Shade: central and broadly connected.
- Red Dusk: begins sliding toward the western edge.
- Root Night: becomes an exit staging ground but loses direct access to the crown.
- Glass Dawn: remains safe only if people leave immediately.

### Wagons in the Forked Roots

- Long Shade: accessible by two root gullies.
- Red Dusk: one gully closes; heavy wagons require dismantling or animal power.
- Root Night: people can still be extracted, but intact wagons cannot.
- Glass Dawn: the pocket is carried east beyond the last walkable root.

### Soilbearer Road

- Long Shade: both scar sites, heavy materials, cool mounds, and paired beetle streams remain reachable.
- Red Dusk: the detour lengthens; blackgrass warmth reaches the upper-wheel oil; heavy materials must already be placed or moved by animals.
- Root Night: gates climb and open on the deeper exhale; a working road becomes a narrow crownward service path.
- Glass Dawn: old-ground delivery is complete or missed; western mound continuity folds into the moving mat.

### Lantern Canopy

- Long Shade: the lattice is broad and shaded; the broken bough supplies severed-light evidence; intact cups can still be rehung.
- Red Dusk: six-click releases begin; smoke, blackgrass warmth, and unrecovered animals threaten lower branches.
- Root Night: most cups are orderly, early, dark, or destroyed; glasswings and wet pollen lines mark routes toward the crown.
- Glass Dawn: reflection hardens unopened cups and folds emptied ones; the living release is over.

### Warm Rain Basins

- Long Shade: the western spare lip, animal ledge, heavy containers, pipe, and lower return are reachable from ordinary terraces.
- Red Dusk: the chain climbs and spills faster; large diversions must already be installed or moved with specialized access.
- Root Night: high bowls require rope, flight, climbing, or a redirected lower course; central water strongly marks memory and crown routes.
- Glass Dawn: eastern bowls steam and remaining flow is committed to crown and root cooling.

### Hollow of Kept Voices

- Long Shade: individual cadences are faint but distinguishable; several useful cord families remain dry.
- Red Dusk: measured warm rain reveals separate fall, storage, ground, overflow, and interval families.
- Root Night: personal detail blurs while compatible course relations become vivid and a living braid can reach the descending crown.
- Glass Dawn: reflected heat silences outer cords; only a course already transferred or copied survives ordinary access.

### Blackgrass Burn

- Long Shade: the distant front and old scar can be surveyed; exceptional fuel and heavy boundaries can still be moved.
- Red Dusk: the fire crosses the wagon-scar and restored-corridor segments; soil must pass before heat and both margins need active keepers.
- Root Night: a bounded front or held ember can cross the crownward seam on the long central exhale; a separate runaway front retains its own route and fuel.
- Glass Dawn: reflection creates a second ignition risk, turning late preparation into containment and departure.

### Root-Breath Chamber

- Long Shade: sealed beneath the mound, but traction sequence and burden response can be observed elsewhere.
- Red Dusk: vents open in sequence; cooling and one preliminary-breath test become possible through service seams.
- Root Night: the chamber opens, one load band may be favored, and the unique deep exhale creates the only clean separation interval.
- Glass Dawn: the inward draw closes the chamber, hardens eastern tissue, and commits anyone inside to immediate western exit or continued migration.

### Crown of Unfallen Rain

- Long Shade: high and inaccessible.
- Red Dusk: visible through converging channels.
- Root Night: descends within reach as the Bearer breathes.
- Glass Dawn: hardens, separates, or travels east according to the established state.

### Glass Verge

- Long Shade: visible only from high ground.
- Red Dusk: western exits are broad; eastern exits are already reflecting heat.
- Root Night: routes narrow and require named departure groups.
- Glass Dawn: the last ordinary ground leaves the forest.

## State tracks

Record human, animal, property, and route states as **intact**, **strained**, **broken**, or **restored**. Record ecological currents as **absent**, **weak**, **working**, or **strong**:

- caravan roster and knowledge state;
- injured-person movement capacity;
- upper-wheel people, animals, and intact salvage;
- split-freight people, Bell, and eastern cargo;
- low-hollow people, western climb, and grain team;
- draft-animal control and strain;
- soilbearer corridor and surviving mound continuity;
- lantern branches and living pollen route;
- western, central, eastern, and return basin branches;
- selected river course: fall, storage, ground, overflow, interval confirmation, transfer path, and surviving transcription;
- blackgrass wagon-scar, restored-corridor, and crownward-seam segments, including both margins and any separate runaway front;
- Bearer burden map, cooling path, preliminary breaths, favored load band, three or more timing cues, and deep-exhale state; and
- crown knots by state: empty shell, stormglass, viable, mature, attached, separated, or torn;
- broad salt shelf, wet middle ledge, and crown service root capacities;
- first reflection, deep exhale, inward draw, and direct-sun signals; and
- rain-seed custody, cradle circulation, and departure route.

A restored human or route state need not return to its original form. A repaired soil road may use dismantled wagon beds. Basin circulation may be redirected through canvas troughs. A river memory may be incomplete but stable enough for a smaller watershed.

For soil, light, and rain, also record what was **taken within tolerance**, **borrowed**, **spent**, or **fouled**, the visible receiver response, and the later option opened or closed. Do not upgrade a current because the intervention was costly or well-intentioned; use the physical signs in the encounter and ecology operating sheet.

## Departure groups

Keep separate lists for:

- people committed to leave at the first safe exit;
- people willing to remain until missing survivors are found;
- people willing to pursue the rain-seed;
- people who have not heard the current plan;
- people choosing to travel with the forest; and
- people physically unable to use a proposed route.

Do not treat silence, absence, injury, or dependency as consent. Record the route each group heard, the burdens it can physically use, and the visible change expected to close it.

## Root Night and Glass Dawn sequence

Use `CROWN-VERGE-OPERATING-SHEET.md` once the crown descends.

1. At **first reflection**, send heavy westbound groups while crown workers diagnose receiver knots.
2. Before the **deep exhale**, complete final living paths, prepare the wet cradle, and place people who require the service root.
3. During the **deep exhale**, resolve one prepared clean separation and concurrent crossings.
4. At the **inward draw**, reduce route capacities exactly as established and record who intentionally remains.
5. At **direct sun**, ordinary western footing ends; continue eastbound play from the actual people, supplies, current states, and damage aboard.

Show every signal before closing an opportunity. Work already underway can finish when its preparation and route support it. Do not grant time for an entirely new heavy operation after its physical window has visibly passed.
