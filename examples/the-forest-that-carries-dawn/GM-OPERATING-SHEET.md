# GM Operating Sheet: The Forest That Carries Dawn

Use this sheet beside the roster and current-specific operating aids. It is a compact procedure for running the moving route graph without flattening the authored clue graph.

## Opening state

Write these facts before play begins:

- **Stage:** Long Shade.
- **Roster:** forty-two total; twenty-nine at camp; four heard but unreached; six believed with named wagons; three missing without location.
- **Known pressure:** the forest moves east; the western salt slides backward; direct dawn will carry the roots into the Glass Waste.
- **Immediate commission:** account for people, find a westward route, determine the dawn deadline, and avoid spending lives or property under false assumptions.
- **Possible later work:** test the old rain-seed records only through named volunteers after the commission checkpoint.
- **Current states:** all six unverified; soil route damaged; no crown knot diagnosed.
- **Runaway fire:** none at opening.
- **Departure groups:** none yet committed by informed choice.

Do not begin with a complete explanation of the ecology. Give the physical facts and let evidence accumulate unevenly.

## Commission checkpoint

Do not treat the opening rescue order as permanent authority over the whole caravan. Once one usable westward route is known and every missing person is reached or assigned to an active rescue, record four groups separately: immediate departure, unfinished rescue, voluntary repair or investigation, and undecided. Record owner consent for any private material before seed work spends it. If no one volunteers for later work, proceed to departure without manufacturing another mandate.

## Migration-turn loop

Run one loop whenever a group reaches a region, undertakes a substantial intervention, or the fiction advances toward a stage change.

### 1. Carry forward exact state

Name the people, animals, loads, current states, route states, and materials physically present. Do not let copper, canvas, teams, or NPCs exist in two places.

### 2. Show two physical changes

Choose local signs that reveal movement or pressure: tightening roots, climbing basins, warming reflection, opening vents, skipped lantern clicks, changing voices, advancing smoke, feeder tension, or narrowing salt.

At least one sign should concern the active region. The other may show a neighboring dependency or the approaching stage.

### 3. Ask for an objective and method

Examples:

- free named people;
- move a litter or animal team;
- identify a source, path, receiver, or tolerance;
- test one ecological relation;
- repair, bypass, borrow, spend, or deliberately refuse an intervention;
- carry a report or material to another group;
- prepare a departure route; or
- diagnose and correct one crown defect.

Resolve the action actually attempted, not an assumed completion of the encounter.

### 4. Deliver observable clues

A clue requires its described observation or a physically equivalent manifestation of the same source process. Do not hide an obvious result behind a check. System uncertainty may affect time, precision, exposure, or material loss.

Record:

- clue title;
- authored source region;
- carrier, if the evidence has moved; and
- which group currently knows it.

### 5. Resolve intervention and cost

Update the smallest concrete state that follows:

- person or animal movement;
- route burden or accessibility;
- material commitment;
- current state;
- contamination, structural damage, or runaway fire;
- later option opened or closed; and
- time or stage pressure.

A costly attempt does not automatically improve a current. Use its physical receiver sign.

### 6. Report or preserve separation

When groups are divided, knowledge is not shared until a messenger, signal, returned animal, marked flow, or reunion carries it. Record what each group knows and which plan each departure group has actually heard.

### 7. Check the local closing sign

Announce the sign before removing an opportunity. Work already underway may finish if its preparation and route support it. A wholly new heavy operation does not begin after its window closes.

### 8. Advance stage only when the fiction changes

Long Shade usually supports two substantial interventions, more if groups work credibly in parallel. Do not count questions or quick observations as full interventions. Advance when the moving forest, not an invisible action counter, changes the situation.

## Physical access shorthand

Use four marks beside every region:

- **Open:** ordinary people and appropriate loads can reach it.
- **Narrow:** people or light loads only; name special access.
- **Carried:** the region is inaccessible, but a physical carrier may bring evidence or function onward.
- **Closed:** no new western intervention; preserve consequences and eastbound continuation.

## Uneven-clue rule

Do not regularize support during play.

- Broad revelations may appear repeatedly without requiring the GM to force every clue.
- A three-clue revelation can be missed if the party avoids all relevant processes.
- One destroyed or inaccessible source still leaves at least two authored sources for every revelation.
- A moving carrier retains its original source region.
- Receiver diagnosis may show a missing relation without awarding an unobserved earlier clue.
- Once observed, a clue remains known even if its evidence is destroyed.

## Split-party board

For every active group, record:

| Group | Names | Current region | Objective | Critical materials/animals | Clues known only here | Next closure | Rendezvous/report method |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

When a group reports, require five items:

1. people and movement states;
2. observed clues and source regions;
3. currents or routes changed;
4. materials and animals committed; and
5. the next visible closure sign.

No NPC, team, wagon component, or current-carrying object can support two groups simultaneously.

## Failure-forward ladder

Choose the smallest consequence supported by the method.

1. **Exposure:** injury, heat, smoke, wet footing, separation, or strain.
2. **Time:** the next physical closure begins.
3. **Material:** a rope, cup, canvas run, copper piece, wagon section, or stored water is spent or fouled.
4. **Capacity:** a route loses a burden class; a person or animal needs assistance.
5. **Ecology:** a current remains weak, a neighboring relation strains, or runaway fire gains a route.
6. **Human consequence:** a named person is left elsewhere, misses the plan, refuses the burden, or chooses a different departure.

Do not jump directly to death, total route loss, or adventure termination unless the fiction clearly warrants it and the table accepts that scale.

## Route-shape responses

### Rescue only

Let rescue be a complete achievement. Do not force seed lore. At the verge, show what remains unknown and what continuing east would cost.

### Seed first

Keep all thirteen missing names visible. Other caravan members act on their own priorities with the labor and information available. A good seed does not erase abandonment.

### Escape first

Remove departing labor, carriers, animals, and property from later availability unless explicitly left. A volunteer remainder can continue inward.

### Fire avoided

Record the choice. Heat remains absent or weak; the crown can show a sealed coat. Do not create a surprise alternate maturation method.

### Root chamber reached late

Allow external timing cues and one narrow correction supported by prior observation. Remove the preliminary test, broad burden map, and any unprepared second chance.

### Deep exhale missed

No clean separation. Offer only consequences already supported: leave the seed, tear it, carry an earlier damaged knot, depart, or continue east.

## Root Night handoff

Before opening the crown, verify:

- who is rescued and who remains unresolved;
- which people have chosen early departure;
- where each animal team and major load is;
- soil, light, rain, memory, heat, and pulse states;
- any runaway fire;
- living paths physically reaching inward;
- at least three potential deep-exhale cues; and
- the route each active group expects to use at dawn.

Do not delay all departure planning until after crown work begins.

## Glass Dawn sequence

1. **First reflection:** send heavy groups; diagnose knots; complete only staged path work.
2. **Deep exhale:** resolve one prepared clean separation and simultaneous service-root crossings.
3. **Inward draw:** reduce capacities; harden the crown; name everyone intentionally remaining.
4. **Direct sun:** end ordinary westward movement and continue from the actual eastbound state.

Run crown and departure scenes in parallel. A group at the verge does not know the seed workers succeeded until a signal, messenger, or arriving seed proves it.

## Final record

Record separately:

- every person westbound, unresolved, or eastbound;
- injuries and movement assistance;
- each animal and major load;
- each current state and persistent damage;
- runaway fire;
- every crown knot and its condition;
- whether separation was clean, torn, or missed;
- whether a demonstrated surplus remained;
- route and signal used by every departure group;
- seed custody and transport state;
- what Merewash can actually attempt; and
- compensation, repair, or continuing obligations.

Do not reduce the ending to “saved the caravan” or “got the seed.”
