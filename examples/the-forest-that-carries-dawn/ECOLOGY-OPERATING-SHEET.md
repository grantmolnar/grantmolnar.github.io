# Ecology Operating Sheet: The Forest That Carries Dawn

Use this sheet when workers observe, test, borrow from, damage, or repair one of the migration's living currents. It does not replace encounter prose. It keeps interventions legible across moving regions and prevents the ecology from behaving like six interchangeable quest resources.

## The operating rule

Every current has five parts:

1. **Source:** where the current begins or is stored;
2. **Path:** the organism, channel, or repeated behavior that carries it;
3. **Receiver:** the root, stem, cord, crown knot, or body that uses it;
4. **Visible sign:** what shows that the current is moving and still alive; and
5. **Tolerance:** what can be borrowed, redirected, delayed, or contaminated before the relationship fails.

When the party intervenes, name the current and all five parts that are presently known. A clear experiment changes one condition, waits for one root pulse or other stated interval, and checks both the intended receiver and at least one neighboring receiver. A broad action such as “study the basin” should become a concrete question such as “does water from this lower bowl feed the lantern stems or the cooling roots?”

Do not hide the basic result behind a roll when the setup could answer the question. A roll, spell cost, or system-specific test may determine speed, exposure, precision, material loss, or whether the party can act before the region moves. It should not make an observed current arbitrarily inscrutable.

## Current states

Record each current as one of four functional states.

| State | Meaning at the table |
| --- | --- |
| Absent | The source, path, or receiver has been destroyed, missed, or fully severed. Another current cannot substitute for it. |
| Weak | Some of the current reaches its receiver, but the path is late, contaminated, reduced, or unstable. Strong neighboring currents may keep it usable at a cost. |
| Working | Enough arrives alive and in sequence to support ordinary migration and a viable convergence. |
| Strong | The current has surplus, redundant paths, or unusually clean delivery. It may absorb one later strain or strengthen a neighboring weak current. |

No current replaces another. A strong current can compensate for one *weak* adjacent relationship when the fiction supports it; it cannot stand in for an absent current. Record the physical means of compensation rather than applying an abstract bonus.

## Observation procedure

Use observation to establish a baseline before changing the ecology.

1. **Choose a marker.** Soil color, beetle direction, pollen, temperature, mineral trace, vibration, sound, or a harmless carried substance can make a current visible.
2. **Name two receivers.** Identify the expected receiver and one neighboring system that might also depend on the current.
3. **Wait for the relevant interval.** This is usually one root pulse, one basin spill, one six-click lantern sequence, or the arrival of the next soilbearer stream.
4. **State the result physically.** Say where the marker appears, what opens or closes, and what remains unchanged.
5. **Record uncertainty.** Distinguish “this path carries water” from “this path can spare water” and “this cup shines” from “this cup contributes living dawn.”

## Experiment procedure

A sound experiment asks one question and risks a bounded cost.

1. State the question.
2. Establish the untouched baseline.
3. Change one condition or divert one measured portion.
4. Protect against the most obvious contamination or route hazard.
5. Check the source, path, intended receiver, and one neighboring receiver after the stated interval.
6. Restore the condition when possible and record what did not recover.

A failed experiment still answers something unless the setup itself was destroyed. Its consequence should be late timing, material loss, a strained current, a closed route, an exposed character, or a new contamination—not “nothing is learned.”

## Accommodation is not diplomacy

The party may use vibration, shade, moisture, scent, warmth, timing, or repeated signals to make an organism's existing behavior useful. This can resemble negotiation at the table, but the ecology is not making promises.

Human negotiation still matters. People own copper, canvas, grain, planters, wagons, labor, and time. State who must consent, what immediate danger authorizes, what material will remain recoverable, and what claim survives dawn. Organisms respond to conditions; survivors decide whether to spend what belongs to them.

## Taking, borrowing, spending, and returning

Use four distinct verbs.

- **Take within tolerance:** A bounded amount may be consumed without changing the current's functional state. Examples include one shallow tray from an intact old-ground mound or gradual drinking from the western basin's spare overflow. The taken material is not returned, but the source and path continue to meet their receivers.
- **Borrow:** The current is diverted temporarily and given a credible return path before the next critical stage. Examples include moving an intact lantern cup with its stem, running basin water through a temporary drinking gutter while returning unused overflow, or carrying scent-water through copper and back to the road.
- **Spend:** The intervention consumes a nonrenewable portion or exceeds sustainable yield during this migration. A cut lantern cup, a stripped old-ground mound, or a basin emptied into sealed barrels is spent even if the choice is justified.
- **Foul:** The material remains present but can no longer perform its ecological function. Oil-soaked soil, salt-contaminated water, smoke-clogged pollen, and overheated pellets are fouled.

Do not describe taken, spent, or fouled material as borrowed. A later repair may create another route or a surplus; the consumed material remains part of the reckoning.

## Cross-current effects

Use these interactions when earlier work should matter later.

- Living soil teaches roots to accept water and gives lantern pollen a viable surface.
- Warm rain keeps soil pellets alive, carries lantern pollen, cools the burn margins, wakes fungal cords, and marks root pulse through temperature.
- Captured dawn travels most reliably in working rain over living soil.
- Bounded heat renews the soil corridor and opens the seed coat; uncontrolled heat sterilizes soil, dries rain paths, and opens lantern cups too early.
- Remembered river course shapes the convergence but requires warm rain to become legible.
- The Bearer's pulse opens gates, raises basins, releases the crown, and sets the only safe separation interval.

A strong current should create a concrete later advantage: a broader route, redundant carrier, cooler margin, confirmed measure family, second fire boundary, additional breath cue, or one absorbed strain. Do not convert it into a generic bonus detached from the ecology.

## Recording an intervention

After a meaningful intervention, write one concise ledger line containing:

- the migration stage;
- the current and region;
- the source, path, or receiver changed;
- the material or labor committed;
- the immediate visible result;
- the new current state; and
- one later option opened, preserved, strained, or closed.

Example:

> Red Dusk — Warm rain — western spare lip diverted through Ira's uncut copper and returned to the lower cooling gutter; all forty-two people can receive one drink, the lantern branch remains working, and the copper is recoverable before Root Night.

## Session 3 control points

By the end of the soil, light, and rain regions, the GM should be able to answer:

- Is the soilbearer road absent, weak, working, or strong?
- Which old-ground mounds remain intact?
- Did living soil reach the crownward gates before they climbed?
- How many lantern branches remain orderly, early, dark, or destroyed?
- Does living pollen have a wet route toward a known crown knot?
- Which basin branches remain working, and what water was borrowed, spent, or fouled?
- Were Moth and Reed recovered, left on a safe route, or driven into another region?
- Which people and owners heard the actual material proposal?
- What later access, cooling, surplus, or redundancy did the work create?

Do not decide rain-seed viability yet. Record these three currents precisely enough that the late-current regions and final crown can display the result rather than revise it retroactively. Use the Session 4 control points in `MEMORY-FIRE-AND-BREATH.md` before entering the crown.


## Session 5 crown handoff

At the Crown of Unfallen Rain, translate each current state into a visible receiver sign rather than averaging them.

- An absent current leaves an empty-shell defect.
- An unsupported weak current produces stormglass behavior.
- All six working through one complete circuit produce a viable knot.
- Bounded heat and continuing internal pulse mature the knot.
- Continuing living-soil delivery and another attached mature knot establish a demonstrated surplus.

Use `CROWN-VERGE-OPERATING-SHEET.md` for the exact crown circuit, separation conditions, route capacities, and dawn signals. Use `AFTERMATH-AND-SEASONAL-STATES.md` for transport and establishment. No earlier current becomes stronger merely because the party reaches the finale.
