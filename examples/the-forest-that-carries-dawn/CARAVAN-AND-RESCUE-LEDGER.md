# Caravan and Rescue Ledger: The Forest That Carries Dawn

This sheet fixes the opening roster, the three missing-wagon pockets, the limits of emergency authority, and the procedures for rescue, triage, salvage, and informed departure.

## Fresh-play party setup

The caravan always begins with forty-two people. For a fresh group, either adopt four to six present-and-able roster entries as player characters or replace the same number of such entries one for one. Never add the adventurers on top of the roster. Replacement characters begin at camp and preserve only the replaced entries' opening capacity; their personal histories and priorities belong to the players.

The Saltward Four are one demonstrated assignment of four roster positions. Their names and specialties are not required for the authoritative adventure. When a procedure calls for a route recorder, property recorder, animal handler, or ecological observer, let a player character take the function before assigning it to a named NPC.

The caravan contains forty-two people. Twenty-nine begin at the surviving camp. Thirteen begin among the missing wagons. At the opening count, four of those thirteen have been heard but not reached, six are believed to be with named wagons, and three are missing without a reliable location.

## The three rescue pockets

### Upper wheel

**People:** Tovan, Deme, Salen, and Miri.  
**Animals:** Clove and Sedge, still harnessed.  
**Property:** spice wagon, three sealed spice chests, one leaking oil jar, iron-rimmed wheels.  
**Ecological pressure:** the rear axle blocks the soilbearer road and crushed beetles beneath it.

- During Long Shade, the people are secure while the rear axle holds.
- The first clear warning is a widening split through the axle block.
- At Red Dusk, oil reaches the polished soil track unless caught or discarded.
- At Root Night, the wagon frame can no longer be recovered intact. People and animals remain reachable by separate lines.

The strongest rescue usually lowers the four people before unharnessing the team. Preserving the wagon may require leaving the team attached as a counterweight. Saving the team may require cutting the frame and spilling cargo into a controlled catch. Neither cost should be concealed.

### Split freight bed

**People:** Pelen, Yaro, Besh, Corda, and Fenn.  
**Animals:** Bell on the eastern half; Crook has crossed toward the low hollow.  
**Property:** Pelen's wagon frame, Ira's copper pipe, Corda's climbing rope, reservoir fittings.  
**Ecological pressure:** a crushed sequence of root gates prevents laden soilbearers from passing.

- During Long Shade, the western half remains broad enough for a stretcher.
- The first clear warning is daylight opening beneath the central break.
- At Red Dusk, Bell and most pipe stock move beyond an ordinary jump.
- At Root Night, Pelen can still be freed, but the eastern half becomes a separate moving pocket in the fiction.

Freeing Pelen safely takes support under the frame before the pin is cut. Pulling him out without support succeeds quickly but worsens the leg and removes him from later fieldwork. Saving Bell requires a handler across the widening break or a line the animal will trust.

### Low hollow

**People:** Jun, Iri, Mael, and Daro.  
**Animals:** Crook is present; Moth and Reed have moved toward the Warm Rain Basins.  
**Property:** grain wagon, passenger cart, seed grain, manifests, blankets, one handbarrow.  
**Ecological pressure:** warm rain rises through the pocket, and pale shoots root into spilled grain.

- During Long Shade, the hollow has water, shade, and a narrow western climb.
- The first clear warning is rain reaching the passenger-cart floor.
- At Red Dusk, the western climb will take people and hand loads but no intact wagon.
- At Root Night, the easiest route leads deeper toward the basins; returning to camp requires climbing or a redirected root.

Daro needs assisted movement. Mael can walk but may panic if rescuers depart without a specific return promise. Jun can lead Crook and help track the grain team. Iri will remain with Daro until a workable carrier exists; this is triage, not refusal of rescue.

## Running a rescue operation

Do not resolve a pocket with one general rescue check. Each operation should name five things:

1. **People:** Who is being moved or stabilized now?
2. **Load:** What weight or tension currently keeps them safe or trapped?
3. **Route:** Where will they stand when the operation succeeds?
4. **Means:** Which people, animals, tools, cargo, or ecological features bear the work?
5. **Abandonment:** What is knowingly left unsupported, unrecovered, or delayed?

A successful operation achieves its named human objective. Strong preparation may also preserve the supporting animal or property. A poor result should not convert a clear rescue into arbitrary death. Apply a consequence that follows from the attempted method:

- the route closes sooner;
- a rescuer ends on the far side of the next pulse;
- an animal becomes loose, strained, or frightened;
- a tool or cargo load is lost;
- an injury worsens from walking to carried movement;
- oil, grain, iron, or cut roots complicate the surrounding ecology; or
- the group succeeds but cannot attempt intact salvage before the stage changes.

Name the consequence immediately. Record it in the migration ledger and on Hessa's board.

## Triage and movement

Use functional movement states rather than vague injury labels.

| State | What it means | Typical requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Walking | Can use an ordinary stable route independently | A guide and time to hear the route plan |
| Assisted | Can move with one steady supporter or hand line | One capable companion; no heavy personal load |
| Carried | Cannot use the proposed route on foot | Two carriers, a litter, or a calm draft animal |
| Stabilize first | Movement now risks preventable serious harm | Treatment, bracing, cooling, or release from load |
| Voluntary remainder | Immediate danger has been addressed and the person chooses a later task or route | A real alternative, current information, and a recorded choice |

A person under immediate load does not meaningfully consent to remain in danger. Free or stabilize them before treating continued participation as voluntary.

## Burdens and routes

These are benchmarks, not a separate encumbrance subsystem.

- One capable traveler can guide one assisted person or carry one substantial hand load.
- Two capable travelers can carry one person on a prepared litter.
- One calm draft animal can carry a litter or two balanced repair loads.
- A complete team can pull one intact wagon only on a broad, stable root road.
- A narrow root path takes people, animals in single file, and hand loads; it does not take an intact wagon.
- A wagon dismantled into two loads becomes movable after intact salvage has ceased to be possible.

When a route changes, state which burdens it still accepts. Do not announce only that the route is “harder.”

## Emergency use of property

The crisis does not erase ownership, but ownership does not forbid immediate life-saving use.

### Company stores

Orra may release company rope, canvas, tools, common food, and wagon fittings. A property recorder notes the use. No additional consent is needed.

### Property with a present owner

Ask the owner when time permits. Most owners have a condition rather than a flat refusal. Ira will lend pipe for a recoverable trough. Corven will spend seed grain to save a named person. Della will yield one medicinal planter if living soil is demonstrably necessary.

### Property whose owner is absent

The party may use it without prior consent when it is the least destructive workable means to save a named person from immediate danger. Hessa records what was used, why alternatives failed, and who witnessed the decision. Ecological repair, seed work, and faster salvage are not automatically immediate life-saving necessity.

### Compensation and later claims

A promise of compensation does not make a destructive choice free. It does keep the choice from vanishing after the adventure. Record the owner, the material, the use, and whether the owner later ratifies, disputes, or forgives the claim.

## Principal practical aims

No principal NPC is a concealed antagonist. Their aims conflict because the situation cannot honor every obligation at once.

### Orra Venn

- **Asks:** account for every person, establish two departure routes, preserve a functioning caravan core.
- **Will concede:** the caravan may divide; company wagons and stores may be dismantled; a voluntary seed group may remain.
- **Will not accept:** declaring unresolved people dead, calling silence consent, or spending private cargo on a speculative goal without a defensible rule.

### Hessa Clay

- **Asks:** use names, update the board after every return, carry the actual proposal to people expected to risk it.
- **Will concede:** Tovan may choose repair work after rescue; several groups may leave at different times.
- **Will not accept:** “everyone is coming,” “the injured agreed,” or any plan that counts absent people as cargo.

### Nahl Reed

- **Asks:** preserve observations, test each part of Sio's account, and avoid draining or cutting before understanding circulation.
- **Will concede:** rescue takes labor away from investigation; a safe departure without a seed is a successful outcome.
- **Will not accept:** taking the only mature knot, treating resemblance as proof, or claiming Merewash's need settles ecological uncertainty.

### Pelen Marr

- **Asks:** free the trapped people, then repair the damage caused by the wagons.
- **Will concede:** his own frame, tools, and labor; a worse prognosis for his leg if that is the informed cost of an urgent rescue.
- **Will not accept:** calling the accident sabotage or pretending he owns Ira's copper and the Pell family's cargo.

### Sio Tern

- **Asks:** test all six inherited lines against material evidence and keep alternate readings visible.
- **Will concede:** the account may be wrong, incomplete, or accurate only for the previous crossing.
- **Will not accept:** becoming a priest of the migration merely because no one else remembers it.

### Jun Alder

- **Asks:** rescue the low-hollow group, recover the animals if possible, and hear what continuing east would actually cost.
- **Will concede:** return to camp long enough to report, help others reach an exit, and defer a final choice until immediate danger has passed.
- **Will not accept:** being dragged west as a child who cannot choose or praised into remaining as a symbol of wonder.

## Reports and informed departure

Every returning group should answer four questions at camp or send them by a reliable courier:

1. Who was physically seen?
2. What route is open now, and what burdens can use it?
3. What change will close or alter it?
4. What plan did each person actually hear and accept?

A person may consent to leave without endorsing the seed attempt. A person may volunteer for repair without agreeing to cross the Glass Waste. A group that has not heard a revised plan remains unresolved even if someone else thinks the plan is obviously best.

## Session 2 completion state

This ledger establishes the exact opening human state, rescue procedures, triage language, animal capacities, property rules, and six principal practical positions. Later sessions may add ecological operating detail and route fallbacks, but they should not alter the forty-two-person opening count or retroactively turn a practical disagreement into hidden malice.
