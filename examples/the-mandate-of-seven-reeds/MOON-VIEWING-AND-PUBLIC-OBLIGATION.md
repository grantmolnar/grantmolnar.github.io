# Moon-Viewing and Public Obligation

This is the GM-facing operating aid for the Evening of the Chrysanthemum Moon. The event is optional. It can convert private concessions into public political obligations, preserve face during relinquishment, expose incompatible bargains, and establish a remembered sequence for the Mandate. It cannot enact an office or substitute for the Chamber of Seven Reeds.

## Operating principle

Every ceremonial act must distinguish four things:

1. **what happened before the court;**
2. **what political obligation the witnesses now remember;**
3. **what authority was expressly included or excluded;** and
4. **what operative clause, covenant, proclamation, or ledger entry must follow.**

Applause is not consent. Silence is not consent. A symbol is not an office. A beautiful sequence is not a complete settlement.

## Preparation checklist

Choose no more than three public obligations. For each, answer:

- What private bargain already exists?
- What exact act will make one part visible?
- What service is honored, and when does its emergency claim end?
- What power is accepted, surrendered, limited, or expressly excluded?
- What is the district, resource, trigger, condition, and term?
- Which third parties and other Reeds bear a changed burden?
- Who must witness the act?
- How may the recipient narrow, redirect, or refuse it?
- What operative follow-up makes the obligation executable?

Do not stage a bargain whose speakers have not agreed on these answers. The event should test agreements, not manufacture consent from surprise.

## Ceremonial instrument matrix

| Instrument | Can create | Cannot create safely | Primary failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seating | Visible cooperation on one defined matter; a missing successor or affected witness made visible | Permanent coalition, endorsement of every Reed, equality of rank | Rivals describe adjacency as a standing bloc |
| Poem | Memorable burden, restraint, sequence, or honorable account | Detailed jurisdiction, supply, appeal, succession, or emergency procedure | Metaphor broadens beyond the plain proposition |
| Gift | Repayment, honor, transfer of custody, or bounded undertaking | Several meanings at once; authority over resources carried by unnamed people | Acceptance is treated as consent to an unstated office or burden |
| Public thanks | Exact credit, compensation, and honorable closure of emergency service | Perpetual gratitude or title created by relief | Praise renews the claim it was meant to settle |
| Refusal | Boundary, narrower acceptance, substitute, condition, or return to drafting | Consent inferred from silence or humiliation | A cornered delegate complies publicly and obstructs implementation |
| Witnessed concession | Political obligation with named scope, term, conditions, dependencies, and follow-up | Operative law by implication | The audience remembers a broader promise than the speaker made |

## Recommended sequence

1. **Announce the evening's limited purpose.** Shun states that no act grants legal office.
2. **Seat responsibilities.** Arrange adjacency around the Reeds involved; announce disclaimers against permanent blocs.
3. **Name burdens before honors.** Reedwater witnesses and affected officers state labor, land, stores, routes, sacred duties, and records.
4. **Exchange poems or plain recitals.** Record the proposition before the art.
5. **Present or redirect gifts.** State one primary meaning and every physical burden.
6. **Offer thanks with a date and release.** Preserve service while closing emergency claims.
7. **Receive concessions and refusals.** Allow correction, narrowing, substitution, witnessed refusal, or withdrawal.
8. **Read back the obligation ledger.** Harunori and Shun compare exact words, intended meaning, and operative follow-up.
9. **Fill or leave empty the Emperor's cup.** Fill it only after reciprocal obligations to Imperial peace are real.

## Public obligation ledger

For each completed exchange, record:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Public words and object | What the court actually witnessed |
| Plain meaning | What the participants said the act meant |
| Service honored | The prior political debt preserved |
| Authority affected | Power accepted, relinquished, limited, or excluded |
| Condition and term | When the obligation matures and ends |
| Third parties | People, districts, offices, and communities affected |
| Dependencies | Other bargains or clauses that must remain true |
| Refusal or release | How the promise may be narrowed, discharged, or reopened |
| Operative follow-up | Clause, covenant, proclamation, transfer, or ledger entry |
| Breach consequence | What becomes politically or procedurally available after denial |

## Refusal ladder

1. **Correction:** reject the proposed meaning, preserve the act.
2. **Narrow acceptance:** accept a smaller office, district, resource, or term.
3. **Substitution:** offer another route, procedure, object, or responsible officer.
4. **Witnessed refusal:** establish a red line and the reason it protects the Mandate.
5. **Withdrawal:** end the public exchange and return the matter to private drafting.

A refusal is productive when it leaves a lawful next move. A refusal extracted through humiliation is usually a delayed obstruction.

## Third-party burden test

Before applause, ask:

- Which households provide labor, animals, boats, lodging, or rice?
- Which district receives less protection, worse access, or later reconstruction?
- Which sacred community must move, restore, or alter practice?
- Which office must pay, record, inspect, proclaim, or hear appeal?
- Which successor inherits the work, records, debts, and unresolved claims?
- Which party not present would describe this bargain differently?

If none of the speakers bears the material cost, the omitted party should be named before the act continues.

## Four usable event architectures

### A. Paired surrender

Two rivals perform reciprocal relinquishments in one sequence, followed by exact public credit. Best for Harvest and Sword. The principal risk is that one concession is immediate while the other depends on an unfunded transition.

### B. Chain of service

Several offices accept bounded undertakings that make one project possible: design, transport, consultation, records, review, and local burden. Best for Roads and Waters. The principal risk is that the court celebrates the chain while omitting the district or household carrying it.

### C. Public boundary

A power refuses a title, veto, secrecy claim, or emergency authority while accepting a narrower lawful duty. Best for Scorpion inspection, Phoenix consultation, Dragon review, or Seppun command. The principal risk is treating refusal as disloyalty and losing the useful narrower path.

### D. Delayed release

A temporary executor publicly commits to transfer once named capacity exists, and the empty place of succession is made visible. Best for fixed-term clan execution and provincial transition. The principal risk is writing conditions so vague that temporary authority never ends.

## Consequence guide

| Event result | Immediate effect | Drafting consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Reciprocal obligations witnessed | Retraction becomes costly; face is preserved | Import exact recitals and conditions into the Chamber |
| Useful refusal witnessed | Red line and lawful alternative become public | Draft the narrower path or explain why it was rejected |
| Third-party burden exposed | Ceremony slows or changes direction | Amend burden, resources, standing, or remedy before proceeding |
| Gift redirected | Meaning is narrowed without total rejection | Record the correct office, recipient, and undertaking |
| Delegate humiliated | Apparent concession, concealed retaliation | Expect obstruction, demand reaffirmation, or release the promise |
| Emperor's cup left empty | Private bargains proved politically fragile | Draft without relying on public obligation and preserve a fallback |
| Program broken | Incompatibility becomes public | Carry the dispute openly into the Chamber or Second Audience |

## Skipping the event

Skipping the moon-viewing is a complete route. The commission may take private agreements directly to the Chamber, where legal scope is easier to control. They lose public credit, reciprocal sequence, and a face-saving form of relinquishment. No necessary encounter, revelation, or valid Mandate depends on attending the evening.
