# Grain and Sword Settlement: The Mandate of Seven Reeds

This document records the complete Session 2 bargaining architecture for Harvest and Sword. It is a GM-facing drafting aid, not a hidden correct answer. The two Reeds should be negotiated together because every plausible military commission depends upon grain, every credible civilian granary needs protection, and the manner of demobilization determines whether the Emperor appears to reward mobilization or abandon those whom it saved.

## The parties inside the parties

### The Crane delegation

| Courtier | Governing concern | Necessary protection | Concession available | Failure pressure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Doji Masuyo | Civilian Harvest, public authorship, durable Crane influence | Military quotas cannot be changed at the granary door; Crane service must enter the record precisely | Imperial title, outside audit, Lion field command, shorter Crane tenure | Converts every later concession into a public-credit dispute |
| Daidoji Yorishige | Barges, escorts, losses, and repayment | Relief routes remain protected; military orders do not quietly become Crane debts | Daidoji escorts may serve beneath Lion operational command | Produces the full creditor ledger and exposes unfunded harmony |
| Asahina Chiharu | Seed rice, temple stores, displaced households, and the next harvest | Seed reserves require a second authority and public finding | Blesses a visible transfer of granary keys and supports local successors | Opposes her own delegation if tenure or honor is purchased with seed rice |

### The Lion delegation

| Courtier | Governing concern | Necessary protection | Concession available | Failure pressure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Akodo Tadanobu | One lawful chain of command with predictable support | Fixed military supply, defined emergency authority, removal and succession | Civilian granaries, outside audit, revocable Imperial title, shorter term | Refuses a decorative command rather than govern by a rival's daily favor |
| Matsu Kiyoka | Villages and duties that currently depend on Lion patrols | No checkpoint or granary guard leaves before a named replacement arrives | District-by-district withdrawal and surrender of civilian keys | Makes immediate withdrawal synonymous with abandonment |
| Ikoma Noritada | The public and historical meaning of Lion service | The record must distinguish disciplined relief from punished ambition | Turns staged demobilization into the completion of honorable service | Teaches future Lion commanders that swift aid will be rewritten as conquest |

The principal delegates can commit their clans, but internal support changes what they can concede without appearing negligent or dishonorable. A proposal that satisfies Masuyo and Tadanobu while ignoring Yorishige, Chiharu, Kiyoka, and Noritada will look stable in court and fail during implementation.

## Four kinds of grain

A Harvest clause should distinguish the stores below. Treating them as one pool creates apparent simplicity and actual conflict.

| Store | Primary purpose | Ordinary authority | Emergency access | Political danger |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Household rations | Keep displaced and surviving households alive | Civilian Harvest wardens | Harvest may accelerate distribution within published allotments | Military or courtly priorities can silently displace villages |
| Seed rice | Preserve the next planting and harvest | Civilian wardens with a second custodian or sealed register | Requires a named emergency finding by an outside authority | Consuming it makes present relief appear successful by transferring hunger into the future |
| Garrison allotment | Feed the lawful force actually retained | Harvest owes a fixed schedule to Sword | A bounded variance may be certified for a stated term | Without a guarantee, civilian officials can manipulate command; without a ceiling, command can consume Harvest |
| Emergency reserve | Meet flood, riot, invasion, or severed convoy | Separate keys or dual authorization | Opened only under defined triggers, with public quantity and repayment | Every office will call its own failure an emergency unless the trigger and debt are exact |

The military allotment should be stated in a form that can decline with the roster. A fixed quantity is easy to proclaim but may become excessive after withdrawal. A percentage follows the harvest but makes the garrison share every agricultural shock. A district guarantee protects remote patrols but may burden the villages least able to supply them. No formula removes politics; the Mandate should identify which risk it chooses.

## Harvest clause matrix

| Clause | Crane opening position | Lion requirement | Imperial concern | Provincial consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Title | Miya commissioner or Crane stewardship | Any title that does not interfere with the supply covenant | Office must remain visibly Imperial and revocable | Villagers need one name to petition and obey |
| Execution | Crane-trained civilian wardens for three years | Civilian execution is acceptable if quotas are enforceable | Emergency service must not mature into permanent title | Existing Crane competence keeps stores moving but may reproduce Crane dependence |
| Assessment | Village food and seed entered before military needs | Military needs must not be reduced by undisclosed local adjustments | One office cannot invent both its needs and the proof they were met | Transparent categories reveal who actually bears scarcity |
| Garrison supply | Fixed monthly allotment after civilian and seed assessment | Guaranteed quantity, route, and delivery date | Supply must not transfer granary jurisdiction to Sword | Predictability prevents armed bargaining at storehouse doors |
| Emergency variance | Outside certification, thirty-day duration, debt to Sword | Fast enough to answer a real military emergency | Trigger and termination must be exact | Temporary excess should not become a permanent village burden |
| Seed reserve | Dual authority and public finding before use | Beyond military reach except a declared catastrophe | The Empire should not borrow invisibly from the next harvest | Preserves future survival at the cost of present flexibility |
| Audit | Outside inspection; no Crane self-certification | Inspection cannot halt scheduled supply without cause | Accounts and Harvest cannot validate themselves | Reveals spoilage, diversion, and inflated relief claims |
| Succession | Two trained wardens per district | Replacement must not interrupt deliveries | Every office needs a successor before failure | Local competence can outlast clan tenure |
| Relief debt | Repaid by Accounts over several harvests | Must not reduce the garrison allotment unpredictably | Compensation is separate from jurisdiction | Merchants, temples, pilots, and households receive an answer rather than praise |
| Public credit | Exact proclamation naming Crane relief | Must also name Lion security and discipline | Gratitude cannot create title | Public memory determines future trust, obligation, and resentment |

## Sword clause matrix

| Clause | Lion opening position | Crane requirement | Imperial concern | Provincial consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Title | Imperial office under Seppun supervision | No Lion claim that emergency service created ownership | Office cannot appear extracted from the Throne | The garrison receives a lawful name rather than a clan occupation |
| Execution | Tadanobu as commander for three years | Command does not reach civilian granary policy | One legible chain must remain removable | A known commander can act and be blamed |
| Ordinary authority | Patrols, discipline, escorts, bandit response | Barge escorts remain tied to relief routes | Duties must be specific enough to prevent gradual expansion | Villages know which force answers which danger |
| Emergency authority | Thirty days for flood, riot, or invasion | Does not automatically open seed stores or rewrite all quotas | Trigger, renewal, and termination require outside participation | Fast command remains possible without becoming ordinary government |
| Supply | Scheduled allotment with convoy priority | Fixed ceiling, published route, and recorded excess | Readiness cannot depend on informal coercion | Soldiers need not bargain at granary doors |
| Requisition | Narrow power over transport and emergency stores | No seizure of civilian vessels, seed, or village allotments | Roads and Harvest retain their own jurisdictions | Emergency needs do not become general military preference |
| Removal | Seppun removal for refusal, excess, or failure | Removal must be usable before harm becomes permanent | A commission without removal is disguised clan title | The province has recourse against a commander who invokes necessity |
| Succession | Second officer invested before command begins | Successor inherits limits as well as duties | Death or disgrace must not reopen the occupation | Orders survive a person without becoming indefinite |
| Term | Three years, negotiable downward | Review after first autumn and reduction with local replacement | Temporary mobilization must remain temporary | The province gains time to restore its own capacity |
| Public submission | Standards lowered before commission | Crane escorts surrender independent command in the same settlement | Obedience must precede office | The settlement is seen as an Imperial peace rather than a bargain under arms |

## The demobilization sequence

The sequence below can be accelerated, but each omitted stage leaves a distinct unresolved claim.

1. **Submission:** Lion and Crane armed forces lower their standards beneath an Imperial banner before receiving new authority.
2. **Granary transfer:** Lion officers and Crane guards surrender civilian storehouse keys to named wardens.
3. **Road release:** emergency tolls and general convoy priority pass to the future Roads office; Sword retains only defined emergency precedence.
4. **Core commission:** a reduced garrison accepts the Imperial Sword commission and its removal clause.
5. **District replacement:** remaining forces depart only as named patrols, escorts, or lawful detachments assume their duties.
6. **Autumn review:** the court measures the harvest, remaining threats, local replacements, actual roster, and continuing supply burden.

The order matters. Commission before submission looks like reward for occupation. Withdrawal before replacement looks like courtly abandonment. Key transfer without a supply covenant moves armed bargaining from the storehouse interior to its gate. Review without authority to reduce the roster merely records inertia.

## Compatible settlement packages

These packages illustrate coherent combinations. The commission may combine clauses, but every change should preserve the relationship between authority, supply, surrender, and replacement.

### Package A: Paired Imperial commissions

**Harvest:** Miya title, Crane civilian execution for one year and one planting season, outside audit, local successor training.  
**Sword:** Seppun title, Tadanobu as commander for one year, fixed declining supply, district-by-district replacement, autumn review.  
**Public acts:** Crane transfer granary keys; Lion lower standards; both acts appear in the first proclamation.  
**Compensation:** Accounts repays documented Crane relief debts separately.

**Advantages:** Strong Imperial authorship, proven executors, precise reciprocal relinquishment, and a short path to local succession.  
**Risks:** Miya and Seppun capacity may be largely nominal; Crane and Lion staff can dominate their titleholders; local replacements may not be ready after one year.

### Package B: The long stewardship

**Harvest:** Crane title and execution for three years, outside audit, dual custody of seed reserves, fixed garrison allotment.  
**Sword:** Imperial title, Lion execution for three years, strong removal clause, matching three-year term and autumn reviews.  
**Public acts:** Both clans renounce the other's Reed and accept witnessed limits.  
**Compensation:** Crane debt repaid slowly; Lion supply guaranteed as a first charge after household and seed requirements.

**Advantages:** High administrative continuity and sufficient time to rebuild provincial capacity.  
**Risks:** The two clans may become a durable grain-and-sword compact; matching terms can become mutual protection; the Otomo may see emergency occupation translated into paired incumbency.

### Package C: Provincial restoration

**Harvest:** Crane wardens execute for one planting season, then local district wardens assume authority under an Imperial commissioner.  
**Sword:** Tadanobu commands the transition; most Lion forces depart within six months as a restored provincial garrison and rotating clan detachments replace them.  
**Public acts:** The witnesses countersign transfer records; Crane and Lion train their successors in public.  
**Compensation:** Relief debt and military losses are assessed by Accounts after the first harvest.

**Advantages:** Reedwater gains real agency; clan tenure is visibly transitional; Kiyoka receives named replacements rather than promises.  
**Risks:** The province lost much of its trained administration; local appointments can reproduce old household monopolies; rapid succession may make Crane and Lion personnel indispensable without legal title.

### Package D: Emergency command, narrow Harvest

**Harvest:** A civilian council under Miya title holds all ordinary stores; Crane personnel advise but do not execute as a clan office.  
**Sword:** Tadanobu receives broad but short emergency command through the next flood season, with strict seed and granary limits.  
**Public acts:** Crane receive extensive proclamation credit and repayment; Lion receive the only major operational commission.  
**Compensation:** Accounts begins repayment immediately through Imperial reserves and future provincial revenue.

**Advantages:** Clearly separates food from force and answers immediate security risk.  
**Risks:** Crane may regard credit without execution as elegant dispossession; civilian council capacity is uncertain; broad emergency command can reach Roads and Waters indirectly.

## Clause interactions and amendment consequences

| Change | Likely Crane response | Likely Lion response | Structural consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Increase Sword supply without reducing civilian allotments | Demands exact source or debt entry | Supports if deliverable | Creates an unfunded promise unless Harvest or Accounts bears the difference |
| Permit military access to seed rice | Chiharu opposes; Masuyo may lose internal support | Tadanobu accepts only under extraordinary trigger | Present readiness is purchased with future famine |
| Remove the autumn review | Demands shorter Harvest tenure or stronger audit | Matsu support rises; Tadanobu becomes less flexible | Emergency arrangements become ordinary through inertia |
| Order immediate Lion withdrawal | Crane gain negotiating power but lose protection for southern routes | Kiyoka resists; Tadanobu requests named replacements | Court may win authority while losing actual security |
| Give Crane relief debt priority over garrison supply | Yorishige supports | Lion treats command as financially hostage | Accounts dispute becomes military crisis |
| Give garrison supply priority over seed and household stores | Crane delegation fractures | Short-term Lion readiness rises | Province can obey and starve simultaneously |
| Replace exact public credit with equal thanks | Both principal delegations object | Both principal delegations object | Removes the political compensation that makes relinquishment possible |
| Grant Harvest to Crane until debt is repaid | Crane gain indefinite leverage | Lion distrust deepens | Every audit dispute extends jurisdiction |
| Make Lion withdrawal contingent on full local readiness | Crane fear permanent occupation | Kiyoka supports | The office controlling the readiness finding can prolong the occupation |
| Pair standard-lowering with immediate proclamation of commission | Crane demand a matching key-transfer ceremony | Lion can accept | Makes obedience precede office without producing an interval of humiliation |

## Questions the settlement must answer in text

1. Which stores can the Sword office reach without a second authorization?
2. Does the garrison allotment decline automatically with the roster?
3. Who records excess supply as debt, and against which office?
4. Who holds each key after the transfer ceremony?
5. Which checkpoints change hands first, and who receives them?
6. What happens to a village whose replacement patrol does not arrive?
7. Who may declare, renew, and end emergency command?
8. What public act demonstrates that the clans obeyed before receiving office?
9. What exact language credits Crane relief and Lion security?
10. Which losses are compensated without granting jurisdiction?
11. What can the autumn review reduce, remove, or extend?
12. Who succeeds the Harvest wardens and Sword commander if either office fails tomorrow?
