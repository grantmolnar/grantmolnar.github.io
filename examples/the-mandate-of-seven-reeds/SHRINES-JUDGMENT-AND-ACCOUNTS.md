# Shrines, Judgment, and Accounts

This aid records the session-3 political subsystem joining Phoenix sacred jurisdiction, Dragon review, and Scorpion inspection. It is not a single intended settlement. It identifies the powers hidden inside three broad offices, the procedures that make those powers usable, and several compatible architectures the player characters may adapt.

## The governing problem

The three delegations agree on one proposition that the rest of court finds uncomfortable: an office cannot be trusted merely because its purpose is honorable.

- Shrines can preserve obligations that engineers and governors would otherwise erase, but sacred necessity can become an unanswerable veto.
- Judgment can make divided offices answerable to one law, but recusal and appeal can become delay without remedy.
- Accounts can expose discrepancies that no office can see from inside its own books, but secrecy and inspection can become self-certifying power.

A stable settlement therefore does not ask which clan is most trustworthy. It separates the powers each office needs, names the procedure for using them, and gives every exceptional power a reviewer, deadline, and remedy.

## Phoenix internal ledger

### Isawa Etsuko

**Office sought:** A meaningful Shrines charter; title is negotiable.  
**Public ground:** Reconstruction must preserve right relations among communities, places, rites, and spirits.  
**Actual need:** A consultation duty that has standing, reasons, emergency rules, and consequences when ignored.  
**Acceptable concession:** Kaiu execution, Imperial title, Dragon procedural review, local succession.  
**Red line:** The office disturbing a site cannot be the final judge of whether consultation mattered.  
**Pressure style:** Demands names, rites, witnesses, and resulting duties whenever another courtier invokes “the kami” in the abstract.

### Shiba Koremori

**Office sought:** None independently; he wants the charter to survive contact with danger and distance.  
**Public ground:** Ritual obligations require living people who can reach the site safely and act before the emergency passes.  
**Actual need:** Deadlines, substitutes, emergency bypass, worksite protection, and a recorded spiritual debt after bypass.  
**Acceptable concession:** Shiba guards under Kaiu operational command; broad immediate action under short sunset.  
**Red line:** Officials may not manufacture permanent freedom from consultation by keeping an emergency open.  
**Pressure style:** Converts ceremonial language into routes, dates, escorts, and named people placed at risk.

### Asako Chise

**Office sought:** Local standing and succession rather than durable Phoenix administration.  
**Public ground:** Great Clan specialists did not create Reedwater’s ritual communities and should not replace them.  
**Actual need:** Household keepers, displaced congregations, and local deputies recognized in the charter.  
**Acceptable concession:** A Phoenix commissioner who teaches, coordinates, and leaves.  
**Red line:** Compensation paid only for structures while the community that maintained them is dispersed.  
**Pressure style:** Produces registers, family histories, and the names of people whom courtly abstractions have rendered invisible.

## Dragon internal ledger

### Kitsuki Harunori

**Office sought:** A common Judgment circuit; permanent Dragon title is unnecessary.  
**Public ground:** Divided offices require shared records, stated reasons, recusal, and review.  
**Actual need:** Evidence that can be compared, preserved reasons, and review outside the interested office.  
**Acceptable concession:** Imperial title, rotating clan seats, local petition officers, fixed term.  
**Red line:** Status or secrecy may restrict access to a record but cannot make contradictory records agree by decree.  
**Pressure style:** Lets each delegation propose its own judge until the pattern of self-judgment becomes embarrassing.

### Mirumoto Daizen

**Office sought:** No title; he wants review that can act before the dispute becomes moot.  
**Public ground:** Law that arrives after the grain is eaten, shrine dismantled, or witness removed is only history.  
**Actual need:** Substitute jurisdiction, deadlines, narrow interim restraint, and enforceable remedies.  
**Acceptable concession:** Emergency orders remain effective while reviewed; Judgment does not take operational command.  
**Red line:** Recusal without replacement or appeal that automatically suspends every contested act.  
**Pressure style:** Asks who carries the order, by what hour, and what remains in force until then.

### Togashi Sorin

**Office sought:** None.  
**Public ground:** Law must hear people who bear a burden even when they hold no title to the office or land.  
**Actual need:** Broad but testable standing and remedies that restore access, grain, labor, records, or office.  
**Acceptable concession:** Claims tested rigorously; compassion is not itself proof.  
**Red line:** A court available only to those with status enough to enter it.  
**Pressure style:** Introduces the claimant the proposed procedure forgot, then asks what Judgment is supposed to repair.

## Scorpion internal ledger

### Bayushi Namika

**Office sought:** Narrow inspection beneath outside title.  
**Public ground:** Reedwater’s emergency records cannot be compared and therefore cannot distinguish necessity, error, favoritism, and theft.  
**Actual need:** Access, comparison, protected reporting, and independence from the inspected office.  
**Acceptable concession:** Relinquish Accounts title, accept reciprocal inspection, serve Otomo or Miya authority.  
**Red line:** Findings may not be quietly buried by the office they embarrass.  
**Pressure style:** Offers a useful power, names why it is dangerous, and requires an explicit decision about who constrains it.

### Shosuro Mirei

**Office sought:** A protected reporting channel.  
**Public ground:** Some witnesses cannot testify openly before records and livelihoods are secured.  
**Actual need:** Separation of source identity from testable substance, sealed review, and protection against retaliation.  
**Acceptable concession:** Anonymous reports trigger preservation and inquiry, not sanction by themselves.  
**Red line:** Immediate disclosure of every source to the official being inspected.  
**Pressure style:** Removes information from the stage to demonstrate what a rule of perfect publicity would cost.

### Soshi Kureha

**Office sought:** Custody rules rather than broad discretion.  
**Public ground:** Inspection is meaningless if the record can change after it is seen.  
**Actual need:** Numbered copies, witnessed seals, duplicate indexes, and a chain of custody.  
**Acceptable concession:** Inspectors do not punish, remove, or rewrite the offices they inspect.  
**Red line:** Records seized without an index or reviewed without preserving which version was examined.  
**Pressure style:** Follows the document rather than the accusation and asks who could have changed it at every transfer.

## Accounts clause matrix

| Power | Minimum workable assignment | Dangerous concentration | Useful counterweight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Access | Statutory entry to defined records and sites | Unlimited search by private suspicion | Numbered inquiry and stated scope |
| Comparison | Common forms across Reeds | Inspector creates the categories after seeing the target | Public record standards |

## The sacred-consultation sequence

1. **Notice:** mark the work, site, date, and acting office before labor begins when time allows.
2. **Speaker:** identify the local keeper, affected households, temple authority, specialist, or provisional representative.
3. **Examination:** inspect the place and relevant records together.
4. **Response:** state the obligation, evidence, acceptable alternatives, and irreparable consequences.
5. **Decision:** the acting office accepts, conditions, or overrides the claim and assigns cost.
6. **Remedy:** restoration, relocation, compensation, or explanation occurs on a named date.
7. **Review:** Judgment tests standing, notice, reasons, recusal, and fulfillment without deciding theology.

## The recusal-and-appeal sequence

1. The magistrate discloses the material relationship.
2. The charter determines whether recusal is automatic, reviewable, or waivable.
3. Jurisdiction passes immediately to the first substitute.
4. If the substitute declines or misses the deadline, it passes to the failure substitute.
5. The transfer order states what administration continues and what irreversible act may pause.
6. Evidence and reasons are preserved even when identities or routes remain sealed.
7. The decision states remedy, responsible office, implementation date, and appeal.

A recused magistrate may preserve records or prevent imminent destruction. The magistrate may not decide the merits indirectly by choosing what survives for the substitute.

## The inspection-and-secrecy sequence

1. Open a numbered inquiry through scheduled audit, discrepancy, or complaint.
2. State the initial scope and record any expansion.
3. Copy, seal, or index records with witnesses and an outside duplicate register.
4. Separate witness identity from testable factual substance.
5. Classify the preliminary result as error, unresolved discrepancy, plausible necessity, or suspected misconduct.
6. Send the finding to the named reviewer; inspectors do not impose final sanction.
7. Enter a public disposition and assign a later review date to sealed material.

### The secrecy ladder

- **Public rule:** authority, scope, recusal, and deadlines are known.
- **Confidential source:** identity is restricted while substance is tested.
- **Sealed interim finding:** preservation and retaliation risks are addressed.
- **Reviewable finding:** outside authority can inspect method and evidence.
- **Public disposition:** responsibility and remedy enter Imperial memory.

## Cross-office interactions

### Shrines and Waters

Shrines can identify obligations and constrain methods. Waters retains engineering command. Emergency bypass creates a spiritual and material debt rather than erasing jurisdiction.

### Shrines and Judgment

Judgment reviews whether consultation was real, not whether the magistrate would interpret the spirit differently. This distinction must appear in the charter or both offices will attempt to govern the other.

### Accounts and Judgment

Accounts discovers and preserves discrepancies. Judgment tests evidence and decides sanction or remedy. Judgment may inspect the inspectors’ conflicts and methods.

### Accounts and Shrines

Protected reporting can shelter vulnerable keepers, while public dispositions keep sacred claims from resting permanently on secret testimony. Shrine stipends, relocation costs, and ritual labor belong in the common account.

### Accounts and Roads

Common manifests make Unicorn movement auditable. Inspectors may compare records without acquiring power to set tolls, seize routine cargo, or govern convoy priority.

### Judgment and Sword

Emergency military orders remain effective for a short term. Review can end renewal, require compensation, or support removal without turning magistrates into commanders.

## Dangerous combinations

- **Shrines title + classification + final review:** sacred obligation becomes self-certified jurisdiction.
- **Judgment + prosecution + enforcement command:** the court can manufacture the emergency that justifies its own orders.
- **Accounts + secret sources + sanction:** an inspector can punish on evidence no outside authority may test.
- **Recusal without substitutes:** any powerful office can abolish jurisdiction by multiplying conflicts.
- **Secrecy without expiration:** temporary witness protection becomes permanent invisible authority.
- **Publicity without stages:** witnesses disappear, records change, and only the safest information survives.
- **Emergency bypass without remedy:** every ordinary inconvenience becomes a reason never to consult again.

## Compatible packages

These packages are examples, not intended solutions.

### Package A: Imperial frame, specialized execution

- Miya or Otomo title to Shrines and Accounts; Seppun or Imperial title to Judgment.
- Phoenix specialists coordinate Shrines and train local successors.
- Dragon organize a mixed circuit with rotating seats.
- Scorpion inspectors receive access, preservation, and protected reporting but no sanction.
- Autumn review can end all three special commissions separately.

**Strength:** visible Imperial authorship and clear outside title.  
**Cost:** high coordination burden and risk that Imperial title becomes nominal dependence on clan executors.

### Package B: Local Shrines, Dragon circuit, Scorpion inspection

- Reedwater keepers and district representatives hold ordinary Shrines execution.
- Phoenix commissioner classifies damaged sites and trains deputies for one year.
- Dragon hold fixed-term Judgment execution beneath Imperial title.
- Scorpion inspect Accounts beneath Otomo protection and reciprocal audit.

**Strength:** strongest local continuity and clean review architecture.  
**Cost:** local representatives may be vulnerable to clan pressure and require real stipends, escorts, and petition access.

### Package C: Phoenix title, mixed implementation

- Phoenix hold Shrines title for a fixed term; local keepers execute ordinary rites.
- Kaiu Waters may override after consultation under emergency rules.
- Judgment seats rotate; no Dragon permanent title.
- Accounts belongs to Miya administration with Scorpion comparison teams.

**Strength:** powerful spiritual coordination during reconstruction without surrendering all execution.  
**Cost:** the Phoenix title may outlast the emergency unless succession tests are measurable.

### Package D: Emergency reconstruction with retrospective teeth

- Waters receives broad immediate authority for one season.
- Shrines consultation may be bypassed only under named triggers and creates automatic restoration debt.
- Judgment reviews every bypass within three days and every renewal before it acts.
- Accounts preserves all orders, manifests, and compensation debts; sealed sources receive fixed review dates.

**Strength:** fastest material response.  
**Cost:** requires unusually strong records, review, compensation, and willingness to remove officials after useful action.

## Questions for the drafting chamber

1. Who may speak for a shrine when the old keeper is dead or disputed?
2. Which obligations can condition work, and which can stop it?
3. What survives an emergency bypass as a debt?
4. Which relationships trigger automatic recusal?
5. Who acts when both the judge and first substitute are conflicted?
6. What remains in force during appeal?
7. Which parts of inspection belong to Accounts, Judgment, and the titleholder?
8. What may remain sealed, who may open it, and when must secrecy be justified again?
9. Who inspects the inspectors?
10. What concrete event ends each imported commission?
