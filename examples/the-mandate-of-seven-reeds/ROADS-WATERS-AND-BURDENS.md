# Roads, Waters, and Burdens

This is the session-4 GM aid for the Unicorn, Crab, and Reedwater subsystem. It turns movement, reconstruction, requisition, labor, displacement, and provincial standing into clauses that can be combined, amended, and enforced. It does not prescribe one correct Mandate.

The political principle is simple: **every office may name what it needs, but no office may treat the province as an uncounted reserve.** Roads and Waters consume the same animals, carts, laborers, rice, timber, guards, routes, and administrative attention that the other Reeds also require. Reedwater's witnesses make those collisions visible.

## Roads clause matrix

| Power | Questions the Mandate must answer | Narrow grant | Broad grant | Primary danger |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Title | Who speaks for the road network in the Emperor's name? | Miya or another Imperial commissioner | Unicorn title and execution | Emergency success hardens into jurisdiction |
| Route planning | Who sets ordinary routes, seasonal alternatives, and repairs? | Executor follows a published plan | Executor may redraw routes under review | One district's convenience becomes another's isolation |
| Execution | Who maintains stations, bridges, ferries, remounts, and schedules? | District or contracted execution | Province-wide executor | Local capacity is replaced rather than trained |
| Priority | Which traffic moves first, and who may change the order? | Published classes with short emergency variance | Executor or commander controls priority | Status or military leverage replaces law |
| Requisition | Who may take animals, carts, boats, lodging, fodder, and tools? | Named officials under a receipt schedule | Any officer invoking necessity | The next season's transport is consumed invisibly |
| Valuation | Who records condition and value? | Public schedule with appeal | Requisitioning office values its own taking | Compensation becomes whatever the debtor prefers |
| Repayment | Which office owes, in what form, and by what date? | Named debt and staged payment | General promise of later relief | Acknowledged debts are never operationally restored |
| Tolls | Which traffic pays, at what crossings, and where does revenue go? | Proclaimed maintenance toll | Executor sets and keeps tolls | Movement becomes private taxation |
| Customs | Who compares manifests and may cargo be detained? | Access, preservation, short detention, outside sanction | Inspector controls seizure and route | Inspection becomes road government |
| Closure | Who closes a route, for how long, and what alternative remains? | Cause, duration, alternative, reopening officer | General security closure | Dependence can be manufactured for bargaining |
| Succession | Who receives horses, records, staff, and seals after the term? | Named trained district successors | Renewal by default | Temporary execution becomes permanent expertise monopoly |
| Review | What measures matter? | Travel time, closures, requisition repayment, toll revenue, local readiness | General Imperial pleasure | Review becomes ceremonial praise |

### Recommended ordinary priority classes

1. Imperial dispatches and authenticated corrections.
2. Immediate threats to life, dikes, fire, disease, or public safety.
3. Scheduled seed, relief, shrine relocation, and essential reconstruction traffic.
4. Ordinary military, official, merchant, and private movement according to the published calendar.

An emergency may elevate one class for a short period. The order must state which traffic is displaced, when the change expires, and who restores the ordinary sequence.

## The requisition sequence

A valid taking should pass through all eight steps.

1. **Authority:** ordinary schedule or named emergency order.
2. **Identification:** property, service, owner or responsible household, and destination.
3. **Condition:** witnessed state before service.
4. **Valuation:** published category or stated later method.
5. **Return or loss:** receiving record for return, injury, consumption, or destruction.
6. **Debtor:** the benefiting office accepts the obligation.
7. **Repayment:** coin, replacement in kind, tax credit, fodder grant, or another named form with a date.
8. **Appeal:** identity, condition, valuation, nonreturn, and nonpayment may be challenged without retaliation through future road access.

The sequence is not decorative paperwork. It prevents successive officials from treating the same household as untouched and prevents Accounts from inheriting a debt with no author.

## Waters clause matrix

| Power | Questions the Mandate must answer | Narrow grant | Broad grant | Primary danger |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Title | Who answers publicly for the water system? | Imperial titleholder | Kaiu or clan title | Responsibility becomes a claim to the province |
| Survey and design | Who measures, proposes alternatives, and states consequences? | Kaiu technical service | Waters executor controls all design | Political sacrifice hides inside certification |
| Ordinary execution | Who maintains dikes, canals, ferries, gates, and drains? | District officers under standards | Central commission | Local maintenance skill withers |
| Labor assessment | Who may call households, specialists, soldiers, or hired crews? | One cumulative ledger and ceilings | Executor calls labor as needed | Every office consumes the same people independently |
| Materials | Who opens quarries, fells timber, buys rope and iron, and controls stores? | Purpose-bound stockpiles | Executor redirects all provincial materials | One work solves itself by destroying another |
| Work-site command | Who directs sequence, guards camps, and changes daily orders? | One commander within camp charter | Military command over all nearby districts | Reconstruction becomes occupation |
| Sacred consultation | Who identifies speakers, notice, examination, and remedy? | Procedure with emergency bypass | Shrines veto or engineer ignores | Delay or erasure becomes unreviewable |
| Relocation | Who chooses, warns, moves, compensates, and restores communities? | Separate political authorization | Executor moves whatever the plan requires | Displacement appears as technical necessity |
| Emergency action | Which acts preserve site safety, the plan, or require political sacrifice? | Three-level authority | General necessity power | Convenience and catastrophe become indistinguishable |
| Indemnity | Who answers when another office changes the plan? | Recorded amendment transfers cost and risk | Engineer bears all blame | Other offices gain consequence-free amendment power |
| Maintenance | Who inspects and repairs after construction? | Funded local succession | Renew the emergency commission | The province inherits drawings without capacity |
| Review | What evidence can change design, personnel, or powers? | Technical, fiscal, burden, and consequence review | Honorary assessment | Failure cannot be attributed or corrected |

## The two major designs

The major designs are useful because neither is a concealed correct answer.

### Eastern wall

**Advantages**

- closes before the next heavy rain;
- uses fewer labor days and less timber;
- protects the principal granaries;
- preserves the present western road; and
- requires less integrated emergency authority.

**Burdens**

- relocates Reed Bank and one neighboring village;
- translates Brother Hojun's shrine community;
- leaves some graves unmoved before work begins;
- gives the relocated households poorer fields; and
- increases downstream pressure unless a spill basin is purchased and preserved.

### Northern channel

**Advantages**

- preserves the two villages;
- leaves the shrine community close to its river;
- creates greater long-term water capacity; and
- reduces dependence on one embankment line.

**Burdens**

- cuts the current western road;
- requires a new bridge, ford, and two remount stations;
- consumes nearly twice the timber and labor;
- continues into planting season;
- crosses land claimed by three samurai households; and
- leaves one district under weaker protection until the second phase ends.

Any hybrid is a new design. It must receive a new burden entry rather than being treated as the moral average of the two plans.

## The household burden ledger

The common ledger is the session's central instrument. It may be kept by Accounts, a Miya implementation office, a joint clerkship, or a provincial commission. Custody is negotiable; common use is not.

For each household, work group, shrine community, association, or local samurai house, record:

| Burden category | Minimum fields |
|---|---|
| Labor | People, skill, office authorizing, dates, location, prior service, return date |
| Military service | Mustered person, patrol duty, equipment, duration, household substitution |
| Transport | Animal, cart, boat, driver, condition, destination, debtor, return or loss |
| Lodging and fodder | People or animals housed, quantity, duration, authorizing office, repayment |
| Rice and stores | Type of rice, quantity, purpose, whether debt, tax, gift, or requisition |
| Tools and materials | Item, custodian, work purpose, condition, replacement duty |
| Displacement | Household, land, water, road, work, graves, shrine, possession date |
| Sacred service | Named duty, people required, calendar, substitution limits |
| Administrative service | Messenger, record copying, petitions, local deputy work |
| Remedy | Exemption, substitution, wage, tax credit, replacement, payment date, appeal |
| Release | Date the burden ends and earliest lawful date of another call |

### Ledger principles

- **Cumulative:** every Reed reads the same prior burdens.
- **Specific:** bodies, carts, and rice are named rather than described as available.
- **Asymmetric when justified:** dangerous and skilled work may be distributed unequally, but the reason is recorded.
- **Time-aware:** planting, harvest, funerals, shrine calendars, and earlier service matter.
- **Resource-aware:** a local successor without stores, horses, records, or pay is not a successor.
- **Remedial:** an accurate record that cannot cancel, sequence, compensate, or review a collision merely documents ruin.

## The three levels of emergency authority

### Level 1: site safety

The Waters executor may stop work, evacuate a camp, consume assigned material, and alter work sequence to prevent immediate injury. A daily record is required; outside preauthorization is not.

### Level 2: plan-preserving emergency

Under named physical triggers, the executor may reinforce, open temporary relief, redirect crews, or close a work route within the approved risk map. A second officer witnesses the act, and review follows within three days.

### Level 3: political sacrifice

Flooding an inhabited district, abandoning a protected route, destroying a protected site, consuming seed rice, or changing which district receives primary protection requires a named titleholder or Imperial emergency authority. If communication is impossible, the executor may act only to prevent greater imminent loss; the act automatically creates notice, review, a public account of alternatives, and a restoration debt.

The distinction gives engineers enough freedom to save lives without giving them covert sovereignty over which lives and districts receive protection.

## Work-camp charter

Every large camp should name:

1. work-site command;
2. the source and duration of each labor call;
3. food, lodging, pay or tax credit, and medical care;
4. funerary and sacred provision;
5. military discipline versus ordinary Judgment;
6. petitions and protected safety reports;
7. conflict rules among Crab, Lion, provincial, and other guards;
8. family notification and treatment of death or injury;
9. route and date of return; and
10. the officer who closes the camp and accounts for its stores.

## Provincial standing clause matrix

| Form of standing | Minimal form | Strong form | Capture risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Receipt | District witnesses receive the original proclamation | They receive corrections and implementation schedules automatically | Officeholder controls who counts as a witness |
| Ledger access | Affected parties inspect their own entries | Recognized representatives inspect district-wide collisions | Confidential details become political weapons |
| Petition | Patron presents the claim | Sponsor supplies access but cannot withdraw the claimant's petition | Patron owns or edits testimony |
| Review trigger | Officials decide whether complaints warrant review | Objective thresholds automatically open review | Thresholds are gamed or become rigid |
| Return testimony | Witnesses may be invited at autumn | Renewal cannot occur until required Reedwater testimony is heard | Court selects only grateful witnesses |
| Local succession | Surviving households receive offices | Qualification, training, resources, pay, and removal are explicit | Status preserves failed or corrupt incumbents |

Standing does not make the witnesses lawmakers or equal-status delegates. It creates formal ways to show that implementation premises are false and requires the governing offices to answer.

## Resource-and-burden ledger by settlement architecture

The following packages are coherent starting positions. Each can be amended. Each has costs.

### Package A: Imperial spine, clan execution

**Allocation**

- Miya title and dispatch for Roads.
- Unicorn execution for one year and one planting season.
- Imperial title for Waters with Kaiu execution for eighteen months.
- Scorpion inspection of common manifests and stockpile records.
- Dragon review of requisition, recusal, and relocation procedure.
- Local successors trained beneath both commissions.

**Resource logic**

- Roads receives a protected remount, bridge, and station budget.
- Waters receives purpose-bound timber, stone, rope, iron, and camp rice.
- One burden ledger governs both commissions.
- Emergency diversions transfer debt to the benefiting office.

**Political virtue**

The Throne visibly holds both Reeds while using proven clan competence.

**Political cost**

The Imperial titleholders may depend on clan information, and local succession can become perpetually almost ready.

### Package B: Provincial commission with service covenants

**Allocation**

- Natsugawa Kenta and vetted provincial deputies execute ordinary Roads and Waters.
- Unicorn and Kaiu specialists hold fixed service covenants for routes, design, dangerous works, and training.
- Miya carry orders and certify district succession.
- Accounts pays contractual debts and preserves the common ledger.

**Resource logic**

- Provincial stores, horses, and staff must be assigned immediately rather than after training.
- Specialist covenants state deliverables, refusal rights, deadlines, and replacement.
- Great Clan officers cannot withhold expertise to force renewal.

**Political virtue**

Local Imperial government is restored early, and clan service does not become office.

**Political cost**

The provincial administration is damaged and may fail under pressure; rejecting technical advice must have a clear consequence.

### Package C: Emergency reconstruction directorate

**Allocation**

- For six months, a Waters directorate receives work-site command, materials, assigned guards, and emergency transport priority.
- Roads remains Miya-titled with Unicorn execution, but Level-2 Waters movements outrank ordinary traffic.
- Seppun certify extraordinary force and Level-3 triggers.
- Judgment, Shrines, Accounts, and provincial witnesses retain review and standing.

**Resource logic**

- A protected emergency stockpile is created.
- Civilian labor calls have ceilings; soldiers discharge part of the burden.
- Every exceptional priority, closure, diversion, and bypass expires automatically.

**Political virtue**

The province can move quickly before the next rains without pretending ordinary administration already exists.

**Political cost**

The directorate resembles a temporary government and may build constituencies around its renewal.

### Package D: Distributed corridors and basins

**Allocation**

- Different districts or functions receive different executors: Unicorn western routes, Crane southern barges, provincial officers local roads, Kaiu major works, local crews ordinary maintenance.
- One Miya dispatch law, one requisition chain, one burden ledger, and one review circuit bind the distributed system.

**Resource logic**

- Stockpiles and debts are assigned by corridor or project.
- Shared crossings have named lead and substitute authority.
- The Mandate pays explicitly for duplication where resilience is desired.

**Political virtue**

No single executor can close or own the province, and existing successful networks remain usable.

**Political cost**

Coordination is expensive. Without one priority law and one burden ledger, distribution becomes several private systems beneath an Imperial title.

## Dangerous combinations

- **Roads priority + Sword emergency command without expiry:** military movement can displace every civilian obligation indefinitely.
- **Roads requisition + Accounts control:** the taking office can value and forgive its own debt.
- **Waters labor + Sword muster without a common ledger:** the same households are called twice and blamed for failing both duties.
- **Waters materials + unrestricted emergency diversion:** every other office's stores become a hidden reserve.
- **Kaiu execution + final relocation authority:** political sacrifice disappears inside technical certification.
- **Shrines veto + no emergency bypass:** sacred consultation can become control of the engineering calendar.
- **Emergency bypass + no restoration debt:** necessity permanently erases the obligation bypassed.
- **Witness hearing + no later standing:** court legitimacy is borrowed once and never exposed to consequence again.
- **Local succession + no resources:** office returns to Reedwater while capability remains with the Great Clans.

## Amendment test

Whenever a Roads or Waters clause changes, ask all five questions.

1. **What new authority exists?**
2. **What resource carries it?**
3. **Which household, district, office, or community bears the burden?**
4. **Which prior promise is delayed, reduced, or broken?**
5. **Who records, reviews, and remedies the consequence?**

An amendment that answers only the first question is a courtly wish. An amendment that answers all five is a potential Mandate.
