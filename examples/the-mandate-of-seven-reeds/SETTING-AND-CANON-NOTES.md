# Setting and Canon Notes

*The Mandate of Seven Reeds* is an original adventure written for the Rokugan setting. It is not an official Legend of the Five Rings publication.

## Canonical anchors used

- Rokugan is governed by an Emperor served by the Imperial families.
- The Otomo are used as courtly and bureaucratic guardians of Imperial authority.
- The Seppun are used as protectors of the Emperor and the lawful military order around the Throne.
- The Miya are used as heralds, messengers, and administrators concerned with carrying Imperial commands across the realm.
- The adventure includes the Crab, Crane, Dragon, Lion, Phoenix, Scorpion, and Unicorn Great Clans.

## Deliberate flexibility

- The Emperor, reign, and exact year are unnamed.
- Reedwater Province, its dead governor, the seven offices, and every principal NPC are original.
- No metaplot event is required.
- The adventure assumes Otosan Uchi and an operating Imperial court but can be moved to another Imperial residence with nomenclature changes.
- Game-system mechanics are excluded from the authoritative source. A later overlay can add edition-specific skills, honor, glory, status, strife, opportunities, duels, and mass-battle support.

## Adaptation pressure points

A GM placing the adventure in a specific timeline should check:

1. the identity and temperament of the reigning Emperor;
2. the current number and status of Great Clans;
3. the location and condition of the Imperial capital;
4. the office structure surrounding the Emerald Champion and Imperial magistrates; and
5. any active clan wars that would change the meaning of troops already deployed in Reedwater.

## Session 2 name and family-role check

The principal Crane and Lion names introduced or retained in this session were screened against currently indexed official and public Legend of the Five Rings materials. Two names that collided with established characters were replaced. This is a practical collision check rather than a claim of exhaustive uniqueness across every edition, card, fiction, tournament scenario, and fan publication.

The internal delegation roles follow durable family identities rather than edition-specific mechanics: Doji courtiers frame legitimacy, Daidoji officers handle practical protection and supply, Asahina priests guard peace and spiritual obligations, Akodo commanders organize lawful strategy, Matsu officers embody martial honor, and Ikoma historians govern memory and public credit.


## Session 3 family-role and name check

The Phoenix, Dragon, and Scorpion delegation roles were grounded in stable family identities rather than edition-specific mechanics. The official Legend of the Five Rings clan materials describe the Isawa as elementalists who communicate with spirits, the Shiba as the Phoenix warrior and yojimbo family, and the Asako as the clan's scholars; the Kitsuki are investigators associated with physical evidence, while the Mirumoto govern and fight for the Dragon and the Togashi pursue monastic wisdom; the Bayushi are courtly manipulators, the Shosuro combine artistry with intelligence work, and the Soshi specialize in concealment and esoteric methods. The adventure uses those broad identities while keeping each NPC's administrative theory original.

The principal and supporting names used in the three delegations were screened against currently indexed official and public Legend of the Five Rings materials. No exact collision was found for Isawa Etsuko, Shiba Koremori, Asako Chise, Kitsuki Harunori, Mirumoto Daizen, Togashi Sorin, Bayushi Namika, Shosuro Mirei, or Soshi Kureha. This remains a practical search rather than an exhaustive guarantee across every edition, card, fiction, scenario, and fan work.

Session 3 also avoids treating the Kitsuki method as a modern evidentiary system transplanted wholesale into Rokugan. Harunori uses physical and documentary evidence as a check on convenient memory while still operating inside a status-conscious Imperial legal order. Likewise, the Scorpion are neither the secret cause of Reedwater's failure nor generically omniscient; their advantage lies in comparison, source protection, and record custody, each of which the Mandate can divide and review.

## Session 4 family-role, witness, and name check

The Unicorn and Crab roles remain grounded in broad, durable setting identities rather than edition-specific mechanics. The Ide are used as diplomats, traders, and institutional negotiators; the Shinjo as riders and practical route commanders; and the Iuchi as specialists able to recognize spiritual and cultural obligations along traveled landscapes. The Kaiu provide engineering and fortification expertise, the Hida provide hard military and work-site security, and the Yasuki provide procurement, trade, and material accounting. The adventure uses those identities to create internal disagreement rather than treating either clan as a single policy position.

The principal supporting names Ide Sachiko, Shinjo Boroldai, Iuchi Nergui, Kaiu Masashige, Hida Chihaya, and Yasuki Kameyo were screened against currently indexed official and public Legend of the Five Rings materials. No exact same-family collision was found. This remains a practical search rather than an exhaustive guarantee across every edition, card, fiction, scenario, and fan work.

Rei, Brother Hojun, Saburo, and Natsugawa Kenta are not treated as a democratic delegation or a unified “voice of the people.” Their access remains status-constrained and mediated through Imperial procedure, sponsors, records, and recognized duties. Their political force comes from implementation knowledge, material capacity, sacred continuity, and local office rather than modern representational authority. House Natsugawa is an original minor vassal house of the fictional Reedwater governorship.

## Session 5 court-practice posture

The Evening of the Chrysanthemum Moon, its seven reflected pools, the empty Imperial cup, and its ceremonial docket are original constructions for this adventure. They are not presented as a universal named institution of Rokugan or as an edition-specific social subsystem.

The scene preserves the setting's status hierarchy: the party does not abolish precedence or treat seating as egalitarian representation. Its influence concerns adjacency, sequence, ceremonial meaning, and the placement of recognized witnesses within an event authorized by the Imperial court. Poems, gifts, public thanks, refusals, and witnessed relinquishments are used as political instruments whose force comes from reputation, memory, and face; operative authority still requires the Mandate, a covenant, proclamation, transfer record, or other explicit Imperial procedure.

The Reedwater witnesses are permitted to state implementation facts and burdens, not to vote as equals of Great Clan delegates. Their presence complicates courtly performance by making absent costs visible while retaining the status constraints established in Session 4.

## Session 6 court-practice posture

The Chamber and Second Audience use original procedures designed for the adventure rather than claiming to reproduce a single canon-era Imperial bureaucracy. Their political assumptions remain setting-consistent: hierarchy matters; public form and remembered obligation matter; the Emperor does not require a vote to command; service, submission, face, and institutional capacity are distinct; and a samurai may owe obedience while preserving a precise objection in the record.

The outcome architectures are intentionally era-flexible. A campaign may substitute named Imperial officials, offices, court factions, or historical pressures without changing the Seven Reeds model. No outcome assumes a specific reigning Emperor or metaplot settlement.
