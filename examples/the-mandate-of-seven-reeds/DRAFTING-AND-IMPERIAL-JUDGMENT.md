# Drafting and Imperial Judgment: The Mandate of Seven Reeds

This is the GM-facing operating aid for the Chamber of Seven Reeds and the Second Audience. It converts the political concessions developed elsewhere into an instrument that can be executed, amended, proclaimed, challenged, and reviewed.

The commission is not trying to discover an intended allocation. There is no canonical Mandate. The drafting problem is to make authority, capacity, constraint, burden, and transition visible enough that the Emperor can choose among real political objects rather than elegant aspirations.

## Dispatch posture

The Chamber and Second Audience do not ask whether enough interests ratify a compact. They ask whether the Emperor can issue an order that present possessors will enact, named recipients can receive, provincial households can survive, and later review can correct. Every elegant clause must therefore terminate in a submission, recipient, resource, route, receipt, or remedy.
## The settlement stack

A complete settlement uses several instruments. Do not force every political function into the operative Mandate.

| Instrument | Governing use | What belongs here | Principal danger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mandate | Durable Imperial allocation | Title, execution, limits, resources, emergency powers, succession, term, review, remedy | Courtesy language silently changes authority |
| Recitals | Purpose, interpretation, honor | Disaster, Imperial purpose, services, public relinquishments, reasons for exceptional structure | Praise is later treated as title or renewal |
| Schedules | Changeable implementation detail | Districts, stores, routes, transfer dates, deputies, work phases, review dates | A schedule alters central power without proper amendment |
| Side covenants | Reciprocal operational duties | Supply, repayment, training, custody, compensation, inspection cooperation | Breach quietly transfers an unrelated office |
| Proclamations | Public intelligibility and obedience | Who commands, lawful burdens, emergency limits, petition routes, closure of old claims | Simplification erases a safeguard or creates a broader claim |
| Registers | Evidence and institutional memory | Burdens, requisitions, protected sources, transfers, debts, corrections, reviews | Record custody becomes authority over the underlying office |
| Submission instruments | Conversion of possession into lawful service | Court act, matched field order, receiving officer, effective hour, receipt, keys, seals, standards, ledgers, rosters, inventories | Symbolic surrender or an undeliverable order leaves practical command unchanged |

A single bargain may require entries in several instruments. A Lion Sword commission might appear in the Mandate, receive grain through a covenant, transfer command through a submission instrument, reach districts through a proclamation, and end through a schedule tied to replacement capacity.

## Universal clause matrix

Complete this matrix for each Reed before the Chamber certifies either proposal.

| Field | Required question | Common evasion | Minimum repair |
|---|---|---|---|
| Imperial title | Who answers to the Throne? | Naming an honored figure with no power or information | Reporting duty, appointment and removal rule, conflict voice |
| Execution | Who performs daily work and gives routine orders? | “The office” acts without a person, district, or chain | Named executor, boundary, ordinary order chain |
| Audit and inspection | Who may see, compare, preserve, report, restrain, and refer? | Treating access as sanction or secrecy as final judgment | Separate powers, outside review, publication or correction point |
| Resources | What people, stores, revenue, transport, records, and specialists are assigned? | “Available provincial resources” | Inventory, custodian, lawful priority, replacement obligation |
| Ordinary emergency action | What may happen immediately within the approved purpose? | Unlimited action whenever delay is inconvenient | Trigger, scope, notice, duration, record, retrospective review |
| Extraordinary sacrifice | Who may impose irreversible district-level loss? | Hiding sovereignty inside technical or military discretion | Named political authority, alternatives considered, remedy and review |
| Ordinary succession | Who inherits after routine vacancy or expiration? | Naming a successor without training or records | Transfer package, preparation duty, capacity test |
| Failure succession | Who acts during accusation, refusal, recusal, incapacity, or conflicting orders? | Requiring disputed holder to authorize its own replacement | Automatic trigger, independent certifier, interim power |
| Term and transition | When does authority begin, narrow, transfer, or end? | “Until the emergency ends” | Date or observable condition, certifier, consequence of noncertification |
| Review and remedy | Who may petition, where, when, and for what relief? | A later audience with no interim protection or power to change anything | Standing, route, deadline, interim order, available remedies |

## Drafting sequence

### 1. State the Imperial purpose

Write one sentence. It should identify the governed object and public end, not the coalition that negotiated it. Examples include preserving Reedwater through the next rains, restoring lawful provincial government, or rebuilding without rewarding unauthorized mobilization.

### 2. Mark forbidden or temporary combinations

Identify combinations that should not be held by one interest. If a dangerous combination is permitted temporarily, specify why capacity requires it and which additional safeguards make the risk bearable.

### 3. Complete all seven clause matrices

Do not begin with the easiest offices and leave the hard ones as prose. A blank field is a decision to let future power fill it.

### 4. Draw dependency cords

Link each clause to the stores, roads, records, people, reviews, and successor capacities it needs. Treat changed burdens as dependencies, not externalities.

### 5. Import public obligations

For every moon-viewing obligation or witnessed concession, identify:

- the recital preserving honor and exact public meaning;
- the operative clause, if any;
- the schedule or covenant making the promise executable;
- the third-party burden and remedy; and
- the release rule if a material amendment changes the bargain.

### 6. Write submission instruments

Name what changes hands or status in court and in the field, the receiving officer, courier and acknowledgment route, effective hour, inventory, narrow holdover duty while the order travels, and remedy for incomplete transfer.

### 7. Write first-forty-days orders

Name the first field orders, supply and pay, petitions, transfers, replacements, proclamations, and review dates. A settlement without a first morning is unfinished.

### 8. Read three hostile versions

- **Distance reading:** What will a village, patrol, shrine, merchant, or work camp understand?
- **Conflict reading:** What happens when two lawful actors disagree?
- **Retention reading:** How will a holder interpret ambiguity to preserve present power?

## Amendment classification

| Amendment class | Changes | Required process | Effect on promises |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scribal correction | Name, date, reference, obvious inconsistency | Record and correct proclamation | None unless meaning changes |
| Conforming amendment | Text brought into line with already accepted bargain | Notify affected offices; update dependencies | Preserved unless practical burden changes |
| Material amendment | Title, execution, resources, emergency power, succession, term, remedy, public meaning, or third-party burden | Suspend dependent promises; reaffirm, narrow, replace, or release | Private assent reopens; public act remains a political fact |
| Structural amendment | Dangerous powers recombined, outside check removed, purpose changed, or proposals no longer answer same command | Reread affected Reed and every dependency; Chamber may withhold certification | Coalition and fallback assumptions must be rebuilt |

## Material-amendment checklist

Before accepting a material amendment, answer:

- Which titleholder, executor, auditor, successor, and affected witness must be notified?
- Which resources or schedules become insufficient?
- Which public concession changes meaning?
- Which private promise is released or must be reaffirmed?
- Which third party acquires a new burden?
- Which proclamation and register require correction?
- Does the fallback still remain complete and distinct?
- Does the amendment create or remove a dangerous combination?
- What first-forty-days order changes?

## Imperial question sequence

The Emperor may ask many questions, but the audience is easiest to run in six tests.

### Test 1: Authorship

- What Imperial purpose governs the whole instrument?
- Which beneficiary surrenders something because the Throne commands it?
- Which service is honored without becoming a source of title?
- Would the clauses still stand if every clan denied making a bargain?

### Test 2: Obedience

- What order changes in Reedwater tomorrow?
- Which present possession has ended, narrowed, or become commissioned service?
- Which officer must obey across clan lines?
- What happens if the current holder denies that the emergency has ended?

### Test 3: Capacity

- What people, stores, pay, transport, records, and specialists carry each office?
- Which successor possesses capacity rather than only legitimacy?
- Which amendment consumes the same resource twice?
- Which office fails first if roads or records are interrupted?

### Test 4: Constraint

- Who inspects the inspector and judges after recusal?
- Who may halt an engineer without quietly inheriting engineering command?
- Which secret must become public before final authority is exercised?
- Which dangerous combination is intentionally retained, for how long, and why?

### Test 5: Burden legitimacy

- Which district and household categories bear the most?
- Which demands accumulate across Reeds?
- Which burden may be challenged before it becomes irreversible?
- Which compensation is funded and which is only promised?

### Test 6: Transition

- What ends by date, what ends by capacity, and who certifies either?
- What records, debts, and unresolved claims survive transfer?
- What can autumn review change?
- What does Reedwater govern for itself after the term?

## Six outcome architectures

### A. Imperial Spine

Imperial families hold title, appeal, and failure succession; Great Clan specialists execute under bounded commissions. This maximizes visible Imperial authorship but fails if Imperial capacity is only nominal.

### B. Paired Custodies

Rival or unlike interests share linked powers with outside remedies. This makes reciprocal surrender real but can create paralysis if every action requires consensus.

### C. Reedwater Succession

Temporary outside execution is organized around funded local transition. This offers the strongest long-term legitimacy and the weakest immediate capacity unless training, records, and resources are concrete.

### D. Reconstruction Directorate

Waters, Roads, materials, and limited emergency powers are concentrated for a hard term beneath independent checks. This maximizes pre-rain capacity while creating the clearest risk of temporary sovereignty becoming permanent.

### E. Honored Occupation

Present emergency arrangements continue for one harvest under Imperial commissions, submission, audit, compensation, and a demobilization sequence. This preserves functioning relief while risking a precedent that successful mobilization earns regularized control.

### F. Narrow Imperial Peace

The Emperor closes the most dangerous claims and continues essential relief but postpones much of the final structure. This can prevent war honestly or entrench temporary holders through delay.

None is automatically superior. Evaluate each through authorship, obedience, capacity, constraint, burden legitimacy, and transition.

## Imperial dispositions

| Disposition | Meaning | Immediate GM consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Adopted substantially as presented | Architecture survives; details may still be corrected | Test whether promised submissions occur |
| Adopted with amendments | One or more dependencies reopen | Require the commission to price and conform changes publicly |
| Primary joined to fallback clauses | Emperor selects a hybrid | Identify unreaffirmed promises and new burdens |
| Fallback adopted | Bargaining instrument becomes government | Record patrons harmed and capacities lost |
| Narrow Imperial peace | War is prevented; full settlement deferred | Choose which temporary power becomes the next campaign pressure |

## Final consistency audit

Before play reaches the Second Audience, confirm:

- every Reed has title, execution, audit, resources, emergency rules, succession, term, review, and remedy;
- every dangerous combination is separated or explicitly time-bounded;
- every public obligation has an operative home or is identified as honor only;
- every material amendment has reopened its dependencies;
- every submission changes practical command, custody, records, or legal status;
- the fallback is complete, distinct, and supportable;
- the first forty days can occur with named resources;
- Reedwater standing survives adoption into implementation and review;
- the autumn review can actually alter offices or remedies; and
- no outcome is presented as the adventure's concealed correct answer.
