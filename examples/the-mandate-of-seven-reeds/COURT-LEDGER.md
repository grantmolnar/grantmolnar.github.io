# Court Ledger: The Mandate of Seven Reeds

This ledger records the standing political geometry. Later concessions should move from these interests under visible pressure rather than overturning them for convenience.

| Power | Principal voice | Public claim | What they actually need | What they can relinquish |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The Emperor | Unnamed for era flexibility | Restore Reedwater and Imperial peace | A working province, visible obedience, and a settlement remembered as his act | Particular allocations, permanent simplicity, personal credit for every detail |
| Otomo | Otomo Kazetada | Prevent conquest disguised as relief | Divided reinforcing powers and a meaningful Imperial review | Public office, uniform structures, gratitude |
| Seppun | Seppun Tomoe | Restore lawful command | A traceable, removable military chain and visible clan submission | Direct Seppun command, permanent Imperial garrison |
| Miya | Miya Shun | Carry the Emperor's word | Dates, routes, transitions, petitions, and honest correction | Preference among most substantive allocations |
| Crane | Doji Masuyo | Preserve the harvest the Crane saved | Civilian granaries, bounded requisition, durable public credit | Sword, permanent title to Harvest, exclusive audit |
| Lion | Akodo Tadanobu | Preserve order the Lion restored | Real command, guaranteed supply, honorable demobilization | Harvest, Accounts, permanent commission |
| Phoenix | Isawa Etsuko | Restore right relations with the river and shrines | Sacred boundaries, ritual communities, and non-self-judging consultation | Exclusive Shrines title, veto over all engineering |
| Dragon | Kitsuki Harunori | Keep officeholders answerable to law | Recusal, appeal, review, and preserved records | Permanent Dragon control of Judgment |
| Scorpion | Bayushi Namika | Prevent invisible theft and contradictory accounts | Manifest access, protected reporting, inspectors independent of inspected offices | Public title to Accounts, ceremonial prominence |
| Unicorn | Ide Sachiko | Reopen movement through the province | One dispatch law, bounded priority and closure, valued requisition, compensated transport, viable crossings | Permanent Roads title, sole customs authority, control of sanction |
| Crab | Kaiu Masashige | Prevent the next breach | Design consequences stated honestly, command equal to work, cumulative labor, protected materials, bounded emergency action | Ceremonial title, permanent tenure, freedom from audit, final political authorship |
| Reedwater witnesses | Rei, Hojun, Saburo, Natsugawa Kenta | Make the Mandate answer the province it governs | Cumulative burdens, living communities, valued requisition, local capacity, standing through implementation and review | A single unified position, gratitude on command, authority equal to Great Clan delegates |


## Administrative grammar

This is an allocation ledger, not a vote count. For every concession, record the provincial office affected, present possessor, act of submission, ordinary executor, field recipient, resources, effective hour, acknowledgment route, and remedy. Clan support is implementation evidence; Imperial issuance remains the source of authority.

## Session 2: the Crane and Lion delegations

### Crane internal ledger

#### Doji Masuyo

**Office sought:** Civilian execution of Harvest under an Imperial title.  
**Public ground:** The Crane built the only functioning southern relief network.  
**Actual need:** A durable record of authorship, influence over the officials trained by that network, and protection from military alteration of grain allotments.  
**Acceptable concession:** Shorter tenure, Imperial title, outside audit, Lion Sword command, local successors.  
**Red line:** A commander may not change village quotas or reach seed rice by ordinary order.  
**Pressure style:** Makes concessions conditional upon exact public language rather than withdrawing them outright.

#### Daidoji Yorishige

**Office sought:** None independently; he wants the relief system funded and protected.  
**Public ground:** Boats, pilots, guards, temple stores, and private guarantees have already been spent.  
**Actual need:** Repayment, route protection, and a rule assigning losses incurred under military orders.  
**Acceptable concession:** Daidoji escorts may serve under Lion operational command on defined relief routes.  
**Red line:** The Sword office cannot seize Crane vessels or transfer its costs into an unexamined relief debt.  
**Pressure style:** Replaces graceful abstractions with names, quantities, creditors, and damaged property.

#### Asahina Chiharu

**Office sought:** None.  
**Public ground:** The next harvest and the displaced shrines are obligations, not stores of convenience.  
**Actual need:** Seed rice distinguished from rations and military supply; local and spiritual communities preserved through transition.  
**Acceptable concession:** Publicly blesses the transfer of granary keys and supports Imperial title or local succession.  
**Red line:** Seed rice may not be opened by the Harvest executor or Sword commander acting alone.  
**Pressure style:** Opposes her own delegation quietly and concretely when political victory consumes future survival.

### Lion internal ledger

#### Akodo Tadanobu

**Office sought:** Execution of Sword beneath a revocable Imperial commission.  
**Public ground:** The Lion restored order and still carries duties no replacement can yet perform.  
**Actual need:** One command chain, guaranteed supply, defined emergency authority, and removal that does not make command decorative.  
**Acceptable concession:** Civilian Harvest, outside audit, Seppun title, fixed term, reduced force.  
**Red line:** The granary holder cannot vary military supply as a daily instrument of control.  
**Pressure style:** Refuses powers he considers corrupting, then uses that refusal to make his remaining demand appear unavoidable.

#### Matsu Kiyoka

**Office sought:** No permanent office; she wants duties transferred before positions are abandoned.  
**Public ground:** Eleven villages have asked Lion patrols not to leave.  
**Actual need:** Named replacements for every granary guard, road patrol, and checkpoint.  
**Acceptable concession:** District-by-district withdrawal, surrender of civilian keys, and a smaller retained garrison.  
**Red line:** Immediate withdrawal into an administrative vacuum.  
**Pressure style:** Converts political timetables into a list of villages that will be unguarded before nightfall.

#### Ikoma Noritada

**Office sought:** None independently; he governs the meaning of Lion submission.  
**Public ground:** Service, discipline, death, and withdrawal must enter the record accurately.  
**Actual need:** A proclamation in which the Lion restored order and then accepted the Emperor's peace.  
**Acceptable concession:** Almost any bounded military settlement if the relinquishments are witnessed and named.  
**Red line:** Language describing the whole deployment as conquest, misconduct, or an embarrassment corrected by court.  
**Pressure style:** States only true events while arranging them into the history future commanders will remember.

## The Grain and Sword exchange

A stable paired settlement normally contains all of the following:

1. Harvest distinguishes household rations, seed rice, garrison allotments, and emergency reserves.
2. Sword receives predictable supply without authority to assess, own, or audit the granaries.
3. The garrison allotment declines with the military roster or is reviewed whenever the roster changes.
4. Emergency excess requires an outside finding, expires, and becomes a recorded debt of Sword.
5. Lion officers surrender civilian granary keys; Crane escorts surrender independent armed command.
6. Lion standards are lowered before the new commission is proclaimed.
7. No patrol or checkpoint is abandoned before a named replacement receives it.
8. Crane relief debt is compensated through Accounts rather than by extending Harvest jurisdiction.
9. The first proclamation credits unequal acts precisely rather than thanking all clans identically.
10. The autumn review can reduce force, revise supply, replace executors, and measure local succession.

The bargain fails if either side's concession remains merely verbal. The Crane require civilian keys and recorded limits; the Lion require a supply covenant and replacement schedule. Public credit is the political consideration that allows both delegations to relinquish authority without claiming they were defeated.

## The core dangerous combinations

- **Harvest + Sword + Accounts:** food, force, and self-certification create a private government.
- **Waters without labor or materials:** responsibility without capacity makes failure politically useful to everyone else.
- **Roads + customs without appeal:** movement becomes a toll privilege capable of punishing rivals.
- **Shrines without review:** sacred necessity can become an unanswerable administrative veto.
- **Judgment held by an office's beneficiary:** accountability becomes an internal explanation of why the office was right.
- **Imperial title without implementation:** the Throne receives blame for decisions made elsewhere.

## The first-session non-negotiables

A credible Mandate must:

1. require at least one visible act of obedience from the forces already in Reedwater;
2. assign material burdens before ceremonial honors;
3. identify title, execution, audit, and succession for every Reed;
4. prevent one Great Clan from holding Harvest, Sword, and Accounts together;
5. state a route and timetable for implementation;
6. preserve a forum for provincial petitions and inter-office disputes; and
7. remain recognizably an Imperial command rather than a treaty among clans presented for ratification.


## Session 3: the Phoenix, Dragon, and Scorpion delegations

### Phoenix internal ledger

#### Isawa Etsuko

**Office sought:** A meaningful Shrines charter; title is negotiable.  
**Public ground:** Reconstruction must preserve obligations among communities, places, rites, and spirits.  
**Actual need:** Protected standing, reasons, emergency rules, remedy, and outside review of procedure.  
**Acceptable concession:** Kaiu execution, Imperial title, Dragon procedural review, local succession.  
**Red line:** The office disturbing a site cannot decide finally whether consultation mattered.  
**Pressure style:** Demands a named spirit, speaker, rite, witness, and resulting duty whenever another courtier invokes sacred necessity abstractly.

#### Shiba Koremori

**Office sought:** None independently; he wants a charter usable under pressure.  
**Public ground:** Ritual duties require living people who can reach the site and act safely.  
**Actual need:** Deadlines, substitutes, escorts, emergency bypass, and a restoration debt after bypass.  
**Acceptable concession:** Shiba guards under Kaiu operational direction; immediate work under a short sunset.  
**Red line:** An emergency cannot remain open merely to abolish consultation.  
**Pressure style:** Converts spiritual language into routes, dates, people, and risks.

#### Asako Chise

**Office sought:** Local standing and succession rather than durable Phoenix execution.  
**Public ground:** Great Clan specialists did not create Reedwater's ritual communities.  
**Actual need:** Household keepers, displaced congregations, and local deputies recognized and funded.  
**Acceptable concession:** A Phoenix commissioner who teaches, coordinates, and leaves.  
**Red line:** Compensation for buildings without continuity for the people who maintained them.  
**Pressure style:** Produces registers and family histories naming those court has made invisible.

### Dragon internal ledger

#### Kitsuki Harunori

**Office sought:** A common Judgment circuit; permanent Dragon title is unnecessary.  
**Public ground:** Divided offices require shared records, stated reasons, recusal, and review.  
**Actual need:** Comparable evidence, preserved reasons, and judgment outside the interested office.  
**Acceptable concession:** Imperial title, rotating seats, local petition officers, fixed term.  
**Red line:** Status or secrecy cannot make contradictory records agree by decree.  
**Pressure style:** Lets every delegation choose its own judge until the pattern of self-judgment becomes visible.

#### Mirumoto Daizen

**Office sought:** None; he wants review that arrives before the dispute becomes moot.  
**Public ground:** Law after the grain is eaten or shrine dismantled is only history.  
**Actual need:** Substitute jurisdiction, deadlines, narrow interim restraint, and enforceable remedies.  
**Acceptable concession:** Emergency orders remain effective for a short term while reviewed.  
**Red line:** Recusal without replacement or appeal that stops every contested act.  
**Pressure style:** Asks who carries the order, by what hour, and what remains in force.

#### Togashi Sorin

**Office sought:** None.  
**Public ground:** Law must hear people who bear a burden even when they hold no formal title.  
**Actual need:** Broad but testable standing and remedies that restore more than reputation.  
**Acceptable concession:** Claims tested rigorously; compassion is not proof.  
**Red line:** A court open only to those powerful enough to enter it.  
**Pressure style:** Introduces the claimant the proposed procedure forgot.

### Scorpion internal ledger

#### Bayushi Namika

**Office sought:** Narrow inspection beneath outside title.  
**Public ground:** Reedwater's records cannot distinguish necessity, error, favoritism, and theft because they cannot be compared.  
**Actual need:** Access, preservation, protected reporting, and independence from the inspected office.  
**Acceptable concession:** Relinquish Accounts title, accept reciprocal inspection, serve Otomo or Miya authority.  
**Red line:** Findings may not be buried privately whenever they inconvenience a favored office.  
**Pressure style:** Offers a useful dangerous power and requires the commission to assign its restraint.

#### Shosuro Mirei

**Office sought:** A protected reporting route.  
**Public ground:** Some witnesses cannot appear openly before records and livelihoods are secured.  
**Actual need:** Separation of source identity from testable substance, sealed review, and protection from retaliation.  
**Acceptable concession:** Anonymous reports preserve records and open inquiry but do not prove misconduct.  
**Red line:** Immediate disclosure of every source to the inspected official.  
**Pressure style:** Removes information from the stage to demonstrate the cost of perfect publicity.

#### Soshi Kureha

**Office sought:** Custody rules rather than broad discretion.  
**Public ground:** Inspection fails if the record can change after it is seen.  
**Actual need:** Numbered copies, witnessed seals, duplicate indexes, and chain of custody.  
**Acceptable concession:** Inspectors do not punish, remove, or rewrite the offices they inspect.  
**Red line:** Unindexed seizure or judgment based on an unpreserved version.  
**Pressure style:** Follows the document through every hand rather than following the accusation.

## The Shrines, Judgment, and Accounts exchange

A stable three-office settlement normally contains all of the following:

1. Shrines identifies who may speak for a site, what consultation must contain, and what remedy follows an override.
2. Sacred sites are distinguished as anchored, translatable, seasonal, or attached to displaced communities.
3. Emergency work may bypass consultation only under a named trigger and creates a recorded restoration debt.
4. Judgment reviews standing, process, recusal, reasons, and remedy without claiming authority to decide theology.
5. Every mandatory recusal transfers jurisdiction to a named substitute under a deadline.
6. Appeal states what administration continues and what irreversible acts may pause.
7. Accounts separates access, comparison, preservation, protected reporting, interim restraint, and sanction.
8. Inspectors do not impose final punishment or control the office they inspect.
9. Witness identities and methods may be sealed, but the rules, reviewer, deadlines, and final disposition remain reviewable.
10. Phoenix, Dragon, and Scorpion special commissions each have independent succession and expiration tests.

The exchange fails when one office's honorable purpose is treated as sufficient control. Phoenix expertise cannot review itself, Dragon recusal cannot abolish jurisdiction, and Scorpion secrecy cannot become permanent authority.


## Session 4: the Unicorn, Crab, and Reedwater subsystem

### Unicorn internal ledger

#### Ide Sachiko

**Office sought:** Roads execution beneath a Miya title for one year and one planting season.  
**Public ground:** The western network works because different stations accept one sequence of promises.  
**Actual need:** One priority law, valued requisition, bounded closure, common manifests, and funded local succession.  
**Acceptable concession:** Shorter tenure, outside audit and review, provincial successors, no Roads title.  
**Red line:** A general permission to take necessary transport or let several offices command the same station independently.  
**Pressure style:** Makes every clause travel from gate to gate and asks what receipt returns to the household that carried it.

#### Shinjo Boroldai

**Office sought:** None independently; he wants one dispatch chain capable of refusing delay.  
**Public ground:** Emergency messages, seed, and works cannot all receive first priority.  
**Actual need:** Ranked traffic classes, short recorded emergency variance, and one command at each station.  
**Acceptable concession:** Miya dispatch, Seppun certification, and provincial operational succession.  
**Red line:** Simultaneous priority orders from several Reeds.  
**Pressure style:** Moves markers on the remount board so each new priority visibly delays another journey.

#### Iuchi Nergui

**Office sought:** No formal office; she wants routes to account for the communities and obligations that make crossings work.  
**Public ground:** Ferries, bridges, fords, roads, households, seasons, and rites cannot be separated on the ground.  
**Actual need:** Crossing records, relocation burdens, route succession, and joint treatment of physical and sacred constraints.  
**Acceptable concession:** Phoenix consultation and Kaiu engineering judgment.  
**Red line:** A map with no households, maintainers, or seasonal conditions on it.  
**Pressure style:** Names who must still be standing at the crossing after the temporary executor leaves.

### Crab internal ledger

#### Kaiu Masashige

**Office sought:** Waters execution for eighteen months beneath Imperial title.  
**Public ground:** The next breach will test authority, labor, and materials rather than ceremonial responsibility.  
**Actual need:** Work sequence, assigned labor, protected stores, site safety, honest design authorship, and funded maintenance succession.  
**Acceptable concession:** Sacred consultation, fiscal and procedural review, fixed term, provincial maintenance.  
**Red line:** Certifying a political compromise as his technical recommendation or bearing blame for powers denied to him.  
**Pressure style:** Draws the load path through authority, resource, burden, deadline, and consequence.

#### Hida Chihaya

**Office sought:** Work-site command and a coherent camp charter.  
**Public ground:** A camp of hundreds is a settlement with food, law, sickness, worship, families, and armed authority.  
**Actual need:** One command inside the site, cumulative labor counts, rotations, soldiers sharing heavy work, and reviewable discipline.  
**Acceptable concession:** Lion outer security, provincial ordinary jurisdiction, Miya proclamation, Dragon review.  
**Red line:** Labor quotas stated only as bodies or several uniforms issuing orders to the same workers.  
**Pressure style:** Reads names and asks what household, planting, funeral, or prior service each number conceals.

#### Yasuki Kameyo

**Office sought:** No title; she wants purpose-bound materials and enforceable debts.  
**Public ground:** Every timber, cart, rope, ration, and fitting is already promised somewhere.  
**Actual need:** Custody, authorized alternatives, replacement duties, common manifests, and priced amendments.  
**Acceptable concession:** Outside audit and non-Crab title or execution.  
**Red line:** “Available provincial stores” and consequence-free diversion.  
**Pressure style:** Adds the material, transport, labor, and delay column to every virtuous amendment.

### Reedwater witness ledger

#### Rei of Reed Bank

**Standing sought:** Receipt, burden-ledger access, petition, review triggers, and return testimony.  
**Public ground:** A lawful quota still fails if it ignores household size, prior service, planting, or relocation livelihood.  
**Actual need:** Cumulative and calculable burdens, complete relocation packages, and authority to refuse false capacity.  
**Acceptable concession:** Harsh levies and even relocation if the whole exchange is explicit and survivable.  
**Red line:** Equal quotas among unequal villages or “higher ground” without water, seed, fields, roads, and possession dates.  
**Pressure style:** Totals what every office has promised itself from the same households.

#### Brother Hojun

**Standing sought:** Recognized voice for displaced shrine communities and their successors.  
**Public ground:** A shrine is a living pattern of households, rites, graves, stipends, and place rather than a replaceable building.  
**Actual need:** Translation procedures, community continuity, restoration debts, and protected calendars.  
**Acceptable concession:** Relocation or loss under necessity when obligations move with the community.  
**Red line:** Compensation that replaces structures while dissolving the people who maintained them.  
**Pressure style:** Names the household, rite, grave, and successor hidden inside a convenient sacred abstraction.

#### Saburo of the Three Crossings

**Standing sought:** Independent road testimony under protection that does not own it.  
**Public ground:** Public service can bankrupt the carriers and households an open-road order assumes.  
**Actual need:** Condition records, usable valuation, named debtors, operationally timed repayment, and public tolls.  
**Acceptable concession:** Tolls, requisition, delayed coin, and Unicorn sponsorship under common rules.  
**Red line:** Acknowledged but unpayable debt or remedies useful only to large associations.  
**Pressure style:** Distinguishes coin, tax credit, replacement, and trade priority by who can actually survive each remedy.

#### Natsugawa Kenta

**Standing sought:** A provincial succession process for local samurai houses and officers.  
**Public ground:** Imported commissions can govern a season but cannot leave a province without recognized local records, seals, and people.  
**Actual need:** Deputies, qualification, training, pay, records, resources, and lawful removal.  
**Acceptable concession:** Great Clan executors, testing of local houses, and loss of office for incompetence or misconduct.  
**Red line:** Absorption into clan vassalage or restoration of local status without capability.  
**Pressure style:** Shows which order cannot be carried after the banners leave because no recognized officer receives it.

## The Roads and Waters exchange

A stable settlement normally contains all of the following:

1. Roads separates title, execution, priority, requisition, valuation, tolls, customs, closure, succession, and review.
2. Every requisition creates a named, valued, dated debt before the property leaves local use.
3. One priority hierarchy governs all stations, with bounded emergency variance and a minimum route for messages, food, and petitions.
4. Waters separates design, execution, labor, materials, camp command, consultation, relocation, emergency action, indemnity, and maintenance.
5. Engineers state the district, road, shrine, land, labor, material, and time consequences of every design.
6. All Reeds read one cumulative household burden ledger before issuing another call.
7. Site safety, plan-preserving emergency action, and political sacrifice receive different authorities and review.
8. Work camps have one operational command and a written relation to military, local, Judgment, and sacred authority.
9. Reedwater witnesses retain receipt, access, petition, review-trigger, and return-testimony standing after the court disperses.
10. Local successors receive stores, animals, records, staff, pay, and training rather than ceremonial seals alone.

The exchange fails if Roads preserves movement by silently consuming the province's transport, Waters preserves the province by hiding sacrifice inside engineering, or the witnesses are heard only long enough to decorate a decision already made.

## Session 5: public obligation and court-wide pressure

### The evening's political officers

| Officer | Ceremonial function | Political danger checked |
|---|---|---|
| Miya Shun | Host, plain-language announcement, docket, later proclamation | Applause or poetry treated as consent |
| Isawa Etsuko | Cups, pauses, sacred objects, burden-aware ritual order | Sacred people or objects used as scenery |
| Doji Masuyo | Graceful formulation and face-preserving concession | Beauty broadens obligation |
| Ikoma Noritada | Historical sequence and honorable submission | Withdrawal remembered as defeat or service as title |
| Ide Sachiko | Gift meaning, ownership, transport, replacement, redirection | Generosity conceals requisition or future duty |
| Shosuro Mirei | Masks, roles, recitations, protected public form | Performance creates a broader promise than words |
| Kitsuki Harunori | Exact words, prior stated meaning, later evidentiary record | Court memory silently expands or contracts |
| Seppun Tomoe | Banners, weapons, submission, command consequence | Military symbolism detached from actual obedience |
| Otomo Kazetada | Precedent, excluded powers, absent burdens, Imperial authorship | Ceremony implies an office grant or private treaty |

### Public obligation formula

A stable witnessed concession records:

1. the service or claim acknowledged;
2. the exact act surrendered, accepted, limited, or performed;
3. the office, district, resource, trigger, condition, and term;
4. powers expressly excluded;
5. third parties and dependent Reeds;
6. witnesses and record custody;
7. refusal, release, or reopening conditions; and
8. the clause, covenant, proclamation, transfer, or ledger entry that will make it operative.

### Coalition geometry

The court event reveals coalitions as dependency systems rather than collections of endorsements. A viable public bargain normally includes:

- someone who relinquishes or submits;
- someone with capacity to execute;
- someone who supplies or records resources;
- someone outside the executor who audits or hears appeal;
- an affected witness or office able to identify hidden burdens;
- a successor or review point; and
- Imperial proclamation that states when emergency claims end.

A clan may oppose the Mandate and still implement it under honorable submission. A clan may praise the Mandate while preserving an independent command. Record support, submission, capacity, and dependency separately.

### The four public chains

- **White keys beneath red standards:** Crane Harvest custody, Lion Sword command, Seppun commission, Miya proclamation, Accounts repayment, district replacement.
- **Road beside the new water:** Crab design, Unicorn transport, Phoenix consultation, Reedwater burdens, Scorpion records, Dragon review.
- **Mask before the open ledger:** Scorpion inspection, outside custody, protected reporting, Dragon appeal and recusal, Miya correction.
- **Empty cushion of succession:** current executor's transfer promise, future holder's capacity, assigned resources, public timetable, autumn review.

No chain remains stable if treated as a bilateral errand. Changing one term should trigger a review of every listed dependency.

## Session 6: drafting, submission, and Imperial judgment

### The Chamber's governing distinction

The final settlement is a stack rather than a single sheet. The operative Mandate assigns power and limits; recitals preserve purpose, service, and relinquishment; schedules carry changing implementation details; side covenants carry reciprocal supply, repayment, training, and custody; proclamations make obedience intelligible; registers preserve burdens, debts, transfers, and corrections; submission instruments convert present possession into lawful service or withdrawal.

### Draft certification

For every Reed, record:

- Imperial title;
- ordinary execution;
- audit and inspection;
- assigned resources;
- ordinary emergency action;
- extraordinary sacrifice;
- ordinary succession;
- failure succession;
- term and transition;
- review and remedy; and
- cross-Reed dependencies.

The Chamber certifies internal completeness, not wisdom. It may certify a harsh Mandate if the burden and remedy are explicit. It should refuse to certify a gracious Mandate whose resources, successor, or first orders are imaginary.

### Amendment ledger

Classify every change as scribal, conforming, material, or structural. Material changes reopen dependent promises. Structural changes require the affected Reed and all red-cord dependencies to be reread. Public concessions remain political facts even when the clause they anticipated disappears.

### Primary and fallback

Both proposals must answer one Imperial purpose and be complete enough to govern. Record the failure each answers, dangerous power retained, capacity lost, major beneficiaries and losers, provincial burden, required submissions, unreaffirmed promises, first-day difference, and autumn-review difference.

### Submission register

For every present emergency possession, record the holder, court act of submission, authenticated field order, named receiving officer, courier and acknowledgment route, effective time, inventory, narrow holdover duty while the message travels, and incomplete-transfer remedy. A symbol without changed command, custody, records, subordinates, or resources is not submission.

### The Emperor's six tests

- **Authorship:** Does the settlement remain an Imperial command rather than a coalition treaty?
- **Obedience:** What present possession changes legal status or command?
- **Capacity:** Are people, stores, routes, pay, and records assigned?
- **Constraint:** Can dangerous powers be inspected, stopped, appealed, and succeeded?
- **Burden legitimacy:** Are cumulative costs named, contestable, and remedied?
- **Transition:** Does temporary power end or transfer through observable conditions?

### Durable outcomes

The Imperial Spine, Paired Custodies, Reedwater Succession, Reconstruction Directorate, Honored Occupation, and Narrow Imperial Peace are all usable architectures. None is canonical. Record their deliberate weakness as future political material rather than flattening the result into a single success label.


## Session 7: demonstrated settlement and surviving fault lines

The named demonstration adopts **Reedwater Succession under the Hundred-Day Brace**. This is one tested architecture, not the adventure's canonical answer.

### Demonstrated allocation

| Reed | Title | Initial execution | Outside constraint | Intended succession |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Harvest | Miya / Imperial | Crane civilian wardens through first harvest | Outside audit, protected seed, village standing, fixed Sword covenant | District granary wardens |
| Waters | Imperial | Kaiu Hundred-Day Brace and northern-channel works | Political-sacrifice authority outside executor, burden ledger, Dragon review, provincial chair | Reedwater maintenance officers |
| Roads | Miya | Unicorn through first planting season | Common manifests, compensated requisition, minimum open route, restoration debt | District station stewards |
| Sword | Seppun | Lion under declining roster | Removal, supply limits, outer term, no automatic reversion | Natsugawa Kenta's provincial patrol structure |
| Judgment | Imperial | Dragon-administered common circuit through autumn | Recusal, substitutes, deadlines, records, appeal | Mixed provincial bench |
| Shrines | Imperial protection | Phoenix consultation and ritual coordination | Local speakers, emergency debt, remedy and review | Reedwater shrine communities |
| Accounts | Imperial / Miya custody | Scorpion inspection without title | Dragon review, divided custody, public disposition | Provincial clerks beneath Imperial audit |

### Demonstrated reciprocal relinquishments

- The Crane return Harvest title, armed escort command, indefinite execution, and patron ownership of witnesses.
- The Lion return civilian granary control, independent standards, automatic resumption, and claims of fealty from emergency dependence.
- The Phoenix surrender permanent title and invisible veto while retaining consultation, coordination, and remedy.
- The Dragon surrender permanent control of every recusal while retaining the initial common circuit.
- The Scorpion surrender Accounts title and final secrecy while retaining protected-source inspection.
- The Unicorn surrender toll privilege, private valuation, and unilateral closure while retaining Roads execution.
- The Crab surrender political-sacrifice authority, road closure without account, and self-certified continuation while retaining technical command.
- The Imperial families accept that title and review require assigned clan and provincial capacity rather than seals alone.

### Imperial amendment

The Emperor adds one transition rule across all seven Reeds:

> No holder may use a lack of successor capacity caused by its own failure to provide required records, resources, instruction, or staged authority as grounds for renewing the power thereby retained.

This amendment prevents a transition condition from becoming an executor-controlled perpetuity. It does not force transfer to a genuinely failed successor; lawful failure succession remains available.

### Surviving fault lines

- The northern channel may consume too much planting-season labor.
- The Hundred-Day Brace may report a failure that justifies narrowing its own power.
- The substitute western route may not carry stone wagons during rain.
- Provincial patrols may not reach capacity before Lion district terms expire.
- Crane-trained wardens may remain dependent on Crane stores and credit.
- Protected Scorpion sources may preserve evidence while undermining public confidence.
- Dragon procedure may become the slowest or most durable outside office.
- Restoration and requisition debts may exceed Accounts resources.
- Reedwater's standing may expose burdens the adopted Mandate cannot afford to remedy.

These are campaign consequences of the chosen architecture, not inconsistencies to remove during the voice pass.
