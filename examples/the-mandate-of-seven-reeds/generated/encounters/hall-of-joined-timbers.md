# Hall of Joined Timbers

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** court, crab, engineering, waters

A polished beam bears nothing; broken timbers carry the room. Kaiu Masashige demands authority equal to the work while Reedwater’s witnesses force the court to price labor, land, sacred ground, displacement, and the district chosen to receive the water.

## Opening View

Kaiu Masashige asks the commission to step onto a frame made from broken dike timbers. Iron straps pass their weight through the ugly joints. Nearby, one flawless ceremonial beam bears only Hida Chihaya’s work-camp roster and Yasuki Kameyo’s material debts. A fresh survey line cuts seed fields, shrine ground, and three houses. Masashige taps the brace. The river tests the arithmetic, not the seal.

## Encounter Material

## The beam that does not bear weight

Kaiu Masashige invites the Witnesses onto the beautiful ceremonial beam. Lacquered and sound, it lies flat on the floor and supports nothing.

Beside it, muddy timbers joined with iron straps form a low frame. Their load passes visibly from brace to brace when the Witnesses step onto it.

“That,” he says, tapping an iron strap, “is government. The weight does not care what we intended the beam to mean.”

He does not mean that ceremony is useless. He means that a title carrying no labor, materials, decision rights, or emergency chain cannot bear the weight assigned to it. The court may give Waters to anyone it likes. The river will test the joints rather than the calligraphy.

## The Waters Reed is a chain of burdens

Masashige divides Waters into eleven powers and duties.

1. **Title:** who answers publicly for the province's water system.
2. **Survey and design:** who measures, proposes alternatives, and certifies the physical consequences.
3. **Ordinary execution:** who maintains dikes, canals, ferries, gates, and drainage.
4. **Labor assessment:** who calls households, soldiers, specialists, and hired crews.
5. **Materials:** who opens quarries, fells timber, purchases rope and iron, and assigns transport.
6. **Work-site command:** who directs crews, guards camps, and changes daily sequence.
7. **Sacred consultation:** who identifies affected sites and conducts the required procedure.
8. **Relocation and compensation:** who moves households and restores land, stipends, graves, routes, and livelihoods.
9. **Emergency authority:** who may cut, breach, divert, flood, or abandon in immediate danger.
10. **Indemnity and blame:** who answers when a design fails or another office overrides it.
11. **Maintenance, succession, and review:** who remains after the emergency commission ends.

The court's most dangerous temptation is to divide these powers so thoroughly that no one can build, then call the resulting paralysis a system of checks. The second temptation is to reunite them all in one engineer and call competence sufficient accountability.

## Three Crab positions

### Kaiu Masashige: authority equal to the work

Masashige wants Waters execution for eighteen months beneath an Imperial title. He speaks in loads, sequences, failure points, and substitutions. He draws while others speak, then turns the board so the cost faces them. He will accept Phoenix consultation, Dragon review, outside audit, and provincial maintenance succession. He will not accept responsibility for a design whose labor, materials, guards, or emergency authority another office may withhold.

He does not demand the right to choose every political sacrifice. His distinction is exact. An engineer may choose how to brace a failing section, where to place crews, and which stockpile to open within the approved plan. An engineer should not alone decide which village may be flooded, which shrine community displaced, or which road cut for a generation. Those are allocations of Imperial protection.

Masashige's red line is being ordered to certify a compromise design as though it were his recommendation. If the court chooses the northern channel to preserve villages at the cost of road access and labor, he will build it and record the cost. If it chooses the eastern wall to protect the harvest at the cost of relocation, he will build that and record who chose. He will not let political authorship disappear into his technical seal.

He exerts pressure by drawing a load path through every proposal. For each duty he asks what authority, labor, material, road, guard, and deadline actually carries it.

### Hida Chihaya: camps are settlements before they are finished

Chihaya commands the Crab guards protecting quarries and temporary work camps. She knows that a camp of eight hundred laborers is not merely an engineering resource. It is food, discipline, sickness, gambling, worship, family separation, crime, and armed authority gathered beside households already exhausted by the flood.

She wants one work-site command and one law for camp discipline. Lion patrols may guard the outer roads, Crab officers may secure dangerous work, and provincial samurai may hear ordinary local complaints, but the boundaries must be written. She opposes a camp where every uniform can issue labor orders and no magistrate knows which chain owns the act.

Chihaya also demands a labor rotation that preserves planting, harvest, funerals, and household survival. She is willing to use soldiers for heavy work and to reduce civilian calls when the garrison remains large. She will accept Dragon review of punishments and Miya proclamation of camp rules.

Her red line is a quota expressed only as bodies. A district that sends one hundred laborers from two hundred households has made a different sacrifice from a district that sends one hundred from two thousand. A household already furnishing a cart, a soldier, and shrine labor cannot be counted as untouched because Waters has not yet written its own order.

Chihaya exerts pressure by reading names. When a courtier offers “three hundred men for ten days,” she asks which households lose planting, which duties those people already bear, and who brings them home.

### Yasuki Kameyo: timber, stone, rope, and debts that become policy

Kameyo procures the material that makes Masashige's drawings possible. She has contracts for mountain timber, stone from two quarries, river rope, iron fittings, barges, fodder, and winter rice for the camps. Every contract was made under emergency uncertainty. Several sellers accepted Crab guarantees because no Imperial Accounts office remained alive to sign.

Kameyo wants materials protected from diversion and the debts entered into Accounts. She is less concerned than Masashige with who executes Waters; she is more concerned that a commission receiving responsibility cannot discover its timber reassigned to bridges, its carts taken for grain, or its work rice consumed by a garrison whose own allotment was never written.

She supports common manifests and outside audit. Her preferred rule gives every stockpile a purpose, custodian, authorized alternatives, and replacement obligation. Emergency diversion remains possible, but the office taking the material inherits the debt and schedule it disrupts.

Kameyo's red line is the phrase “from available provincial stores.” In Reedwater, every store is already promised, and availability means the last official to arrive has not yet learned whose promise will be broken.

She exerts pressure by pricing amendments. A pious, strategic, or merciful change to the plan produces a new column of timber, transport, labor, and delay. The price does not make the change wrong. It prevents the court from purchasing virtue with resources assigned to someone absent.

## Two designs, neither innocent

Masashige's eastern wall and northern channel are not a false choice between efficiency and compassion. Each creates a different province.

### The eastern wall

The eastern wall can be substantially closed before the next heavy rain. It protects the main granaries and preserves the present western road. It requires fewer labor days and less timber.

It also moves two villages, Reed Bank among them, and translates the shrine community maintained by Brother Hojun. The relocated households receive higher ground but poorer fields. Their ancestral graves cannot all be moved before construction. The shortened line also directs greater flood force toward low pasture farther downstream unless a spill basin is purchased and kept empty.

### The northern channel

The northern channel preserves the two villages and leaves the shrine community near its river. It spreads water into an older course and creates more long-term capacity.

It cuts the current west road, consumes nearly twice the timber and labor, and requires a new bridge and two remount stations. Work continues into planting season. The channel crosses land claimed by three samurai households and leaves one district outside the strongest protection until the second phase is complete.

### Amendments are new designs

The court may propose walls, gates, causeways, partial channels, staged relocation, or seasonal work. Masashige will evaluate them. Every amendment must be treated as a new allocation rather than a graceful compromise between the two drawings. The resource-and-burden ledger records what changes.

An engineering plan allocates:

- immediate and future flood risk;
- labor days and the season in which they are taken;
- timber, stone, iron, rope, carts, and animals;
- roads opened, cut, delayed, or rerouted;
- villages, fields, shrines, graves, and patrol districts relocated;
- households protected first and households asked to wait;
- emergency powers made necessary by delay; and
- blame when the chosen risk arrives.

## One burden ledger

Masashige and Rei agree on a rule neither delegation expected to share: no Waters labor order should issue until the province has one ledger of household burdens.

The ledger records, by household or recognized work group:

- adult labor already called by Harvest, Waters, Roads, Sword, or Shrines;
- military service and patrol support;
- carts, animals, boats, lodging, fodder, and tools requisitioned;
- rice contributed, advanced, or withheld for seed;
- displacement, damaged land, and loss of access;
- specialist duties that cannot be replaced by an ordinary laborer;
- exemptions, substitutions, wages, tax credits, and repayment dates; and
- the next season in which another call may lawfully be made.

The ledger does not promise equal burden. Flood risk is unequal, and specialized work cannot be distributed by arithmetic alone. It forces every unequal demand to acknowledge what the household already carries.

Chihaya proposes a ceiling on consecutive service and a right to offer a qualified substitute. Rei supports both but warns that wealthy households will purchase substitutes from poorer ones unless the exchange is recorded and limited. Kameyo proposes wages for specialized and dangerous work. Tadanobu may supply soldiers for heavy labor if Sword receives credit for reduced civilian levies rather than additional authority.

## Emergency sacrifice

A flood emergency may require cutting a dike, opening a gate, abandoning a work site, diverting water into fields, or closing a road. The Mandate must decide who can make which choice.

Masashige proposes three levels.

### Site safety

The Waters executor may stop work, evacuate a camp, consume assigned material, or alter construction sequence to prevent immediate injury. These decisions require a daily record but no outside preauthorization.

### Plan-preserving emergency

The executor may reinforce, cut a temporary relief channel, or redirect crews within the approved risk map when a named trigger occurs. A second officer witnesses the act, and review follows within three days.

### Political sacrifice

Flooding an inhabited district, abandoning a protected route, destroying a shrine structure, seizing seed rice, or changing which district receives primary protection requires a titleholder or named Imperial emergency authority. If communication is impossible and delay would produce greater loss, the executor may act, but the action creates an automatic hearing, restoration debt, and personal account of the alternatives known at the time.

This distinction denies the court its favorite refuge. It cannot grant Masashige broad emergency authority and later describe every sacrifice as engineering. It also cannot force him to wait for a poem while a work camp is collapsing.

## Work camps and local law

A stable work-camp charter names:

- the work-site commander;
- the source and duration of each labor call;
- food, lodging, pay or tax credit, and medical or ritual provision;
- the line between military discipline and ordinary Judgment;
- the handling of disputes between soldiers, laborers, and local households;
- the right to report unsafe orders without desertion being presumed;
- funeral and family-notification duties;
- the date and route of return; and
- the officer who closes the camp and accounts for its stores.

Hojun asks that displaced shrine keepers travel with camps affecting their communities. Chihaya agrees if their authority over work is limited to named sacred and funerary duties. Harunori insists camp punishments and deaths enter the common record. Namika wants sealed reporting for retaliation claims. None of these provisions removes command. They make command govern something real.

## Waterworks offices the Mandate can create

### Imperial Waters title, Kaiu execution, provincial maintenance

An Imperial commissioner holds title. Masashige executes the approved design for eighteen months. The commission controls assigned labor and materials, accepts consultation and audit, and trains district maintenance officers. The autumn review may change design only with a new burden ledger.

### Emergency directorate with a hard sunset

For six months, Waters receives integrated authority over works, transport priority, assigned guards, and materials under Seppun-certified triggers. Title remains Imperial, Judgment retains petition and recusal, Accounts preserves all diversions, and every extraordinary power expires unless separately renewed after public review.

### Provincial commission with Crab contract

Local samurai and village officers hold daily Waters administration. Kaiu specialists design, supervise the dangerous phases, and certify maintenance standards under a service covenant. This preserves local authority but requires stronger material guarantees and a clear answer when provincial officers reject technical advice.

### Staged design with priced delay

The court may begin the eastern emergency wall while funding surveys and rights-of-way for a later northern channel, or begin northern works while constructing temporary protection for the exposed district. The package is viable only if the ledger states which provisional works may be abandoned, who bears duplicate cost, and what decision at autumn ends the uncertainty.

## Failure modes

- **Responsibility without labor or materials:** the executor becomes a designated future culprit.
- **Labor without a common ledger:** the same household serves every Reed until none of them function.
- **Materials without custody:** each emergency solves itself by consuming another office's plan.
- **Engineer as final political author:** displacement and sacrifice vanish inside a technical seal.
- **Consultation without deadlines:** the river becomes the only authority able to end debate.
- **Emergency authority without levels:** every convenience becomes rescue, or every rescue waits for permission.
- **Local succession without training and stores:** the Crab leave and the province inherits drawings.

## What to record

- the chosen design and rejected alternatives;
- who holds title, design, execution, labor, materials, work-site command, and review;
- the complete household burden ledger and service ceilings;
- the custody and replacement rule for every major stockpile;
- work-camp law, security, petitions, and return;
- sacred consultation and relocation obligations;
- the three levels of emergency authority;
- who may choose a political sacrifice and who reviews it;
- indemnity when another office changes the plan; and
- the trained, funded provincial maintenance succession.
