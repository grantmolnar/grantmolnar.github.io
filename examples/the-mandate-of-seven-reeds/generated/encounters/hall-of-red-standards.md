# Hall of Red Standards

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** court, lion, military, garrison

Akodo Tadanobu can lower the Lion standards without abandoning the villages beneath them if command remains real, supply is written, withdrawal follows replacement, and obedience enters the record as completion rather than disgrace.

## Opening View

Seven spearheads disappear beneath white silk. Akodo Tadanobu’s roster separates standards lowered, keys released, roads opened, troops recommissioned, and villages still exposed. Matsu Kiyoka returns three village markers beneath a departing unit while Ikoma Noritada revises the history’s heading. A runner waits over the first withdrawal order. Its replacement line is blank; the standard cannot descend faster than protection can change hands.

## Encounter Material

## Standards under restraint

The seven furled standards behind Akodo Tadanobu belong to the companies that entered Reedwater. White silk covers each spearhead under Imperial order. The wrapping has not been tied tightly enough to hide the shape of the blade.

Tadanobu has arranged three objects before him: a granary key, a patrol roster, and a strip of red cloth cut from a standard lost in the flood. “Which do you require first?” he asks. “Food, duty, or the dead?” There is no harmless answer. The key means control of food, the roster means responsibility for villages that now depend on Lion patrols, and the cloth means the dead whose service cannot be concluded as an embarrassing excess.

The northern force prevented several storehouses from being looted and executed two soldiers for theft before the Imperial order arrived. It also established checkpoints, changed convoy priorities, requisitioned rice, and made local officials answer to Lion officers. Tadanobu does not deny any of this. He denies that restoring the old absence of command would be more lawful merely because it would be less visibly military.

## Three Lion positions

### Akodo Tadanobu: one chain, bounded by law

Tadanobu speaks as though every clause were an order handed down at dusk. He wants the Sword Reed under a real Imperial commission and will accept a Seppun titleholder, outside audit, civilian granary wardens, and a fixed term. He requires one commander for ordinary patrols, a written method for declaring emergency authority, and supply that cannot be withheld to manipulate military decisions. “Limit the order,” he says. “Do not make it impossible to give.”

He is not seeking Accounts or Harvest. He considers both dangerous temptations for a commander. He does want the military allotment written before he lowers a single standard, because a garrison ordered to protect the province while depending on a rival's daily favor is not a chain of command. It is a hostage arrangement with uniforms.

### Matsu Kiyoka: the villages that bowed

Kiyoka commands the northern checkpoints. She speaks rarely in court and has sent Tadanobu a list of eleven villages whose headpeople asked the Lion not to leave. Some fear bandits. Some fear displaced families. Some fear the return of officials who fled during the flood. Several simply prefer the authority they have seen to the authority they have been promised.

Kiyoka does not demand permanent occupation. She demands that withdrawal follow replacement. If a checkpoint is surrendered, a named patrol must assume its road before sunset. If granary guards depart, civilian wardens and an escort must already hold the keys. Order a clean, immediate withdrawal and Kiyoka can obey; Tadanobu cannot describe the resulting abandonment as wise.

Her position becomes especially difficult because some young Matsu officers have begun treating local gratitude as fealty. Kiyoka has stopped this twice. She needs the Mandate to prove that obedience does not mean leaving those villagers to whichever courtier wins the wording.

### Ikoma Noritada: what the Empire will remember

Noritada is the delegation's historian and public advocate. He has compiled the names of the Lion dead, the soldiers punished for theft, the villages secured, and the orders issued before authority was clear. He can turn the withdrawal into either disgrace or culmination without saying anything false.

He does not need the Lion to keep every checkpoint. He needs the final proclamation to say that the Lion restored order, accepted the Emperor's peace, and placed disciplined strength beneath lawful command. If the Mandate calls the deployment presumptuous, he will advise every Lion commander in the Empire that swift relief may later be rewritten as ambition. If the Mandate calls it conquest, Tadanobu's moderate position becomes almost impossible to sustain.

Noritada is also the best route to honorable demobilization. He proposes that each stage be witnessed and named: standards lowered, keys surrendered, roads released, detachments recommissioned, and local replacements presented. A sequence creates deeds the Lion can own. A single order to depart creates only absence.

## The Lion commission

Tadanobu's opening proposal is a paired military instrument.

1. **Title:** the Sword Reed remains an Imperial office under Seppun supervision.
2. **Execution:** Tadanobu receives a revocable three-year commission as provincial commander.
3. **Ordinary authority:** the commander controls patrols, garrison discipline, convoy escorts, and response to banditry.
4. **Emergency authority:** flood, riot, or invasion may activate broader command for thirty days; renewal requires a second Imperial officer.
5. **Supply:** Harvest owes a fixed monthly allotment and convoy priority stated in the Mandate.
6. **No granary seizure:** the commander may not alter village allotments or open seed stores except under the emergency procedure.
7. **Removal and succession:** Seppun Tomoe or her named successor may remove the commander, and a second officer is invested before the first takes command.
8. **Review:** the commission is reviewed after the first autumn, when the province's own garrison and patrol capacity can be measured.

Tadanobu will shorten the commission if the transition duties are exact. He will accept one year more readily than three years with a vague promise that local forces will eventually be ready.

## Demobilization as an ordered campaign

The Lion will not accept the word *withdrawal* as though the army can vanish between clauses. Tadanobu offers a six-stage sequence.

### First: visible submission

At an Imperial ceremony, each Lion standard is lowered beneath a Seppun banner. The act occurs before the commission is granted, so office cannot be described as the reward for refusing obedience.

### Second: the granary keys

Lion officers surrender civilian granary keys to named wardens. They retain access only to the garrison allotment and emergency stores defined by the Harvest charter.

### Third: release the roads

The Lion abandons toll collection and general convoy priority at designated checkpoints. Roads receives those powers, while the Sword office keeps priority for named emergencies.

### Fourth: recommission the core

A smaller force accepts the Imperial Sword commission. Officers swear to the office and its term rather than to the continuation of the emergency occupation.

### Fifth: replace the rest

The remaining companies depart district by district as provincial patrols, clan detachments, or other lawful replacements assume named duties. No withdrawal occurs without a receiving officer.

### Sixth: autumn judgment

The court reviews whether the retained garrison is still necessary, whether local replacements exist, and whether the supply burden matches the province's actual harvest. The review can reduce the force without declaring its original service wrongful.

The sequence can be compressed if time demands. Removing stages has consequences. Omitting visible submission makes the commission look extracted. Omitting the key transfer leaves Harvest under armed supervision. Omitting replacement vindicates Kiyoka. Omitting review makes the emergency army ordinary by inertia.

## The supply problem

The Lion require food for soldiers, horses, wounded, and camp laborers. Masuyo's fixed quota can satisfy them only if the Mandate answers four questions.

- Is the quota stated as rice, assessed value, or a proportion of the surviving harvest?
- Who bears losses when a convoy is delayed or spoiled?
- May the commander borrow from emergency reserves, and who certifies repayment?
- Does the quota fall automatically as companies depart, or must Harvest wait for a new order?

Tadanobu prefers a scheduled quantity that declines with the roster. Kiyoka prefers a district guarantee so remote patrols are not starved by central accounting. Noritada prefers any formula clear enough that later historians can tell whether the garrison exceeded it.

The Lion will accept Crane wardens if the wardens cannot reduce military supply without a recorded finding. They will accept Scorpion inspection if inspectors cannot halt a convoy merely by opening an inquiry. They will accept Dragon appeal if emergency orders remain effective until reviewed rather than being suspended by every objection.

## Sword transitions the Mandate can order

### Trade duration for replacement

Tadanobu will reduce his commission to one year if the Mandate funds provincial patrol training and names the first three checkpoints to transfer. Kiyoka accepts the shorter term because replacement has become a duty rather than a hope.

### Renounce Harvest in open court

The Lion will return every civilian granary key and publicly disclaim authority over village allotments. In exchange, the Crane must acknowledge the fixed military quota and surrender independent armed command of their barge escorts.

### Make the record exact

Noritada accepts a proclamation crediting the Crane with feeding the south if it also credits the Lion with securing the northern stores and disciplining its own soldiers. He rejects a bland formula thanking all clans equally; equal language would conceal unequal acts and make every delegation fight over placement.

### Lower standards before receiving office

Tadanobu will perform visible submission before the final draft is adopted if the Emperor's herald reads the proposed commission immediately afterward. This preserves the crucial order—obedience first, office second—without leaving the Lion publicly disarmed and politically uncertain.

## Pressure and failure

Ask whether the Lion will obey an immediate withdrawal and Tadanobu says yes. Treat obedience as proof that the order is harmless and Kiyoka produces the replacement ledger: which named officer reaches each village before nightfall?

Let Harvest vary military supply at discretion and Tadanobu refuses the Sword Reed rather than accept decorative command. He may then demand that the Emperor choose another clan and bear responsibility for the transition.

Grant the Lion authority to seize seed rice and Noritada warns that the first history of the Mandate will show soldiers eating the province’s future under a lawful seal. Call the deployment conquest in open court and the younger Matsu captains stop arguing about how to leave; they begin arguing about vindication.

## What to record

- who holds the title and daily execution of Sword;
- who can remove the commander and who succeeds him;
- what activates and terminates emergency command;
- the fixed military supply formula and how it declines with demobilization;
- which stores remain beyond military reach;
- the order in which keys, checkpoints, and companies are transferred;
- who replaces each departing force;
- what visible act establishes submission before office;
- how Lion service and misconduct enter the public record; and
- what the autumn review is empowered to change.
