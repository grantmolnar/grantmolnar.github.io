# Chamber of Seven Reeds

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** court, drafting, procedure, mandate

Red cords expose every dependency among office, work, stores, emergency power, succession, remedy, proclamation, and the field receipt that makes a capital order real.

## Opening View

One amended word pulls three red cords taut. Otomo Kazetada strips courtesy from the clause and exposes an empty office; Miya Shun leaves its route and effective hour blank. Kitsuki Harunori follows the cords into obligations the amendment would reopen. Seppun Tomoe refuses to certify the resulting command. Around them, the ten boxes beneath each Reed fill unevenly. Both complete proposals have just become drafts again.

## Encounter Material

## The table that will not forgive courtesy

The Chamber of Seven Reeds occupies a room deliberately too narrow for grandeur. Each clan may send one principal, one adviser, and one clerk. The Reedwater witnesses rotate through two places at the lower end of the table. The restriction prevents a gallery from turning every correction into triumph or insult. No delegate can look backward for permission from a wall of matching colors. Here a promise must survive the sound of its own wording.

Seven lacquered tablets stand upright in a rack above the drafting table. Beneath each tablet lie nine shallow boxes. At first they appear empty. As the scribes work, each receives a slip naming one part of government: title, execution, audit, resources, ordinary emergency action, extraordinary sacrifice, succession, term, and review. A tenth strip, laid crosswise beneath all nine, names the forum for remedy.

Otomo Kazetada calls the arrangement a courtesy to the truth. Miya Shun calls it a way to discover whether a proclamation contains a government. Kitsuki Harunori calls it evidence prepared before the offense.

The change in kind should be palpable. Earlier audiences were about claims, concessions, and pressures. Here every graceful phrase must be attached to a person, a resource, a deadline, a record, a field recipient, and a consequence. The table will accept almost any allocation of provincial power. It will not conceal who commands on the first morning, who receives the order, or what happens when the capital learns that Reedwater could not obey it.

## The officers of the Chamber

### Otomo Kazetada: keeper of the Imperial purpose

Kazetada chairs the drafting. The Chamber, not Kazetada, assigns each Reed. He prevents the Mandate from reading like a treaty among powers that happen to tolerate an absent Emperor. When a clause begins with a clan’s service rather than an Imperial need, he places a blank slip over the clan name and asks whether the sentence still commands anything.

He is most severe about recitals that flatter a coalition into authorship. The clans may be thanked. Their emergency service may be named exactly. Their concessions may be preserved in the record. The Mandate itself must remain an order issued because the Emperor governs Reedwater, not a compact that binds him because enough delegations signed it.

Kazetada's pressure is subtraction. When a clause grows elaborate, he removes every honorific and asks whether authority, burden, and remedy remain. When a coalition claims that a phrase is essential to face, he asks which operative duty changes if the phrase is removed. If none changes, the phrase belongs in a recital or proclamation. If a duty does change, the court must stop calling it mere courtesy.

### Miya Shun: reader for distance

Shun reads each clause toward Reedwater, never toward the room. He imagines a rain-soaked village headman holding only the proclamation, one schedule, and a tired messenger. He marks names that depend on court context, deadlines counted from events no district can observe, offices whose boundaries cannot be explained aloud, and remedies that require a petitioner to reach Otosan Uchi before the harm becomes permanent.

He insists that every Reed produce a first proclamation, a transfer notice, and a correction route. A Mandate may contain technical schedules unavailable to every household, but the public order must say who may command, what burdens may be imposed, when emergency authority ends, where objections go, and which prior emergency claims are closed.

Shun's pressure is repetition. He reads the same clause first as a herald, then as an executor, then as an affected household. Each reading reveals a different ambiguity.

### Kitsuki Harunori: reader for conflict and failure

Harunori maintains the contradiction ledger. He tests self-judgment, missing substitutes, and remedies that arrive after the harm has become architecture. If the Sword executor depends on Harvest for rice, he asks who decides whether a shortage is necessity or retaliation. If Waters may flood a district and Judgment receives reports from the engineer’s own clerk, he asks whether appeal exists before or only after the sacrifice.

He places a red cord between clauses that depend upon one another. An amendment to either end requires both to be reread. By the end of drafting, the table may hold a web of cords linking grain to garrison, roads to materials, sacred consultation to emergency works, Accounts to every compensation promise, and succession to every temporary commission.

Harunori's pressure is the failed holder. For each power he asks what happens if the named official dies, refuses, lies, cannot travel, is accused, receives contradictory orders, or claims the emergency has not ended.

### Seppun Tomoe: reader for obedience

Tomoe tests whether submission changes command in the field. A lowered standard, surrendered key, returned dispatch rod, or sealed resignation has no value if officers, guards, horses, ledgers, and stores remain under an independent chain. When a surrender remains symbolic, she draws one hard line through it. “Again,” she says.

She will accept staged submission when immediate replacement would endanger Reedwater. She will not accept decorative submission followed by practical autonomy. A clan may retain execution under an Imperial commission. It may not retain the same power merely because the Mandate uses a softer noun.

Tomoe's pressure is the first conflicting order. She asks whose messenger is obeyed when a former commander and a new titleholder issue different instructions on the same morning.

### The rotating provincial chairs

Rei, Brother Hojun, Saburo, and Natsugawa Kenta take turns in two chairs marked only **burden** and **continuance**. They do not possess a veto over the Mandate. They possess standing to identify a household, district, shrine community, office, or livelihood that a clause consumes without naming.

The burden chair asks who pays in rice, labor, land, danger, delay, or silence. The continuance chair asks whether the named successor has people, records, stores, routes, training, and lawful recognition sufficient to act.

A clan adviser may challenge the relevance of provincial testimony. Harunori decides whether the testimony bears on an operative consequence. Kazetada decides whether it belongs in the Mandate, a schedule, a covenant, a proclamation, or the record of reasons. The witnesses cannot simply be thanked and dismissed once their facts become inconvenient.

## Five documents, one chain of provincial command

The Chamber separates five instruments that courtiers often try to compress into one beautiful sheet.

1. **The Mandate:** the operative Imperial assignment of powers, duties, limits, succession, term, and review.
2. **The recitals:** the stated disaster, governing purpose, services already rendered, public relinquishments, and reasons for unusual divisions of authority.
3. **The schedules:** districts, stores, routes, work phases, transfer dates, review dates, named deputies, and other details expected to change without rewriting the entire Mandate.
4. **The side covenants:** supply, repayment, training, custody, compensation, and other reciprocal duties whose breach triggers a remedy but does not automatically transfer the central title.
5. **The proclamations and registers:** what households, officers, shrine communities, merchants, and local magistrates must be told; how corrections, burdens, exemptions, and claims are recorded.

The distinction is not decorative. A recital preserves honor and interpretation but does not command an officer. A schedule may be amended by the authority named in the Mandate, while the central allocation remains intact. A covenant can make military readiness reliable without giving Sword control of Harvest. A proclamation can make a lawful order intelligible without reproducing every legal qualification.

Any bargain imported from the moon-viewing must be assigned to one or more of these instruments. If no one can say where it belongs, the promise is politically real but administratively unplaced.

## The ten-part clause test

Complete ten fields for each Reed.

### 1. Imperial title

Who answers publicly to the Throne for the office? Title may carry appointment, dismissal, and the duty to report without carrying daily execution. If title is joint or rotating, the Mandate must say who speaks when the holders disagree.

### 2. Ordinary execution

Who performs the daily work and gives routine orders? Execution may be divided by district, function, season, or project. A division must name the boundary and the forum for disputes at that boundary.

### 3. Audit and inspection

Who may see records, compare claims, preserve evidence, issue interim warnings, and refer misconduct? These powers need not all belong to the same actor. Final sanction should not hide inside access or secret reporting.

### 4. Resources

Which stores, taxes, customs, labor quotas, vehicles, animals, specialists, stipends, and records are assigned? Authority without resources is an invitation to requisition outside the Mandate. Resources without a duty are a private treasury.

### 5. Ordinary emergency action

What may an executor do immediately to preserve life, records, stores, routes, or the approved plan? The trigger, duration, notice, and retrospective record must be named.

### 6. Extraordinary sacrifice

Who may flood a district, close a road for a season, divert seed rice, impose a general muster, move a shrine community, or suspend an ordinary appeal? This power should be narrower, more visible, and more reviewable than site safety or routine emergency action.

### 7. Ordinary succession

Who inherits the office after death, expiration, promotion, or ordinary replacement? What training, records, seals, and resources pass with it?

### 8. Failure succession

Who acts during accusation, recusal, refusal, incapacity, conflicting orders, disappearance, or unlawful retention? The failure successor should not depend on the disputed holder's permission.

### 9. Term and transition

When does the commission begin, mature, narrow, transfer, or end? Which conditions are calendar dates and which are capacity tests? Who certifies that a condition has been met?

### 10. Review and remedy

Who may petition, where, by what route, with what interim protection, and what remedies are available? Review may correct an act, compensate a burden, remove an officer, narrow an emergency power, or trigger failure succession. It should not be a promise that every decision will later be discussed.

## Drafting in six passes

### Pass one: purpose before beneficiaries

Write one sentence stating what the Mandate accomplishes. It may emphasize Imperial peace, survival through the next rains, reconstruction, lawful transition, or provincial restoration. The sentence will not settle every dispute, but it determines how the Emperor hears later tradeoffs.

Kazetada rejects a purpose defined as balancing clan claims. Claims are inputs. Reedwater is the governed object. The Throne's peace is the authority.

### Pass two: dangerous combinations

Before assigning names, mark combinations no single interest may safely control. Harvest, Sword, and Accounts remain the clearest. Waters design, sacrifice authority, and Judgment may be another. Roads priority, requisition, and customs intelligence may be another. Shrine classification, consultation, and final appeal may be another.

A dangerous combination may be permitted for a short emergency term, but only deliberately. If so, it must strengthen audit, failure succession, term, and first review rather than pretending the danger disappears because the holder is competent.

### Pass three: office matrices

Fill the ten fields for all Seven Reeds. Blank fields are allowed during drafting and forbidden at certification. A shared field must name a tie-breaker or escalation path. A conditional field must name who observes the condition.

This pass often exposes bargains that were never actually complete. A clan may accept execution but refuse audit. Another may accept a public title but demand resources sufficient to direct the executor. A third may offer a successor without funding the people who make succession possible.

### Pass four: cords of dependency

Harunori links every cross-office dependency. Grain supply links Harvest, Sword, Roads, Accounts, and review. Dike construction links Waters, Roads, Shrines, Judgment, Accounts, and the burden ledger. Inspection links Accounts, record custody, protected reporting, sanction, and public correction.

No amendment may be considered local merely because it changes one tablet. The cords show which other clauses, covenants, public promises, and provincial burdens must be reopened.

### Pass five: submission and first-day orders

Tomoe and Shun require an implementation packet for the first forty days. It names the visible acts of obedience, transfer of seals and records, dispatches to named field officers, receiving acknowledgments, staggered effective hours, narrow holdover duties while messages travel, replacement timetables, provisional pay and supply, and the first forums for petition.

A Mandate that begins at autumn review is not a government. Know what happens the morning after the audience.

### Pass six: read for distance, conflict, and malice

Shun reads the proclamations. Harunori assumes every officeholder will interpret ambiguity in favor of retaining power. Kazetada asks whether the instrument sounds like the Emperor's order. The provincial chairs ask whether a household can survive obedience. Tomoe asks which soldiers, guards, clerks, engineers, and messengers will receive a changed command.

Only then will the scribes certify a draft as internally complete. Certification means the instrument can be obeyed and challenged. It does not mean it is wise, merciful, or politically stable.

## Amendment discipline

The Chamber recognizes four kinds of change.

### Scribal correction

Names, dates, references, and obvious inconsistencies may be corrected without reopening assent if no power, burden, remedy, condition, or honor changes. Shun records the correction for proclamation.

### Conforming amendment

A clause may be changed to match an already accepted bargain or dependency. The affected offices are notified, but no promise is released unless its practical burden changes.

### Material amendment

A change is material if it alters title, execution, resources, emergency power, succession, term, remedy, third-party burden, or the remembered meaning of a public concession. Every dependent promise is suspended until it is reaffirmed, narrowed, replaced, or released.

### Structural amendment

A change is structural if it recombines dangerous powers, removes an outside check, changes the Imperial purpose, or makes the primary and fallback proposals cease to answer the same command. The entire affected Reed and every red-cord dependency must be reread. The Chamber may refuse to certify the draft before the audience.

Public relinquishments remain facts even when their intended clause disappears. A Lion standard lowered before witnesses cannot be made unseen by moving Sword to a different commission. The political debt created by that change must be answered, but the vanished clause does not automatically survive as law.

## Two executable provincial governments

Maintain one primary Mandate and, if needed, one fallback. Both must be complete enough to govern Reedwater. Both must begin from the same Imperial purpose. Their differences must be listed Reed by Reed, together with changed beneficiaries, burdens, submissions, resources, and first-day orders.

A fallback is not a pile of concessions removed from the primary. It is a second allocation with its own capacity and costs. Its supporters need not endorse it publicly, but the commission must know who will obey, who will obstruct, and which relinquishments remain valid.

The Chamber marks the two drafts with different seal cords. A promise attached only to the primary is tied in white. A promise attached only to the fallback is tied in black. A promise common to both is tied in gold. The colors are not shown to the Emperor as a voting map. They prevent a fallback whose implementation secretly depends on people committed only to the first draft.

Answer five questions about the fallback:

1. What failure in the primary makes the fallback preferable?
2. Which dangerous power is reduced, and which capacity is lost with it?
3. Which clan or Imperial family will obey despite receiving less?
4. Which provincial burden becomes heavier or lasts longer?
5. What must the Emperor amend if he chooses it immediately?

## Preparing acts of submission

Before the Second Audience, every intervening power should perform or schedule at least one act showing that emergency possession did not create permanent right.

Possible acts include:

- returning a granary key while retaining a written supply claim;
- lowering a field standard and accepting an Imperial commission;
- delivering a customs ledger into outside custody while preserving protected sources;
- surrendering a road checkpoint token while accepting route execution;
- sealing an emergency requisition register and opening the ordinary repayment process;
- naming the date on which a shrine commission transfers to trained local keepers;
- delivering an engineer's authority to the Imperial titleholder while retaining technical command within the approved plan; or
- presenting a roster of officers, clerks, stores, and pending orders that will pass to a successor.

Each act requires an effective time, recipient, inventory, continuing duty, and remedy for incomplete transfer. Ceremony may make the act honorable. The schedule makes it real.

## Pressure on the command chain

Closed doors do not entitle the drafters to peace.

A delegation may attempt to move a costly condition from the Mandate into a side covenant where breach appears less public. Another may insist that a recital praising service implies renewal. A technical adviser may discover that an amendment consumes labor or timber already promised elsewhere. A provincial witness may identify a household category absent from the burden ledger. A courtier may withdraw assent after learning that a rival's public concession was narrower than reported.

Useful Chamber complications include:

- the primary proposal depends on a successor who cannot act before winter;
- the fallback preserves Imperial clarity but requires twice the local labor;
- the Lion supply covenant and Crane seed reserve use the same rice;
- a sacred-consultation deadline makes an emergency engineering schedule impossible unless a bypass and remedy are written;
- the Scorpion inspection procedure protects sources but delays the public correction Shun needs to stop a false tax demand;
- a Roads closure protects work crews while preventing the magistrate's circuit from reaching two districts;
- a public gift transferred custody of keys whose underlying stores were never inventoried; or
- a clan accepts a term but rejects the person authorized to certify that the term has ended.

These are not traps with single answers. They force every amendment to reveal which promise, capacity, or burden it preserves.

## Leaving the Chamber

The Chamber produces four bundles for the final audience:

1. the primary Mandate, recitals, schedules, covenants, proclamations, and first-forty-days orders;
2. the fallback bundle, equally complete but marked by a separate cord;
3. the submission register, naming acts completed and still due; and
4. the contradiction and burden memorandum, listing risks the commission knowingly accepted.

Kazetada asks the Witnesses to sign the memorandum, not the Mandate. The Emperor authors the order; their names certify only that the court has heard its price.

Refusal does not cancel the Second Audience. It enters the record, perhaps preserving their honor after an unrecognizable amendment, perhaps convincing the Emperor that they brought advocacy rather than judgment.
