# Pavilion of First Rain

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** court, crane, harvest, relief

Doji Masuyo has made relief look like government. She can surrender command, but not the civilian granaries, seed distinctions, named creditors, and public memory that keep gratitude from becoming spoil.

## Opening View

Doji Masuyo moves one cup of rice into Reed Bank’s empty basin. Six other districts immediately become harder to save. Daidoji Yorishige keeps the barge tallies open; Asahina Chiharu covers the seed measure with her hand. Polished grain, seed rice, and black stones make every prior promise visible. Masuyo leaves the cup where she placed it. The court may move it, but the hunger moves with it.

## Encounter Material

## The rice in the basins

The seven porcelain basins beneath the rain chains are not a courtly conceit. Each is a scale model of one district's surviving grain. Polished rice represents food fit for immediate distribution. Unhulled rice represents the autumn seed reserve. Black pebbles represent relief obligations the Crane have already promised but cannot yet meet. The basin for Reed Bank contains no polished rice at all.

Doji Masuyo asks one Witness to move a cup into Reed Bank. She does not suggest a basin. Every choice leaves another district short. If asked how much rice exists beyond the display, she answers without softness: “Enough to keep the province alive. Not enough to preserve every promise made in its name.”

The Crane relief did not consist of a single generous shipment. Masuyo assembled a chain of coastal warehouses, merchant advances, temple storehouses, village records, river pilots, Daidoji escorts, and household guarantees. White cord was tied around every Crane-funded bale so exhausted storekeepers could distinguish relief rice from taxes, seed, and private stock. Those cords now make the Crane claim visible in nearly every surviving granary.

Masuyo wants the court to understand that visibility as service. Her enemies call it a flag planted in famine.

## Three Crane positions

The Crane delegation is disciplined in public and divided in purpose.

### Doji Masuyo: authorship and civilian rule

Masuyo wants the Crane to execute Harvest for a fixed term. She never says the Crane own the harvest. She says the province already knows which white cord opens a storehouse and which handwriting answers a village petition. Her cadence is generous until the operative sentence arrives; then every date, key, and obligation is exact. She will accept an Imperial titleholder, outside audit, and Lion command of the Sword Reed. Her essential demand is that granary orders remain civilian: village allotments must be assessed before military quotas are deducted, and a commander may not increase his own share at the granary door.

For Masuyo, public credit is not ornament. The words used to describe the relief will determine which household may claim repayment, whose clients appear indispensable, which marriages become plausible, and whether the next governor treats Crane storekeepers as lawful servants or presumptuous creditors. She can surrender jurisdiction more readily than she can accept a record in which the Crane merely assisted an Imperial abundance that arrived by itself.

### Daidoji Yorishige: barges, escorts, and repayment

Yorishige commanded the barges through the flooded southern reaches. He has slept little since returning to court and has no patience for praise that does not replace boats, pay pilots, or settle grain advances. He supports civilian Harvest, but he distrusts any clause that forbids armed force without providing escorts for the next convoy.

He will place Daidoji guards beneath the Lion commander's operational orders if three limits are written plainly: they remain assigned to relief routes rather than general patrols; the Sword office cannot seize their vessels; and losses incurred under military orders are charged to the garrison rather than quietly added to the Crane relief debt.

Yorishige is the delegation's practical compromise. He is also the person most likely to make compromise expensive by presenting an exact account of what it costs.

### Asahina Chiharu: the seed reserve

Chiharu coordinated temple kitchens and shelters for displaced children. She has brought no petition for office. She has brought a sealed jar containing the last seed rice from a drowned village and placed it beside Masuyo's sleeve.

Chiharu insists that seed rice be legally distinct from ration rice and military supply. An emergency commander may open a ration storehouse. Touching the seed reserve should require a second authority and a public finding that the coming harvest has already been sacrificed. She will oppose her own clan if Masuyo trades that protection for longer tenure or greater credit.

Chiharu can, however, make a Crane relinquishment legible as virtue rather than defeat. Protect the seed reserve and village keepers, and she will publicly bless the transfer of the granary keys to an Imperial titleholder.

## What the Crane are actually asking for

Masuyo's opening proposal has seven linked clauses.

1. **Title:** Harvest is invested in a Miya commissioner in the Emperor's name.
2. **Execution:** Crane-trained civilian wardens manage the granaries for three years.
3. **Assessment:** village food and seed requirements are entered before military supply is calculated.
4. **Military allotment:** the Sword office receives a fixed monthly quota and convoy schedule.
5. **Emergency variance:** a Seppun-certified emergency may alter the quota for thirty days, with the excess entered as a debt against the Sword office.
6. **Audit and succession:** an outside auditor may inspect stores, and each district maintains two named successor wardens.
7. **Credit and compensation:** the proclamation names the Crane relief and the Accounts office repays documented advances without converting those debts into jurisdiction.

The proposal is not neutral. Three years of execution would let the Crane train the next generation of provincial granary officials, and a Miya titleholder may depend on Crane information. Ask who will still know the system when the term ends and Masuyo admits the advantage plainly.

## The grain distinctions that matter

The court's easiest language—"the province's rice"—conceals four different stores.

- **Rations** keep households alive and may be distributed during ordinary relief.
- **Seed rice** belongs to the next harvest and should not be consumed without a second authorization.
- **Garrison allotments** are predictable military supply, not a commander's discretionary requisition.
- **Emergency reserves** may be opened only under a named trigger and must produce a public record of who ordered the loss.

A proposal that protects granaries while failing to distinguish these stores will collapse at the first hungry checkpoint. A proposal that distinguishes them without naming who holds the keys will be rewritten by whoever arrives armed.

## Harvest arrangements the Mandate can assign

### Shorten tenure, deepen credit

Masuyo will reduce Crane execution from three years to one autumn and one planting season if the Mandate guarantees repayment, names the Crane relief in the first proclamation, and permits Crane officials to train local successors. This satisfies the Otomo concern about emergency service becoming title while preserving a durable record of Crane competence.

### Surrender the Sword visibly

The Crane still command armed escorts in the south. Masuyo will order their white standards lowered beneath the Imperial banner at the moon-viewing or before the final audience. In return, the Lion must publicly renounce authority over civilian granaries and accept the written quota.

### Put repayment under Accounts

The relief debt can be certified by an outside auditor and repaid over several harvests. Masuyo will accept slow payment. She will not accept a clause granting Harvest until the debt is satisfied; that would turn every disputed figure into a reason for continued Crane rule.

### Give villages standing

The granary charter can require the Reedwater witnesses or district headpeople to countersign changes to village allotments. Masuyo accepts this because local testimony protects her wardens from military pressure. Yorishige dislikes the delay but will accept a narrow emergency exception.

## Pressure and failure

Credit the Lion first and Masuyo does not sulk; she alters the political price. Every Crane concession thereafter must be named in public, and she refuses any closing formula that makes all service indistinguishable.

Demand that the Crane forgive the relief debt as proof of virtue and Yorishige produces the merchants, pilots, widows, and temples whose property funded the barges. The debt is not held by one wealthy house that can renounce it gracefully.

Give the Crane both Harvest execution and its audit, and Chiharu asks who counts the seed jars after an honorable official decides the province can afford generosity. Give the Lion power to change quotas without an outside finding, and Masuyo ends private bargaining and carries the dispute into open court.

## What to record

- who holds the public title to Harvest;
- who issues daily granary orders and for how long;
- how rations, seed rice, military allotments, and emergency reserves differ;
- what fixed supply the Sword office receives;
- who may authorize a temporary variance and how it is repaid;
- which Crane debts are compensated outside the grant of office;
- what public language records Crane service;
- whether local keepers or witnesses have standing to object; and
- what visible act marks the Crane surrender of military command.
