# Theater of a Thousand Sleeves

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** court, scorpion, inspection, customs

Seven empty robes accuse an absent eighth. Bayushi Namika divides inspection into access, preservation, protected reporting, restraint, review, and sanction, exposing where secrecy shelters truth and where it shelters power.

## Opening View

Seven empty robes perform accusation without an accused. A screened witness withholds a public name; Shosuro Mirei catches the message that slips beneath the curtain. Soshi Kureha places two identically sealed customs copies side by side—their totals disagree. Bayushi Namika watches the audience as the accusing sleeve halts above the open ledger. Truth has reached the stage; publication, protection, inspection, and sanction have not yet agreed on an entrance.

## Encounter Material

## The actors with no bodies

The seven robes on the empty stage move through a short play before Namika speaks. Harvest receives a sealed bale. Sword demands it. Roads diverts it. Waters claims the boat. Shrines covers the seal with a prayer strip. Judgment asks for the original order. Accounts enters six different explanations in six different ledgers.

At the end, every sleeve points toward an absent eighth figure. No robe represents that person.

Namika asks whether the missing figure is a thief, a witness, an auditor, or the Emperor. Whatever answer the Witnesses give, she praises the choice and asks who profits from the other three absences. The answer changes with the viewer. That is the point.

The Scorpion did not create that failure. They are unusually well equipped to exploit it. Namika offers the same skills as remedy and waits for the commission to admit why remedy and danger resemble each other.

## Three Scorpion positions

### Bayushi Namika: authority that survives public suspicion

Namika speaks as though every accusation were a gift whose wrapping deserves admiration. “A title would only make people suspicious,” she says, pausing just long enough to acknowledge the joke. She wants access to manifests, the right to compare records across offices, a protected route for reporting discrepancies, and a guarantee that an inspected official cannot dismiss the inspector. She prefers a narrow office whose usefulness is harder to deny and whose limits are plain enough that rivals can accept it.

She will serve under Otomo, Miya, or mixed Imperial authority. She will accept Dragon review and reciprocal inspection. She refuses an arrangement in which her findings may be ignored privately whenever they embarrass a favored clan.

Namika's public concession is powerful: the Scorpion can relinquish Accounts title before the court, allowing other clans to support their involvement without confessing trust. Her private demand is that the relinquishment not reduce inspection to an invitation.

### Shosuro Mirei: the witness who cannot appear

Mirei directs the theater and has spoken only through actors during the first half of the meeting. She coordinated observers at Reedwater's docks, storehouses, and checkpoints. Some are samurai. Some are clerks, boatmen, servants, merchants, or people whose testimony would expose a disobedient superior.

She wants protected reporting that separates the identity of a source from the substance that can be tested. An inspector may know the name. The preliminary report may use a cipher. A reviewing magistrate may examine the sealed identity if credibility becomes decisive. The inspected office does not receive a list of everyone who spoke before safeguards exist.

Mirei is not asking the Mandate to treat rumor as proof. She wants a route by which a frightened person can cause records to be preserved before the official controlling those records learns whom to punish.

### Soshi Kureha: seals, copies, and custody

Kureha maintains the Scorpion's duplicate manifests. She is less interested in secrets than in the physical life of a record: who wrote it, who sealed it, who copied it, when it changed hands, and which version appeared after an inquiry began.

She wants inspectors to copy or seal records they examine. Access without custody lets an office display one ledger today and replace it tomorrow. Custody without an index lets inspectors remove papers and later claim they never existed. Kureha proposes numbered copies, witnessed seals, and a register held outside Accounts.

She opposes giving Scorpion inspectors the power to impose punishment. The same person should not collect the source, interpret the discrepancy, decide whether secrecy is justified, and sanction the accused. Her caution is institutional rather than moral: an office that performs all four functions will eventually produce the conclusions its patrons reward.

## The six powers hidden inside inspection

Namika requires the word *inspect* to be divided before it is assigned.

1. **Access:** enter a storehouse, checkpoint, office, or archive and see the relevant record.
2. **Comparison:** match manifests, seals, dates, quantities, routes, and orders across Reeds.
3. **Preservation:** copy, index, or seal a record so later review concerns the same evidence.
4. **Protected reporting:** convey a discrepancy to an authority that cannot be blocked by the inspected office.
5. **Interim restraint:** briefly halt destruction, transfer, or disappearance while Judgment acts.
6. **Sanction:** punish misconduct, recover goods, remove an official, or alter an office.

The Scorpion seek the first four and a very narrow version of the fifth. They do not need the sixth. Judgment can decide sanction; titleholders can remove; Harvest or customs authorities can recover goods. This division makes Scorpion expertise useful without giving the clan a private government made of ledgers.

## What happened to the customs record

The surviving Reedwater books contain at least five incompatible exemptions.

- Lion checkpoints waved through military supply under emergency orders.
- Crane barges treated relief cargo as outside ordinary customs.
- Unicorn caravans recorded feed and remount exchanges by route rather than by district.
- Crab engineers requisitioned timber and stone against future Imperial compensation.
- Shrine and temple stores moved goods under seals that local collectors hesitated to open.

None of these practices proves theft. Together they make theft, favoritism, duplication, and honest error almost impossible to distinguish after the fact. Namika can identify where quantities fail to reconcile. She cannot decide alone which necessity justified the discrepancy.

Decide whether Accounts is chiefly a tax office, audit office, customs archive, relief-debt registry, or institutional memory. One office may perform all these tasks only if their internal powers are divided.

## The secrecy ladder

Mirei offers five levels rather than a single choice between public and secret.

### Public rule

The mandate, inspection scope, record categories, reporting authority, recusal rules, and maximum periods of secrecy are public. No one should discover the inspector's powers only after being inspected.

### Confidential source

A witness identity may remain known only to the receiving inspector and a named reviewing authority. The substance must be recorded precisely enough to test without exposing the source automatically.

### Sealed interim finding

A preliminary discrepancy may remain sealed while records are preserved and retaliation risks are assessed. The sealed report has a date, custodian, and deadline.

### Reviewable finding

Judgment, Otomo, or another named authority receives the evidence and can test the inspector's method, conflicts, and conclusions. The inspector cannot convert source protection into immunity from review.

### Public disposition

The final disposition states what category of problem occurred, what remedy followed, and what office was responsible. Names or methods may remain protected where necessary, but authority cannot rest forever on an unseen conclusion.

Secrecy without a ladder becomes permanent convenience. Publicity without stages destroys the people and methods that make inspection possible.

## The inspection sequence

Namika's proposed office acts in seven steps.

1. A discrepancy, complaint, or scheduled audit opens a numbered inquiry.
2. Inspectors identify the records and sites within scope; expansion requires a recorded reason.
3. Copies or seals preserve the evidence, with a duplicate index outside Accounts.
4. Witness information is separated into testable substance and protected identity.
5. A preliminary report distinguishes error, unresolved discrepancy, plausible necessity, and suspected misconduct.
6. Judgment or the titleholder decides restraint, recovery, sanction, or closure, subject to recusal.
7. A final disposition enters the common record, and sealed materials receive a later review date.

An inspector may briefly preserve a ledger, cargo, or seal from destruction. An inspector may not stop a grain convoy indefinitely merely by opening an inquiry. Daizen's deadlines and Harunori's substitute jurisdiction make the limit real.

## Inspection powers the Mandate can divide

### Relinquish Accounts title, retain inspection

Namika will publicly support an Imperial or Miya titleholder if Scorpion inspectors receive statutory access, preserved reporting, and outside dismissal. This is the cleanest visible relinquishment in the triangle.

### Reciprocal inspection

The Scorpion accept inspection of their own records, source-handling procedures, and expenses by a Dragon or Otomo reviewer. Witness identities remain sealed unless the reviewer finds a stated reason to open them. Kureha supports this because chain of custody should constrain inspectors first.

### Customs access for bounded Roads

The Unicorn may administer crossings and tolls if manifests use a common form and inspectors may compare them without seizing routine cargo. The Scorpion gain information; the Unicorn gain protection against later accusations based on incomparable books.

### A public performance instead of a public confession

Mirei can stage the sequence of emergency exemptions so every delegation admits its own exception without being singled out as corrupt. The performance creates consent for common records. Individual misconduct found later remains answerable.

## Pressure and failure

Give the Scorpion title, inspection, custody, secret reporting, and sanction, and Namika asks who will inspect her. “The Scorpion” ends the lesson. “No one” proves the court wants an instrument of fear rather than Accounts.

Make every source public and Mirei removes three reports, asking whether the court prefers incomplete truth delivered honorably. If they accept every sealed accusation as fact, she replaces one actor's script with another and asks which unseen author they just empowered.

Allow the inspected office to dismiss inspectors and Kureha returns a ledger to its owner, then asks the Witnesses to remember its contents. If inspectors may seize records without an index or witness, she asks why the court should trust the copy more than the original.

Exclude the Scorpion because their expertise is dangerous and Namika agrees—then asks which safer delegation can compare the books before the final audience. Refusal is a choice to leave the problem with those who benefit from it.

## What to record

- who holds title and daily execution of Accounts;
- which inspection powers are granted separately;
- which records and sites inspectors may access;
- how copies, seals, and custody are witnessed;
- who may dismiss an inspector and for what cause;
- how witnesses report without making rumor dispositive;
- how long preliminary findings may remain sealed;
- which authority reviews methods, conflicts, and conclusions;
- what narrow interim restraint inspectors possess;
- who decides recovery, sanction, and removal;
- what final disposition becomes public; and
- who inspects the inspectors.
