# Hall of Open Roads

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** court, imperial, miya, implementation

Miya Shun sends every elegant clause across a broken bridge. Dates, messengers, recipients, acknowledgments, petitions, and remembered corrections decide whether the Mandate reaches Reedwater as law or arrives as rumor.

## Opening View

A courier returns an order addressed to a dead officeholder. Another changes the western journey by two days. Miya Shun places twin transition ledgers beside an empty travel stool and asks that one promising clause be read to the absent headwoman who must obey it. His brush stops wherever the sentence lacks a date, route, recipient, acknowledgment, or correction path. The road has begun editing the court.

## Encounter Material

## Read the order to the empty stool

Shun sets a travel stool before the commission. On it lie a rain-stiff proclamation, a courier's route tally, and a blank correction slip. He requires the intended settlement to be read aloud as though a village headwoman sat there with no knowledge of the court's bargains and no clerk able to explain what the capital meant.

At every unnamed office he raises one finger. At every date counted from an event Reedwater cannot observe, a second. At every courteous ambiguity, he places the correction slip over the sentence and asks who may amend it, how the village learns of the change, and what the collector will say it means at the gate. He will not let grief, rank, or fine brushwork excuse an order that arrives beautifully and too late.

## The implementation problem

The central post road is broken in two places. Western messages can move through Unicorn remount stations. Southern dispatches can travel on Crane barges. Northern reports pass through Lion checkpoints. Several villages receive orders only through temple messengers or merchants.

A mandate that depends on simultaneous proclamation will fail. A mandate that changes authority before its replacement arrives will create gaps. A mandate that names duties without identifying the person who explains them will be rewritten by every collector, patrol leader, and local magistrate.

## The two-copy transition ledger

Shun keeps one transition ledger in the Chamber and sends one authenticated copy toward Reedwater. Every transfer names the court instrument, the field order, the receiving officer, the route, the expected arrival, the effective hour, and the proof of receipt. A key displayed in Otosan Uchi does not change a granary door by sympathy. A lowered model standard does not redirect a patrol until the officer beneath the field standard receives the changed command.

Where routes differ, offices may change at different hours. The old holder retains only the duties required to prevent a gap and may not expand, renew, requisition for a new purpose, or treat delayed receipt as a fresh grant. The new holder cannot command before the order reaches the named field chain. Shun calls this staggered obedience; courtiers who dislike the phrase are invited to repair the roads.

Shun requires an implementation schedule with:

- the order in which offices change hands;
- the messenger route for each district;
- the date each temporary authority expires;
- the name or title that receives petitions during transition;
- the receiving officer and acknowledgment route for every changed field order;
- the narrow duties that continue if a messenger is delayed;
- the treatment of orders already issued under emergency authority; and
- a method for publishing corrections without pretending the first proclamation never happened.

## Reedwater's witnesses

Shun can admit four provincial witnesses behind the outer screen of a formal hearing. They cannot address the Emperor directly without extraordinary leave, but their statements can enter the record through Miya questions.

He proposes Rei, headwoman of Reed Bank; Brother Hojun, keeper of a drowned riverside shrine; Saburo, master of a stranded caravan association; and Natsugawa Kenta, a low-ranking provincial samurai whose vassal house lost both land and records. Their interests do not align, and none has been summoned merely to certify the court’s compassion.

Refuse live testimony and Shun supplies written depositions instead. The Great Clans will then quote the portions favorable to them.

## What Shun wants

Shun wants the Mandate to be comprehensible enough that a local official cannot honestly misunderstand it. He wants the relief schedule protected from ceremonial delay. He also wants the dead governor's failures acknowledged without turning his cousin into the convenient cause of every weakness the court itself tolerated.

He will support almost any allocation if its messages, transitions, and corrections are sound. He opposes every arrangement that expects Reedwater to pause between one authority ending and another learning the road.

## What to record

- the order in which new offices take effect;
- the routes by which each district receives the proclamation;
- who answers petitions during the transition;
- whether provincial witnesses are admitted;
- how corrections are issued and remembered; and
- which emergency orders remain valid after the Mandate.
