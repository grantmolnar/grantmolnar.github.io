# Stone-and-Moss Court

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** court, dragon, law, audit

Kitsuki Harunori turns the clean face of law into the earth. Judgment must reach evidence, recuse without vanishing, permit urgent action, preserve appeal, and remain an Imperial circuit rather than Dragon possession.

## Opening View

Kitsuki Harunori turns the central stone, hiding its clean face against the earth. An emergency order waits with Mirumoto Daizen; Togashi Sorin brings a claimant the ordinary rules cannot seat. Between them lies an accused official’s unopened seal. Harunori removes the first judge’s name from the circuit and sets out recusal, substitute jurisdiction, interim protection, appeal, and remedy. Dusk will not recuse itself.

## Encounter Material

## The stone turned over

The clean central stone has not been scrubbed. Harunori turned it over before the Witnesses arrived. Its hidden side bears the stains of years beneath the soil, while the surface everyone remembers is now face-down.

He places seven wooden tablets around it, one for each Reed, and asks for a magistrate beside every office. Whenever they name a judge from the clan holding title, execution, or audit, Harunori moves that judge onto the central stone. Soon the clean face is crowded. “Remarkable,” he says. “Impartiality appears to require a very large stone.”

This is his argument without accusation: every delegation supports impartial law until impartiality requires someone else to judge its own necessity. The Mandate can divide offices carefully and still fail if every dispute returns to the power whose conduct produced it.

Harunori does not ask for permanent Dragon possession of Judgment. He prefers an object to an accusation and a question to a conclusion. He asks what record exists, whose hand can alter it, what standard governs the decision, and what happens while review is pending. His politeness gives people time to notice that they have placed themselves on the overturned stone. What he asks them to build is a circuit that survives the first recusal, missing magistrate, emergency order, and petitioner too low in status to be heard conveniently.

## Three Dragon positions

### Kitsuki Harunori: evidence and stated reasons

Harunori wants Judgment to preserve records, identify conflicts of interest, and require reasons that can be compared with what actually occurred. He knows the Kitsuki method is regarded by many courts as graceless at best and presumptuous at worst. He does not propose to replace testimony, status, oath, and reputation with a footprint held up triumphantly before a daimyo.

He does insist that rank cannot make two contradictory granary ledgers agree, that a sealed order cannot authorize an act dated before it was written, and that a magistrate should not call a conclusion inevitable when the record preserves alternatives. Evidence is not a weapon against etiquette. It is a restraint on convenient memory.

Harunori will accept Imperial title, rotating clan magistrates, or a mixed circuit. He requires preserved reasons, access to records, and automatic recusal where a judge's clan materially benefits from the office under review.

### Mirumoto Daizen: a judgment that arrives in time

Daizen commands the small Dragon escort and has served as an enforcing magistrate. He distrusts procedures designed by people who never had to carry an order to armed officers before dusk.

He wants every appeal path to have a substitute judge and a deadline. Recusal without replacement allows a powerful respondent to disable the forum by implicating every available magistrate. Appeal without interim authority lets each office suspend an unwelcome order by filing a petition. Retrospective review without a remedy becomes a history lesson delivered to the injured.

Daizen supports immediate emergency orders that remain effective during review, but only for a short stated term. He also wants Judgment to possess a narrow power to preserve records, seal disputed stores, or stay an irreversible act. It should not take command of a dike or garrison merely because litigation exists.

### Togashi Sorin: the claimant law does not expect

Sorin is a monk whose travel-stained robe makes him easy to mistake for an attendant until he speaks. He asks what happens when the right claimant is a ferryman's widow, a displaced keeper, a child adopted after the flood, or a village whose surviving headman owes his office to the commander it must accuse.

He does not oppose Harunori's evidence. He opposes the belief that a complete record exists before someone has been allowed to speak. Sorin wants standing rules broad enough to admit those who bear a duty or burden even when they hold no title to the disputed office or land.

He also asks what Judgment is for. Punishment may be required, but many Reedwater disputes need restored grain, rebuilt access, replacement labor, a corrected proclamation, or a new official—not merely a declaration that the former official acted wrongly. Sorin presses the Dragon toward remedies that repair relationships rather than perfecting the court's account of failure.

## The common circuit

Harunori proposes a Judgment structure with four layers.

1. **Local petition officers** receive complaints, preserve initial records, and can order a brief pause when an irreversible act is imminent.
2. **A three-seat provincial circuit** hears disputes among Reeds, claims against officeholders, and appeals from local petition officers. One seat is Imperial; the other two rotate among clans not materially interested in the dispute.
3. **An emergency reviewer** examines extraordinary orders after they take effect. Review does not automatically suspend the order, but it can terminate renewal, require compensation, or refer misconduct.
4. **An autumn panel** reviews patterns rather than isolated cases: repeated recusals, missing records, unpaid remedies, and offices that invoke emergency authority as ordinary practice.

Judgment is not the auditor, prosecutor, commander, or shrine authority. It decides whether those offices used their own powers lawfully and whether promised remedies occurred.

## Recusal as a chain, not an absence

The Dragon delegation offers a sequence for every contested office.

### Disclosure

A magistrate states the clan, household, debt, kinship, command, or office relationship that may affect judgment. Secret conflicts discovered later are treated more severely than disclosed conflicts managed openly.

### Decision

Some conflicts require automatic recusal: the judge's clan holds the office under review; the judge helped issue the disputed order; the judge or household has a direct relief debt; or the judge would review an inspector the judge appointed and may dismiss.

Other conflicts may be waived by all parties or reviewed by the Imperial seat. Not every acquaintance empties the court.

### Substitute jurisdiction

The charter names who acts next. A first substitute and a failure substitute are appointed before the office begins work. A recused magistrate may preserve records and prevent immediate destruction but may not decide the merits.

### Deadline

The substitute accepts or declines the case promptly. If the deadline expires, jurisdiction passes automatically rather than waiting for a fresh favor from court.

### Interim order

The circuit states what remains in force while review proceeds. Reversible administration usually continues. Irreversible destruction, transfer, execution, or dispersal may be paused narrowly.

### Recorded reason

The recusal, substitute, deadline, and interim order enter the public procedural ledger even when evidence or witness names remain sealed.

Without these steps, recusal becomes either a fiction or a veto. With them, a clan can surrender judgment over itself without surrendering access to law.

## Evidence, testimony, and records

Harunori identifies five records the Mandate must preserve across all Reeds.

- the order as issued, including date and seal;
- the office's stated ground for acting;
- the material record used, such as manifests, maps, rosters, shrine registers, or grain counts;
- objections and consultations received before action when time allowed; and
- the result, including costs, injuries, transfers, and promised remedies.

Witness identities may be sealed. Military routes may be summarized. Sacred materials may be restricted to qualified readers. The existence of a record, the office holding it, and the date it must become reviewable cannot remain secret indefinitely.

Harunori refuses a Mandate in which every office keeps its own archive in a different vocabulary. He supports Miya copies and Scorpion comparison because common law requires records that can meet each other.

## Judgment circuits the Mandate can establish

### Dragon execution, Imperial title

A Seppun or Otomo titleholder may appoint Harunori to organize the circuit for one year. The Dragon receive no permanent claim, and local or mixed successors must be trained before autumn review.

### Rotating seats with fixed recusals

Each Great Clan may name qualified magistrates, but automatic conflicts are written in advance. This reduces the appearance of Dragon tutelage while preserving the circuit's structure. Daizen insists on preselected substitutes so rotation does not become vacancy.

### Emergency action, retrospective teeth

Sword and Waters may act immediately under defined triggers. Judgment cannot halt necessary action merely because an appeal is filed. It can end renewal, compel restitution, remove an official through the titleholder, and publish repeated misuse at autumn review.

### Protected petitioners, tested accusations

Namika can provide confidential reporting channels; Sorin supports broad standing; Harunori requires corroboration before sanction. The combination protects witnesses without making anonymous accusation itself a judgment.

## Pressure and failure

Give Judgment power to suspend every contested order and Daizen asks how many objections a rival needs to stop a convoy. If they deny all interim power, Harunori asks what appeal remains after a shrine is dismantled, a seed store consumed, or a witness removed.

Require recusal without a substitute and Sorin quietly identifies relationships until every magistrate is disqualified. The exercise is not mockery. It demonstrates how easily purity can abolish jurisdiction.

Give the Dragon permanent Judgment and Harunori asks whether future Dragon offices will still be judged by Dragon magistrates. If the answer is yes, the principle has failed. If the answer is no, permanent Dragon title is unnecessary.

Treat low-status testimony as beneath the court and Sorin asks whether the Mandate governs only those permitted to enter it. If they accept every claim without testing, he asks whether compassion has become a method by which the most practiced liar acquires a village's labor.

## What to record

- who holds title and execution of Judgment;
- who may petition and what burdens create standing;
- which conflicts require automatic recusal;
- who acts as first and failure substitute;
- how quickly jurisdiction transfers;
- what remains effective during appeal;
- which irreversible acts may be paused;
- what records every Reed must preserve;
- which witness identities or materials may remain sealed and for how long;
- what remedies Judgment may order or recommend; and
- how the autumn panel detects patterns no single case reveals.
