# Hall of the Chrysanthemum Throne

**Role:** necessary, start  
**Tags:** court, opening, imperial, politics

The flood left Reedwater's practical hands alive but severed every lawful joint above them. The Witnesses receive seven days to allocate authority without commanding the province themselves.

## Opening View

An attendant brushes river silt from seven water-darkened tablets. Beside the bare mat, Miya Shun’s courier case holds a dispatch that reaches only a washed-out bridge. The Emperor thanks every clan whose intervention saved Reedwater, then names the limit of that gratitude: seven days. “Tell me what the province is for,” he says, “and which present power must yield when the answer becomes law.”

## Encounter Material

## The disaster before the court

Six weeks ago, while Reedwater slept, the eastern dike gave way along an old buried joint. The governor had called the holders of the Seven Reeds, their seal-bearing chief clerks, and the officers empowered to succeed them into the compound for the annual closing of summer accounts and renewal of autumn warrants. Conflicting levy totals kept the council working after midnight. Water reached the compound before the alarm reached the outer gate. It killed Imperial Governor Miya Shigeyuki and the people whose countersignatures could lawfully appoint, replace, or overrule the seven provincial offices. By dawn the post road ended in brown water, three shrines had lost their ground, the autumn seed rice floated among roof beams, and seven commissions possessed seals but no living hands.

District clerks, granary wardens, patrol captains, ferrymen, shrine keepers, and village officials survived. Their narrow commissions let them keep doors open, repeat existing orders, and preserve life within named districts. None may appoint a province-wide successor, alter another Reed's priority, open a sealed reserve for a new purpose, or judge a conflict among offices. The flood destroyed the lawful joints of government without destroying all of its practical skill.

Each commissioner was in Reedwater when the dike failed. They may have arrived as escorts, magistrates, engineers, courtiers, priests, merchants, local retainers, or clan representatives, but each saw enough of both relief and burden to become inconveniently credible. They need not be neutral, multi-clan, or free of patronage. Their reports already sit in the Imperial record, and their experiences cross enough rival commands that no claimant can dismiss the whole commission as another delegation's instrument. For a homogeneous group, distinguish the characters by field duty, sponsor, debt, or recorded disagreement; one clan mon cannot make five witnesses share one conclusion.

The Great Clans answered the catastrophe before the Imperial bureaucracy could. Lion troops secured the northern granaries. Crane barges fed the southern villages. Crab engineers marked a new line for the dikes. Unicorn caravans reopened part of the western road. Phoenix priests tended displaced shrines. Scorpion magistrates sealed customs houses whose records no longer agreed. Dragon investigators began preserving claims before memory hardened into precedent.

Every intervention saved lives. Every intervention left behind a key, a checkpoint, a debt, a shrine register, or a habit of being obeyed.

The first Imperial holding orders recognized necessary action without granting provincial title. Those orders now overlap. The Emperor has commanded every field force to remain within its present boundary and summoned delegates bearing full letters of authority, copies of current field commissions, and named couriers. Senior commanders, storekeepers, and work chiefs may remain in Reedwater. Their delegates can bind them only through authenticated orders that name a receiving officer and an effective hour.

## The Seven Reeds

The seven lacquered tablets represent offices once coordinated by the dead governor:

1. **Harvest:** granaries, seed rice, crop assessments, and emergency distribution.
2. **Waters:** dikes, canals, ferries, work levies, and flood authority.
3. **Roads:** post stations, bridges, remounts, convoy priority, and requisitions.
4. **Sword:** the provincial garrison, musters, patrols, and emergency command.
5. **Judgment:** magistrates, appeals, land disputes, and crimes involving officeholders.
6. **Shrines:** sacred boundaries, ritual labor, temple stipends, and relations with the local kami.
7. **Accounts:** taxes, customs, audits, relief debts, and the records by which future obligations will be remembered.

The offices need not be held by seven different powers. An office may belong to an Imperial family, a Great Clan, a joint commission, or a rotating stewardship. Title, daily execution, audit, and emergency succession may be separated. The Emperor will accept a complicated instrument if it can be obeyed. He will reject a beautiful instrument that merely postpones the conflict.

## The Imperial command

The Emperor names the commissioners **Witnesses of the Broken Dike**. The title is temporary and carries no rank outside this business. It grants access to the named delegations, authority to request records through Imperial officers, standing to enter findings and objections, and the duty to submit a primary proposal and any honest fallback. It grants no command in Reedwater and no power to promise that a bargain will be adopted.

The common holding order ends at the close of the seventh day. The Emperor will not renew every emergency possession again as one undifferentiated act. A Kaiu estimate warns that another serious breach may occur within six weeks; the Mandate must leave enough time for proclamations, transfers, and work orders to reach Reedwater before the weather makes the court's delay physical.

> In seven days, We will place these offices into living hands. Bring Us a mandate that protects Our people, restores Our peace, and does not teach the Empire that troops sent without leave are the surest road to reward.

Return with one primary proposal; keep a second only as an honest fallback. They are not required to satisfy every clan, collect a fixed number of endorsements, or discover a hidden correct solution. They must assign authority, burden, oversight, succession, and review in a form the Emperor can plausibly impose.

## What the commission can and cannot command

A delegation's support is evidence that an allocation can be implemented. It is not a vote granting the Throne authority. The Witnesses do not acquire a Reed by investigating it, and their temporary commission cannot move one soldier, open one granary, or redirect one ferry. A recommendation may overrule a claimant, but then it must name the officers, stores, routes, and lawful coercion that make obedience possible without that claimant's help.

Every concession has three possible layers:

1. **A court undertaking:** what the delegation promises to accept if the Emperor adopts the relevant clauses.
2. **A submission instrument:** what present claim, token, key, standard, ledger, or commission changes legal status.
3. **A field order:** the authenticated instruction sent to a named receiving officer, with an effective hour, inventory or roster, acknowledgment route, and narrow holdover rule if the message is delayed.

Courtesy may preserve face around these acts. It cannot substitute for them. A promise made in Otosan Uchi does not move a checkpoint or open a granary until the corresponding chain of command changes in Reedwater.

## The claims laid beneath the tablets

The senior delegates do not repeat their credentials. They place the remains of emergency rule beneath the seven tablets: Masuyo's white cord around a granary key, Tadanobu's folded roster beneath a covered standard, Etsuko's flood-stained bell clapper, Harunori's paired copies of one disputed order, Namika's sealed report, Sachiko's broken remount tally, and Masashige's iron strap from the temporary dike. Each object saved lives. Each now supports a claim about who should command, inspect, inherit, or be repaid.

Kazetada, Tomoe, and Shun place nothing among them. Kazetada asks which powers become dangerous when held together. Tomoe asks what field officer will obey the proposed transfer. Shun asks what order can reach that officer before the next rain. Their questions do not compete for a Reed; they test whether the court's gratitude can become an Imperial government rather than seven rewarded emergencies.

The Emperor lets every claimant name the service, debt, surrender, and continuing capacity attached to the object before them. His silence is not neutrality. He wants Reedwater governed, the relief preserved, the mobilized clans made obedient, and the final instrument remembered as an act of the Throne—not a treaty the clans permitted him to seal.

## Decisions before the audience ends

### Name the governing purpose

Tell the court what the Mandate is for. Plausible answers include restoring the province, preventing clan war, protecting the harvest, rebuilding the dikes, reaffirming Imperial authority, or preserving the people of Reedwater. Every later clause will be read against this purpose.

### Accept or resist provisional orders

The Emperor offers to freeze the current emergency zones until the common holding order ends in seven days. Accepting preserves relief but lets temporary arrangements mature. Replacing them immediately creates uncertainty but prevents any clan from claiming seven additional days of successful administration. Attach provisional limits, reporting duties, or a ban on expansion if seven more days would otherwise enlarge the emergency claim.

### Set the audience calendar

Choose whether Imperial-family consultations, clan audiences, Reedwater testimony, or an early drafting session frames the week. The order changes who frames the problem before the others respond.

### Decide whether the work is public

Open hearings make contradiction costly and give witnesses protection. Private consultations permit sharper concessions but let every delegation describe the Witnesses differently afterward. A mixed calendar works only when the public record is named in advance.

## What to record

- the purpose announced for the Mandate;
- whether current emergency zones were frozen, narrowed, or replaced;
- which consultations receive priority;
- whether Reedwater witnesses will be admitted;
- whether the commission promises one proposal or reserves a fallback;
- which clan intervention receives the first public credit; and
- which delegates possess authority to bind which field chains.
