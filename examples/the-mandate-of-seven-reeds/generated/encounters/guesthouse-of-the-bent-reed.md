# Guesthouse of the Bent Reed

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** court, province, witnesses, consequences

Four flood-stained cloaks wait beyond the innermost wall. Reedwater’s witnesses expose the arithmetic hidden inside honorable bargains and demand standing that survives after the court no longer needs their testimony.

## Opening View

An attendant lifts one flood-stained cloak; Rei catches the hem before it leaves the mat. Brother Hojun opens a shrine register, Saburo adds toll receipts and a cracked cart pin, and Natsugawa Kenta writes two successor names beneath every Reed. Their papers face the offices that created the burdens, not the sponsors who admitted them. Rei’s tally begins tomorrow, whether or not the court has finished listening.

## Encounter Material

## The court outside the court

The guesthouse lies beyond the innermost palace wall because none of its Reedwater guests possesses the status to move freely through the Emperor's court. Their distance is architectural and political. Every Great Clan delegation can reach them. They cannot reach any delegation without permission.

The four flood-stained cloaks across the new mats are therefore not a rustic gesture. Rei refused to let attendants remove the garments because the stains are the one piece of Reedwater the witnesses are permitted to place in the room without a sponsor translating it first.

Miya Shun has secured a narrow procedure. The witnesses may answer questions placed through a Miya examiner; their statements enter the working record; and a sponsoring samurai may request that a witness attend a relevant clan consultation behind a screen. They still cannot address the Emperor directly, issue demands as equals to Great Clan delegates, or claim to represent “the people” as a unified estate.

The first political task here is not to discover what Reedwater wants. Reedwater wants incompatible things. The task is to give consequence-bearing testimony standing without pretending status has vanished.

## Four witnesses, four kinds of leverage

### Rei of Reed Bank: subtract the people already absent

Rei lays duty sticks across the mat, then removes one for every adult already serving a granary, patrol, ferry, shrine, road crew, or displaced household. She leaves the ropes from two grain sheds coiled beside the tally: one shed was recovered after she cut it free during the flood; the other fed three villages downstream.

Rei has brought duty sticks, seed tallies, household registers copied before the flood, and a new ledger kept on scraps cut from relief bales. She knows which households contain children, elders, draft animals, carpenters, boatmen, wounded veterans, shrine keepers, and adults already absent on service. Her numbers are incomplete. They are more complete than any number currently assigning burdens. When a courtier calls one more levy small, she asks, “Small after which subtraction?”

Rei will accept a harsh labor levy if it is calculable, cumulative, and survivable. She opposes equal quotas among unequal villages because equal numbers can be a polite method of choosing which smaller place fails. She wants a household to know before the collector arrives:

- what is owed;
- which prior service counts;
- who may authorize a change;
- what substitution is permitted;
- when another call may occur;
- where an appeal can be heard; and
- what happens to the household while its workers are absent.

She does not ask the court to spare Reed Bank from relocation automatically. If the eastern wall protects more lives and the village must move, she wants land, water access, seed, graves, shrine obligations, road access, and the date of possession written as one package. “Higher ground” is not compensation if its fields cannot support the households placed there.

Rei's leverage is refusal of false capacity. Every clan wants her to confirm that the province can bear its preferred burden. She will confirm exactly what it can bear and name what must be surrendered to do so.

### Brother Hojun: carry the year with the shrine

Hojun lays two bell clappers beside a household calendar marked with lamp duty, ford cleaning, burial care, festival carrying, and the promises families made after earlier floods. The bells remain under water. The calendar is why replacing them would not settle the claim.

He has brought the clappers from two bells. The bells themselves remain under water. Courtiers frequently offer to replace them. “A roof can be rebuilt in a season,” Hojun says. “Who rebuilds the year?”

Hojun accepts that sacred places may be translated, combined, or even lost under necessity. He rejects the assumption that payment for a building concludes the obligation. If a community moves, the Mandate must account for its households, stipend, calendar, graves, water access, ritual objects, and authority to teach successors. If emergency work bypasses consultation, the restoration debt belongs to the office that benefited rather than to an undefined future piety.

He and Rei disagree over timing. Rei will move before the next rain if the material package is sound. Hojun may demand a delay for rites that preserve continuity. He and Kenta disagree over authority. Kenta wants local samurai to confirm shrine keepers. Hojun insists that the community's knowledge cannot be recreated by appointment alone.

Hojun's leverage is continuity. The Phoenix can bless a policy; the Crab can preserve or move a structure; only the surviving community can make the resulting institution live in Reedwater.

### Saburo of the Three Crossings: name the debtor before the wheel turns

Saburo empties requisition receipts onto the mat and sorts them first by horse, cart, boat, room, and day lost—not by the honor of the officer who took them. Sachiko's invitation gets him through the palace gate. It does not own his testimony, and he says so before the Miya examiner records his first answer.

Saburo has brought a packet of requisition receipts. Some bear Lion seals, some Crane cords, some Crab work marks, and some only the brush signature of an officer whose unit has moved. Several describe the same animal differently. None names the office that must pay. Saburo names horses before offices and dates before honor; he understands Roads from beneath the wheel.

He supports tolls at expensive repaired crossings if the schedule is public and the revenue returns to maintenance. He accepts requisition when the taking creates an enforceable debt. He opposes both practices when they are disguised as spontaneous service expected from households too low in status to demand a receipt.

Saburo distinguishes compensation forms. Tax credit helps a landed household with a future assessment. It does not replace a dead horse before planting. Priority access to trade helps an association. It does not feed an innkeeper required to lodge soldiers for a month. Replacement in kind helps only if quality and delivery are recorded.

He and Rei disagree over valuation. Saburo wants market replacement. Rei fears that market language will let large associations price every village cart beyond the tax value used when officials take it. They can support a schedule with categories, condition records, and appeal.

Saburo's leverage is continued movement. The court can order a road open. It cannot make ruined carriers, ferrymen, and stable households possess what the order assumes.

### Natsugawa Kenta: leave an office that can answer

Kenta places three surviving copies of the lost Natsugawa archive before the examiner: a patrol roster kept by a village, a tax boundary copied by Accounts, and an irrigation judgment preserved by a ferryman. His elder brother and the house originals died in the flood. The scattered copies prove that local authority once connected offices; they do not prove that Kenta can restore it by inheritance alone.

Kenta wants local samurai households preserved as Imperial vassals rather than absorbed into whichever Great Clan executes the Reeds. His courtesy is stiff because he must ask Great Clan lords to prepare for their own departure. He does not demand the restoration of every local office as though bloodline were competence; he demands a path by which local authority can become real again, and a date by which imported banners cease to be its excuse.

Kenta accepts Great Clan specialists and temporary executors. He asks that each office name provincial deputies, training, pay, records, and a date when local officers either qualify for succession or are removed through a stated process. He opposes automatic inheritance of office by surviving households; some fled, stole, or simply proved incompetent.

Kenta and Rei disagree over exemptions. Provincial samurai households have long claimed service obligations that reduce village levies in theory. Rei's records show several households contributing neither soldiers nor labor after the flood. Kenta offers to place every local household, including samurai houses, into the burden ledger according to its lawful duty. That concession costs him support among the very families he wants to preserve.

His leverage is implementation. A Great Clan commission can govern Reedwater for a season with imported people. It cannot leave behind a province unless someone local receives seals, records, resources, and an authority other offices recognize.

## They are not one delegation

The witnesses can issue a joint statement only on narrow principles.

They agree that:

- burdens must be counted cumulatively rather than office by office;
- requisitions must create named debts;
- relocation must restore a community's means of life, not merely assign new ground;
- local succession requires resources and review, not ceremonial appointment; and
- testimony must remain available after the Mandate is proclaimed.

They disagree about:

- whether Reed Bank should move for the eastern wall;
- how long sacred rites may delay emergency work;
- whether market value or assessed value should govern compensation;
- which provincial households deserve succession;
- how heavily merchants may profit from emergency scarcity;
- how much labor Reedwater can bear before the next harvest; and
- whether Great Clan execution is a necessary apprenticeship or the beginning of permanent dependency.

A patron may obtain one witness's endorsement. It cannot honestly claim all four without accepting the constraints that make their agreement difficult.

## The burden hearing

Convene a structured burden hearing if the court has been pricing offices without adding their demands together. Each office presents what it demands from Reedwater during the next year. Rei enters the demands into one ledger while the other witnesses test the assumptions.

The hearing should include:

1. **Harvest:** rice distributions, seed protection, granary labor, and tax timing.
2. **Waters:** labor days, camps, materials, relocation, and maintenance.
3. **Roads:** carts, animals, lodging, tolls, closures, and replacement.
4. **Sword:** recruits, patrol support, provisions, billeting, and checkpoint succession.
5. **Judgment:** petition routes, witness travel, record copying, and local deputies.
6. **Shrines:** ritual labor, relocated communities, stipends, and protected dates.
7. **Accounts:** taxes, debt repayment, inspection demands, and documentation.

The ledger exposes collisions rather than producing an automatic fair allocation. The same household may appear as a source of seed rice, two laborers, one cart, a patrol recruit, lodging, and shrine duty. Cancel, sequence, compensate, or openly preserve the collision.

A useful rule is **one burden, one author, one debtor, one date of release**. Shared work remains possible, but one office must own the demand rather than every beneficiary treating it as free.

## Standing that survives the audience

The witnesses distrust one magnificent hearing followed by a year of silence. They ask for five durable forms of standing.

### Receipt of proclamation

Named district witnesses receive the same official text and correction notices as officeholders. They need not depend on a collector's paraphrase.

### Access to the burden ledger

Village heads, recognized associations, shrine communities, and provincial deputies may inspect the entries affecting them and challenge duplication or mistaken identity.

### Petition without patron ownership

A clan may sponsor travel or protection, but the petition enters under the claimant's name. The sponsor cannot withdraw it merely because the testimony becomes inconvenient.

### Trigger for review

A threshold of unpaid requisitions, consecutive labor service, unfilled local offices, displaced households, or repeated emergency extensions automatically places the matter before Judgment or the autumn review.

### Return testimony

At the autumn review, Reedwater testimony is required before commissions are renewed. The court may reject it. It may not claim there was no authorized way to hear it.

These provisions do not make the witnesses legislators. They make implementation answerable to people capable of showing that its stated premises are false.

## Sponsorship and capture

Each witness needs protection to enter the court, but sponsorship creates risk.

- The **Miya** can authenticate testimony and carry it without claiming substantive agreement.
- The **Unicorn** can sponsor Saburo's travel and route evidence but benefit from his road claims.
- The **Phoenix** can protect Hojun's sacred testimony but may elevate it above other burdens.
- The **Dragon** can preserve Rei's and Kenta's documentary evidence but may turn living scarcity into a procedural problem.
- The **Crab** can demand labor testimony because bad numbers endanger the works while still preferring the answer that keeps crews full.
- The **Crane** and **Lion** can each protect witnesses in districts they relieved or secured and may quietly exclude criticism of those systems.
- The **Scorpion** can protect identities and records but make honest testimony appear secretive by association.

Use multiple sponsors, Miya custody, or a common witness charter to keep access from becoming ownership. The best protection is not a perfectly neutral patron. It is a procedure in which no patron owns the words.

## Provincial terms the witnesses can support

### Endorsement of a harsh but legible settlement

Rei will publicly support substantial labor and tax burdens if the aggregate ledger, service ceilings, seed protections, appeal, and dates of release are real. Her endorsement can blunt accusations that every limit on clan office is sentimental weakness.

### Conditional relocation covenant

Rei and Hojun can support the eastern wall if Reed Bank and the shrine community receive land, water, seed, road access, graves and rites, stipends, succession, and a date-by-date move. Their support does not erase the sacrifice. It records consent to a specified exchange.

### Road compact

Saburo will persuade the Three Crossings association to maintain service through the next rains if valid requisitions are consolidated, partial immediate replacement reaches single-animal households, and the rest becomes Accounts debt with review. Sachiko gains capacity without receiving private control of the association.

### Provincial succession compact

Kenta will recruit surviving local samurai into deputy offices under Great Clan specialists if qualification, pay, records, burden, and removal are explicit. He accepts that some households will fail. The clans accept that competence does not entitle them to inherit the province indefinitely.

### Joint refusal

If the proposed Mandate assigns ceremonial titles while leaving cumulative burdens uncounted, the four witnesses may refuse to praise it. Their refusal is not a veto. It deprives the court of the easiest story that Reedwater received harmony gratefully.

## Failure modes

- **Testimony without standing:** the court hears facts once and builds no mechanism by which later facts matter.
- **A single “voice of Reedwater”:** patrons select the witness whose suffering endorses their preferred policy.
- **Compensation without livelihood:** relocated households receive land or money but lose water, roads, seed, graves, or work.
- **Local succession by status alone:** surviving officeholders inherit the failures that preceded the flood.
- **Local succession without resources:** the clans surrender seals and keep the horses, records, stores, and trained staff.
- **A burden ledger with no remedy:** the province receives an accurate account of its destruction.
- **Patron protection as ownership:** testimony becomes another asset exchanged among delegations.

## What to record

- each witness's independent position and sponsor;
- the burdens each office demands from every district;
- collisions canceled, sequenced, compensated, or deliberately preserved;
- the valuation and repayment form for requisitions;
- the complete relocation package for every displaced community;
- provincial deputies, qualifications, resources, and removal procedures;
- who receives proclamations and corrections;
- access, petition, review-trigger, and return-testimony rights; and
- any statement the witnesses refuse to make for the court.
