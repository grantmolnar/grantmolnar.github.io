# Ministry of Divided Ink

**Role:** necessary  
**Tags:** court, imperial, otomo, drafting

Otomo Kazetada cuts each Reed into title, execution, audit, and succession, then asks which powers may safely share a seal. His ink makes gratitude expensive and concentrated authority visible.

## Opening View

The fourth scribe has nothing to write. Request, refusal, and witness already fill the first three pages; CONSEQUENCE remains blank. Otomo Kazetada divides one proposed office into title, execution, audit, and succession, then circles the missing hands. Grain seals, military rosters, and dismissal orders arrive from separate screens. He offers the commission a paper knife. “Which clan do you intend to make too strong?”

## Encounter Material

## Four cuts beneath one tablet

Kazetada lays one Reed tablet across four slips of paper: **title**, **execution**, **audit**, and **succession**. His paper knife holds the first three flat while the fourth curls upward in the room's dry air.

“Which clan do you intend to make too strong?” he asks. Denying the premise earns a small smile. Naming one earns ink, paper, and his undivided attention. He asks who gives daily orders, who spends the resources, who preserves the complaint, and who acts when the holder dies, refuses, is accused, or cannot reach Reedwater. Then he asks which of those hands shares a seal with the person who profits when the arrangement fails.

The old governor gathered too many of these functions in one household because coordination was convenient. Kazetada turns the succession slip face-up. The flood has already supplied his objection: too much of the province depended on one room remaining alive.

## What Kazetada wants

Kazetada will help draft any instrument that preserves Imperial authorship and prevents one Great Clan from holding Harvest, Sword, and Accounts together. He prefers asymmetry: a Crane title with Miya execution and Dragon audit is safer than three identical joint committees whose members can form a permanent bloc.

He particularly opposes awarding permanent authority because a clan arrived first after the flood. Emergency service may justify reimbursement, public credit, or a limited commission. It must not become a doctrine that mobilization creates title.

He does not require every office to remain weak. The dikes need decisive command. The roads need schedules. The garrison needs one chain of orders. His objection is to an arrangement in which the same power can raise troops, control food, and certify its own accounts.

## Allocations Kazetada can separate

### Ministry drafting staff

Kazetada assigns senior scribes if the commission permits the ministry to mark every clause that concentrates mutually reinforcing powers. The marks are advisory, but hiding them from the final audience would be an insult.

### Imperial title, clan execution

He offers precedents in which an Imperial family holds an office while a Great Clan performs the work. This preserves the Throne's visible authority while rewarding expertise. It also lets the Imperial titleholder revoke or replace the executor.

### A deliberately ungrateful settlement

Kazetada suggests separating compensation from jurisdiction. The Crane can be repaid for rice, the Lion commended for security, and the Crab granted materials without turning gratitude into office. This is politically expensive because every clan prefers an honor that continues to govern.

### The Otomo price

Kazetada does not ask for an office. He asks that the Mandate include a review that allows the Throne to reopen any arrangement that produces an exclusive clan bloc. The clause can be narrow, timed, and public. It cannot be meaningless.

## Failure and pressure

Demand a simple award to one clan and Kazetada will explain how to write it, then arrange for the Emperor to see the precedent it creates. Make every office joint and he exposes the paralysis under the courteous plural.

If treated as an enemy, he becomes one without telling a single lie. He arranges the true objections in the order most damaging to the proposal, then apologizes for having found so graceful a sequence.

## What to record

- which combinations of powers the commission treats as dangerous;
- whether title and execution will be separated;
- who may audit an officeholder from the same clan;
- whether the Otomo review clause is accepted; and
- what compensation is kept outside the grant of office.
