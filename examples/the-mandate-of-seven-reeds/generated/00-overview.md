## Synopsis

Six weeks after the eastern dike drowned Reedwater’s governor and every lawful successor to the Seven Reeds, district deputies still keep granaries, patrols, ferries, shrines, and accounts moving inside commissions too narrow to govern the province. Great Clan relief commands overlap beneath a common Imperial holding order that expires in seven days. The Emperor names the adventurers Witnesses of the Broken Dike and orders them to turn rescue into government before gratitude, possession, and the next rains harden emergency service into title.

## Premise

You were in Reedwater when the water came. You saw which orders reached hungry villages, which checkpoints kept roads open, which shrines lost their people, and which acts of rescue also created debts and habits of command. The Emperor does not call you neutral. He calls your testimony inconveniently cross-cutting. For seven days you will serve as the Witnesses of the Broken Dike, placing Harvest, Waters, Roads, Sword, Judgment, Shrines, and Accounts into living hands while making every beneficiary surrender enough power for the others to obey.

## Explanation

The flood hides no master conspiracy, and no claimant owns the uniquely lawful settlement. The governor had gathered the seven officeholders, their seal-bearing clerks, and their first lawful substitutes to close the summer accounts when the dike failed. District clerks, wardens, patrol officers, shrine keepers, and other practical deputies survived, but their commissions permit continuation, not province-wide succession, changed priorities, or judgment among the Seven Reeds. Every Great Clan intervention saved lives. Each also left troops, ledgers, debts, routes, shrines, and a local habit of obedience. The court is not assembling a coalition: the Emperor can compel obedience, and no count of clan assent grants him authority. The problem is to allocate seven provincial offices among hands that already hold incompatible pieces of them, make present possessors surrender keys, standards, records, routes, and work camps, and dispatch orders that can survive distance. Separate title from execution, fasten authority to burden, give emergency power an ending, preserve honor while extracting real relinquishment, and state which costs the restored province must bear. The Witnesses recommend and draft; the Throne adopts. Adoption becomes government only when the Throne issues, so every operative clause needs a submission instrument, a named field recipient, an effective hour, an acknowledgment route, and a remedy when the order arrives late or fails. The finished Mandate must travel, command, be received, fail, be corrected, and remain recognizably the Emperor's act rather than a treaty the clans allowed him to seal.
