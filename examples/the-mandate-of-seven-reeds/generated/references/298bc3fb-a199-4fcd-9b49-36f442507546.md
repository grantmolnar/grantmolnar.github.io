# Brother Hojun

**Reference ID:** `298bc3fb-a199-4fcd-9b49-36f442507546`  
**Kind:** Person  
**Aliases:** Hojun, Keeper Hojun  
**Tags:** Reedwater, shrines, community, consultation

## Summary

The keeper of a drowned riverside shrine speaks for displaced shrine communities, their obligations, and the successors who must carry them after reconstruction.

## Reference Material

## Bell, keeper, calendar, household

Brother Hojun kept a riverside shrine now lost to the flood. He speaks for a living pattern of households, rites, graves, stipends, calendars, and place. Moving or rebuilding a structure does not by itself preserve the community that maintained it or the obligations attached to it.

## What crosses when a shrine must move

Hojun asks a sacred procedure to name a speaker, deadline, witness, reason, override, recorded debt, remedy, and successor. He distinguishes anchored obligations from those that can be translated, seasonal duties from permanent ones, and a displaced congregation from an empty site. Consultation matters only if the resulting obligation can enter engineering, routes, burdens, and review.

## Necessity leaves a restoration debt

Hojun can accept relocation, translation, or loss under necessity when obligations move with the community and the restoration debt remains visible. He does not own theology for all Reedwater, veto every work, or decide whether a particular site is sacred. Any classification, consultation, bypass, remedy, calendar, or succession appointment remains live procedure rather than permanent biography.
