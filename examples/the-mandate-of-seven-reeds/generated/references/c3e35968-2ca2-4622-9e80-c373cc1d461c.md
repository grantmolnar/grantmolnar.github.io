# Kitsuki Harunori

**Reference ID:** `c3e35968-2ca2-4622-9e80-c373cc1d461c`  
**Kind:** Person  
**Aliases:** Harunori, Magistrate Kitsuki Harunori  
**Tags:** Dragon, Judgment, review, recusal

## Summary

The Dragon magistrate requires comparable evidence, stated reasons, recusal with a substitute, timely appeal, preserved records, and judgment outside the interested office.

## Reference Material

## Set the conflicting records side by side

Harunori treats Judgment as a common circuit rather than a Dragon possession. Offices may keep specialized records, but status, secrecy, and vocabulary cannot make contradictory accounts agree. A decision must identify the evidence used, the reason given, the authority exercised, and the record preserved for later review.

## Recuse to a named substitute

An interested judge must step aside to a named substitute under a deadline. Review that arrives after grain is eaten, a shrine is dismantled, or a road closes is only history; appeal that stops every contested act can become another veto. Harunori therefore distinguishes interim restraint, continuing administration, irreversible action, and enforceable remedy.

## Standing enough to challenge, not to command

People who bear a burden may need a route into Judgment even without formal title, but standing does not make compassion proof. Harunori's sheet decides no current finding, appeal, recusal, sanction, office allocation, clause, or outcome.
