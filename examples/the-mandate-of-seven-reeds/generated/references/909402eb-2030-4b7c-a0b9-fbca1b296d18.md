## Summary

The Scorpion principal separates inspection into access, preservation, protected reporting, restraint, review, and sanction so useful secrecy cannot become invisible rule.

## Reference Material

## Access, preservation, report, restraint, review, sanction

Namika distinguishes access to records, preservation of what was seen, protected reporting, temporary restraint, outside review, and final sanction. Combining them lets an inspector discover, rewrite, conceal, and punish through one office. Separating them makes inspection usable without pretending it is harmless.

## Seal the source; test the substance

Some witnesses cannot appear openly before their records, livelihoods, or families are secured. Source identity and method may be sealed while the reported substance is tested through documents, comparison, deadlines, and an identified reviewer. An anonymous report may preserve evidence and open inquiry; it does not prove misconduct.

## Inspection ends before title begins

Namika can serve beneath outside authority, accept reciprocal inspection, and relinquish public ownership of Accounts. Her sheet does not reveal a source, establish a finding, preserve a current seal, impose restraint or sanction, assign Accounts, or settle a bargain.
