# Miya Shun

## Summary

A senior Imperial herald who tests whether an order can cross Reedwater's broken roads with dates, recipients, acknowledgments, corrections, and a return path for petitions.

## Reference Material

## Read it at the drowned gate

Miya Shun is a senior herald and cousin to Reedwater's dead governor. He has postponed public mourning until the province receives orders that can survive the journey. He reads every clause as though a village headwoman heard it without the court's private bargains and raises a finger at every unnamed office, uncertain date, or courteous ambiguity.

## Date, courier, recipient, correction

Shun requires a proclamation chain: a dated command, a named messenger and recipient, an effective hour, a route for receipt, authenticated acknowledgment, remembered corrections, and a petition path back to lawful review. He distinguishes the capital act from the field transfer and the field transfer from the work that follows. An elegant clause that cannot arrive, be understood, or be challenged is not provincial government.

## The record that survives the journey

Shun can host a formal proceeding, announce an undertaking in plain language, keep a docket of the meaning participants claim for it, and protect a narrow route by which Reedwater witnesses enter the working record. He does not treat applause, verse, hospitality, or gratitude as consent. His office carries the Emperor's words; it does not choose the substantive allocation, coalition, burden, or outcome those words may contain.
