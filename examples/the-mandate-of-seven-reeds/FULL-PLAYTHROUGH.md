# Full Playthrough: The Mandate of Seven Reeds

This is one road through the court, not the road hidden behind it. These five Witnesses choose the costly northern channel, accept a short concentration of emergency authority, and make that concentration spend itself on provincial succession rather than harden into occupation.

The five player characters are Doji Kiyoe, Akodo Naritomo, Kitsuki Sadae, Kaiu Masami, and Ide Qulan, collectively titled the **Witnesses of the Broken Dike**. Their full design appears in `PARTY-DESIGN.md`.

## Fixed route

```text
Throne -> Miya -> Unicorn -> Witnesses -> Crane -> Lion -> Phoenix -> Crab
       -> Seppun -> Otomo -> Scorpion -> Dragon -> Moon -> Chamber -> Audience
```

The route visits all fifteen encounters. It postpones the Ministry of Divided Ink and the Stone-and-Moss Court until implementation, provincial burdens, clan execution, and engineering risk have acquired names. Law must therefore bind choices already made visible; it cannot invent a dry province from clean categories.

## Session One: The bare mat

### Hall of the Chrysanthemum Throne

The five Witnesses kneel before seven water-darkened tablets. Each recognizes something in the first recital that can be used against another.

Kiyoe hears the Crane barges thanked before any office is assigned. Naritomo hears the Lion army described as held at the ford by Imperial command rather than condemned for arriving. Masami hears the drowned shrines named among the losses that reconstruction must repair. Qulan hears that seven proclamations must reach seven offices. Sadae hears the Emperor refuse to count supporters.

The Emperor asks for the Mandate's purpose.

Kiyoe proposes, “To preserve the people and stores of Reedwater until ordinary government can be restored.”

Naritomo objects to “until.” He says the first duty is preserving the Emperor's peace through the next rains; ordinary government cannot be invoked as though its officers already exist.

Qulan objects to “stores.” Roads, ferries, shrines, graves, patrols, and village records are not stores. Sadae observes that every noun added to the purpose will later become a claimant's preferred interpretation.

Masami gives the sentence that survives:

> The Mandate will preserve Reedwater through the next rains, restore lawful provincial government, and ensure that emergency service creates answerable duty rather than private title.

The Emperor permits the existing emergency zones to continue for seven days, but the Witnesses add three conditions before accepting: no zone may expand; every new requisition must be entered in a common provisional ledger; and every holder must disclose what would be transferred if its present authority ended. The request is bold enough that Otomo Kazetada looks up. The Emperor grants it without praise.

The title does not make the Witnesses governors. It gives them access to records and delegates, standing to enter findings and objections, and responsibility for a primary and fallback. Each delegation has arrived with a full letter of authority, a copy of its current field commission, and a named courier. Any concession remains a court undertaking until Imperial adoption and a matched field order change the corresponding chain in Reedwater.

The calendar is mixed. Claims to office will be heard privately when precision requires it. Burdens, acts of submission, and the eventual Mandate will enter the public record. Reedwater witnesses will be admitted before drafting rather than displayed at the final audience. The Witnesses announce that they will maintain a complete fallback proposal.

Their first visible disagreement follows immediately. Kiyoe asks to begin with the Crane and Lion because grain and soldiers cannot wait. Qulan asks to begin with Miya Shun because no promise matters until they know how it reaches the province. Naritomo calls the choice backwards: the court already knows roads carry orders. Qulan answers that the emergency contains seven different lawful warrants for the same horse.

The Emperor directs them toward the Hall of Open Roads. Naritomo bows to the decision and writes down his objection.

Before leaving, the Witnesses establish the principal openings. Otomo can divide authority before blame; Seppun can recognize real clan command beneath an Imperial sword; Miya can support a village-by-village instrument; the Crane can yield command if Harvest remains civilian; the Lion can share administration if command remains operative; and the Phoenix can accept reconstruction that preserves sacred obligations. They also record the Emperor's unanswered question: which present holder will surrender something because this is Imperial peace rather than a bargain it prefers?

### Hall of Open Roads

Miya Shun does not ask who deserves an office. He places seven versions of one emergency order before the Witnesses. Each is accurate. One has no effective date. One names a collector but no correction process. One reached a village after the officer it appointed had left. One refers to a bridge that no longer exists. One was copied by a temple messenger who corrected a place name and thereby sent the order to the right district without authority to alter Imperial text.

Shun asks each character to read the order as a different recipient.

Naritomo reads it as a patrol commander and cannot determine whether the supply priority supersedes village allotments. Kiyoe reads it as a granary warden and cannot determine whether “reserve” includes seed rice. Masami reads it as a work-site commander and cannot identify who may close the west road. Sadae reads it as a magistrate and cannot tell where appeal lies after the named judge recuses. Qulan reads it as a courier and asks which route is supposed to remain open while three offices claim emergency priority.

The Witnesses adopt Shun's proclamation test. Every Reed must name the acting officer, effective hour, route of notice, correction route, and first petition forum. Material corrections must reach the same recipients as the original order. The first forty days must be drafted at the same time as the offices, not appended after the court declares itself finished.

Shun opens a two-copy transition ledger. The Chamber copy records the legal instrument; the Reedwater copy names the receiving officer, courier route, expected arrival, acknowledgment, and the narrow duties the former holder retains if the road delays the message. Authority may change district by district. It may not expand merely because receipt is late.

Shun offers four places behind the outer screen to Reedwater witnesses. Kiyoe asks whether the witnesses will be selected for reliability. Sadae asks who defines reliability. Shun says the surviving provincial records name three people whose testimony every delegation has already used and one minor samurai whose family once carried local seals. Admitting them will not make them agree.

The Witnesses accept all four. Kiyoe asks that their factual statements be authenticated. Qulan insists authentication must not become sponsorship. Shun agrees to carry testimony without claiming agreement with it.

The surviving post-road schedule reveals a viable route to the Unicorn delegation. More importantly, it shows that the western relief road is open only because village horses, temple lodging, merchant carts, and contradictory warrants have been treated as one invisible resource. The Witnesses go next to the Courtyard of Bells.

### Courtyard of Bells

Ide Sachiko receives Qulan as a colleague and an embarrassment. The duplicate manifests he preserved prove the route's success. They also prove that three offices requisitioned the same households because each saw only its own order.

Shinjo Boroldai presents three travelers: an Imperial courier on a Unicorn horse, a village messenger leading an exhausted mare, and a porter walking because the promised cart animal was taken by a Crane convoy. He asks which one demonstrates that the road is open. Qulan answers, “The one whose return is recorded.” None is.

The first hard bargain of the campaign takes shape.

The Witnesses offer Miya title over Roads and Unicorn execution through the first planting season. There will be one dispatch law, one public manifest form, no private toll privilege, a minimum open route for food, messages, and petitions, and two provincial successors trained at each district station. Requisition should be valued before taking where possible; when impossible, it creates an automatic Accounts debt before the animal, cart, lodging, or labor is used.

Sachiko accepts the architecture and rejects the word “automatic” unless someone can identify the debtor, value, beneficiary, and repayment order. Sadae supplies a provisional chain. Qulan adds a rule that the office benefiting from an emergency diversion inherits the debt. Masami objects that a failing dike cannot wait for an argument over which office benefited. Qulan agrees; the entry may follow immediate site safety, but the debt dates from the taking, not from later recognition.

Iuchi Nergui raises the question the Witnesses have avoided. The northern-channel design would cut the present west road. A substitute route needs two remount stations, a new bridge, eighty horses, and a ford that cannot bear stone wagons during rain. Roads can accept the loss only if Waters prices and funds the replacement.

Masami has not yet endorsed the northern channel. She nevertheless accepts the principle that an engineering choice cannot describe a destroyed road as somebody else's inconvenience.

Sachiko invites Saburo of the Three Crossings to testify. The invitation unlocks the court outside the court. The Witnesses end their first session with three provisional commitments:

- Miya title and Unicorn execution for Roads;
- one cumulative requisition debt rather than seven stories of necessity; and
- no reconstruction design accepted until lost movement is entered among its costs.

Naritomo remains unconvinced. He tells Qulan that a road with three petition rights and no patrol is a fine place to be robbed. Qulan tells him the next session will ask how many patrols one household is expected to feed.

## Session Two: The court outside the court

### Guesthouse of the Bent Reed

Rei of Reed Bank has placed six bowls on the floor. They contain seed rice, household rice, a work token, a patrol token, a broken cart pin, and mud from the graveyard. She asks which burden the court would prefer to count first. The Witnesses decline to choose.

Saburo produces the horse ledger. Qulan's signature appears beside the first twelve animals. Later warrants from Lion patrols, Crane convoys, and Kaiu survey teams refer to the same pool without acknowledging his taking. Four horses returned. Three died. Five disappeared into other relief chains.

Qulan does not defend the emergency. He states the route each animal served and asks that the debt be entered against the benefiting office rather than against an undifferentiated Imperial gratitude. Saburo accepts the admission and refuses to endorse the Roads proposal until single-animal households receive partial replacement before the next planting.

Naritomo then sees his own checkpoint warrants in the ledger. He argues that the patrols kept the road usable. Rei answers that lawful necessity does not make the same horse exist twice. Naritomo concedes the point and proposes that military requisitions be charged against Sword even when issued through Roads. Qulan agrees if Roads remains responsible for recording them.

Brother Hojun describes the eastern wall. It would move Reed Bank and the shrine community to higher ground. Kiyoe asks whether the Crane can provide seed and stipends. Hojun says the question is necessary and incomplete. The community's obligations include graves, ford rites, lamp households, burial ground, water access, and the order in which displaced keepers continue service. A building can be replaced before a community can.

Masami places her own emergency order on the floor. It bears her name. She explains how cutting the orchard wall reduced pressure on Reed Bank and flooded a minor household's pasture. The loss is absent from the current provincial tally because the household's records washed away. She asks Sadae to enter the decision and the alternatives known at the time into the common record.

This changes the tone of the hearing. The witnesses no longer speak only as people upon whom experts act. One expert has made herself answerable before asking for authority to act again.

Natsugawa Kenta presents the local patrol roster. It is too thin to replace the Lion immediately. Naritomo says so. Kenta agrees. He asks whether the Mandate will use that fact to fund training or to explain three years later why training never began.

The Witnesses grant the five forms of provincial standing:

1. the same proclamations and corrections received by officeholders;
2. access to the cumulative burden ledger;
3. petition without patron ownership;
4. a defined trigger for autumn review; and
5. return testimony after the court's assumptions meet Reedwater.

Kiyoe argues that provincial petitioners should need an authenticated factual basis. Rei asks whether a granary warden needs one before reducing an allotment. Kiyoe revises her proposal: emergency orders may operate, but a petition supported by the burden ledger, a district officer, or two affected households receives a hearing and interim protection where the burden would otherwise become irreversible.

The witnesses do not endorse a design. Rei will accept relocation only as a complete package of land, water, seed, graves, shrine obligations, road access, and possession dates. Hojun prefers the northern channel. Saburo prefers whichever design restores the west road fastest and pays for what it consumes. Kenta prefers the design the provincial administration can eventually maintain.

The Witnesses leave with a more difficult standard: provincial legitimacy does not mean asking Reedwater whether it approves. It means letting Reedwater preserve evidence, challenge implementation, trigger review, and inherit authority with resources.

### Pavilion of First Rain

Doji Masuyo asks Kiyoe to move one cup of rice into the empty Reed Bank basin. Kiyoe moves unhulled rice from a wealthier district. Asahina Chiharu asks whether Kiyoe has just fed Reed Bank or taken its next harvest. Kiyoe returns the cup.

The Witnesses separate the four stores: household relief, seed rice, garrison allotment, and emergency reserve. The military covenant may draw from the garrison allotment. A Seppun-certified emergency may vary it for thirty days and enter the excess as Sword debt. Seed rice requires a separately named trigger, outside concurrence, and an account of next year's deficit.

Masuyo accepts Miya title, Crane civilian execution through the first harvest, outside audit, district successor wardens, and local standing at granaries. She initially asks for three years. Kiyoe does not support her. She says the Crane relief has already built the training network; if it cannot produce successors after one harvest, three years will merely make the failure respectable.

Daidoji Yorishige demands repayment for barges, escorts, lost cargoes, and merchant advances. The Witnesses agree that Accounts will distinguish reimbursement, compensation, honor, and office. Public credit will name the Crane relief in the first recital and proclamation, then close the emergency claim rather than renewing it annually.

Masuyo asks what the Crane receive for surrendering independent armed command over the barges. Naritomo offers a fixed escort schedule under Sword, with civilian granary wardens retaining authority over cargo classification. Kiyoe insists that the military commander cannot reassign relief barges without a defined emergency. Naritomo accepts if the emergency authority includes a repayment entry and a named end.

Chiharu places the sealed seed jar beside the draft. She will bless the transfer of white granary keys to an Imperial titleholder if seed, district wardens, and village standing are written before the ceremony.

Kiyoe records her own grain exception from the flood. She does not present it as wrongdoing. She presents it as evidence that the supply covenant must be real enough that civilian officers need not choose between starving soldiers and surrendering Harvest by improvisation.

By the end of the session, Kiyoe has won most of what she wanted and changed its meaning. The Crane will execute Harvest not because their relief created title, but because the Emperor commissions a proven network to train the people who will replace it.

## Session Three: White keys and red standards

### Hall of Red Standards

Akodo Tadanobu places a granary key, a patrol roster, and a strip of lost standard cloth before Naritomo. He asks which the Lion should surrender first.

Naritomo answers, “The key. The roster after a receiving officer is named. The cloth never.”

Ikoma Noritada says the answer is usable. Matsu Kiyoka asks whether it is honest. Several villages asked the Lion not to leave. The court may call those requests dependence, fear, gratitude, or improper fealty. A withdrawal order still needs someone at the road before dusk.

The Witnesses draft a staged Sword commission:

- Seppun holds title and removal authority.
- Tadanobu executes a real but revocable command.
- The roster declines by district as named provincial patrols, clan detachments, or other lawful replacements accept duty.
- Harvest owes a fixed allotment that declines with the roster.
- The commander may not alter civilian allotments or open seed stores except through the defined emergency process.
- Independent emergency command ends after thirty days unless renewed by a second Imperial officer.
- Autumn review measures replacement capacity and obedience, not merely quiet roads.

Tadanobu asks for three years. Naritomo proposes one hundred twenty days with renewal possible at autumn review. Kiyoka calls that a courtier's number. Eleven villages cannot be trained by arithmetic. Qulan proposes capacity gates with an outer date: each district transfers when its patrol roster, stores, receiving officer, and communications meet stated measures; no Lion company remains beyond one hundred twenty days without a public review and explicit renewal.

Naritomo resists. A failed provincial patrol could make the outer date dangerous. Sadae adds failure succession: Seppun may recommission a clan detachment or combine districts temporarily, but the current executor cannot certify its own indispensability. The record must distinguish a local failure from withheld training or stores.

That clause wins Naritomo. It preserves command when necessary and makes obstruction visible.

The Lion publicly renounce civilian Harvest. In return, the Crane recognize the fixed military quota and surrender independent armed command over their escorts. Tadanobu will lower the red standard before receiving it back beneath Imperial commission. Kiyoka will publish the district replacement schedule. Noritada will record the withdrawal as completion of a successful emergency campaign rather than expulsion.

The reciprocal relinquishment becomes the first stable joint support for the Mandate. It is not friendship. Kiyoe and Naritomo still disagree over the first month's military allotment. They now disagree inside a shared instrument rather than through competing possession.

### Shrine of Listening Water

The Phoenix delegation begins with bells that do not agree. One was recovered from a drowned shrine. One was cast for a proposed replacement. One belongs to a temple that sheltered refugees and now claims the stipend intended for the lost community. Each can be rung. None answers who speaks for the obligation.

Isawa Etsuko distinguishes sacred title, recognized speaker, ritual execution, site judgment, and remedy. Shiba Koremori asks who protects the laborers and displaced households while learned people debate. Asako Chise asks whether the court intends to preserve shrine buildings or the communities that kept them meaningful.

The Witnesses create a consultation sequence: notice, recognized speaker, site examination, written response, decision, record, remedy, and review. The recognized speaker may be a Phoenix priest, a local keeper, or another person named by the affected community and accepted under the charter. No one receives a permanent monopoly merely by arriving with superior rank.

Masami insists on three levels of action. Immediate site safety may proceed. Plan-preserving emergency work may proceed within an approved design and create notice and debt. Political sacrifice—moving a village, destroying a shrine structure, abandoning a protected route, or redirecting flood risk—requires named Imperial authority or an automatic hearing if communication is impossible.

Etsuko accepts emergency bypass if the bypass creates a spiritual debt: restoration, substitute rites, stipends, records, and a deadline rather than an apology.

Hojun's register of displaced keepers becomes the seed of local succession. Phoenix specialists will coordinate reconstruction and train recognized Reedwater speakers. The Shrines office will not become hereditary Phoenix title.

The Witnesses also establish that sacred consultation requires a speaker, procedure, and remedy; that recusal without substitute jurisdiction is a hidden veto; and that public obligations need exact scope. The ritual order of cups gives them access to the Evening of the Chrysanthemum Moon, but they do not yet commit to attending.

Masami leaves frustrated. The court now has procedures for deciding whether her crews may proceed, but no approved design. The next session must choose where the water goes.

## Session Four: The price of the dike

### Hall of Joined Timbers

Kaiu Masashige presents the eastern wall and the northern channel without recommending either as morally superior.

The eastern wall can close before the next heavy rain. It protects the granaries and preserves the west road with less timber and labor. It moves Reed Bank and another village, translates Hojun's shrine community, and pushes greater force toward downstream pasture unless a spill basin is purchased and kept empty.

The northern channel preserves the villages and the shrine community. It creates greater long-term capacity. It cuts the west road, consumes nearly twice the labor and timber, requires new remount stations and a bridge, continues into planting season, and leaves one district outside the strongest protection until its second phase.

Masami supports the northern channel as the better province and the eastern wall as the safer calendar. She proposes a hundred-day emergency brace protecting the most exposed embankments while the northern channel begins. The brace will be abandoned when the channel carries water. Its timber, labor, and earthworks therefore become deliberate duplicate cost.

Qulan accepts the northern channel only if Waters funds the substitute western route, remount stations, bridge, and temporary ford. He demands that road capacity lost during construction be recorded as part of the engineering budget. Masami calls the demand expensive and correct.

Naritomo asks whether soldiers may discharge part of the labor burden. Hida Chihaya agrees if the burden ledger records soldier labor against the districts those soldiers otherwise patrol, rather than pretending military work is free. Kiyoe assigns camp rice outside village seed and household allotments. Yasuki Kameyo requires purpose-bound timber, rope, stone, and iron so every office cannot treat Waters stockpiles as general emergency stores.

The Witnesses adopt the northern channel with the hundred-day brace. This is the playthrough's largest substantive choice. Reed Bank remains in place; Three Crossings and western road households bear transport disruption; the exposed district receives temporary works and priority evacuation routes; the whole province bears additional labor and material cost. The choice is neither cheapest nor fastest.

The Waters commission gives Kaiu execution over design, assigned labor, materials, and work-site command. It does not let the directorate choose political sacrifice. That requires a named Imperial authority, notice to the provincial chair where possible, and automatic review. Masami's own emergency cut enters the burden ledger with the harmed household and restoration debt.

Local maintenance officers begin training immediately. Kaiu specialists certify technical competence but do not control the autumn decision about whether transfer occurs. Miya records, Dragon review, and provincial testimony can establish that training or resources were withheld.

Masami gains the authority she asked for and loses the authority she was most tempted to assume.

### Garden of White Gravel

Seppun Tomoe has raked seven roads toward the Imperial chrysanthemum. One bootprint crosses them. Naritomo recognizes the lesson before she speaks: command is legible where it changes direction.

Tomoe accepts the proposed Lion commission, the hundred-day Waters guards, and Unicorn convoy escorts if each traces to an Imperial commission, a named superior, a term, removal, and failure succession. She will not allow emergency forces to remain because withdrawal is difficult and call the difficulty obedience.

The Witnesses prepare paired acts of submission and field orders:

- Crane granary keys will transfer to the Miya titleholder and return as execution keys after inventory.
- The Lion standard will be lowered and returned under Seppun commission with the declining roster attached.
- Unicorn checkpoint tokens and requisition books will return before Sachiko receives Roads execution.
- Kaiu Masami will place the engineer's rule beneath the Imperial Waters seal and receive it back with the approved design and limits.
- Scorpion Accounts custody, if accepted later, must open to divided record control.

Tomoe asks what happens if Kenta's provincial patrol fails after a Lion district withdraws. Naritomo answers too quickly that the Lion resumes command. Sadae corrects him: the named failure successor acts under Seppun authority; it may be Lion, but no automatic reversion belongs to the former executor.

Naritomo is angry because the distinction sounds designed to shame his clan. Tomoe says it is designed to prevent officers at the scene from deciding that history has restored their title. She offers Naritomo a sharper honor: the Lion will have trained a force capable of receiving an Imperial duty from them.

He accepts.

The Witnesses now have material capacity, military obedience, and provisional transition. They still lack a complete division of title, inspection, recusal, secrecy, and amendments. The route turns at last toward the Imperial ministries.

## Session Five: Masks, ledgers, and the judge who leaves

### Ministry of Divided Ink

Otomo Kazetada has followed the Witnesses' provincial route with interest. He says they have learned to describe every burden except the burden of making the Emperor appear to ratify arrangements already negotiated by others.

His scribes place title, execution, audit, resources, emergency authority, succession, term, review, and remedy beneath each Reed. The Witnesses' bargains immediately expose blanks.

Harvest has no failure successor if the Crane executor is accused by the auditor. Sword has no rule for conflicting Imperial orders. Roads has no independent valuation appeal. Waters has an approved political-sacrifice authority but no substitute if that titleholder is unreachable. Shrines has a recognized-speaker process but no answer when two communities claim the same sacred duty. Accounts does not yet exist as an office at all; the Witnesses have treated records as a property of every other Reed.

Kazetada supports the architecture because no Great Clan holds Harvest, Sword, and Accounts together. He offers Imperial titles and drafting staff. His price is a public autumn review able to renew, narrow, replace, compensate, remove, remedy, and activate failure succession.

Kiyoe asks whether such a review makes every one-harvest promise unstable. Kazetada says the promise is already unstable. The clause decides whether instability appears as law or as surprise.

The Witnesses accept. Each outside executor must train a named successor, transfer records and resources, and disclose failure measures. Current executors cannot certify the end of their own necessity. If they withhold training, records, or assigned resources, the resulting lack of capacity cannot justify automatic renewal.

The Ministry also separates gratitude from office. Crane relief debts, Lion service, Unicorn losses, Kaiu work, Phoenix rites, Dragon records, and Scorpion sources may all receive compensation or credit through different instruments. None becomes title by recital.

Kazetada asks what Accounts will be. The Witnesses have no answer they trust. They go to the theater.

### Theater of a Thousand Sleeves

Bayushi Namika stages an Accounts hearing in which every actor wears the sleeve of an office and no actor appears bodily on stage. A Crane sleeve requests a military variance. A Lion sleeve certifies receipt. A Unicorn sleeve records a wagon. A Kaiu sleeve diverts it. A provincial sleeve petitions. A Scorpion sleeve reports that the wagon never existed under the number used. The audience cannot tell whether the problem is theft, correction, emergency renumbering, or a protected witness.

Namika offers the Scorpion no title. She asks for access, comparison, preservation, protected reporting, interim restraint, and referral for sanction. Soshi Kureha proposes divided custody: common records in Miya custody, sealed source identities under two-key access, findings reviewable by the Dragon circuit, and final dispositions public. Shosuro Mirei insists that a frightened clerk cannot be protected if every intermediate report names the source by inference.

Sadae accepts the need for confidentiality and rejects a secret final authority. The parties agree on the secrecy ladder: public rule, confidential source, sealed interim finding, reviewable finding, public disposition. A source may remain protected after the disposition; the legal reason for the disposition may not.

Namika then offers a private fallback draft. It would preserve the primary architecture but replace the Dragon circuit with a narrower Imperial-Scorpion review if the Dragon obstruct inspection. She suggests letting the Dragon know that a fallback exists without revealing its clauses.

Sadae believes the proposal is designed to make her negotiate under a secret threat. She discloses the offer to the full commission and refuses to use it. The GM records the result plainly. The current fallback has been politically foreclosed. A controlled fallback remains possible in principle, but this draft can no longer function as bounded leverage.

Namika does not deny what she attempted. She says Sadae has correctly destroyed one instrument and not yet replaced it. The distinction matters.

The Witnesses accept bounded Scorpion inspection, divided records, reciprocal audit, protected sources, and public dispositions. Namika publicly relinquishes Accounts title before the moon-viewing. In exchange, the Mandate will make inspection real enough that the title is unnecessary.

### Stone-and-Moss Court

Kitsuki Harunori turns over a stone. Beneath it is an appeal against the person who placed it. The absurdity becomes the day's governing image.

The Witnesses build the common circuit: evidence preservation, written reasons, disclosure of conflicts, recusal, substitute jurisdiction, deadlines, interim orders, appeal, and remedies. Mirumoto Daizen insists that recusal cannot suspend work indefinitely. Togashi Sorin asks who may petition when the law does not expect the claimant, including shrine communities, displaced households, and clerks protected from their own office.

Sadae proposes Dragon execution for one year. Qulan asks why the office designed to end clan dependency should begin with a year of Dragon control. Sadae reduces the term to autumn review and adds a mixed provincial bench trained from the first day. Dragon magistrates administer the initial circuit and act as substitutes where local conflicts are unavoidable. They do not inherit every recusal by default.

Harunori then addresses the destroyed fallback. Two complete proposals may serve one Imperial duty if their differences, burdens, and supporters are disclosed. The Witnesses write a new fallback openly: immediate submission, protected seed, fixed supply, hundred-day Waters authority, bounded Roads, and a later commission for unresolved offices. It is weaker than the primary and genuinely executable.

The GM reopens the fallback conclusion. The Witnesses establish it from a lawful, disclosed alternative rather than Namika's folded threat.

The Stone-and-Moss Court also reveals one danger in the primary. The Waters directorate, Roads execution, and Sword emergency authority can each issue priority orders to the same district. The common circuit can review the conflict later, but a courier needs an order now. The Witnesses add a priority rule: immediate site safety, then life-preserving movement, then food and petitions, then ordinary project schedules; conflicts beyond site safety receive Miya dispatch and Seppun emergency resolution, with automatic review.

The architecture is finally complete enough to be made public. The Witnesses elect to attend the moon-viewing.

## Session Six: Moonlight and ink

### Evening of the Chrysanthemum Moon

The Witnesses prepare four public obligations and are told to reduce them to three. They refuse. The garden officer answers that the evening may contain four bargains, but only three may receive full ceremonial emphasis. The Witnesses must decide which relationship can survive less beauty.

They choose to make Grain and Sword, Roads and Waters, and Accounts and Judgment the formal program. Provincial succession will appear as the empty cushion present in every arrangement rather than a separate performance.

#### White keys beneath red standards

Chiharu places the sealed seed jar before Masuyo. Tadanobu lays the strip of lost standard cloth beside it. Kiyoe recites the four grain stores. Naritomo recites the declining roster.

Masuyo promises to return the white keys to Miya title and accept district standing, outside audit, and one-harvest execution. Tadanobu promises to return every civilian granary key, lower the red standard, accept Seppun removal, and train the provincial patrols that end his commission. Each promise names the fixed supply covenant as a dependency. Neither may later describe the other as unconditional surrender.

Ikoma Noritada gives the public thanks. The Emperor's court will remember that Crane relief prevented famine and Lion command preserved the granaries. The thanks close the emergency claims as sources of title. Accounts debts remain payable.

#### The road beside the new water

Sachiko offers a remount bell as an undertaking rather than a gift of ownership. Masashige offers an engineer's rule under Imperial cord. Qulan names the substitute western route, bridge, stations, horses, ford limits, and restoration debt. Masami names the hundred-day brace, northern channel, exposed district, duplicate cost, and limits of engineering authority.

The bargain becomes public: Roads will execute the substitute network; Waters will fund what its design consumes; every extraordinary closure names its author, term, burden, and route of petition; the directorate cannot certify the end of its own emergency; Roads cannot refuse a lawful site-safety closure merely because its debt is expensive.

#### The mask before the open ledger

Namika offers a mask to the Miya custodian and receives no seat of title. Kureha places common manifests beneath an open ledger. Sadae reads the secrecy ladder and the substitute-jurisdiction clause.

The Scorpion relinquish Accounts title and retain bounded inspection. The Dragon accept protected sources and timely interim restraint. Miya custody, Dragon review, and public disposition prevent either inspector or judge from owning the record alone.

#### The empty cushion of succession

No one sits on the cushion reserved for the future provincial officers. Rei's petition cord lies across it. Kenta's incomplete patrol roster, Hojun's register of keepers, Saburo's horse ledger, and the list of district granary successors rest beneath the cord.

The Witnesses do not pretend Reedwater already possesses the capacity named in the primary. They promise records, stores, training, standing, staged authority, and an autumn test that can transfer power or expose why it was not transferred.

The Emperor's cup remains empty. The evening records obligations among the parties; it does not pretend to invest them with Imperial law.

### Chamber of Seven Reeds

The Chamber turns every moonlit sentence into a stack of documents.

The Mandate grants authority. Recitals give credit without title. Schedules govern demobilization, training, roads, works, and first orders. Covenants carry supply, service, repayment, relocation, and sacred restoration. Proclamations translate the offices for districts. Registers preserve submission, burdens, sources, and successors.

The primary is titled **Reedwater Succession under the Hundred-Day Brace**. Its long-term design is the northern channel. Its immediate protection is a temporary brace and evacuation network. Harvest, Roads, Sword, Judgment, Shrines, and Accounts remain separate from the short Waters concentration.

During drafting, the journal records an incorrect consequence: that the northern channel has already been adopted as the complete Waters design without an intermediate brace. The Witnesses catch the mistake when Shun reads the first forty days and cannot find the temporary protection orders. The mistaken operation is voided rather than silently rewritten. The corrected entry states that the primary adopts the northern channel as the long-term design, authorizes the hundred-day brace and temporary protections, funds substitute roads and surveys, and requires autumn review of phase-two burdens.

The correction matters because several promises depend on the distinction. Qulan accepted a cut road only with substitute capacity. Masami accepted duplicate works only with explicit abandonment criteria. Rei accepted the northern channel because Reed Bank remains. The exposed district accepted delay only with temporary protection and return testimony. Calling the channel simply “adopted” hides the first season in which the Mandate must function.

The ten-part clause test finds three more defects.

First, the provincial successors are named but some resources remain in clan schedules rather than successor schedules. The Witnesses move training stores, duplicate records, initial pay, and office seals into the first-forty-days bundle.

Second, failure succession in Judgment names a substitute judge but not custody of the case record. Sadae adds automatic transfer of records, interim orders, and deadlines.

Third, the autumn review may renew or replace but does not expressly remedy burdens already imposed. Rei's chair adds compensation, restoration, correction, and debt enforcement.

The Witnesses prepare acts of submission and a complete fallback. They also write a dependency register. If the Emperor materially changes the Waters term, Roads capacity, Lion roster, Crane supply covenant, Scorpion secrecy ladder, or provincial standing, affected promises must be reaffirmed rather than presumed.

They leave the Chamber with no clan fully satisfied. That is not their measure. The primary can be proclaimed, supplied, challenged, succeeded, and amended without becoming unrecognizable.

## Session Seven: The broad sheet

### The Second Audience

The broad Imperial sheet lies between primary and fallback. The Witnesses state the purpose in the words first accepted before the throne:

> The Mandate will preserve Reedwater through the next rains, restore lawful provincial government, and ensure that emergency service creates answerable duty rather than private title.

The acts of submission occur before offices are presented. Each visible object is paired with an authenticated field order naming the receiving officer, effective hour, inventory or roster, acknowledgment route, and remedy for refusal or delay.

Masuyo returns the white keys. They are inventoried and returned as execution keys beneath Miya title. Tadanobu lowers the red standard and receives it beneath a Seppun commission with the declining roster attached. Sachiko returns road tokens and requisition books before receiving execution of Roads. Namika delivers common records to divided custody and retains a sealed source register under two-key access. Masami places the engineer's rule beneath the Waters seal and receives it with the approved design, hundred-day term, political-sacrifice limits, and automatic hearing. Etsuko and Hojun lodge the shrine register naming local speakers and restoration debts. Rei places the petition cord across the provincial chair.

The Emperor asks which district pays most.

The Witnesses do not answer “Reedwater.” Qulan names Three Crossings and the western road households for animals, delay, stations, and bridge work. Masami names the district left outside the strongest protection during the northern channel's first phase. Kiyoe names the households whose grain and labor support both reconstruction and the garrison. Rei, permitted to speak, adds that the burden will change by month and that the ledger must be able to show when the answer changes.

The Emperor asks who may stop the engineer without assuming the engineer's work.

Masami answers that site safety remains hers; political sacrifice belongs to the Imperial Waters titleholder; the common circuit may issue interim restraint; Seppun emergency authority resolves immediate conflicts of force; and anyone who stops the approved work must identify the order, burden, substitute action, and record. She does not claim the arrangement is fast in every case. She claims it is fast where speed is technical and answerable where speed is political.

The Emperor asks what the strongest beneficiary surrenders because this is his peace.

Naritomo answers for the Lion: granary keys, independent standards, automatic resumption, and the right to call local dependence fealty. Kiyoe answers for the Crane: Harvest title, indefinite execution, patron ownership of witnesses, and the doctrine that relief debt is jurisdiction. Masami answers for the Hundred-Day Brace: the right to choose the sacrificed district, close Roads without account, and certify its own continuation.

The Emperor asks what happens if provincial capacity is not ready because the current executor did not train it.

The Witnesses' draft says the review may investigate. The Emperor finds that insufficient. He dictates an amendment:

> No holder shall make its own failure to deliver the records, resources, instruction, or staged authority required by this Mandate a cause for renewing the power thereby retained.

The amendment applies across Harvest, Waters, Roads, Sword, Judgment, Shrines, and Accounts. It is material. Every outside executor must reaffirm the affected transition promises.

Masuyo accepts. Tadanobu accepts after Tomoe confirms that a genuinely failed local force may still trigger lawful failure succession. Sachiko accepts. Masashige accepts. Harunori accepts. Etsuko accepts. Namika says the Scorpion did not expect to be thanked for training their replacements and will try to bear the omission.

The Emperor asks for the fallback. Sadae presents it openly: immediate submission, protected seed, fixed military supply, hundred-day Waters authority, bounded Roads, and a later commission for unresolved offices. She states that it exists if the court concludes the primary's successor resources are invented or the common circuit cannot begin. She also states that the fallback would leave Reedwater under outside administration longer and should not be mistaken for prudence without cost.

The Emperor adopts the primary with the transition amendment and two conforming corrections. The Waters titleholder must name a political-sacrifice substitute before the first messenger leaves. The autumn review receives an express power to enforce restoration debt against an office after its original commission ends.

The first orders depart before the audience disperses:

- inventory the four grain stores and transfer the white keys;
- lower and recommission the red standard;
- open the cumulative burden ledger in Reed Bank and Three Crossings;
- release partial horse replacement to single-animal households;
- begin the hundred-day brace and northern-channel surveys;
- mark the substitute western route and fund its first two stations;
- appoint Kenta's first patrol trainees and district receiving officers;
- establish the Dragon-administered circuit and its provincial bench;
- divide custody of Accounts records and publish the first inspection rule;
- recognize Hojun and the other local shrine speakers;
- deliver proclamations and correction routes to every named district witness; and
- convene the autumn review with Rei holding the first rotating provincial chair.

The court names the result **Reedwater Succession under the Hundred-Day Brace**. The Emperor does not call it balanced. “The river will perform that audit,” he says.

As the Witnesses withdraw, a Miya courier kneels at the threshold with the eastern rain report. The first drops darken the travel paper before the last seal is dry. The orders leave the palace ahead of the weather.

## Final architecture

### Harvest

- Imperial title in Miya custody.
- Crane civilian execution through the first harvest.
- Household relief and seed assessed before military allotment.
- Declining fixed supply covenant for Sword.
- Outside audit and district successor wardens.
- Village petition standing and protected seed triggers.

### Waters

- Imperial title with Kaiu execution for one hundred days.
- Northern channel as the long-term design.
- Temporary brace, evacuation, and exposed-district protections.
- Assigned labor, materials, guards, and work-site command.
- Named political-sacrifice authority, automatic hearing, and restoration debt.
- Provincial maintenance succession and an end not certified by the directorate itself.

### Roads

- Miya title and Unicorn execution through the first planting season.
- One dispatch law, minimum open route, common manifests, and no private toll privilege.
- Prior valuation where possible and automatic debt where it is not.
- Waters-funded substitute route, bridge, remount stations, and restoration obligations.
- District station successors and public correction routes.

### Sword

- Seppun title, removal, and emergency supervision.
- Lion execution under a declining district roster and an outer limit of one hundred twenty days without explicit review.
- Fixed supply, no granary seizure, and bounded emergency variance.
- Transfer to Kenta's provincial patrol structure as named capacities are met.
- Failure succession without automatic reversion to the former executor.

### Judgment

- Imperial title and Dragon-administered common circuit through autumn review.
- Written reasons, recusal, substitute jurisdiction, deadlines, interim orders, appeal, and remedies.
- Protected-source procedure and timely emergency review.
- Mixed provincial bench, records, pay, and staged authority from the first forty days.

### Shrines

- Imperial protection without permanent Phoenix title.
- Phoenix consultation and ritual coordination during reconstruction.
- Local recognized speakers and community succession.
- Emergency bypass with record, remedy, spiritual debt, and review.
- Restoration of communities and obligations rather than buildings alone.

### Accounts

- Imperial title and Miya custody of common records.
- Scorpion access, comparison, preservation, protected reporting, and interim restraint.
- Dragon review and public disposition.
- Two-key access to source identities and no permanently secret final authority.
- Debts surviving the commissions that created them.

### Provincial standing and transition

- Proclamations and corrections delivered to named district witnesses.
- Access to the common burden ledger.
- Petition without patron ownership.
- Review triggers and return testimony.
- Autumn review empowered to renew, narrow, replace, compensate, correct, remove, remedy, enforce debt, and activate failure succession.
- No executor may use withheld training, records, resources, or staged authority as grounds for renewal.

## Political consequences

- Kiyoe obtains civilian Harvest and loses the longer Crane claim. Her own emergency grain exception becomes the reason the supply covenant is enforceable rather than an embarrassment hidden from the record.
- Naritomo obtains real command and accepts a short outer term, visible submission, local training, and no automatic resumption. His idea of victory changes from holding the line to creating the force that can receive it.
- Sadae obtains the common circuit and accepts protected sources, deadlines, interim action, and provincial succession. She destroys one secret fallback and helps build a lawful one.
- Masami obtains the Hundred-Day Brace and the northern channel. She loses the power to choose political sacrifice, conceal road costs, or certify her own necessity. Her earlier orchard cut becomes an entered debt.
- Qulan obtains bounded Roads, a common requisition chain, substitute capacity, and local successors. He accepts lawful closures and a reconstruction priority that sometimes outranks movement.
- The Crane and Lion receive public thanks that close rather than renew their emergency claims.
- The Phoenix preserve sacred obligations without acquiring a veto.
- The Dragon create one law without owning every judgment indefinitely.
- The Scorpion gain real inspection without title or final secrecy.
- The Unicorn gain execution without toll privilege.
- The Crab gain authority equal to technical work without authority equal to the entire province.
- The Imperial families retain authorship, title, removal, proclamation, and review while assigning the resources required to make those powers more than court language.
- Reedwater bears a costly design and continued outside execution. It also gains records, compensation, standing, successor resources, and a review designed to transfer power rather than to admire performance.

## What remains unresolved

The Mandate does not make every conflict disappear.

- The northern channel may consume too much labor during planting.
- The Hundred-Day Brace may fail before the channel is useful.
- The substitute western route may not support stone wagons in rain.
- Kenta's provincial patrols may not be ready by the district transfer dates.
- Crane wardens may train successors who remain politically dependent on Crane stores.
- Scorpion source protection may create suspicion even when it works exactly as written.
- The Dragon circuit may be too slow or too influential.
- The autumn review may discover that the court assigned remedies without enough money to pay them.
- Reed Bank may survive the design and still resent the Empire that made its survival a courtly instrument.

These are not omissions concealed by the ending. They are the first campaign consequences created by this demonstrated government.
